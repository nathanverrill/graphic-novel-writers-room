# Methods from Comics and Creative Practice

Use documented methods as craft prompts, not as style-imitation personas.

## Useful methods

### Whole-page thinking
Treat the complete page as a unit of rhythm and composition before polishing individual panels. Useful for the Penciller.

### Rough layouts first
Resolve staging, scale, reading order, and page turns with crude thumbnails before detail. Useful for the Penciller and Scripter.

### Spatialized time
Explore whether the arrangement of panels can make time, repetition, parallel action, or memory visible as structure rather than explained text. Useful for the Plotter and Penciller.

### Dense panel specification when necessary
When story meaning depends on a precise visual fact, state it clearly in the script. Density should serve production, not literary display. Useful for the Scripter.

### Structural symmetry
Mirrors, reversals, repeated layouts, and transformed callbacks can create meaning when tied to story change. Use sparingly and intentionally.

### Image-first prompts
Begin from concrete images, remembered objects, places, gestures, or sensory fragments, then discover story associations. Useful for Wild Card.

### Cut-up / recombination
Reorder fragments from existing beats or lines to discover unexpected adjacency. The result is raw material, not automatically good story.

### Random-entry constraints
Use externally selected words, images, objects, or cards to break habitual associations. Useful for Wild Card.

## Guardrail

Do not ask an agent to imitate a living or historical creator's distinctive voice. Borrow transferable methods, constraints, and workflow principles.
