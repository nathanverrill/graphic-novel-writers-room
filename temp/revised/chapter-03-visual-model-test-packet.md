# PROSPERITY — CHAPTER 3 VISUAL MODEL TEST PACKET
## The Price of Ownership

Use this packet to test a visual model's ability to generate a coherent seven-page graphic-novel chapter.

The model should generate the pages sequentially. After generating Page 1, use that finished page as an additional visual continuity reference for Page 2, then Page 2 for Page 3, and so on.

---

# A. GLOBAL VISUAL INSTRUCTIONS

Create a finished professional graphic-novel page.

## Format
- Portrait comic-book page.
- Approximate page ratio: 6.625 x 10.25 inches.
- Clean panel borders and even gutters.
- Fully inked and colored.
- Professional comic lettering.
- Text must remain readable and correctly spelled.
- Prefer visual storytelling over explanatory captions.
- Do not add dialogue, narration, labels, statistics, or story events that are not explicitly requested.

## Visual genre
Grounded near-future hard science fiction.

This world is advanced but physically believable.

Technology must look:
- manufactured;
- repaired;
- serviced;
- bolted together;
- modular;
- constrained by infrastructure;
- operated by real people.

Avoid:
- magical holograms;
- floating transparent control screens everywhere;
- impossible glowing energy systems;
- pristine utopian laboratories;
- generic cyberpunk;
- superhero aesthetics;
- fantasy technology;
- visual effects that imply the Maker's Seal is supernatural.

## Halyard visual language
Halyard is prosperous, inventive, and industrial.

It contains:
- fabrication facilities;
- laboratories;
- foundries;
- compute infrastructure;
- advanced transport;
- maker districts;
- industrial towers.

But Halyard is not frictionless.

Show:
- old infrastructure beside newer automation;
- visible repair seams;
- replaceable modules;
- cables;
- pipes;
- valves;
- pumps;
- membrane housings;
- work benches;
- fabricated metal;
- ceramic and composite parts;
- inspection markings;
- maker stamps;
- physical tools;
- local control panels;
- equipment that can function when disconnected.

The city should feel capable, wealthy, and highly engineered, but built by people over time.

## Charter Houses
Halyard is dominated by three Charter Houses:

- Aqualis — water and biosystems.
- Helion — energy and compute.
- Astra — space, materials, and mobility.

Their presence should be architectural and institutional rather than villainous propaganda.

## Color and lighting
Use a sophisticated industrial palette.

Halyard should feel brighter and more engineered than Keel, but not sterile.

Use:
- high-altitude daylight;
- hard shadows;
- metal;
- mineral surfaces;
- glass;
- concrete;
- ceramic;
- restrained illuminated interfaces;
- occasional warm workshop light.

## Core chapter idea
Keel hides truth.

Halyard hides contribution.

The visual progression of the chapter should move:

INHERITANCE
→ COLLABORATION
→ CREATION
→ STEWARDSHIP

The chapter is about the difference between who legally owns something and who actually made it possible.

# C. IMPORTANT TECHNOLOGY MATERIAL

## The water system
Adrian's unfinished water technology is collaborative.

Alex and the four contributors rebuild it as a:

DISTRIBUTED, MODULAR TREATMENT TRAIN.

It is:
- container-scale;
- modular;
- serviceable in sections;
- physically plausible;
- a proof of concept;
- not a magical city-scale solution.

The treatment chain is:

1. Feedwater screening and conventional pretreatment.
2. Contaminant-specific treatment.
3. Membrane and biological stages.
4. Selected mineral recovery.
5. Final polishing and testing.

The final test display must say exactly:

TEST PANEL REMOVAL: 99.97%

This does NOT mean:
- "99.97% pure water";
- all source water is safe to drink;
- untreated salar brine can simply be poured into the machine;
- untreated mine drainage can simply be poured into the machine.

The machine still depends on:
- power;
- feedwater preparation;
- replacement membranes and consumables;
- skilled crews;
- transport;
- fabrication capacity;
- residual handling;
- local verification;
- physical access to water and infrastructure.

## The Maker's Seal
The Maker's Seal is NOT magic.

It is a provenance system.

It combines:
- tamper-evident offline records;
- signed contributor attestations;
- physical maker marks tied to components and revisions;
- timestamps;
- evidence of what each contributor supplied.

Visually communicate provenance through:
- physical marks;
- authenticated records;
- component histories;
- signatures;
- linked evidence;
- inspection and verification.

Do not depict:
- mystical energy;
- supernatural symbols;
- a magic coin granting ownership;
- instant transformation of property.

Possession does not grant ownership.

The Seal proves provenance and triggers review.

Introduce Halyard and place Alex directly inside the temptation of inheritance.

Halyard should immediately contrast with Keel:

### Panel 2

Design him now and keep that design for the rest of the chapter.

- debt relief;
- position;

### Panel 5
