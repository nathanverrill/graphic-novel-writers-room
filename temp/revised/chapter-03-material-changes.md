# Chapter 3 — Material Changes

## Open-item resolutions applied

### 1. Purification scale
**Resolution:** Container-scale modular proof of concept with limited throughput. Halyard can fabricate more units, but deployment is constrained by power, feedwater, membranes, crews, transport, maintenance, and operating access.

**Why:** Preserves the fixed decision that the technology changes bargaining power without ending scarcity while matching the research on difficult logistics and infrastructure in the Andean high desert.

### 2. Meaning of the 99.97% result
**Resolution:** Replaced the ambiguous phrase **"99.97% water purity"** with **"TEST PANEL REMOVAL: 99.97%"** for a specified feedwater at the final treatment stage.

**Why:** The regional research identifies arsenic, boron, salts, acid drainage, and other source-specific hazards. A universal purity claim would overstate what one treatment train can establish.

### 3. Treatment chain
**Resolution:** The prototype is a polishing and recovery system inside a larger treatment train: screening/pretreatment → source-specific contaminant treatment → membrane/biological stages → selected mineral recovery → final polishing/testing.

**Why:** Prevents the prototype from functioning like a magic box and makes Lina, Suri, Tomas Reed, and Jalen technically indispensable.

### 4. Potable-water claim
**Resolution:** The prototype does not automatically produce certified drinking water. Potable use requires local testing, residual handling, and authorization.

**Why:** Distinguishes laboratory performance from real-world deployment and preserves scarcity and public-health stakes.

### 5. Missing contributors
**Resolution:** Gave the four collaborators distinct functional contributions:
- Lina Vey: membrane chemistry
- Tomas Reed: fabrication/modular hardware
- Suri Anik: microbial systems
- Jalen Orr: controls/sensing/integration

**Why:** Converts "erased contribution" from exposition into a causal requirement: the system cannot be reconstructed without the people omitted from the record.

### 6. Maker's Seal mechanism
**Resolution:** The Seal is a provenance mechanism using tamper-evident offline records, signed contributor attestations, physical maker marks, timestamps, and evidence.

**Why:** Keeps it physical and legible on the page while avoiding a magical ownership device. Possession does not grant authority.

### 7. 71.3% disputed-assets reveal
**Resolution:** The 71.3% figure now means assets with disputed ownership or contribution claims, not property instantly reassigned by the Seal.

**Why:** Aligns with the fixed constitutional rule that safeguard records trigger publication, freezes, audit, and human adjudication.

### 8. Alex's authority
**Resolution:** Alex can restore contributor rights within Adrian's estate because he controls that inheritance choice. He cannot adjudicate all of Halyard.

**Why:** Reinforces the book's central distinction between having power and having legitimate authority.

### 9. Charter Houses
**Resolution:** The Houses remain materially useful after the reveal because they still provide capital, certification, fabrication, infrastructure, and operating capacity.

**Why:** Avoids making concentrated institutions cartoonishly useless and preserves the existing story rule that the Houses do not simply disappear.

### 10. Lock's argument
**Resolution:** Strengthened Lock's case: consolidated ownership provides certification, liability, fabrication, and deployment responsibility.

**Why:** Makes Alex's refusal a meaningful choice rather than an obvious rejection of a villain.

### 11. Public sequence
**Resolution:** The Maker's Seal publishes Halyard provenance records immediately after activation; disputed registry entries freeze and enter audit/review while the Measure's Keel findings remain active in parallel.

**Why:** Preserves the fixed sequence in which the Measure reveals first, the Maker's Seal reveals second, and the Covenant later joins them into the restored governance system.

### 12. Alex and Ada
**Resolution:** Did not force a romance decision into Chapter 3. Their bond stays intimate and important, but the chapter's turning point remains about ownership and stewardship.

**Why:** The relationship-status open item is not necessary to resolve the Chapter 3 ownership plot, so the revision avoids adding unsupported romantic canon.

## Structural effect

The chapter now has a cleaner causal chain:

**inherit estate → discover missing contributors → fail to reconstruct without them → build collaboratively → prove bounded technical result → verify provenance → expose registry conflict → refuse sole ownership → trigger human review → create regional interdependence**

The material change is not a new plot. It is a more rigorous version of the existing one, with the technology, ownership mechanism, and constitutional consequences made explicit enough to survive later scripting and visual production.
