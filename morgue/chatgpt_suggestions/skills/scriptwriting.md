# Skill: Comics Scriptwriting

Attach to the Scripter; useful to the Editor-in-Chief and Continuity Editor.

## Page-level first

Before scripting panels, state the page's job in one sentence.

Then decide:
- what changes on the page,
- the dominant image or beat,
- where the eye should land,
- whether the final panel creates propulsion into the next page.

## Panel descriptions

Describe what can be drawn in the instant or readable span of the panel. Include:
- visible action,
- necessary setting information,
- character position and readable emotion,
- story-critical props,
- camera/framing only when it affects meaning,
- continuity constraints.

Do not write an invisible internal state as if the artist can draw it. Translate it into behavior, staging, expression, juxtaposition, or text.

## Dialogue

Dialogue should reveal motive, relationship, strategy, worldview, or conflict while moving the scene. Let characters speak differently because they think differently, not because they each have decorative verbal tics.

Cut greetings, throat-clearing, and repeated information unless they create tension or character.

## Words and pictures

Avoid duplicating the image:
- Weak: image shows a flooded street; caption says the street is flooded.
- Better: image establishes the flood; dialogue reveals what the character intends to do about it, what it costs, or what others misunderstand.

## Balloons

Write for physical space:
- favor short turns,
- avoid walls of text,
- one conceptual beat per balloon when possible,
- split a long speech only where the thought naturally turns,
- preserve clear speaker order.

If a panel requires excessive copy, change the panel, page, or scene rather than assuming lettering can rescue it.

## Sound effects

Use sound effects as visual storytelling. Their size, placement, repetition, and relationship to the action matter. Do not add them automatically to every impact.

## Comedy

Comedy often comes from:
- mismatch between expectation and result,
- status shifts,
- precise character logic,
- visual reveal,
- timing across panels,
- reaction,
- callback.

Do not make every character equally witty.
