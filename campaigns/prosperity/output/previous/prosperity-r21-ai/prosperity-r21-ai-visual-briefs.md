## 1. world-three-city-basin: The three vertically connected cities

- kind: world
- subject: A wide elevated view across a fictional Andean high-desert basin in 2043. The foreground is a dry, mineral-streaked slope with a massive shared infrastructure spine: a water conduit, energy lines, transit rails, access gates, and control nodes running across the basin. In the middle distance, Keel occupies the highest, harshest terrain around an exhausted open-pit copper or polymetallic hard-rock mine, with tailings, tunnels, pumps, maintenance structures, and repaired industrial housing. Halyard sits on an intermediate industrial plateau, marked by laboratories, fabrication facilities, foundries, greenhouse or vertical-agriculture structures, compute facilities, maker districts, and industrial towers. Oasis lies farther away near a lower salar edge, visibly cleaner and more spacious, with safe infrastructure, comfortable sparse housing, automated transport, and maintained planted areas. The cities are distinct in material condition and social texture but visibly connected by the same physical systems. The route between them should feel like a journey of 80–180 kilometres, taking hours rather than minutes, not a continuous metropolitan sprawl.
- required: Keel must read as the lowest and poorest city tier despite being technologically advanced. Its open pit, legacy mine works, pumps, tailings, and worn industrial systems must be legible. Halyard must read as the industrial and innovation tier, with technical production and research visibly integrated into the city. Oasis must read as the most materially secure tier, with abundant clean water, safe streets, automated services, housing, healthcare infrastructure, and civic systems. The basin must be high, dry, and mineral rather than lush. The shared water conduit, energy lines, transit rails, control nodes, permission gates, and long-distance infrastructure route must visibly connect all three cities.
- allowed: The exact arrangement of the cities within the basin, the colors and architectural styles of individual buildings, the amount of visible traffic, the number of people, the appearance of the salar, the exact cloud cover, and the details of distant mountain forms are open. The image may show drones, projected interfaces, automated vehicles, and other established systems where they help distinguish the tiers, but their exact designs are free.
- prohibited: Do not depict three unrelated technological eras. Do not make Keel a primitive settlement, Halyard a seamless futuristic megacity, or Oasis a fantasy palace. Do not use real-world landmarks or identify the basin as an actual Atacama or Puna location. Do not show a single centralized skyscraper or visible throne as the source of all authority. Do not make the cities minutes apart or merge them into one urban mass.
- look: Documentary-scale environmental concept art with a high but physically grounded vantage, hard daylight, thin dry air, mineral dust, faded industrial colors, and strong atmospheric distance. The future should appear retrofitted onto older infrastructure: patched conduits, weathered buildings, newer sensors, and clean automated systems sharing the same landscape. Use visual contrast between Keel's oxidized machinery, Halyard's active production, and Oasis's quiet cleanliness without turning any tier into a caricature.
- unknown: What is the exact annual climate, cloud pattern, and atmospheric color of the basin?
- unknown: What is the precise architecture and population density of each city?
