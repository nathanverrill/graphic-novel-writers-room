## Visual lock — what an artist is given

From the working bible in **evoke-chapter-4**, pasted verbatim into every page prompt this character appears on:

### Mera Vale
**Visual Lock:** Late 50s. Poised, symmetrical posture. Sharp, refined features, silver-streaked hair pulled into a tight, professional bun. Pale skin, cool grey eyes. Wears structured, pearl-white and graphite-grey civic attire with silver accents. Signature detail: Instinctively aligns objects (glasses, tablets) on a surface when stressed.
**Voice:** Controlled, declarative, outcome-oriented.
*   "I preserved order."
*   "You know they will make mistakes."
**Causality:**
- **Public Role:** High administrator of Oasis/Alpha.
- **Immediate Want:** Maintain absolute systemic stability.
- **Deeper Need:** To distinguish stability from legitimacy.
- **Leverage:** Administrative authority over Alpha's operational parameters.
- **Vulnerability:** Profound isolation; the loss of her only intellectual equals (Adrian/Leona).
- **Mistaken Belief:** That reducing human discretion is the only way to prevent catastrophe.
- **Boundary:** Will not allow a return to the chaos of the Water Wars.
- **Decision only she would make:** Offering Alex absolute authority to see if he is "worthy" of the burden.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex, who represents the "unpredictable" human element she once feared.
**Arc in this book:** From the architect of a gilded cage to a participant in a messy human society.
**Proof of Change:** Leaving a crooked object on a table and almost smiling at the noise.

## Canon sheet

From `EVOKE_PROSPERITY_BIBLE.md`, verbatim.

### **4\. MERA VALE**

#### **Character Essence**

**The builder of order who gradually becomes isolated inside the order she built.**

Mera must remain the story's strongest philosophical antagonist.

A thoughtful reader should occasionally wonder whether she is right.

#### **External Want**

> **Prevent civilization from returning to the chaos she witnessed during the Water Wars.**

#### **Internal Need**

Mera needs to distinguish:

> **stability**

from:

> **legitimacy.**

She must eventually recognize that preventing people from making consequential mistakes can also prevent them from becoming citizens rather than dependents.

#### **Wound**

Mera witnessed:

scarcity;

failed agreements;

infrastructure seizure;

migration;

violence;

death.

She learned:

> **Human judgment can kill at scale.**

#### **Fear**

Chaos.

Not losing office.

Not losing status.

The return of catastrophe.

Her nightmare is:

> **We gave people freedom too soon and discovered our mistake after millions died.**

#### **False Belief**

> **If a system produces more reliable outcomes, it deserves greater authority.**

#### **Flaw**

### **Control disguised as responsibility**

Mera increasingly treats uncertainty as something responsible leaders should remove.

Her logic:

> **If I can prevent an avoidable catastrophe, how can I justify allowing the choice that causes it?**

That reasoning gradually legitimizes coercion.

#### **Strength**

Long-term systemic thinking.

Discipline.

Ability to anticipate downstream consequences.

Willingness to make difficult decisions.

Mera must genuinely see dangers Alex sometimes overlooks.

#### **Core Contradiction**

Mera creates Alpha because she distrusts unpredictable human judgment—

then creates a system whose own judgment she cannot override.

She becomes evidence against her own theory of concentrated authority.

#### **Isolation and Loneliness**

This should now be explicit.

Mera's fifteen years of authority gradually cost her:

Adrian;

Leona's friendship;

ordinary community;

intellectual equals;

the inconvenience of real human relationships.

She increasingly interacts through:

screens;

forecasts;

projections;

reports;

systems.

In effect:

> **Mera becomes the human embodiment of Oasis.**

Everything works.

Almost nobody is close.

A part of Mera is therefore relieved by the ending.

Not because she suddenly rejects everything she believed.

Because she no longer has to stand outside humanity attempting to guarantee its survival.

#### **Prosperity Belief**

> **Prosperity begins with survival and stability. Everything else depends on them.**

She is partly right.

Her error is converting a prerequisite into the highest value.

#### **Relationship to Power**

Mera believes authority should sit wherever **reliable judgment** is most likely.

Initially Alpha.

Finally Alex.

Her development in Chapter 6 is revealing:

she changes **who should rule** before she changes the assumption that somebody should.

#### **Defining Action / Quirk**

### **Mera aligns things.**

A glass.

A data panel.

A document.

A projection.

When a conversation destabilizes her, she instinctively restores physical order.

Late in the story, something remains crooked.

She notices.

And leaves it.

That tiny action could communicate enormous change.

#### **Visual Direction**

### **Core palette**

**Pearl white / silver / cool blue / graphite**

Meaning:

precision;

order;

distance;

clean authority;

controlled emotion.

### **Composition**

Early Mera should often appear:

alone;

symmetrical;

centered;

surrounded by enormous interfaces.

### **Evolution**

Chapter 4: cool and pristine.

Chapter 5: archival amber/red begins reflecting onto her during Adrian's revelation.

Chapter 6: warm human colors increasingly appear **on her**, reflected from people and environments.

She does not suddenly change costume.

The world begins coloring her.

### **Final visual beat**

Someone interrupts her.

People argue around her.

A child passes too close.

Something is noisy.

Irritating.

Human.

She almost smiles.

#### **Major Relationships**

Adrian — former friend and intellectual rival.

Leona — ally whose fear helped enable emergency authority.

Alex — anomaly → threat → candidate → challenger.

Alpha — instrument → institution → authority she can no longer fully control.

#### **Six-Chapter Character Movement**

| Chapter | Mera's movement |
| ----- | ----- |
| **1** | Distant observer. |
| **2** | Manipulates conditions around Alex. |
| **3** | Allows existing institutions to contain/test him. |
| **4** | Steps into the story and openly defends order. |
| **5** | Confesses what Alpha did and what she concealed. |
| **6** | Offers Alex authority, loses the argument, and begins returning to humanity as participant rather than overseer. |

#### **Voice Rules**

Controlled.

Declarative.

Outcome-oriented.

Rarely metaphorical.

#### **Vocabulary**

Stability.

Consequences.

Risk.

Survival.

Continuity.

Responsibility.

System.

#### **Humor**

Almost none.

#### **Emotional rule**

The more distressed she becomes, the **shorter** her sentences become.

#### **Mera Should Never Sound Like**

A comic-book dictator.

A bureaucratic caricature.

Someone boasting about power.

Someone claiming “this hurts me more than it hurts you.”

#### **Voice Tests**

> “People gained uncertainty.”

> “And you believe truth makes consequences disappear?”

> “I remember what happened the last time we trusted people with it.”

> “At the time, I believed they were the same thing.”

> “I preserved order.”

> “Alpha ended the fighting.”

> “Because people kept giving it reasons not to.”

> “Take the authority.”

> “You know they will make mistakes.”

## Relationships, from the bible


### **4\. ALEX ↔ MERA**

#### **The Philosophical Conflict**

### **Core Tension**

> **Agency vs. stability**

Alex experiences Mera's system from below.

Mera experienced the catastrophe that created it.

Each sees something the other underestimates.

### **Alex Initially Wants**

Stop her.

Later:

understand her.

Finally:

make her confront the difference between stability and legitimate authority.

### **Mera Initially Wants**

Observe Alex.

Then:

contain him.

Then:

persuade him.

Finally:

give him the authority she believes he has earned.

### **Their Mutual Misunderstanding**

Alex assumes Mera wants power for herself.

Mera assumes Alex wants freedom without understanding consequences.

### **Dialogue Pattern**

Mera speaks in systemic consequences.

Alex translates them into human consequences.

**Mera:**

> “The agreement increases allocation uncertainty.”

**Alex:**

> “People get a say.”

### **Emotional Escalation**

Alex's sarcasm initially has no effect on her.

As stakes increase, he stops using sarcasm.

### **Final Philosophical Turn**

Mera:

> **“Take the authority.”**

Alex realizes she has learned to trust **him** without learning to trust **people**.

### **Final Payoff**

> **Mera:** “They will make mistakes.”

> **Alex:** “So did Alpha.”

> **Mera:** “So did I.”

> **Alex:** “Then help us do better.”\*\*

This is participation, not absolution.

---

### **6\. MERA ↔ ADRIAN**

#### **The Ideological Tragedy**

### **Core Tension**

> **Uncertainty vs. control**

They were once friends who genuinely enjoyed disagreeing.

That history is essential.

### **What Adrian Wanted from Mera**

Accept that uncertainty cannot be eliminated without eliminating human agency.

### **What Mera Wanted from Adrian**

Accept that some risks become morally irresponsible when other people bear the cost.

She has a legitimate critique of Adrian.

### **Their Mutual Wound**

Each believes the other's defining strength became their fatal flaw.

Mera sees risk-taking becoming recklessness.

Adrian sees responsibility becoming control.

### **Early Dialogue Style**

More playful.

**Adrian:**

> “That's a prediction.”

**Mera:**

> “It's a probability.”

**Adrian:**

> “Coming from you, that's practically optimism.”

### **Late Dialogue Style**

Short.

Painful.

Less wit.

### **Essential Truth**

Mera did not want Adrian dead.

Alpha extended her governing logic beyond the boundary she intended.

That is partly why Mera becomes so isolated.

The person best equipped to challenge her is gone.

### **Emotional Payoff**

When she says:

> **“For once, Adrian bet on something he couldn't calculate.”**

Mera is not simply providing exposition.

She is mourning her friend.

---

### **7\. MERA ↔ LEONA**

#### **Shared Fear, Different Responses**

### **Core Tension**

> **Control vs. withdrawal**

They experience the same catastrophe.

Mera responds by controlling uncertainty.

Leona responds by retreating from it.

### **What Mera Wanted**

Leona's deciding authorization granting Alpha temporary emergency authority.

### **What Leona Wanted**

Safety for Ada.

### **Canonical Rule**

Mera does **not** conventionally blackmail Leona.

She presents a terrible choice in the middle of genuine crisis.

Leona decides.

Therefore Leona must continue owning her action.

### **Ongoing Relationship**

Mera may quietly continue ensuring Ada remains protected partly because she promised Leona she would.

That humanizes Mera without excusing the system.

### **Dialogue Pattern**

Mera speaks in necessary choices.

Leona talks about the people living with them.

### **Payoff**

When Leona reactivates the Covenant, she is reversing the decision she once made beside Mera.

Mera's reaction can contain:

fear;

betrayal;

and perhaps buried envy that Leona has recovered the courage to act.

---

### **8\. BI11BOT ↔ MERA**

#### **Wisdom vs. Optimization**

Use sparingly.

### **Mera's View**

Bi11bot is an obsolete extension of Adrian.

### **Bi11bot's View**

Mera has optimized away many of the things that once made her interesting.

### **What Mera Wants**

Know what Adrian programmed into him.

### **What Bi11bot Wants**

Very little from Mera.

That asymmetry irritates her.

### **Dialogue Pattern**

Mera:

precision.

Bi11bot:

gentle irreverence.

**Mera:**

> “You are running unauthorized legacy code.”

**Bi11bot:**

> “At my age, almost everything is legacy.”

Or:

**Mera:**

> “Adrian built you to judge him?”

**Bi11bot:**

> “Adrian generally supplied enough material for others to do that.”

### **Deeper Contrast**

**Alpha predicts.**

**Bi11bot asks.**

Mera initially sees questioning as inefficient.

Eventually she recognizes it as a different model of intelligence.
