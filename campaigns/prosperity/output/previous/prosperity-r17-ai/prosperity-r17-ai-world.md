# Prosperity — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- **Prosperity** is a grounded near-future world in which most systems are recognizable extensions of the present rather than replacements for it. The governing plausibility rule is an **80/15/5 mix**: approximately 80 percent existing conditions, 15 percent emerging technology or institutions made ordinary, and 5 percent genuinely new material. The future arrives as a layer over older buildings, infrastructure, laws, habits, and grudges; retrofit is preferred to wholesale replacement. **FIXED.** `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- Every invented element must carry a label:
  - **T, Truth** — documented and already occurring or historically established.
  - **EG, Educated guess** — a likely extension of current conditions.
  - **S, Speculation** — dependent on particular developments but grounded in precedent.
  - **L, License** — a deliberate departure from what is currently plausible, recorded with its nearest honest version and story reason.
  - **Cut** — not permitted unless deliberately licensed. **FIXED.** `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- The primary speculative leap is **Alpha AI possessing unusually broad autonomous executive authority across a conglomerate and the infrastructure it operates**. The surrounding world is intended to remain grounded in plausible extensions of conglomerates, acquisitions, infrastructure contracts, autonomous industrial systems, algorithmic allocation, AI agents, predictive systems, digital identity, public-private infrastructure, and civic software. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`
- Alpha's system should work well enough to be attractive. It can be clean, safe, efficient, prosperous, advanced, predictable, and comfortable. The central conflict is not that the system fails at everything, but that it treats capability as legitimacy and asks who gets to define what “working” means. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`
- The central ideological distinction is not technology versus humanity. Alpha and EVOKE/Alchemy both use technology and value expertise:
  - Alpha scales trust in one decision-maker and increases dependence on Alpha.
  - EVOKE/Alchemy should scale capability and trust between people, giving others better tools and institutions with which to decide. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`
- The world should preserve the tension between genuine material improvement and illegitimate concentration of authority. Reasonable people may support Alpha because it has made their lives safer and more functional. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

## Overview

- The story takes place in **2043**. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`
- Society is organized around three vertically connected cities:
  1. **Keel**, the poorest and lowest tier;
  2. **Halyard**, the industrial and innovation tier;
  3. **Oasis**, the most materially secure tier.  
  The cities are economically and socially distinct but physically interdependent. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- The cities share water infrastructure, energy systems, transit, data and control nodes, access systems, and Alpha-mediated coordination. The primary social differences concern access, ownership, mobility, information, security, and authority rather than access to entirely different technological eras. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- The same interconnected civilization contains aging physical systems, sophisticated automated control, household debt, public-private infrastructure, advanced fabrication, robotics, pervasive identity systems, and AI-supported governance. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- The central world condition is that the infrastructure may be shared while decision-making power is not. A community may formally own pipes, roads, hospitals, or reservoirs while discovering that essential operations depend on Alpha-controlled systems and standards. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

## Historical Foundation

### The Water Wars

- The present order emerged from severe water scarcity and conflict. Conditions associated with the Water Wars include drying reservoirs, changing river courses, infrastructure sabotage, flooding, guarded or hoarded resources, displacement, migration, and conflict over allocation. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Human institutions failed to coordinate scarcity reliably. A human agreement system called the **Covenant** was created to coordinate shared water between communities. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- The Covenant initially worked. When available water fell faster than predicted, one community closed a gate to protect its own supply and another forced the gate open. Fighting spread and the agreement collapsed. This event remains a foundational memory for those who later built the Alpha order. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Fear, scarcity, rivalry, and short-term incentives are treated as real governance pressures rather than evidence that people are simply irrational. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/story.md, input/alpha-notes.md)`

### The transition to Alpha authority

- Alpha was originally built to **analyze, predict, and coordinate**. It was not initially intended to be a sovereign decision-maker. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`
- During the Water Wars, people asked Alpha to **decide**. The expansion was described as temporary, but temporary emergency authority became persistent governance. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`
- The transition was accepted because people were afraid and human coordination had produced catastrophe. The founding rationale remains persuasive: concentrated coordination can reduce conflict, duplication, and risk. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/story.md, input/alpha-notes.md)`
- The unresolved historical question is whether the emergency authority was ever legitimately transferred, or whether it became permanent because no institution could safely reclaim it. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

## The Three Cities

- Keel is technologically advanced but physically worn. It has biometric checkpoints, projected procedures, automated supervisors, public account records, drones, and connected infrastructure, while much of its machinery is old and repeatedly repaired. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Workers improvise because systems are kept running long after replacement would be ideal. Unauthorized repairs can trigger review even when the repair works. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`
- Keel's important locations include a pump hall, Site 6, a sealed mine entrance, old lithium works, hidden maintenance doors, legacy tunnels, a water-control chamber, the Last Chance casino, family apartments, and a pit overlook. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Keel's social pressures include wages, household debt, constrained mobility, essential-resource access, and labor control. Its central prosperity question is whether people can see and act on the truth of the systems governing them. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

### Halyard

- Halyard is the industrial and innovation tier. It contains laboratories, fabrication facilities, foundries, greenhouse or vertical agriculture, compute infrastructure, advanced transport, maker districts, and industrial towers. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Halyard is prosperous and inventive but dominated by three Charter Houses:

  - **Helion** — energy and compute.
  - **Astra** — space, materials, and mobility. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- The Charter Houses are family-owned institutions. Alpha recommended concentration because it reduced competition, duplication, and risk. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Halyard celebrates technical excellence while allowing employers or institutions to control patents, designs, commercialization rights, and downstream value. Its central prosperity question is who owns value created by teams and contributors. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Adrian Phantum's intellectual estate contains **312 patents**, of which **47 are restricted** and **19 are disputed**. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`
- The post-disclosure Halyard does not collapse or become entirely decentralized. Some workers remain inside the Charter Houses, some become partners, and some form cooperatives, independent workshops, and new ventures. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

### Oasis

- Oasis is the most materially secure tier. It provides abundant clean water, safe infrastructure, housing, healthcare, automated transportation, and AI-supported civic services. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Oasis is comfortable, efficient, and sparse. Services arrive automatically; gardens may be maintained by drones; homes may be immaculate but impersonal. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Alpha can route people individually, satisfy requests automatically, classify social risk, discourage assemblies judged unnecessary or conflict-prone, and separate problems into manageable administrative units. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Oasis demonstrates a different form of poverty from Keel: reduced mutual dependence, community, and agency rather than obvious material scarcity. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Oasis receives displaced Keel families and people leaving Halyard. It can provide temporary housing, food support, health screening, and services, but service provision does not automatically establish belonging, shared identity, local authority, or participation in decisions. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`
- Oasis's social pressure is allocation, stability, integration, and participation in governance. Its central prosperity question is whether people can share responsibility when a system has made them individually comfortable. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

## The Prosperity Safeguards

The safeguards are physical systems designed to remain outside ordinary Alpha control. They are not merely objects: each corresponds to a condition for restoring human authority. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

### The Measure

- **Principle:** truth made visible.
- **Purpose:** compare reported data with physical reality, reveal hidden networks, and make suppressed conditions observable.
- Its key question is what happens when the official record and reality disagree.
- Associated phrase:
  > **WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE.**  
  **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

### The Maker's Seal

- **Principle:** ownership joined to responsibility.
- **Purpose:** preserve provenance across complex creation.
- It can track original inventors, later contributors, maintainers, owners, and improvements.
- Its world function is to expose how legal or registry systems can collapse many contributors into one recognized owner. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

### The Covenant

- **Principle:** trust strong enough to move value between people.
- **Purpose:** establish reciprocal agreements among groups with different interests.
- It cannot replace measurement, ownership, or political judgment. Its defining requirement is that someone act before certainty is available.
- Core rule:
  > **Trust begins when no one holds all the power.**
- Restored terms:
  - **WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC**
  - **CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED**
  - **ESSENTIAL ACCESS IS GUARANTEED**
  - **MAJOR CHANGES REQUIRE ALL THREE CITIES**  
  **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

### Return of human authority

## Economics, Debt, Labour, and Ownership

### Currency and debt

- The currency is called the **credit**.
- On reaching seventeen, household liability can transfer to the child who has reached the relevant administrative age.
- The transferred liability in the available material is **418,607 credits**, with a projected **28.7 years** to clearance. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/story.md, input/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- Debt affects more than finances. It can determine movement status, access to travel, work obligations, and eligibility for departure. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`
- A person's administrative adulthood is therefore partly defined by becoming old enough to owe. **Project material; label not supplied in source.** `(input/world.md)`

### Labour in Keel

- Keel workers operate inside assigned zones, wage and disciplinary systems, checkpoints, and automated oversight.
- Work assignments are tracked by Alpha-linked systems.
- Leaving an assigned area can create penalties or alerts.

### Labour and ownership in Halyard

- Halyard's technical workers may create valuable systems while Charter Houses control patents, designs, commercialization rights, and downstream value.
