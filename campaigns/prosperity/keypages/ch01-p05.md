# Chapter 1 — Page 5: THE OLD BOOK

## Page Purpose

Bring the mystery into Alex's home and make his grandmother the hinge between family memory and hidden evidence. Establish the book and bookshelf as ordinary domestic objects before they become mechanisms.

## Page Description

Alex returns to the warm, crowded apartment. His grandmother initially calls him Adrian, then recognizes him. She asks whether his father has come home and says he is 'closer than you think.' She gives Alex an old book and asks him to put it away because Adrian never liked it left out. Alex carries it toward the tall bookshelf.

## Dialogue / On-Page Text

- GRANDMOTHER: Adrian?
- ALEX: Not today, Grandma. It's Alex.
- GRANDMOTHER: Of course. Alex. I knew that. Did your father come home yet?
- ALEX: Not yet.
- GRANDMOTHER: He's closer than you think.
- GRANDMOTHER: Would you put this away for me? Your father never liked seeing it left out.
- ALEX: He had opinions about everything.
- GRANDMOTHER: Especially the things worth protecting.

## Layout

Six panels. P1 is a wide domestic establishing shot. P2 is a small inset close-up of Alex at the door. P3 is a medium kneeling conversation. P4 is a close-up of Grandmother. P5 is a large hands-and-book detail. P6 is a tall narrow panel of Alex approaching the bookshelf.

## Visual / Story Notes

The apartment should feel warm, safe, handmade, and full of history. The book's symbol is the crispest unusual object on the page. Keep the bookshelf visible as a recurring visual anchor.
