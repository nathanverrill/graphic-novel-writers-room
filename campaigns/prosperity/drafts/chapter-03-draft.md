# CHAPTER 3

**THE PRICE OF OWNERSHIP**

## PAGE 1 — THE CITY OF MAKERS

**P1: Arrival (dominant)**  
 *Image:* Halyard at sunrise, seen from the train platform. Suspended rail lines cross between glass laboratories, fabrication towers, vertical farms and foundries. Three illuminated House insignias, each in its own accent color, dominate three separate skyline clusters. Below the towers, workers stream through gated checkpoints in House-colored uniforms. Alex, Ada and Bi11bot are small in the foreground, stepping off the train.

**P2: Three houses (wide)**  
 *Image:* Inside a crowded transit concourse. Three huge holographic ads compete overhead, each in its House's accent color: Aqualis shows clean rivers and atmospheric water towers, Helion shows fusion grids and compute centers, Astra shows orbital stations and advanced materials. Each features a founder's face and a shared slogan.  
 *Ad slogan:* CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.  
 ADA: Three families run almost everything here.  
 ALEX: Alpha allowed that?  
 BI11BOT: Allowed it. Recommended it.

**P3: Fewer questions (street level)**  
 *Image:* Street level beneath the towers. Workers queue at a contribution kiosk, visibly more orderly and better-dressed than Keel, but still queuing. A faded old sign over a shuttered independent workshop reads MORROW & SONS — INVENTORS SINCE 2024, a House insignia stamped crudely over the original name.  
 ADA: Why recommend it?  
 BI11BOT: Efficiency. Less duplication, less risk.  
 BI11BOT: Also fewer people asking who owns what.

**P4: The kiosk (medium)**  
 *Image:* A young engineer feeds a small mechanical component into a scanner. Alex and Ada watch from a few feet away.  
 *Screen:* CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS  
 ALEX: She made it. The House owns it?  
 BI11BOT: The House paid for her time. It claims what she made with it.

**P5: What she keeps (close)**  
 *Image:* Close on the engineer's face as she steps away from the kiosk, unbothered, already moving to the next task. Behind her, the shuttered Morrow & Sons sign from P3 is visible, slightly out of focus.  
 ALEX: And if she leaves?  
 BI11BOT: She keeps the experience. They keep everything else.

**P4: The term (close)**  
*Image:* Close on the screen's lower text, Alex's reflection faint over it.  
*Screen:* CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED

**P5: Whatever he made (close)**  
*Image:* Close on Ada, her eyes narrowing as she reads ahead of him.  
ADA: It means whatever he made might now belong to you. He never worked for the Houses. Or he never let them know he did.

**P6: Watched (strip)**  
*Image:* Wide and low, from across the hall. Several sharply dressed figures in mixed House colors have turned to look toward Alex's terminal, unhurried, already moving his way. Security drones hover at a respectful distance, their lights cold white.

## PAGE 3: THE OFFER

**P1: The approach (wide)**  
 *Image:* Lock crosses the hall at an unhurried pace, flanked loosely by two aides in mixed House colors, drones trailing at a respectful distance. He is composed, silver-templed, dressed in charcoal and platinum with no House crest.  
 *Caption:* DIRECTOR LOCK

**P2: Waiting for an heir (dominant)**  
 *Image:* Lock stops a few feet from Alex, unthreatening, hands loosely clasped. Behind him, Adrian's estate record still glows cold white on the terminal.  
 LOCK: Alex Phantum. Halyard has been waiting a long time for an heir.  
 ALEX: I didn't know I was one.  
 LOCK: Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value.

**P3: The gesture (two-shot)**  
 *Image:* Lock gestures toward the glowing patent record without looking at it, his eyes staying on Alex.  
 LOCK: Your father's work could clear every debt attached to your name.  
 LOCK: It could also secure you a position. Somewhere in Halyard that suits you.

**P4: In exchange (close)**  
 *Image:* Ada steps half in front of Alex, arms crossed, watching Lock's hands, not his face.  
 ADA: In exchange for what?  
 LOCK: A simple assignment of rights.

**P5: Simple (close)**  
 *Image:* Close on Lock's calm, patient expression, utterly sincere.  
 LOCK: You sign. The estate transfers to administration. Your name is clear by tonight.  
 LOCK: No one asks what you did in Keel. No one asks what you're doing here.

**P6: No answer yet (wide)**  
 *Image:* Lock inclines his head, unbothered by silence, and steps back, his aides falling in behind him.  
 LOCK: Take the day. Halyard isn't going anywhere.  
 *(He leaves. Alex doesn't move. Bi11bot's amber eye tracks him out of the hall.)*

## PAGE 4: WHAT HE BUILT WITH OTHERS

**P1: The room (wide)**  
*Image:* A small rented room above a maker district, the noise of the street dulled by thin walls, a single window showing Halyard's towers at dusk. Alex sits on the edge of a narrow bed, turning the chip in one hand, Adrian's smoothed photograph propped against a lamp as it was in Chapter One. Ada stands at the window, arms crossed, watching the towers. Bi11bot waits on the table, unlit.  
*Caption:* THAT EVENING

**P2: Not yet (strip)**  
*Image:* Close on Alex's hand, hovering near his identity band, not touching it.  
ADA: You're thinking about it.  
ALEX: I'm thinking.

**P3: The archive (dominant)**  
*Image:* Bi11bot projects Adrian's patent archive across the bare walls, filling the small room with soft blue light. Most files sit encrypted or fragmented, grey and inert. One cluster glows differently, warmer, more defined: a series of water-related patents connected by incomplete diagrams, catalytic membrane purification, atmospheric moisture capture, mineral recovery, living biofilter regulation, distributed community water nodes. At the cluster's center, faint but unmistakable, the bronze open-hand symbol of the Maker's Seal pulses once.  
BI11BOT: Your father expected someone to come looking.  
ALEX: Can you open them?  
BI11BOT: The files, yes. The ideas? Not alone.

**P4: Four names (medium)**  
*Image:* Four flat, official identification portraits resolve from the projection, each beneath a small House insignia in its accent color: two under Aqualis's jade-teal, one under Astra's steel-violet, one under Helion's amber-orange.  
BI11BOT: He built them with others.  
ALEX: They're all inside the Houses.  
BI11BOT: Exactly where Halyard puts everyone useful.

**P5: Not nothing (strip)**  
*Image:* Alex looks at the four portraits, then at Lock's registry alert still faintly visible as a cold white afterimage at the edge of the projection, the two options sitting side by side in the same room.  
ALEX: Lock's offer clears this by tonight. This takes... what, weeks?  
BI11BOT: Lock's offer clears the debt. It doesn't clear anything else.

## PAGE 5: LINA VEY

**P1: The facility (wide)**  
*Image:* A vast Aqualis purification facility, rows of enormous processing columns and channels. Workers in Aqualis jade-teal uniforms move between stations. Alex, Ada and Bi11bot approach a stooped woman in a maintenance-grade uniform despite the authority in her bearing.

**P2: Frequently (medium two-shot)**  
*Image:* Lina looks Alex over, unimpressed, arms crossed.  
LINA: Adrian Phantum's son.  
ALEX: You knew him?  
LINA: I corrected him. Frequently.  
BI11BOT: Nearly continuously.  
LINA: You think he left this to you? *(She nods at the erased names still visible faintly in Bi11bot's projection.)* He left it to all of us.

**P3: The failing river (dominant)**  
*Image:* Lina leads them to a wide observation window over a contaminated feeder channel, visibly fouled with mineral runoff and industrial residue, flowing out toward the mountain and down toward Keel. A cold white alert glows on the glass.  
*Screen:* DOWNSTREAM PURIFICATION FAILURE / PROJECTED IMPACT: KEEL WATER NETWORK  
LINA: That river feeds what's left of Keel's supply. Aqualis can clean sections of it. Not fast enough, and not without shutting down half the production district.  
ALEX: We just got Keel's water flowing again.  
LINA: Then you bought it a few months, not a future.

**P4: Every piece (medium)**  
*Image:* Lina gestures up at the Aqualis insignia glowing above the facility floor, her expression dry.  
ALEX: Adrian's patents could fix it?  
LINA: Combined correctly, they'd purify the flow and recover what's contaminating it. Every department owns a different piece of the answer.  
LINA: And every House would rather own the failure than share the solution.

**P5: Off the system (strip)**  
*Image:* Close on Lina's hand pressing an old physical data shard into a slot on Bi11bot's chest. A section of the purification design blooms in his projection, a flexible membrane diagram, with the bronze Maker's Seal symbol flashing briefly inside it.  
LINA: He made each of us keep one piece off the system. He worried Halyard would confuse protecting an idea with owning the person who had it.  
ALEX: Will you help us find the other three?  
LINA: *(already walking)* Try keeping up this time.

## PAGE 6: SURI ANIK

**P1: The wing (wide)**  
*Image:* A biofilter research wing, living walls of root systems and algae columns glowing warm green, in sharp visual contrast to the industrial channels of Page 5\. Lina leads Alex, Ada and Bi11bot through it without slowing down. A younger woman in a research coat works alone at a terminal, cross-checking samples no one else seems to be reviewing.

**P2: What Ada sees (dominant)**  
*Image:* Ada's eyes go first to the empty workstations around Suri, not to Suri herself, counting them. Suri hasn't looked up yet.  
ADA *(quiet, to Alex)*: She's the only one still working this bench. Where's her team?  
LINA *(overhearing)*: There isn't one anymore. Budget "efficiency."

**P3: Introductions (medium two-shot)**  
*Image:* Suri finally looks up, recognizing Lina first, then studying Alex's face for Adrian in it.  
SURI: Lina said you might come. I didn't believe her.  
ALEX: Why not?  
SURI: Because the ones who come looking for Adrian usually want what he built. Not who he built it with.

**P4: The piece (close)**  
*Image:* Suri unlocks a small case beneath her station and hands over a second physical shard, worn smooth from handling.  
SURI: The biofilter. It's slow, and it's alive, and it doesn't fail quietly like the mechanical parts do. He trusted me with the part that could actually die.  
BI11BOT: An honest form of trust.

**P5: Why she stayed (close)**  
*Image:* Suri closes the case, her expression unreadable.  
ADA: Why didn't you leave, after they cut your team?  
SURI: Someone has to keep it alive until the rest catches up to it.

**P6: Watched (wide, closing strip)**  
*Image:* As the group leaves the wing, a small overhead sensor swivels to track them, its light a cold, unmistakable white against the warm green. No one in the panel notices it.

## PAGE 7: JALEN ORR

**P1: The core (wide)**  
*Image:* A cavernous Helion compute core, towering server racks and glowing conduits stretching up into haze, the air visibly shimmering with heat. Workers move briskly, heads down. Alex, Ada, Lina, Suri and Bi11bot are small against the scale of it.  
*Caption:* HOUSE HELION — COMPUTE CORE 4

JALEN: You shouldn't be here. Lock's people were already asking about you.

JALEN: Lock knows everything eventually. The question is how much of a head start you get.

**P3: The patrol (dominant)**  
*Image:* A security drone patrol sweeps down the main corridor behind them, cold white light sweeping the walls in a slow, mechanical rhythm. The group presses flat against a server bank, Bi11bot's amber dimmed to almost nothing.  
JALEN *(whisper)*: They don't watch people here. They watch patterns. Five of us standing still too long is already a pattern.

**P4: The node (close two-shot)**  
*Image:* Jalen passes a shard to Bi11bot quickly, hands shaking slightly, eyes still on the corridor.  
JALEN: Distributed water nodes. It routes supply around any single point of failure, so no House, no Alpha, can choke one city by cutting one line.  
ALEX: That's why it's dangerous to them.  
JALEN: That's why it's dangerous to me.

**P5: What he won't do (wide, closing strip)**  
*Image:* Jalen steps back toward the open floor, already putting distance between himself and the group.  
JALEN: I gave you the piece. I'm not coming with you, and I'm not seeing you again. Find Ren. Astra's worse, but at least it's far from here.

## PAGE 8: REN OKAFOR

**P1: The edge (dominant)**  
*Image:* A mining and launch complex on Halyard's outer rim, raw rock cut into terraced pits, a launch gantry rising against open sky in the same silhouette as Chapter One's spacecraft. Steel-violet Astra insignia mark everything from helmets to crane arms. Alex, Ada and Bi11bot pick their way across an active work yard.  
*Caption:* HOUSE ASTRA — EXTRACTION AND LAUNCH COMPLEX

**P2: Still working (medium two-shot)**  
*Image:* Ren stands at a fabrication bay, welding something with total focus, not looking up until she's good and ready. Scorch marks and old patches cover her suit.  
REN: Adrian's kid. Took you long enough.  
ALEX: You knew we were coming?  
REN: Lina doesn't whisper as quietly as she thinks.

**P3: Not careful (close)**  
*Image:* Ren pulls off a welding mask, studying Alex directly, unbothered by the drones visible in the background.  
REN: Lina plays it careful. Jalen plays it scared. I stopped playing a long time ago.  
ALEX: Why?  
REN: Because down here, careful gets you buried under a collapsed shaft same as reckless does. Might as well build something worth being buried for.

**P4: The last two (wide)**  
*Image:* Ren hands over two shards at once, and Bi11bot's full projection finally resolves, all five patent fragments locking together into one complete design: a compact purification and recovery system, membrane, biofilter, distributed nodes, mineral recovery and atmospheric capture, each piece glowing in its collaborator's House color before settling into a single unified bronze-gold schematic.  
REN: Mineral recovery. Atmospheric capture. He saved the stubborn problems for me.  
ALEX: Why these two?  
REN: Because space taught him you can't waste anything. Not water, not air, not people.

**P5: What he was actually building (dominant)**  
*Image:* The group stands together in front of the complete floating schematic, five people where there was one boy a few pages ago. Ren crosses her arms, looking at it with something like pride and grief at once.  
REN: He wasn't building a machine. He was building an argument. That five Houses' worth of stolen credit could still add up to something none of us owns alone.  
ALEX: So now what?  
REN: Now you go build it. And find out if he was right.

## PAGE 9: THE PART HE DIDN'T BUILD

**P1: The build (wide)**  
*Image:* The cluttered room, three nights of work visible in scattered parts and empty cups. Lina studies a connector under a lamp, Ada cross-references Adrian's old notebooks against the schematic, and Alex sits at the center with the half-assembled prototype, Bi11bot's projection hovering above it all.  
*Caption:* THREE NIGHTS LATER

**P2: The failure (dominant)**  
*Image:* Bi11bot runs the full simulation. The five pieces light up in sequence, membrane, biofilter, nodes, mineral recovery, atmospheric capture, each still carrying its House's accent color. At the seams where they should connect, the colors clash instead of merge, and cold white error sparks flare along every joint.  
BI11BOT: Simulated efficiency: eleven percent. It should be closer to ninety.  
LINA: Of course it fails. Five Houses, five sets of standards. None of these pieces were ever meant to talk to each other.

**P3: Why Adrian stopped (medium)**  
*Image:* Lina sets down the connector, tired, finally honest about something she's clearly carried a while.  
LINA: He knew that. He ran out of time before he solved it. That's the part even we couldn't give you.  
ALEX: So it's unfinished.  
LINA: It's unfinished exactly where it matters.

**P4: The test (close)**  
*Image:* Alex ignores the schematic entirely and picks up two physical connectors instead, one from Lina's membrane piece, one from Ren's recovery unit, pressing them palm to palm the way he once tested the pump in Chapter One. He feels where they almost fit and where they don't.  
ALEX: It's not the specs. It's the threading. Everyone built their piece to lock, not to share.  
*(He pulls a thin salvaged sleeve from the spare parts and fits it between the two connectors by hand.)*  
ALEX: It doesn't need a sixth patent. It needs something that doesn't care whose standard it is.

**P5: There you are (dominant)**  
*Image:* Alex seats the hand-built sleeve between the two connectors. The error sparks die, and the schematic's colors finally merge into one steady bronze-gold glow, efficiency numbers climbing fast behind him. Bi11bot's lens clicks, his eyes resting on Alex rather than on the projection, in an expression closer to genuine surprise than his usual dry calm.  
*Screen:* SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 94%  
BI11BOT: Well now. There you are.  
ALEX: ...What?  
BI11BOT: Nothing. Carry on.

## PAGE 10: THE SECOND OFFER

**P1: No knock (wide)**

**P2: How long (dominant)**  
*Image:* Lock crosses to the table, looking at the schematic rather than at Alex, hands clasped as always.  
ALEX: How long have you known?  
LOCK: Since the compute core flagged five people standing still too long in a service corridor. Patterns are Helion's whole business, Alex. I simply read the reports.

**P3: The names (medium two-shot)**  
*Image:* Lock finally looks up at Alex directly, his tone unchanged, almost gentle.  
LOCK: Dr. Vey. Ms. Anik. Mr. Orr. Ms. Okafor. Four employees, across three Houses, who removed restricted intellectual property from their posts.

*Image:* Ada steps forward, jaw tight, before Alex can answer.

**P5: Dawn (dominant)**  
*Image:* Lock turns to leave, his aides parting for him. Behind him, on a small terminal by the door, a cold white countdown resolves on its own, unasked for.  
*Screen:* CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW CONVENES: 07:14:00  
LOCK: You have until then to decide what your father's work is worth, and to whom.  
*(He's gone. The door clicks shut. Alex looks at the schematic, then at the clock.)*

## **PAGE 12: THE FIVE**

**P1: Before dawn (wide)**  
*Image:* Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren crowd into the small room, pulled from sleep, tension obvious in how they stand. Alex, Ada and Bi11bot face them. The schematic still glows on the table between everyone.  
*Countdown:* 05:20:00  
JALEN: You woke us up to tell us you're not signing. That's not a plan, that's a death wish with extra steps.

**P2: The disagreement (medium two-shot)**  
*Image:* Jalen and Lina on one side, visibly frustrated; Ren leaning back, arms crossed, unreadable.  
LINA: He's not entirely wrong. Refuse Lock and the review has nothing to stop it from burying all four of us quietly instead of loudly.  
JALEN: Take the deal. We disappear from the record. That's safety.  
REN: Disappearing from the record is what already happened to us once.

**P3: Alex's pitch (medium)**  
*Image:* Alex stands, pulling up the schematic himself this time rather than waiting for Bi11bot to project it, his hands doing the work.  
ALEX: Open attribution protects you from being erased. It doesn't stop a House from owning it anyway and deciding whether it's worth building.  
ALEX: So don't give them anything to own. Build it so small and so cheap that no House would bother claiming it.

**P4: The reaction (wide)**  
*Image:* A row of reactions across the group. Lina's eyebrows rise, skeptical. Suri leans forward, interested. Ren grins. Ada watches Alex, not the schematic.  
LINA: You want to take an industrial purification system and shrink it to something a single household builds?  
ALEX: I want to take the five hardest problems you all solved and stop pretending only a House has the resources to use them.  
SURI: It's not impossible. It's just never been asked of the design before.

**P5: Try it (dominant)**  
*Image:* Ren steps forward first, offering her hand to Alex, the others falling in behind her one at a time, a loose, uneasy circle forming around the table. The countdown ticks down in the corner.  
*Countdown:* 04:55:00  
REN: Space taught me you build small when you can't build safe. Fine. Let's build small.  
LINA: If this fails, it fails spectacularly, in front of a House review, with my name on it.  
ALEX: Then let's not fail.

## **PAGE 13: SMALL ENOUGH TO OWN**

**P1: The hours (strip)**  
 *Image:* A clock-like sequence within one strip: hands working, parts shrinking, the room's lamp light visibly dimming as dawn approaches outside the window.  
 *Countdown:* 03:40:00 → 02:15:00 → 01:05:00

**P2: The membrane problem (medium)**  
*Image:* Lina and Alex hunched over a magnified projection of the membrane component, far too large to shrink by half in its current form.  
LINA: The membrane needs surface area to work. Shrink the housing and you starve it.  
ALEX: Then don't shrink it flat. *(He folds a thin sheet of scrap into accordion pleats.)* Fold it. Same surface area, a fraction of the footprint.  
LINA: *(studying it)* ...That's not in any of our patents.  
ALEX: It's not supposed to be. It's new.

**P3: The power problem (medium)**  
*Image:* Suri and Jalen work together at a separate bench, the biofilter and the distributed-node logic laid out side by side, clearly mismatched in scale.  
SURI: My biofilter needs a steady draw. Jalen's node logic wants to cycle on and off to save power. They fight each other at this size.  
JALEN: *(reluctantly impressed, watching Alex sketch something between them)* ...Or they take turns. One rests while the other works. It's not elegant.  
ALEX: It doesn't have to be elegant. It has to fit in someone's kitchen.

**P4: The last piece (medium)**  
*Image:* Ren fits a small salvaged heat exchanger from the extraction yard into the compact housing, filing down an edge by hand.  
REN: Mineral recovery and moisture capture both generate waste heat. Industrial scale, you vent it. This scale, you can't afford to waste it.  
ALEX: So feed it back into the biofilter's draw.  
REN: *(a short laugh)* Your father never thought that small. Took someone who grew up rationing to think of it.

**P5: Dawn (dominant)**  
*Image:* Wide shot of the finished unit sitting alone on the table in the thin grey-blue light of dawn through the window, no larger than a toolbox, its surface a quiet, steady bronze-gold where all five components have fully merged. The six of them stand around it in exhausted silence, the countdown reading its final minutes. Bi11bot's projection confirms the design beside it.  
*Countdown:* 00:08:00  
*Screen:* SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 91% / UNIT COST: FRACTION OF INDUSTRIAL SCALE / PATENT STATUS: UNFILED  
ALEX: It's smaller than I thought it'd be.  
ADA: So was the idea that built it. That's probably the point.

## PAGE 14: THE SEAL

**P1: The chamber (wide)**  
*Image:* A formal review chamber, three House representatives and Lock seated at a raised bench, the modest unit on its table below them. Alex stands at the center, Ada, Bi11bot, Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren arranged behind him in a loose line.  
*Countdown (chamber display):* 00:07:00  
REVIEW CHAIR: The estate of Adrian Phantum. Director Lock advises a signed administrative transfer is ready. Mr. Phantum, do you accept?

**P2: The refusal (medium)**  
*Image:* Alex steps forward, hands empty, deliberately not reaching for any signature pad offered to him.  
ALEX: No. The estate isn't mine to transfer. It never was mine alone to begin with.  
ALEX: Dr. Lina Vey. Suri Anik. Jalen Orr. Ren Okafor. They built what my father started. I just built what none of us had built yet.

**P3: Lock, watching (close)**  
*Image:* Lock's composure doesn't crack, but his eyes move, for the first time all chapter, from Alex to the unit on the table and back, genuinely recalculating.  
LOCK: And what is it you've built, exactly?  
ALEX: Something small enough that owning it isn't worth anyone's time. We're releasing the design publicly. Tonight. No patent. No House. Anyone who needs clean water and can afford a toolbox can build one.

**P4: The Seal (close two-shot)**  
*Image:* Lina steps forward from the line, drawing a small bronze medallion from inside her uniform, worn smooth from years of being carried rather than displayed. She presses it into Alex's open palm.  
LINA: Your father gave me this the week before he vanished. Told me to keep it until someone proved they understood what it was for.  
LINA: I've been waiting fifteen years to stop carrying it.

**P5: Response (dominant)**  
*Image:* The medallion warms in Alex's hand, its open-hand symbol flaring bright bronze, and for a brief moment the cold chamber light itself seems to bend warmer around it. The chamber display behind them reads its final seconds. The review bench sits in stunned, formal silence, none of them quite sure what they've just witnessed. Lock alone doesn't look surprised, only thoughtful.  
*Countdown:* 00:00:04 → 00:00:00  
*Caption:* It hadn't woken for a signature, or a claim, or a name on a patent. It had woken for five names, spoken aloud, by someone who didn't need to be the only one who mattered.
