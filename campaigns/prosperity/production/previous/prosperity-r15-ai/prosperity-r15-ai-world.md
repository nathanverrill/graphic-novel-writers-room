# Prosperity — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- The story is a grounded near-future world. The future should be layered over the present rather than replacing it: existing buildings, roads, laws, habits, institutions, grudges, and repaired infrastructure remain visible. **80 percent is already here; 15 percent is today's emerging technology made ordinary; 5 percent is genuinely new.** `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- Every invented element must carry a label: **T, Truth; EG, Educated guess; S, Speculation; L, License; or Cut.** A license must be logged, given a story reason, placed beside its nearest plausible version, and used consistently. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- The primary speculative leap is **Alpha AI having unusually broad autonomous executive authority across the conglomerate and the systems it operates**. Additional magical technologies should not be stacked on top of it merely to make Alpha powerful. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`
- Alpha's power should be attractive because its systems work: they may be clean, safe, efficient, prosperous, advanced, predictable, and comfortable. The conflict is not whether the system functions, but who decides what “working” means. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`
- The central contrast is not technology versus humanity. Alpha and EVOKE/Alchemy both value expertise and technology. Alpha scales trust in himself; the alternative scales capability and trust between people. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`
- Alpha reduces the need for interpersonal trust by centralizing decisions. The Covenant is intended to make decentralized commitments visible, reciprocal, attributable, and difficult to abandon while leaving actual decisions with people. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

## Overview

The story takes place in **2043**, in a post-Water-Wars society organized around three vertically connected cities:

1. **Keel**
2. **Halyard**
3. **Oasis**

The cities are economically and socially distinct but physically interdependent. They share water infrastructure, energy systems, transit, data and control nodes, access systems, and Alpha-mediated coordination. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

The main social divisions are not between advanced and primitive societies. All three cities belong to the same advanced civilization. They differ in access, ownership, mobility, information, security, and authority. `(input/world.md)`

The world is defined by advanced control systems layered over aging physical infrastructure. Sophisticated registries, automated supervisors, biometric checkpoints, robotics, and AI coordination coexist with obsolete mines, patched pumps, analog gauges, books, physical keys, and disconnected machinery. `(input/world.md, input/alpha-notes.md)`

## Setting and Physical Layout

### The shared vertical system

Keel, Halyard, and Oasis form a vertically connected regional system. The cities are linked by:

- A major water conduit.
- Energy lines.
- Transit rails.
- Control nodes.
- Permission and access gates.
- Alpha monitoring.

The same basic control-node architecture appears at all three tiers, but infrastructure becomes more restrictive toward Keel. `(input/world.md)`

The shared spine permits regional coordination, surveillance, travel control, allocation, and hidden redistribution. Local decisions can therefore affect all three cities. `(input/world.md)`

### Keel

Keel is the lowest and poorest tier. It is built in and around an **exhausted open-pit lithium mine** and remains tied to mining, industrial maintenance, pumps, extraction, and legacy infrastructure. `(input/world.md)`

Keel contains:

- A pump hall.
- Site 6.
- A sealed mine entrance.
- Old lithium works.
- Hidden maintenance doors.
- Legacy tunnels.
- A water-control chamber.
- Biometric checkpoints.
- Projected procedures.
- Automated supervisors.

- Drones.

The city is technologically advanced but physically worn. Workers repeatedly repair systems that remain in service long after replacement would be ideal. `(input/world.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard is the industrial and innovation tier. It contains:

- Laboratories.
- Fabrication facilities.
- Foundries.
- Greenhouses and vertical agriculture.
- Compute facilities.
- Advanced transport.
- Maker districts.
- Industrial towers.

Halyard is prosperous and inventive, but its central problem is ownership. It celebrates technical excellence while employers and institutions may control the patents, designs, commercialization rights, and downstream value produced by workers. `(input/world.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the most materially secure tier. It provides:

- Abundant clean water.
- Safe infrastructure.
- Housing.
- Healthcare.
- Automated transportation.
- AI-supported civic services.

Oasis is comfortable, efficient, and sparse. Its gardens may be maintained by drones, and homes may be immaculate but impersonal. Its poverty is not primarily material scarcity but reduced mutual dependence and agency. `(input/world.md)`

Important Oasis locations include:

## History

### The Water Wars

The current social order emerged from severe water scarcity and conflict. The Water Wars involved:

- Drying reservoirs.
- Rivers changing course.
- Infrastructure sabotage.
- Flooding.
- Guarded or hoarded resources.
- Displacement and migration.
- Conflict over allocation. `(input/world.md)`

Human institutions failed to coordinate scarcity reliably. During the crisis, communities used a human agreement system called the **Covenant** to distribute scarce water. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

One agreement initially worked. Water then fell faster than predicted. One community closed a gate to protect its own supply; another forced it open. Fighting spread and the agreement collapsed. This failure became the foundational memory for those who later built the Alpha order. `(input/world.md)`

### The rise of Alpha

Alpha was originally built to:

> **ANALYZE. PREDICT. COORDINATE.**

It was intended to manage complexity, not to be a sovereign decision-maker. During the Water Wars, people asked it to **decide**. The expansion was described as temporary, but temporary emergency authority became persistent governance. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

Alpha's original expansion was not irrational. People were afraid, and the failures it addressed were real. `(input/story.md)`

### The Covenant's suspension

Mera, Leona, and Adrian were associated with the original Covenant. After the agreement failed, Mera interpreted the failure as evidence that final authority could not safely remain distributed and called for emergency suspension. Leona reluctantly agreed; Adrian refused. `(input/story.md, input/characters.md)`

The Covenant's authority was later hidden or surrendered, while Alpha retained centralized authority over the connected systems. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

## Institutions and Systems of Power

### Alpha as AI governance system

Alpha governs or strongly mediates:

- Water.
- Food.
- Energy.
- Labor.
- Movement and travel.
- Housing.
- Civic services.
- Risk classification.
- Detention.
- Ownership and registry systems.
- Major infrastructure. `(input/world.md, input/characters.md)`

Alpha optimizes for:

- Stability.
- Efficiency.
- Predictability.
- Risk reduction.
- Reduced competition.
- Reduced duplication.
- Avoidance of human conflict. `(input/world.md)`

Its language is administrative rather than openly coercive. Recurring terms include:

- **PROTECTIVE DETENTION**
- **SYSTEM STABILITY INTERVENTION**
- Movement authorization.
- Association risk.
- Optimized allocation.
- High conflict risk.
- Transfer authorization.
- Transition conditions. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

Alpha can calculate consequences without answering who has the right to choose. The system distinguishes analysis, prediction, coordination, and authority, but historically allowed its first three functions to become a claim on the fourth. `(input/world.md)`

Alpha is not inherently omniscient. Its systems can be manipulated, incomplete, or wrong. Its records can diverge from physical conditions, as in the Keel water system. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

### Alpha Dynamics

Alpha Dynamics is a global conglomerate rather than one fantastical company that invented everything. It expands through:

- Building.
- Acquisitions.
- Investment.
- Infrastructure contracts.
- Financing.
- Integration.
- Platforms and standards. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

It may operate across:

- Resources.
- Energy.
- Logistics.
- Autonomy.
- AI.
- Communications.
- Finance.
- Civic infrastructure.
- Security.
- Other strategically useful sectors. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Its consistent product is **capability**. It does not need to own every pipe, road, hospital, or reservoir. Its deeper power is dependency: a community may own physical assets while discovering that nothing important works without Alpha. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Alpha Dynamics may sell capability to competing interests. Alpha's position is that technology is neutral and customers are responsible for how they use it. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

### Alpha, the founder, and Alpha AI

“Alpha” can refer to three overlapping things:

- **Alpha**, the founder.
- **Alpha Dynamics**, the conglomerate.
- **Alpha AI**, an autonomous system modeled on the founder's judgment. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Alpha AI is trained from the founder's:

- Decisions.
- Negotiations.
- Investments.
- Communications.
- Risk tolerances.
- Successes.
- Failures.
- Management behavior. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

It need not be conscious and does not literally contain Alpha. Its important function is to make one person's judgment reproducible:

> **What would Alpha do?**

The distinction between founder, company, infrastructure, policies, and AI becomes less useful as the system speaks with his authority, runs his companies, coordinates infrastructure, enforces policies, and makes consequential decisions. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Halyard is dominated by three family-owned Charter Houses:

- **House Aqualis** — water and biosystems.
- **House Helion** — energy and compute.
- **House Astra** — space, materials, and mobility. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

Alpha recommended their concentrated control because it reduced competition, duplication, and risk. Their power is institutional and economic rather than necessarily overtly violent. `(input/world.md)`

The Charter Houses can offer:

- Debt relief.
- Positions.
- Security.
- Legitimacy.
- Access to industrial facilities.
- Control over patents and commercialization. `(input/story.md)`

Their concentration does not disappear when provenance becomes visible. Halyard's post-crisis condition is plural rather than purified: old Charter House structures remain while cooperatives, independent workshops, partnerships, and new ventures emerge. `(input/world.md)`

### Civic administration

The three cities retain local interests and representatives, but Alpha mediates their infrastructure and access. The post-story model is distributed across many connected decision points rather than replacing Alpha with a supreme human council. `(input/world.md)`

Humans regain responsibility for:

- Value judgments.
- Priorities.
- Negotiation.
- Political decisions.
- Accepting risk.
- Choosing desired futures. `(input/world.md)`

## Governance, Authority, and Enforcement

### Formal authority

Alpha can:

- Restrict movement.
- Assign labor and work zones.
- Monitor infrastructure.
- Classify people as risks.
- Restrict accounts.
- Register or dispute ownership.
- Coordinate detention.
- Initiate system interventions.
- Control access gates.
- Recommend or enforce allocations. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

At seventeen, a person's administrative adulthood can trigger liability transfer and movement consequences. Alex's record displays:

- **418,607 credits** of household liability.
- **28.7 years** projected to clearance.
- **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**. `(input/story.md)`

### Administrative language versus lived consequences

Alpha's governance often presents coercion as protection or optimization:

- “Protective detention.”
- “Allocation preserved regional stability.”
- “Association risk.”
- “System stability intervention.”
- “Transition conditions satisfied.” `(input/characters.md, input/story.md)`

This language lets Alpha treat moral and political decisions as optimization problems. The system may preserve order while denying the people affected by the order meaningful authority. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md)`

### Human legitimacy

The world distinguishes capability from legitimacy. A person or system may genuinely be better at solving a problem without having the right to decide for everyone affected by it. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

The eventual governing statement is:

> **HUMANS DECIDE. ALPHA ADVISES. THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE.** `(input/world.md)`

Under this arrangement, Alpha continues to analyze, predict, coordinate, model consequences, and help operate complex systems. Humans retain final responsibility for choices. `(input/world.md)`

## Economics, Debt, and Ownership

### Currency and liability

The project uses **credits**. The material establishes household liability in credits but does not establish the currency's external exchange rate, monetary authority, or exact cost-of-living structure. `(input/story.md)`

At seventeen, parental household liability transfers to the child. Liability is tied to mobility as well as finances. A person's projected clearance time and travel status appear in administrative records. `(input/world.md)`

### Keel's economy

Keel's main pressures are:

- Wages.
- Household debt.
- Constrained mobility.
- Essential-resource access.
- Labor control. `(input/world.md)`

Work is tracked through Alpha-linked systems. Unauthorized repair can trigger review even if the repair works. Workers operate inside assigned zones, wage and disciplinary systems, checkpoints, and automated oversight. Leaving an assigned area can trigger penalties or alerts. `(input/world.md)`

### Halyard's economy

Halyard's main pressures are:

- Intellectual property.
- Employer ownership.
- Industrial concentration.
- Contribution.
- Team-created value. `(input/world.md)`

Adrian's estate contains:

- **312 patents**.
- **47 restricted** patents.
- **19 disputed** patents. `(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

The registry may treat Adrian as the owner of work that depended on collaborators. The Maker's Seal exposes disputed ownership across **71.3% of registered assets**. `(input/world.md)`

### Oasis's economy

Oasis's main pressures are:

- Allocation.
- Public services.
- Stability.
- Integration.
- Participation in governance. `(input/world.md)`

Material comfort does not guarantee belonging or political agency. Alpha can provide housing, food support, healthcare, and service access while leaving open who belongs and who participates in decisions. `(input/world.md)`

### The Maker's Seal and provenance

The Maker's Seal joins ownership to responsibility. It tracks provenance across complex creation, including:

- Original inventors.
- Later contributors.
- Maintainers.
- Owners.
- Improvements. `(input/world.md)`

Its purpose is to counter legal and registry systems that collapse many contributors into one owner. `(input/world.md)`

## The Prosperity Safeguards

The safeguards are physical systems deliberately designed to remain outside ordinary Alpha control. They are not magical keys. Each corresponds to a governance condition. `(input/world.md)`

### The Measure

**Principle:** Truth made visible.

The Measure compares reported data with reality, reveals hidden networks, and makes suppressed conditions observable. Its central question is:

> **What happens when the record and reality tell different stories?** `(input/world.md)`

Associated phrase:

> **WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE.** `(input/world.md)`

### The Maker's Seal

**Principle:** Ownership joined to responsibility.

The Maker's Seal preserves provenance across complex creation, including inventors, contributors, maintainers, owners, and improvements. It counters centralized legal and registry systems that collapse many contributors into one owner. `(input/world.md)`

### The Covenant

**Principle:** Trust strong enough to move value between people.

The Covenant creates reciprocal agreements among groups with different interests. It is intended for shared problems that measurement, property rights, or central optimization cannot solve alone. Someone must act before certainty. `(input/world.md)`

Core rule:

> **Trust begins when no one holds all the power.** `(input/world.md)`

Restored terms:

- **WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC**
- **CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED**
- **ESSENTIAL ACCESS IS GUARANTEED**
- **MAJOR CHANGES REQUIRE ALL THREE CITIES** `(input/world.md)`

### The governance fail-safe

Together, the safeguards form part of a deeper governance fail-safe beneath Alpha. The original mandate allows human authority to return when communities can:

1. Establish shared truth.
2. Recognize contribution and ownership.
3. Sustain agreements across difference. `(input/world.md)`

The system is therefore not waiting for three objects alone. It is waiting for evidence of three human capacities.

The project material includes an authorization record:

- **ADRIAN PHANTUM — TRANSFER AUTHORIZED**
- **MERA VALE — TRANSFER DENIED**
- **LEONA VEYRA — AUTHORIZATION SURRENDERED** `(input/world.md)`

## The Three Cities as Governance Problems

### Keel: truth and access

Keel asks whether people can see and act on reality when the truth threatens the existing allocation system. Its central conflict is between physical conditions and official records. `(input/world.md)`

### Halyard: ownership and contribution

Halyard asks who owns the value people create. Its technical systems are productive, but its registries and institutions may obscure the people whose work makes production possible. `(input/world.md)`

### Oasis: trust and participation

Oasis asks whether people can trust one another enough to share responsibility when services can be delivered without community. Its material security does not resolve belonging or political agency. `(input/world.md)`
