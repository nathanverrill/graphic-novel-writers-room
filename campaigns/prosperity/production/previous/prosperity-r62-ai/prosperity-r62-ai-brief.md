# Prosperity — Brief

## Logline

When seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic Alex Phantum inherits his vanished father’s crushing debt and discovers a hidden trail through Prosperity’s rigged water and ownership systems, he must refuse the power to fix everything himself and build a way for affected people to see, challenge, and share decisions.

## Theme

Freedom is not escape from obligation; it is the ability to make obligations visible and choose them together.

## Tone & comps

- **The Dispossessed** — scarcity, labor, property, and political conflict made concrete through daily life.
- **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — tactile damaged environments, humane opposition, and technology that remains inseparable from ecology.
- **Arrival** — restrained speculative wonder, emotional precision, and revelation carried by images and choices.

Serious, tactile, humane science fiction with dry work-shaped humor. The book begins as a survival mystery, becomes an ownership and infrastructure thriller, and ends in a civic test. Wonder comes from old machinery, hidden routes, physical evidence, and people solving problems together. No lecture explains the world or the theme.

## Audience & rating

Young-adult and adult science-fiction readers, especially ages 15–18.

**PG-13:** peril, coercive institutions, poverty, grief, displacement, implied death, institutional abuse, mild language, and sustained ethical conflict. No graphic gore.

## Format

**Canon — decided:** Three full-color issues, exactly 54 pages total, 18 pages per issue.

- **Issue 01 — Keel:** Pages 1–18
- **Issue 02 — Halyard:** Pages 19–36
- **Issue 03 — Oasis:** Pages 37–54

Trim: **6.625" × 10.25"**. Production pages must use no more than four panels. Prefer one location per page and no more than three named characters in a panel. Splashes and large reveals must earn their space.

Issue 01 ends inside the water conduit as it begins filling behind Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ. There is no train crossing. Issue 02 opens in a Halyard storm drain.

Each issue ends on a question about a person the reader cares about:

- **Page 18:** Has Tomas escaped the rising water?
- **Page 36:** What will Mera choose to do with the third safeguard?
- **Page 54:** Will Alex leave, and what will Ada choose for herself?

## Visual direction

Inked graphic-novel realism with heavy contour, textured hatching, painterly digital color, expressive acting, tactile surfaces, and strong architectural compositions. Interfaces remain sparse enough to read. The art shows systems through pressure, wear, queues, repairs, ownership, surveillance, and consequences.

Prosperity is one mixed civilization, not three ethnic enclaves. Indigenous Andean, Latin American, European-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed, and international populations live across all three cities. Visual difference comes from environment, class, access, occupation, health, clothing, grooming, speech, and institutional power.

- **Keel:** dense, wind-scoured, mineral-stained, repaired, and compressed; rust, charcoal, dust, faded paint, patched machinery, narrow channels, crowded housing.
- **Halyard:** productive and commercially polished; fabrication floors, laboratories, maker districts, engineered greenery, sharper geometry, House checkpoints.
- **Oasis:** spacious, quiet, pale, and controlled; reservoirs, geometric gardens, clean surfaces, formal transit, sparse interiors, and service corridors beneath civic order.

Alpha has no face, body, or consciousness. It appears through white-and-amber nodes, cold interfaces, gates, records, data lines, and administrative typography.

B11 is small, foldable, visibly repaired, scarfed, and expressive through two optical eyes. He is patient, precise, questioning, funny at his own expense, and never menacing. TJ is a mismatched magnetic repair dog assembled from spare parts. TJ never speaks.

## Canon

Everything marked **FIXED** in `characters.md`, `world.md`, and `story.md` is canon. The decisions below settle material left open and supersede conflicting draft details.

### World

- Prosperity is a self-governing charter territory in a fictional Andean high-desert basin. No real country or external jurisdiction is named. The present is late 2043; the decisive Water War crisis occurred in 2028.
- Keel occupies the lowest basin around an exhausted lithium mine and active underground and satellite recovery sites, including Site 6. Halyard occupies the middle slopes as the technical and manufacturing city. Oasis occupies the upper basin and reaches above the cloud layer.
- One vertical infrastructure spine carries water, energy, transit, data, and control nodes through all three cities. Connection does not mean equal access.
- The three Charter Houses are **Caldera House** (water and purification), **Vector House** (compute and energy), and **Orbital House** (extraction, launch, and advanced materials). They were selected by Alpha for scale and administrative efficiency; they did not become dominant by first winning customers.
- Alpha began as a system for analysis, prediction, and coordination. The Covenant authorized emergency decision-making in 2028. That authority persisted through standing rules, records, permissions, and delegated human scope.
- Alpha may execute previously authorized rules automatically. New restrictions, ownership transfers, labor assignments, and emergency orders require human process. After restoration, it remains a restricted caretaker for operation, monitoring, maintenance, accounting, and alerts.
- The 62% reported / 18% delivered water discrepancy is a routing and accounting failure exposed by the Measure. The contaminated feeder channel introduced in Halyard is a separate problem: purification may buy Keel time but cannot replace the main infrastructure.
- The first post-restoration process is a public, temporary, appealable review of Keel’s water allocation. It includes designated participants from Keel, Halyard, and Oasis. No named new civic protagonists are required; the evidence and actions identify them.

### Authority and safeguards

- The **Measure** compares physical flow, pressure, and conditions with official records. It can sense, compare, trace, and verify evidence. It cannot move water, grant authority, or decide what should happen.
- The **Maker’s Seal** is one physical bronze medallion. Lina carries it until the Halyard review and gives it to Alex after he names the four collaborators and refuses sole ownership. Any earlier image is a projection, diagram, or design plate, never a second Seal.
- The **Covenant** consists of three bands: Adrian’s carried by B11, Leona’s kept in her stone-grey box, and Mera’s still dark. Adrian’s and Leona’s bands link when Leona makes a public obligation. Mera’s band does not activate.
- The original Covenant failed because two of three custodians could grant emergency authority and there was no automatic expiration or independent revocation trigger.
- All three safeguards must be physically present at the same legacy node. A human proposal must preserve their separate functions: evidence, attribution, and reciprocal obligation.
- The restoration protocol authenticates Alex only as a convenor of the proposal. Leona publicly supports the obligation, and community-designated participants authorize the bounded transition. Alex receives no permanent office, override, or sovereignty.

### People and story

- Alex begins wanting to leave Keel, clear his debt, and remain responsible only for himself. He ends with the freedom to leave or stay as a genuine choice, while still carrying his debt.
- Alex’s debt is **418,607 credits** at transfer and **402,110 credits** at return. No authority cancels it.
- Ada fronts Alex medicine and a travel token. He misses the first repayment after the Site 6 wage forfeiture, faces Ada directly, agrees to fixed installments, and visibly makes later payments. Their relationship is friendship, not romance.
- Morrow & Sons is an honest independent Keel shop that builds or sells maintainable household purifiers at a posted fair price, pays workers, and makes a modest margin. It visibly contrasts with the Alpha-selected Houses.
- B11 assembles TJ immediately after Tomas is pinned. TJ’s head scouts the gate; his separated parts enter the jammed mechanism and release the support. Alex still chooses to cross the assigned-zone line and perform the rescue.
- B11 is wrong once in Halyard: he counts the registry-visible people and misses those outside it. He admits the error plainly and asks Alex what he would do. He is never a lecturer or substitute for human relationships.
- Adrian’s gambling damaged his family, destabilized the household, and contributed to the debt. The casino chip is evidence of that harm and of later sober probability work; it is not lucky or heroic.
- Lock offers a serious exchange: House administration immediately clears Alex’s debt and name and protects the collaborators from review, in return for assigning Adrian’s estate and rights. Refusal exposes the collaborators to reassignment and loss of access. Lock’s argument for House scale remains valid: the Houses can build the purifier faster and wider.
- Alex releases the household purifier design without exclusive patent ownership. Public attribution survives, but House production and House control of the means of production remain.
- Mera personally selects **TRACK** in Issue 01. This permits observation and suppresses escalation, but Alpha retains independent standing reports. Her later conduit action opens a separate conduit-segment valve to create the viable ascent route; it is not the Keel residential regulator the miners force open. The mechanism remains morally consequential without being physically ambiguous.
- Adrian’s final choice occurs in the old monitoring station. He activates B11’s recording/deployment state, then the sequence cuts before his physical evacuation is shown. The B11 body later found in the workshop was placed there through Adrian’s prearranged route and remains dormant until Alex inserts the ignition key. No impossible handoff is shown.
- Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything. **Nothing else about her is learned.** Her name, appearance, ancestry, community, language, and later fate remain unrevealed.
- Alex refuses Mera’s offer of full executive authority. Alpha rescinds emergency authority only within the bounded process and leaves existing debt, ownership, allocations, and agreements unchanged.
- The new process proves itself when a House representative submits a water allocation that appears correct publicly while diverting physical flow to an industrial branch. Tomas and affected Keel residents compare the record with physical gauges and stop the approval.
- Alex’s refusal has a visible cost: the workshop is sealed as disputed estate property, his temporary travel clearance expires, and he must continue paying Ada and Alpha-recorded debt. He does not receive immediate freedom or a clean slate.

## Non-negotiables

- Exactly 54 pages: three 18-page issues.
- Page 1 begins with Alex as a person at work, not a theme caption or abstract world statement.
- Issue 01 is Keel; Issue 02 is Halyard; Issue 03 is Oasis.
- Issue 01 ends inside the conduit as water begins filling behind Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ.
- No train crossing in Issue 01.
- No real company names. Use Caldera House, Vector House, and Orbital House.
- Three Houses everywhere, never five. B11 everywhere, never B1 or Bi11bot.
- Alex’s mother remains entirely unrevealed except for leaving Adrian after he lost everything.
- Ancestry never predicts intelligence, morality, competence, class, wealth, or ideology.
- The safeguards are learned through use and consequence, never through definitions or lectures.
- Alpha has no face, consciousness, or final authority over human values.
- Alex does not become a ruler.
- B11 remains warm, precise, scarfed, fallible, and non-menacing. TJ remains silent and dog-shaped.
- Debt, House ownership, scarcity, disagreement, institutional resistance, and human error survive the ending.
- The ending demonstrates the new process and removes any explicit homework-style question.
