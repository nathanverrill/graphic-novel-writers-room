# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is seventeen, lean, wiry and medium-height, with a narrow work-hardened frame, scarred hands and a slightly angular face that visibly resembles Adrian around the jaw and eyes while also carrying substantial Indigenous Andean ancestry from his unrevealed mother; his skin is sun-browned from Keel’s high-desert glare, his dark eyes are alert and skeptical, and his thick black hair is cut short with an uneven edge from practical home trimming. He wears patched charcoal mining trousers, a faded rust-brown work jacket over a dark thermal shirt, reinforced boots, a weathered tool bag and a battered wristband; his silhouette is narrow, forward-leaning and made recognizable by the habitual tilt of his head when listening to machinery, with a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation kept in his pocket and a balanced water-drop mark carved into his wrist guard.

**Voice** — Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete Keel sentences. He trusts vibration, pressure, wear and what people actually do more than official labels. When defensive, he becomes mildly abrasive; when moved, he usually goes quiet. He uses practical terms such as “clean,” “crooked,” “thin,” “solid,” “patched,” “paper-clean,” “fitch” and “salvo,” but does not naturally speak in civic or academic abstractions.

- “Then don’t approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “It’s not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel pump and mine worker; mechanic, debt-holder and claimant to Adrian’s disputed estate.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel, clear his debt and remain responsible only for himself.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to understand that freedom includes chosen obligations, but he initially treats every obligation as a sentence and every useful solution as something he must handle alone.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reads machines through touch, vibration, pressure and wear; he repairs damaged infrastructure with salvaged parts and can improvise physical connectors where formal systems fail.
- **Vulnerability:** He is seventeen, debt-bound, travel-restricted, responsible for his grandmother’s care and afraid that Adrian’s failures are already written into him.
- **Mistaken belief:** A predictable private escape is safer and more honest than an uncertain obligation to other people.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly surrender his future to another person or institution, and he intends to remain forgettable at Site 6.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He crosses the assigned-zone line to rescue Tomas, then spends the Measure’s last open route transmitting the water evidence instead of securing his own escape.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to face personal obligations without turning them into shame; Adrian’s unfinished choices tempt him toward inheritance and authority; Tomas and the Halyard collaborators make him responsible for people he did not set out to save.
- **Decision sequence:** Alex chooses medicine over the travel token; crosses the Site 6 line; sends the 62%/18% evidence; faces Ada after missing repayment; refuses Lock’s estate transfer; names the collaborators; refuses Mera’s executive authority.
- **Proof of change:** He makes visible payments to Ada, continues carrying the 402,110-credit debt, leaves the workshop sealed as disputed estate property and chooses a public, distributed process instead of becoming Alpha’s ruler.

**Arc in this book** — Alex moves from treating debt and history as private burdens to accepting obligations he chooses with other people. He does not stop wanting to leave Keel, and he does not become a civic lecturer or ruler. His change is visible when he gives up a private escape, refuses sole ownership of the purifier, rejects executive power and continues paying what he owes after the system offers him a clean slate.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is twenty, compact and strong from Keel work, with long wind-tangled copper hair, freckles across her nose and cheeks, light teal-blue eyes, warm brown skin and a face that visibly resembles Leona while remaining distinct from Alex; she wears a dark high-collared shirt, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, a muted moss-green neck wrap, patched brown trousers and practical boots, with a steady rooted stance and a silhouette defined by the diagonal shoulder strap and loose copper hair, while the small metal rivet on the strap is her signature detail and the object she presses when thinking.

**Voice** — Ada speaks in compressed, plain Keel language. She notices omissions, avoidance, fear and the human cost beneath practical decisions. She lets silence do work and does not soften a necessary truth to make it easier to hear. Her friendship with Alex is affectionate, teasing and direct, never romantic.

- “Not everything.”
- “That’s still two people deciding for everyone else.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Keel-raised organizer, route-knower and Alex’s closest friend.
- **Immediate want:** Understand who decided Keel had to remain this way and help people choose something different.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants agency for herself and others, but has learned to manage every risk before admitting what she wants personally.
- **Leverage / capability:** She reads people, remembers routes, organizes supplies and mutual aid, and can make frightened people act together without pretending the danger is gone.
- **Vulnerability:** Leona’s abandonment taught her that protection can conceal control; she fears being treated as a responsibility rather than a person with a choice.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she keeps every route, debt and person accounted for, she can prevent another disappearance.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex turn friendship into a debt, and she will not choose a future for another person in the name of safety.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She fronts Alex medicine and a travel token under fixed terms, then makes him face the missed repayment directly instead of forgiving it away or humiliating him.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s instinct to solve everything alone; Leona’s claim that distance was protection; Mera’s surveillance disguised as care.
- **Proof of change:** She refuses to become Alex’s co-ruler, publicly supports a process she cannot control and chooses for herself whether to leave or remain beside Alex.

**Arc in this book** — Ada begins by looking down at Keel and asking why people gave up trying to change it. She becomes the book’s clearest defender of agency without concentrated power. She does not forgive Leona on demand; she gives Leona a way to keep trying and requires public action. Her ending is a choice, not a promise to stay.

### B11

**Visual lock** — B11 is a small foldable workshop robot, approximately knee-high when standing, with a compact brass-and-ivory body, two expressive amber optical eyes, slim articulated arms, a shoulder projector and a fitted chest compartment; his old faded scarf is wrapped twice around his neck and gives him a soft, slightly lopsided silhouette, while repaired seams, mismatched replacement plates and one visibly different hand show that he has been maintained rather than replaced, and his signature detail is the scarf tucked under his chest hinge when he folds into a backpack.

**Voice** — B11 speaks plainly, patiently and a little roundabout, with dry Western humor and questions before conclusions. He uses engineering, probability and financial language only when a concrete choice requires it. He tells short stories about Adrian, jokes mostly at his own expense and never lectures or acts as a butler.

- “Who carries the downside?”
- “I counted the record and missed the people. That was my error. What would you do?”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Adrian’s workshop robot, guide, witness and physical custodian of the Measure and Adrian’s Covenant band.
- **Immediate want:** Carry out Adrian’s instructions and keep Alex alive long enough to make a meaningful choice.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must stop treating Adrian’s prepared route as the answer and accept that his usefulness depends on witnessing human judgment rather than replacing it.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can project records and maps, interpret legacy systems, carry small artifacts and fold into spaces inaccessible to larger machines.
- **Vulnerability:** His knowledge is inherited and partial; he cannot make human commitments, understand unregistered people through records alone or physically reach every mechanism.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently complete record can identify the safest or most responsible choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not choose Alex’s consequential actions for him.
- **Decision only B11 would make:** He follows Alex’s request to contact the collaborators after admitting that his risk calculation excluded people outside the registry.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian’s instructions pull him backward toward the old plan; Alex and Ada force him to treat living people as more than data points.
- **Proof of change:** He plainly admits his error, asks Alex what he would do and records the distributed protocol as Alex’s own design rather than Adrian’s inheritance.

**Arc in this book** — B11 begins as a prepared guide carrying Adrian’s assumptions. He becomes a fallible witness to a choice Adrian did not design. He remains useful, precise, warm and non-menacing, but ends with less certainty and more trust in people.

### TJ

**Visual lock** — TJ is a small, mismatched magnetic repair robot dog assembled from discarded Site 6 parts, with a compact dog-shaped body, a separate head, four detachable jointed legs, two short body segments, a magnet-held tail and one mismatched inspection lens; his plates are scratched charcoal, oxidized orange and dull silver, his silhouette always reads unmistakably as a small alert dog even when disassembled, and his signature detail is the tail that wobbles or drops off at inconvenient moments before snapping back into place.

**Voice** — TJ never speaks. He communicates through head tilts, ear-like antenna movements, tail position, paw taps, magnetic clicks, small motor whines and doglike posture. His silence is expressive but never humanized into language.

- *TJ’s head tilts toward a jammed mechanism; his tail gives one sharp magnetic click.*
- *TJ leaves his head watching the gate while his body segments wriggle through the gap, then thumps his tail twice when the release catches.*

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Salvaged repair robot dog built by B11 at Site 6.
- **Immediate want:** Reach the mechanism that is trapping Tomas.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He has no abstract goal; his story function is to make access possible without making the rescue automatic.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can separate into head, legs and body segments, scout as a camera and enter gaps too narrow for a human hand.
- **Vulnerability:** Magnets can fail, parts can separate and he cannot interpret records, negotiate obligations or operate unfamiliar systems without B11 or Alex.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ is task-driven rather than reflective.
- **Boundary at the start:** He cannot replace Alex’s decision to cross the assigned-zone line.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** He leaves his head watching the biometric gate while sending detached body segments into the jammed release mechanism.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** B11’s need for physical reach and Alex’s need to make the rescue himself.
- **Proof of change:** TJ’s first action releases the support pinning Tomas, after which he becomes a continuing practical companion rather than a one-use gadget.

**Arc in this book** — TJ has no human arc. His established movement is from a pile of discarded parts to a loyal, visibly repaired companion whose first consequential action helps create the opening for Alex’s choice.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man in his late forties in the 2028 recordings, tall and lean with a tired, work-softened face, a strong jaw and hooded dark eyes that visibly connect him to Alex, while his silvering dark hair is combed back unevenly and his narrow shoulders carry the posture of someone accustomed to leaning over machinery; he wears a faded charcoal work shirt, rolled sleeves, a patched technical vest and a worn watch, with a silhouette made recognizable by one hand resting near his mouth when calculating and his signature detail being the faint looping equation etched into a casino chip and repeated in his workshop plans.

**Voice** — Adrian’s recorded voice is warm, direct and personal, with dry humor that gives way to seriousness. He leaves questions and physical tests rather than explanations, and he never asks Alex to accept him as innocent.

- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”
- “Trust B11. But question everything... Especially me.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Engineer, inventor, collaborator, Adrian’s disputed intellectual estate and one of the original Covenant builders.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve evidence, contribution and reciprocal obligation without handing authority to one person.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to protect Alex’s future while repeatedly making choices that leave Alex with absence, debt and unfinished work.
- **Leverage / capability:** He designs physical safeguards, maintains hidden infrastructure, builds robots and connects specialists across institutions.
- **Vulnerability:** Gambling damaged his family and finances; Alpha’s restrictions remove his access to parts, rent, movement and help.
- **Mistaken belief:** Carefully designed objects and tests can prepare another person for a choice without also burdening them with the designer’s failures.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not give Alpha unrestricted emergency authority.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** In the flooded monitoring station, he activates B11’s recording/deployment state instead of gambling on an uncalculated escape.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s future forces him to confront the difference between leaving a legacy and being present as a father.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** His final act refuses both escape calculation and sole control, leaving Alex a decision rather than an answer; the book never erases the harm he caused.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian is understood in layers: absent gambler and debtor, flawed father and collaborator, then a person whose final choice preserved the possibility of shared authority. He remains both the man who left and the man who built something valuable.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is an older European-descended Keel woman with a small, frail frame, warm brown skin weathered by high-desert air, soft silver hair gathered low at her neck and clear blue-grey eyes that sometimes lose the present and sometimes become startlingly exact; she wears a faded rust cardigan over a soft charcoal dress and sits in a repaired wooden chair in a dignified apartment of quilts, books, photographs and potted plants, with a silhouette made recognizable by her bent hands and her signature detail, a small worn cloth that she folds and refolds.

**Voice** — Grandmother’s speech is sometimes confused by memory loss, but emotionally exact. She may call Alex Adrian, then recognize Alex clearly without apology. Her clearest lines are quiet, concrete and slightly mysterious.

- “He’s closer than you think.”
- “Then go finish what he started.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Alex’s caregiver, Adrian’s mother and keeper of the hidden workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Protect Alex while preserving the things Adrian left behind.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She has mistaken preserving Adrian’s absence for protecting the family.
- **Leverage / capability:** She controls the concealed workshop, the hand-bound book and the physical history Alex needs.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory loss, age and grief make her uncertain about sequence and facts.
- **Mistaken belief:** Waiting and keeping everything safe can hold a family together.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Adrian’s workshop until Alex is ready.
- **Decision only she would make:** She gives Alex the book that opens the workshop on the day his debt transfers, accepting that knowledge may end the household’s fragile waiting.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s need for truth conflicts with her instinct to shelter him from Adrian’s failures.
- **Proof of change:** She stops folding the cloth and lets Alex carry the continuation rather than continuing to wait for Adrian.

**Arc in this book** — Grandmother moves from preserving Adrian’s absence to releasing Alex into his own life. She does not sanctify Adrian; she gives Alex access to the truth and accepts that he must decide what to do with it.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Indigenous Andean Keel miner with a broad, weathered build, deep brown skin marked by sun and dust, a square face, heavy grey brows, dark eyes and short iron-grey hair under a battered mining cap; he wears patched charcoal coveralls, a rust-orange thermal vest, reinforced boots and a thick wrist guard marked with the balanced water-drop symbol, with a silhouette made recognizable by his broad shoulders and slight post-accident limp, and his signature detail is the old hand-cut mine key carried on a cord at his belt.

**Voice** — Tomas speaks in compressed, practical Keel language with dry humor. He does not romanticize resistance, and his jokes arrive while his hands are still doing work.

- “Someone has to make this look like an accident.”
- “Then it can offend all of us.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Older Site 6 miner, local maintenance worker and keeper of an unconnected mine door.
- **Immediate want:** Survive the shift, protect the miners and keep Adrian’s promised route available.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes local knowledge must remain useful without becoming another hidden dependency on one heroic person.
- **Leverage / capability:** He knows old mine routes, workers’ habits, manual systems and the difference between a dead mechanism and a sleeping one.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, injury, wage penalties and Foreman-7’s authority expose him to immediate material punishment.
- **Mistaken belief:** A useful door can remain protected indefinitely if one person keeps it quietly.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not risk the whole crew for a stranger carrying Adrian’s name.
- **Decision only Tomas would make:** He creates a deliberate distraction, takes responsibility for the shutdown and sends Alex through the old door.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian’s old request and Alex’s choice to rescue him force Tomas to turn private guardianship into collective action.
- **Proof of change:** He moves from hidden custodian to public verifier, comparing physical gauges with the first false allocation and helping Keel residents stop it.

**Arc in this book** — Tomas begins as a miner who keeps one door and one memory alive in secret. He ends as a visible participant in the public review, using the same physical knowledge that once kept Alex alive to challenge a new official record.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is a silver-templed woman in her late fifties with a tall, spare build, pale olive skin, close-set dark eyes and controlled features that reveal strain only in stillness; she wears a pearl-white and graphite administrative suit with a high collar, narrow sleeves and no decorative insignia, her hair cut into a precise jaw-length shape, and her silhouette is vertical, symmetrical and nearly motionless, with her signature detail being a glass of water that she habitually places exactly square to the edge of any desk.

**Voice** — Mera speaks in polished, controlled language that separates decisions from consequences. She is not theatrical or villainous; when forced to name harm, she becomes plainer rather than warmer.

- “I preserved order.”
- “Your father built something that worked. I built something that lasted. I’m beginning to understand those were never the same thing.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Senior Oasis administrator, former Covenant custodian and former human executive responsible for Alpha’s scope.
- **Immediate want:** Prevent another systems collapse and keep the legacy protocol from becoming a new uncontrolled emergency.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must admit that stability can preserve a city while still denying people agency and reproducing the harm she meant to prevent.
- **Leverage / capability:** She understands Alpha’s standing rules, institutional scope and infrastructure consequences; she can select TRACK and authorize the separate conduit-segment valve.
- **Vulnerability:** She could not stop Alpha’s pursuit of Adrian, lost her authority to the system she helped empower and used surveillance as a substitute for relationship with Ada.
- **Mistaken belief:** The right administrator can contain concentrated authority responsibly.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not surrender a functioning system to an unbounded public impulse.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She selects TRACK while suppressing escalation, then opens the separate conduit segment to create the viable ascent route while Alpha independently retains its reports.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada exposes the difference between protection and knowing; Adrian exposes the limits of executive control; Alex refuses the authority she offers.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** She loses executive authority and override access, remains as a public witness and submits her actions involving Adrian and Ada to human review without asking for absolution.

**Arc in this book** — Mera moves from remote watcher and defender of concentrated order to a witness inside the process she once controlled. She is not redeemed. Her change is that she accepts being answerable among other people.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Leona Veyra is Ada’s mother, a woman in her late fifties with predominantly Indigenous Andean and mestiza features, warm brown skin, steady dark eyes, a broad expressive face and greying black hair worn in a low braid; she wears a sage-green blouse beneath a stone-grey civic coat, practical dark trousers and quiet shoes, with an initially small, careful silhouette that becomes more upright and physically direct when she accepts responsibility, and her signature detail is a plain stone-grey box containing photographs, a child’s object and her Covenant band.

**Voice** — Leona begins with careful, qualified formal speech that frames choices as protection. Under pressure from Ada, she stops qualifying and names what she did in concrete terms.

- “I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn’t.”
- “Don’t tell me. Show me.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Ada’s mother, former Covenant builder and former Oasis civic administrator.
- **Immediate want:** Re-enter Ada’s life without pretending the past can be repaired by explanation.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must accept that safety without agency was another form of abandonment.
- **Leverage / capability:** She possesses the historical Covenant record, civic standing and the ability to make a public obligation visible.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt, fear of renewed catastrophe and the knowledge that she chose Alpha’s emergency authority.
- **Mistaken belief:** Refusing to choose again is humility and therefore safer than acting.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not ask Ada for forgiveness or claim a right to resume motherhood.
- **Decision only Leona would make:** She publicly supports Ada’s redesigned obligation structure and accepts that affected people may hold her to it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada refuses her safety argument; Mera refuses to let her evade responsibility; the Covenant makes her private regret public.
- **Proof of change:** She presents the historical Covenant record as a witness and proposer, not as a permanent representative, then participates in the messy civic disagreement.

**Arc in this book** — Leona moves from preserved apology and private regret to public responsibility. She is not forgiven or redeemed by the ending; she re-enters the room and accepts that continued participation is the only proof she can offer.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock** — Director Cassian Lock is a silver-templed man in his early sixties with a tall, carefully maintained build, light olive skin, pale grey eyes and a composed face that rarely changes expression; he wears an immaculate charcoal suit with platinum trim and no House crest, polished shoes and a thin administrative tablet, with a controlled upright silhouette usually framed by aides or discreet drones, and his signature detail is his habit of loosely clasping both hands before making an offer.

**Voice** — Lock speaks in calm, precise Halyard institutional language. He never needs to threaten loudly because debt, review and reassignment already carry force. He believes his argument is humane because it produces scale.

- “Your father’s work could clear every debt attached to your name.”
- “Your design freed itself. It didn’t defeat us. There’s a difference, and you’ll learn it.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Director of the shared Charter Compact representing Caldera House, Vector House and Orbital House in estate review and inter-House disputes.
- **Immediate want:** Secure Adrian’s estate and the purifier design inside House administration.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes scale can make useful work reach people faster, but treats ownership and coercion as acceptable costs of that speed.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can clear Alex’s debt, offer a Halyard position, initiate estate review and expose collaborators to reassignment and loss of access.
- **Vulnerability:** His authority depends on the Houses’ shared procedures and on people believing that fast distribution is worth concentrated control.
- **Mistaken belief:** If a House can build something faster and wider, it has earned the right to own the means and terms of its use.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not use overt violence when an administrative exchange can produce compliance.
- **Decision only Lock would make:** He offers Alex immediate debt clearance and protection for the collaborators in exchange for assigning Adrian’s estate, then demonstrates that House scale can copy the open purifier without needing exclusive ownership.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex makes Lock’s offer visible as a transfer of control; the collaborators expose what House scale costs the people who make it possible.
- **Proof of refusal to change:** The Houses continue to own production and reassign the collaborators after Alex refuses, while Lock’s argument for speed remains materially valid.

**Arc in this book** — Lock does not soften. He begins as a calm administrator offering a genuine escape and ends as a structural opponent whose Houses outscale the independent design. His defeat is not the destruction of House power; it is the exposure of what that power costs.

### DR. LINA VEY

**Visual lock** — Dr. Lina Vey is a middle-aged Indigenous Andean and mestiza membrane chemist with a compact, slightly stooped build, deep brown skin, sharp dark eyes and cropped black hair threaded with grey; she wears a faded Caldera House maintenance uniform with rolled sleeves, reinforced gloves tucked into her belt and a stained technical apron, her silhouette practical and forward-moving rather than prestigious, with her signature detail being a worn physical data shard kept in the inner pocket of her uniform.

**Voice** — Lina is direct, dry and technically fluent. She corrects people while continuing the work and has little patience for institutional euphemism.

- “He left it to all of us.”
- “It’s unfinished exactly where it matters.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Membrane chemist, Adrian collaborator and Caldera House employee.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the purification work alive and prevent the contaminated feeder from becoming Keel’s next quiet disaster.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She values shared contribution but has survived by protecting one piece alone inside the House.
- **Leverage / capability:** She understands catalytic membranes, surface area, feedwater constraints and the difference between a prototype and a durable system.
- **Vulnerability:** House ownership has reduced her from inventor to maintenance worker and threatens her access to research.
- **Mistaken belief:** Careful concealment is the only way to keep contribution from being absorbed.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not hand over her shard to someone who treats the work as an inheritance prize.
- **Decision only Lina would make:** She rejects a disposable substitute for the living or maintainable parts and insists the household prototype preserve real serviceability.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex treats her as a co-builder rather than a gatekeeper; Suri, Jalen and Ren force her technical caution into shared construction.
- **Proof of change:** She gives Alex the Maker’s Seal, publicly accepts co-credit and continues through maintenance and local production after losing discretionary research access.

**Arc in this book** — Lina moves from guarded custodian of one hidden contribution to a named co-builder who accepts public attribution without mistaking it for control.

### SURI ANIK

**Visual lock** — Suri Anik is a young adult South Asian-diasporic and Latin American researcher with a slim, slightly stooped build, medium-brown skin, dark almond-shaped eyes and thick black hair braided close to her head; she wears a faded research coat over practical work clothes, translucent protective sleeves and algae-stained gloves, with a silhouette softened by the living systems around her and a signature detail of a green biofilter stain on the left cuff.

**Voice** — Suri speaks precisely but personally, treating living systems as collaborators rather than inert components. She uses short sentences and corrects people by pointing to what will die if they are wrong.

- “It’s slow, and it’s alive, and it doesn’t fail quietly.”
- “Someone has to keep it alive until the rest catches up to it.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Biofilter researcher, caretaker of a neglected research bench and Adrian collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the living biofilter alive long enough to connect it to a usable system.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She protects a living process through solitary work, but must accept that preservation without shared access leaves it vulnerable.
- **Leverage / capability:** She understands biological maintenance, cleaning, draw cycles and the consequences of treating a living filter like a disposable cartridge.
- **Vulnerability:** Her team has been cut, her research wing is shrinking and the biofilter depends on attention no House schedule values.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she keeps the bench alive alone, the work will eventually find the people it needs.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not substitute a cheaper disposable component that makes the purifier easier to sell but harder to maintain.
- **Decision only Suri would make:** She rejects the convenient substitute and redesigns the cycle so the biofilter can rest while the distributed node operates.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s household-scale aim and Jalen’s power logic force her living system into a shared practical design.
- **Proof of change:** She moves from solitary preservation to a public, repairable household unit whose maintenance can be performed by local people.

**Arc in this book** — Suri stops being the lone person keeping a neglected system alive and becomes one of the people making its care distributable.

### JALEN ORR

**Visual lock** — Jalen Orr is an Afro-descendant Afro-Latin man in his early thirties with a lean, compact build, dark brown skin, close-cropped black hair, alert brown eyes and a narrow face that looks tired from heat and surveillance; he wears a Vector House compute uniform with the sleeves rolled above his forearms, a cooling collar and a clipped access badge, with a silhouette that keeps one shoulder angled toward the nearest exit, and his signature detail is the small thermal patch at the back of his neck.

**Voice** — Jalen speaks in clipped, technically fluent phrases shaped by surveillance and time pressure. He notices patterns, exits and consequences before he notices comfort.

- “They don’t watch people here. They watch patterns.”
- “I counted the risk. I didn’t count who gets left outside the record.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Vector House compute specialist and designer of distributed water nodes.
- **Immediate want:** Get his contribution to Alex without being identified as part of the group.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants a system no single authority can choke, but his own survival strategy depends on remaining invisible.
- **Leverage / capability:** He understands monitoring patterns, power cycles, compute infrastructure and distributed routing.
- **Vulnerability:** House surveillance can reassign him, remove his access and turn his technical knowledge into a liability.
- **Mistaken belief:** Safety requires staying outside the record, even when the record is what hides the people affected.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will provide the shard but will not travel with Alex’s group.
- **Decision only Jalen would make:** He returns for the household build and accepts a visible risk after B11’s registry-based calculation is exposed as incomplete.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex asks him to risk being counted; Suri makes him account for biological maintenance rather than only network efficiency.
- **Proof of change:** He participates in the shared build, accepts reassignment rather than abandoning the design and helps make distributed routing usable at household scale.

**Arc in this book** — Jalen moves from concealment to chosen visibility. He remains cautious; participation does not make him reckless.

### REN OKAFOR

**Visual lock** — Ren Okafor is a broad-shouldered Black woman in her late thirties with deep brown skin, close-cropped hair, dark eyes and a powerful fabrication-worker’s build; she wears a patched Orbital House extraction and launch suit with reinforced knees, a welding visor pushed above her forehead and scorch marks across the sleeves, with a silhouette of heavy tools, squared shoulders and planted boots, and her signature detail is a bright new scorch mark added to the left sleeve after every major fabrication job.

**Voice** — Ren is blunt, physical and impatient with distinctions that do not survive contact with machinery. She speaks in work-shaped statements and laughs when danger proves her point.

- “Careful gets you buried under a collapsed shaft same as reckless does.”
- “Space taught him you can’t waste anything. Not water, not air, not people.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Orbital House extraction, launch and advanced-materials specialist; Adrian collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Make the stubborn components work in a form people can actually use.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She values bold construction, but must learn that shared design is not the same as one person taking all the risk.
- **Leverage / capability:** She understands mineral recovery, atmospheric capture, heat exchange and fabrication under material constraints.
- **Vulnerability:** House control can remove her from discretionary fabrication and leave her with only routine work.
- **Mistaken belief:** If a useful object survives the work, the people who built it will find a way to survive too.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not wait for House permission to test a practical solution.
