# First Read: Prosperity, Issue 01, pages 1–3 (Audition A and Audition B)

I read these as the reader in `rules/reader.md`: 16, it's homework, it's the night before, and I'm half hoping it's AI slop so I can say so. **I read A first.** That means B was not a cold read. By the time I got to B's scanner screen I already knew the debt number, so it hit me less hard than it would have. Keep that in mind wherever I compare the two.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **Issue 01, page 1, panel 1.** A kid with his ear pressed to a shaking pipe and his eyes shut. I didn't know where I was, but I wanted to know what he was listening for. That's a good first picture.
- **Page 1, panel 3: "The manual doesn't have to drink the water."** This is the first line that made me sit up. It's short, it's a bit cocky, and it tells me water matters here without explaining it. I liked him a little after this.
- **Page 1, panel 4.** The supervisor taps over the red warning to make it a green checkmark. I got that straight away with no words: the system says no and a person quietly says yes. It also made the supervisor a person and not just a boss.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** The scanner screen is the best moment in A. "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607" right on top of "EARNED TODAY: 40". I didn't need to do any maths to feel sick. Then **panel 3, "Happy birthday."** comes from a guard who looks sorry for him, and that landed. It's a gut-punch. This is the page where I stopped wanting it to be bad.
- **Page 2, panel 4: "Yeah."** He says one word and has a tight jaw. Good. He doesn't make a speech about it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3, panel 4.** I skimmed the bouncer's speech. "Your dad always thought the next hand would fix the house. Your mother knew better. She left the day he lost everything." That's the backstory read out loud by a guy with a broom. It sounds like a character being used to tell me things.
- **Page 3, panel 1.** The street and the sign. Fine, but nothing happens in it.

### I was confused
- **Page 2.** Who is Adrian? I worked out on page 3 that he's the dad, but on page 2 "GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM" was just a name. And where *is* he? Dead? Gone? In jail? The book never says. I couldn't tell if that's a mystery on purpose or just something left out. **Confused and detached.**
- **Page 2.** "PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER". I guessed it means the dad's debt became his on his birthday, but only because of "Happy birthday". If the guard hadn't said that I wouldn't have got it.
- **Page 3, panel 3.** The bouncer says "Turning seventeen today" and Alex answers "Just doing the math." Doing what math? The debt? The equation on the chip? It doesn't answer what the bouncer said.
- **Page 3, panel 2.** There's an equation etched on the chip and nobody mentions it. **Confused but intrigued**, though only a little.
- Who does Alex live with? Mum left, dad is a question mark. Is he alone?

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Keel | p1 | No. I think it's a mining or pumping town, but I'm guessing from the pictures. |
| Alpha (node, scanner) | p1 p4, p2 p1 | No. Some kind of system that runs everything? No idea. |
| Credits | p2 | Sort of. Money. |
| Liability transfer | p2 | Sort of, from "Happy birthday". |
| Guarantor / Adrian Phantum | p2 | Partly. He's the dad (p3). Where he is: no. |
| Travel status: restricted | p2 | No. Restricted from going where? Why? |
| The chip's equation | p3 | No. |
| The Last Chance | p3 | Yes. A casino where the dad gambled. |

### Lines I read twice
- "Just doing the math." (p3 p3). I still don't know what it answers.
- "Then she was the smart one." (p3 p4). I read it twice to check whether he's angry at his mum, his dad, or himself. I think the dad, but I'm not sure.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: clear. Quiet, good with his hands, bitter.
- Supervisor, Guard and Bouncer: I could tell them apart by their jobs. The guard is the only one who felt like a person, because of that look. The supervisor and the bouncer are basically there to give information.
- There are no other characters I'm meant to care about. Alex is completely alone in these three pages. That's maybe on purpose, but it means there's nobody for him to talk to.

### I felt something
Yes, page 2, the guard saying "Happy birthday". It was pity, and a little embarrassment for him. Nothing on page 3.

### I predicted
He's going to gamble with that chip, probably at the Last Chance, to try and wipe the debt the way his dad did. The equation is a "system" for winning. I'd bet money on it, ha.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his gambling dad's impossible debt on his 17th birthday in a town where a computer controls everything.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 3, panel 4. The scene goes from a real moment on page 2 to a guy explaining the family history. If I were in a shop, this is where I'd close it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. "Then she was the smart one." is a bitter line, not a question. I know his situation but I don't know what he *wants* or what he's going to *do*. Nothing happens to him on page 3; he just walks and gets told things. I'd wait a week or not bother. The only thread pulling me is the chip, and I already think I know where that goes.

## Version B

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1 (splash).** It's one big image: the pump running fine under his hand while the gauge says red. I got the joke of it and liked it: the machine says broken, but he knows better. Since it's one panel, it reads fast.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** Everyone else's balance flickers up and disappears, and his blows up huge above him. That's humiliating in a way I felt. It's public, like everyone getting their grades read out.
- **"GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED."** This made me want to know more. The dad is *missing*, and even the system doesn't know where he is. **Confused but intrigued.**
- **"PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS"** and then **page 2, panel 3, "Twenty-eight point seven years."** said quietly to himself. That number is easier to feel than 418,607. He'd be 45. That hit.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** The workers go around him and nobody speaks. Then a red line comes up: "REVIEW REQUIRED". Something is about to go wrong for him and I don't know what. Good.
- **Page 3, Ada.** A second person who actually likes him. "You look expensive." made me smile. The medicine and the token sitting between them on the railing is a picture I understood without words: one is what he needs, the other is what he wants.
- **Page 3, panel 4: "This time I can afford the first step." / "No. This time somebody fronted it."** This is the best exchange in either version. She's being kind and calling him out at the same time.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 2.** There's a lot on this page: a nine-line scanner screen, a red warning, Alex muttering to himself, the bouncer conversation. I skimmed the second half of the scanner screen because I'd already seen the same numbers in A (which isn't fair to B), and because it's a lot of all-caps.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The bouncer's back-and-forth went past fast and I didn't fully take it in.
- **Page 3, panel 3.** "Fixed installments. Same amount each time. No surprise fees." I slid past this. It reads like a terms-and-conditions joke.

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 4: "It changed something." / "For him?" / "For everybody else."** The dad's last hand changed things for *everybody else*? Who else? The town? His family? I didn't get it, and it felt like it was supposed to be a big line. **Confused and detached.**
- **Page 3, panel 1.** Halyard and Oasis appear as places with no explanation. I can see a nicer town and a shiny thing above the clouds, so I get "rich people up there", but that comes from the picture, not from the book telling me.
- **Page 3.** Travel status is RESTRICTED on page 2, so how does a token let him leave on page 3? Does the token beat the restriction? Is it illegal? I couldn't tell if Ada is helping him break a rule or just buying him a bus ticket.
- **Page 3, panel 2.** His grandmother? This is the first time she's mentioned. So he does have family. Is she sick? It's dropped in and moved past.
- Who is Ada to him? Friend, older sister figure, girlfriend, dealer? She's 20 and brings him medicine on credit. I liked her but didn't know what she is to him.
- **Page 3, panel 4: "You keep saying that."** So he's been talking about leaving for a while, but I've never seen him want it. I'm being told it's an old argument I wasn't there for.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Keel | p1 | Partly. A worn-out mining town with a "pit". Clearer than A thanks to the overlook. |
| White-and-amber node / control system | p1, p2 | No. It's watching, that's all I know. The word "Alpha" never appears, which is fine. |
| Debt transfer day | p2 | Mostly. The screen literally says it, so I got it faster than in A. |
| Adrian Phantum | p2 | Yes, he's the dad (p2 p4), and he's missing. |
| Review required: household liability | p2 | No. A threat I can't read yet. |
| Travel restricted / travel token | p2, p3 | No. They seem to contradict each other. |
| Halyard, Oasis | p3 | No. Only from the picture. |
| Grandmother | p3 | No. |
| Ada Veyra | p3 | Partly. I know her name only because the art says so; nobody says it out loud. |
| The chip's equation | p2 | No. |

### Lines I read twice
- "For everybody else." (p2 p4). I gave up on this one.
- "It changed something." (p2 p4).
- "If you still want out after you sleep." (p3 p3). I read it twice to work out that the token is for leaving. I got it on the second go.
- "No. This time somebody fronted it." (p3 p4). I read it twice because I liked it, not because I was lost.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex and Ada: clearly different people to *look* at.
- But **everybody in B talks in quips.** Alex: "Then don't approve it", "I've had better surprises", "It's my debt face", "twenty-eight-year warranty", "You planning to become a bank?" The bouncer: "That your birthday face?" Ada: "You look expensive", "We can skip the educational portion." By page 3 everyone sounds like the same funny person, like a Marvel movie. I'll be blunt: this is the kind of thing my class would call AI-sounding, because every line is a little zinger. Ada's best lines are the ones that *aren't* jokes ("I didn't ask for today." "I know.").
- The supervisor, guard and bouncer blur together, because all three are there to make a remark at him.

### I felt something
- Page 2, standing alone under his own number while people walk around him. That was shame.
- Page 3, the last two lines: "I'll pay you back." / "I know." That was warm. She trusts him and he hasn't earned it. I wanted that to be true.
- "Happy birthday, Phantum." hit less than in A. Partly that's because I'd already read A. Partly it's because Alex answers with a joke straight away and that lets the air out.

### I predicted
He takes the token, tries to leave, and the "REVIEW REQUIRED" thing stops him or comes after Ada for fronting him. The grandmother gets sicker and that's what keeps him in Keel. He'll use the chip at some point too.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who inherits his missing gambler dad's debt has to decide whether to run for the rich city above, with a friend paying his way.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 2, panel 4, the bouncer exchange ending in "For everybody else." It's a crowded page, and it finishes on a line I didn't understand while every character is cracking jokes. If page 3 hadn't brought Ada in, this is where I'd have stopped.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mostly yes. It isn't a cliffhanger, nothing explodes, but now he wants something (out) and has a way to try (the token), and someone has put something on the line for him (Ada). I want to know if he actually goes, and what that "REVIEW REQUIRED" was about. It's a real question about a person, not a loud noise. I'd read the next one this week, not tonight.

---

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
B. The deciding moment was **B, page 3, panel 4**: Ada saying "No. This time somebody fronted it." and Alex taking the token after a beat. That's the first time in either version that Alex *does* something about his life and someone else gets involved. A ends with him knowing his situation and doing nothing. B ends with him making a move.

To be fair to A: **A's pages 1–2 read cleaner than B's.** "The manual doesn't have to drink the water." is the best line in either version, and A's "Happy birthday." hit me harder than B's. What lost me in A was page 3, the bouncer explaining the backstory. What nearly lost me in B was page 2: too much on one page, a line I couldn't follow, and a group of characters who all talk like stand-up comedians.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A sharp, sad first two pages about a kid crushed by a number, then a man with a broom explains the family history and it stops.
- **B:** Too jokey and too crowded in the middle, but by the end I cared about him and the girl with the medicine, and I wanted to see him try to leave.
