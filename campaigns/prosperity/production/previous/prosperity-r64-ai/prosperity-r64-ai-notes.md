# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a rethink.** The approved 54-page structure and the strongest causal beats are present, but the current package still contradicts canon in production, delays essential context until Issue 03, and turns the Issue 02 and Issue 03 climaxes into procedural explanation. The book should preserve the pump repair, 62/18 discovery, Tomas callback, Ada/Leona confrontation, Adrian’s deployment-state reveal, and “the water isn’t,” while sending the script back for structural and execution repairs.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Page 1 violates the opening non-negotiable

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 1; `brief.md` Non-negotiables.
- **Observation:** Page 1 is a full-page establishing splash of Keel, Halyard and Oasis. Alex first appears on Page 2.
- **Problem:** The brief requires Page 1 to begin with Alex as a person at work, not an abstract world statement. The current page also retains the explicitly discarded captions “Humanity reached the stars…” and “…never escaped the gravity of inequality.”
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer/Layout Agent: make Alex’s pump repair the Page 1 image, using the pump hall to establish Keel, Alpha’s node, repaired infrastructure and the vertical system through concrete action. Remove the abstract theme captions. Move the three-city establishing information to a later visual beat only if it earns its space.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 2. Page 46 execution does not depict the scripted scene

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 03 Page 46; `layouts.md`, Page 46; `thumbnails.md`, Page 46.
- **Observation:** The script calls for Adrian’s flooded monitoring-station recording and B11’s deployment-state activation. The supplied layout and thumbnail depict Leona and Mera in an apartment discussing association risk.
- **Problem:** The finished execution would omit the approved Adrian/B11 reveal and deliver unrelated dialogue. This is a direct script-to-page failure.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Layout Agent: discard the current Page 46 layout and thumbnail and rebuild the flooded monitoring-station sequence in no more than four panels. Letterer: replace the apartment lettering with the Page 46 script lettering. Preserve the cut before Adrian’s evacuation; do not show a physical handoff.
- **Role:** Layout, Letterer.

#### 3. The Issue 01 escape geography is not readable enough to support the cliffhanger

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Pages 16–18.
- **Observation:** Page 16 presents the choice as a private unmapped exit or a public transmission that forces Alex back through Tomas’s door. Page 17 then shows Mera opening a separate ascent route, Ada appearing at the ascent entrance, and Page 18 filling that route with water.
- **Problem:** The reader cannot reliably tell which route each character uses, why Ada is present, or whether the water is helping or threatening the group. The stated cost of Alex’s choice appears to disappear.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer/Layout Agent: stage three distinct routes in visible sequence—(1) the sealed private side passage, (2) the miners’ residential-service route, and (3) Mera’s separate ascent-conduit segment. Show Ada’s physical entrance from the miners’ route before the group reaches the ascent conduit. Show the rising water entering from Mera’s conduit valve, not the residential regulator. Preserve the uncertainty of Tomas’s signal without implying that he entered the conduit.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

### Major

#### 4. The Tomas cliffhanger is not acknowledged until his reappearance

- **Location:** Issue 01 Page 18; Issue 02 Pages 19–36; Issue 03 Pages 52–53.
- **Observation:** Issue 01 ends on Tomas’s lost signal. Issue 02 never asks whether he survived, and Issue 03 introduces him alive at the public review.
- **Problem:** The first issue promises a question about a person the reader cares about, but the next issue behaves as if the question was irrelevant. The delayed answer therefore reads as a dropped thread rather than suspense.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: add one brief Issue 02 acknowledgment—Alex or Ada asks about Tomas and receives no answer. Plant one concrete indication before Page 52 that Tomas may have survived, then let Page 52 reveal his continued work and give him the physical-gauge action that pays off his expertise.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 5. Remove the unsupported `PHANTUM-LINK` implication

- **Location:** Issue 01 Page 18 Panel 3 and any lettering derived from it.
- **Observation:** The status is written as `TOMAS PHANTUM-LINK — SIGNAL LOST`.
- **Problem:** No such system or family connection exists in canon. The phrase falsely implies that Tomas is linked to Alex by blood or a defined technical status.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Replace it with `TOMAS SITE 6 SIGNAL — LOST` or `TOMAS — SIGNAL LOST`. Do not introduce a new term.
- **Role:** Writer, Letterer.

#### 6. Mera’s Page 36 choice is not character-centered

- **Location:** Issue 01 Page 8; Issue 02 Page 36.
- **Observation:** Mera is initially only a back, hand and interface. Page 36 ends on her selecting `SUMMON` from `TRACK / SUMMON`.
- **Problem:** The reader does not yet know Mera’s role, stake, or relationship to Alex. The issue turn is therefore a menu selection rather than a decision by a person the reader understands.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: identify Mera as a senior Oasis administrator before Page 36 and establish that she has limited scope over Alpha. At Page 36, show her choosing to summon Alex because the Seal event exceeds her authority to suppress, while escalation remains unavailable. Preserve the Ada connection and moral history for Issue 03.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 7. The governing premise arrives too late

- **Location:** Issue 01 Pages 8–17; Issue 02 Page 36; Issue 03 Pages 41–52.
- **Observation:** Alpha is active from Page 8, but the Water War, Covenant custodians, two-of-three authorization, and absence of expiration/revocation are not concretely established until Page 41.
- **Problem:** The reader must guess what Alpha is and why Mera matters for most of the book. The restoration climax then supplies the premise instead of paying it off.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: seed one usable fact per issue without a lecture. Issue 01 should show an old emergency authority or standing order attached to Alpha; Issue 02 should show the Covenant’s three custodians or an unreturned authorization in a registry trace; Page 36 should make Mera’s third safeguard visually personal. Keep the fuller explanation on Pages 41–42.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 8. The collaborator sequence is a repeated fetch pattern

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 23–27.
- **Observation:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren each appear in a separate encounter, explain a component, hand it over, and are separated by an access obstacle.
- **Problem:** The reader predicts the architecture after the first encounter. The collaborators blur into their technical specialties, and the issue’s middle loses forward pressure.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter/Writer: retain all four collaborators and their contributions, but make at least two encounters causal rather than sequential. For example, combine Suri and Jalen in one extraction where the biofilter’s survival depends on Jalen’s monitored power route; make Ren’s component necessary to escape or stabilize the first failed test. Each collaborator must make a choice that changes the next action.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer.

#### 9. The contaminated feeder is insufficiently connected to the main water thread

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 19–33.
- **Observation:** The narrative moves from the 62%/18% routing discrepancy to a contaminated feeder, then spends most of the issue on purification and ownership.
- **Problem:** The reader can experience the purifier as a replacement plot. The original water crisis disappears while the second problem takes over.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: keep the problems separate but place them in one visible causal frame—routing determines where water goes; contamination determines whether that water is usable. Return the 62/18 consequence during the prototype or Lock sequence so the purifier remains a bounded response, not the new central crisis.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 10. The 94% and 91% results need an explicit distinction

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 28, 32–33.
- **Observation:** The industrial configuration reaches 94% simulated efficiency; the household configuration later reaches 91%.
- **Problem:** Without a clear transition, the lower number reads as unexplained degradation.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Label Page 28 as `INDUSTRIAL CONFIGURATION — SIMULATED RECOVERY: 94%` and Page 33 as `HOUSEHOLD CONFIGURATION — SIMULATED RECOVERY: 91%`. Show that the lower result is the deliberate cost of shrinking the system while retaining maintenance and feedwater requirements.
- **Role:** Writer, Letterer.

#### 11. The Maker’s Seal’s activation and consequence are unclear

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 24, 34–35; Issue 03 Page 48.
- **Observation:** Lina gives Alex the one physical Seal after the collaborators confirm the build. It warms and displays `CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION REGISTERED`.
- **Problem:** The reader cannot tell what has changed or why possession of the object matters. The Seal risks reading as a chosen-one trigger rather than a contribution-and-responsibility safeguard.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: make the collaborators’ confirmation an action on the working purifier and public contributor record, then let the Seal register that evidence. Show the practical result as preserved attribution and maintenance responsibility—not ownership, sovereignty, or automatic authority.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 12. Adrian’s Covenant band appears without a setup

#### 14. The final review relies too heavily on policy screens

Layout Agent: prioritize faces, hands, gauges, evidence and the approval control over interface text.

## Plausibility ledger

- **Format:** Exactly 54 pages in three 18-page issues is fixed. Any chapter labels, chapter end cards, or inherited 15-page structures are superseded.
- **Opening:** Page 1 must begin with Alex at work. The abstract inequality captions are explicitly set aside.
- **Alpha:** Alpha is a distributed network operating through nodes, records and delegated interfaces. It has no face, body or consciousness. Mera’s actions must remain limited to her human scope; independent reports must continue.
- **Tomas:** Tomas survives Site 6. No Phantum link or family link exists. His Page 18 signal may disappear, but the status must not invent a relationship.
- **Ada’s route:** Ada needs a visible physical route into the old works. The residential regulator, Mera’s conduit valve and the sealed private passage are separate systems.
- **Measure:** The Measure senses, compares, traces and verifies evidence. It does not move water, grant authority or choose an outcome.
- **Maker’s Seal:** One physical Seal exists. Projections and diagrams must not read as a second object. The Seal registers contribution and responsibility; it does not grant sovereignty.
- **Covenant:** Adrian’s band is with B11; Leona’s is in the stone-grey box; Mera’s remains dark. Linking Adrian’s and Leona’s bands does not restore the old emergency authority.
- **B11:** Adrian activates a recording/deployment state in the flooded station. Alex later activates the dormant workshop body. No physical handoff or evacuation is shown.
- **Purifier:** 94% and 91% are different simulated configurations. The household purifier remains feedwater-dependent, requires cleaning and paid replacement parts, and does not end scarcity.
- **Houses:** Only Caldera House, Vector House and Orbital House are canon. Aqualis, Helion, Astra and “five Houses” are inherited-draft conflicts.
- **Morrow & Sons:** The shop remains in Keel. A Halyard page may show its product or service record, not a second location.
- **Debt:** Alex’s balance remains after restoration. The transition from 418,607 to 402,110 must be supported by visible time and payments.
- **Alex’s mother:** No new information may be added beyond her leaving Adrian after he lost everything.
- **Public review:** The process can pause or challenge an allocation. It does not instantly equalize water, erase debt or transfer ownership.
- **Hard-SF bounds:** The restoration node and Distributed Verification Protocol must act only through the established functions of evidence, attribution and reciprocal agreement. No safeguard may perform another safeguard’s job.
