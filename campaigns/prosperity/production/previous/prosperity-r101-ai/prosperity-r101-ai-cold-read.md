# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it straight through, pages 1–64, in one sitting, the night before it was due, the way it'll actually get read. I didn't go back and reread anything except the lines listed under "Lines I read twice."

---

## Issue 00 — Lake Keel, 2099 (pages 1–10)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 1.** A dog-thing stepping off a dock and *coming apart mid-stride* is a good first image. I didn't know TJ was a robot dog yet, but I wanted to see what happened next.
- **Page 1, panel 2.** "Is TJ broken?" / "Not usually." First laugh. I relaxed a bit.
- **Page 1, panel 4.** "Let's see what they came to take." The old man isn't scared. I wanted to know why.
- **Page 3, panel 1.** "The old man with everything." Now I want to know how this guy got everything. That's the actual hook of the issue for me.
- **Page 7.** The lost leg's magnet pulsing underwater, then dragging the head back, then TJ putting himself back together crooked. That was the best stretch. I liked TJ more than any human on these pages.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 3–5.** The head is in one place, the torso in another, the legs somewhere else, plus a drone, a hose and a barge. I gave up tracking which piece of TJ was where and just waited for the result.
- **Page 10.** Four panels that tell me "water is worth stealing" and "they'll come back." I already got that. Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 4, panel 3.** The granddaughter calls him "Alex—", not Grandpa. I stopped and checked whether she really was his granddaughter.
- **Page 5, panel 2.** "It has the route." What route? Route to where? Never answered.
- **Page 8, panel 2.** "I could have started something." Started what? A war? With who? Confused but intrigued. I took it to mean he's scared of something bigger.
- **Page 9.** What did TJ actually bring back? "Water-routing code, no recognizable control node" means nothing to me. "Something they didn't want found." Okay, but I have no idea what it is or why it matters.
- Why does a guy "with everything" just let thieves take his water and not call anyone? I couldn't tell if he's wise, guilty, or in on it.

### Lines I read twice
- "He always sees the part that costs." (p6) Read twice. Still not sure what it means.
- "Water paid for it?" / "Or water made him rich." (p3) Isn't that the same thing? I read it three times looking for the difference.
- "I could have started something." (p8)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, the girl and TJ: clear.
- Thief 1 and Thief 2: no difference at all and I didn't need one. Fine.
- The girl has no name. By page 10 I didn't care about her. She's there to ask questions.

### I felt something
- Mild fondness for TJ on page 7, coming back together crooked. That's the only real feeling in this issue.

### I predicted
- That the book would be about how Alex got rich off water, and that it was something shady.
- That the thieves would come back at the end of the book. (They don't. See the last issue.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A rich old man who controls a lake lets water thieves go because he's hiding something about how he got rich.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. It's ten pages and the dog is weird enough to keep me going. Then page 11 jumps to "2043" and I nearly stopped, because I'd just figured out who these people were.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 10's barge light turning back is a pretty soft cliffhanger. Nobody's hurt and I barely know anyone. What actually makes me turn the page is page 3: how did this guy get "everything"? I'd keep going, but out of curiosity, not need.

## Issue 01 — Keel, 2043 (pages 11–28)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 11, panel 2.** The gauge reads `FLOW: NOMINAL` while water is leaking on his hand. Got it right away: the machine is lying. Good.
- **Page 12.** Best page in the book. He turns seventeen and a scanner hands him his missing dad's debt: 418,607 credits, 28.7 years, travel restricted. "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "Twenty-eight point seven." That landed. I'm on his side now. A kid getting his parent's debt dumped on him is something real people my age could actually be scared of.
- **Page 15.** The medicine loan with Tomas. "I need the money. Not a story." Small and real. I liked Tomas immediately.
- **Pages 16–17.** The notebook, the chip fitting the mark, the bookcase that isn't a bookcase, the secret workshop. Standard but it works. I wanted to see what was in there.
- **Page 18.** B11 waking up. "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." and "A fact with a poor record." B11 is funny and I liked him right away.
- **Page 26, panel 4.** "Go. I'm about to become paperwork." Great line. Tomas is the best human in the book.
- **Pages 27–28.** `REPORTED TO KEEL: 62%` / `DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%`. Finally a clear, simple, angry fact. The whole issue paid off here. I understood exactly what was wrong and who was getting cheated.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 13–14.** Walking and talking in riddles past a casino sign and a ration channel. Skimmed.
- **Pages 19–21.** Projected routes, "Measure case," a node, "legacy protocol," "Alpha trace." My eyes slid off this. It felt like setup for something I wasn't being told.
- **Page 22.** Another scanner screen. By now there have been a lot of screens.

### I was confused
- **Who is Ada?** Sister? Girlfriend? Best friend? Neighbour? She's on almost every page and I never found out. She brings food and his spare wrench, so she's close to him, but how close?
- **Whose grandmother?** (p15–16.) I assumed Alex's. Then she disappears after page 16 and the medicine is never mentioned again, even though it's the reason he took the loan.
- **Page 19.** "Do you want answers or an exit?" / "The exit." Then he looks at the medicine and says "Answers first." Why does looking at the medicine change his mind? I don't get the connection.
- **Page 20.** He plugs something in and his travel restriction gets "confirmed" and Tomas gets moved to Site 6. Did Alex *cause* that? Did Alpha? I couldn't tell if this was bad luck or a trap.
- **Pages 22–24, the order of events.** He's sent to Site 6 (p22). Then suddenly it's Friday at the clinic and he lost the money (p23). Then he's at Site 6 and Tomas is pinned (p24). Did Friday happen before Site 6 or after? And how did he lose the money? "Forfeited shift" — forfeited why? I was lost on time here.
- **Page 25.** B11 says "I predicted we could leave him and remain unseen." Alex: "You were wrong." But the beam *did* turn back, so wasn't B11 right? I read this three times and still don't know who was wrong about what.
- **Page 26.** "Adrian asked me to keep this door closed… From anyone who came for the work." So why does Tomas open it now? Because Alex saved him? Probably, but it goes by fast.
- **The eyes.** In Issue 00, TJ has a **brass ring around his right eye**. Here TJ has "two plain ceramic eyes" and **B11** is the one with the brass ring. Was I supposed to notice that? Is B11 inside TJ in 2099? Is B11 dead? I noticed it and nothing ever explains it. Confused but intrigued at first. By the end of the book, just confused.

Yes, mostly. Page 28 is a real payoff and a real cliffhanger: he's just told the whole town, the gate slams and beams are closing in on Site 6, where Tomas is. Tomas is someone I care about standing in that danger. I'd turn to Issue 02 now.

## Issue 02 — Halyard, 2043 (pages 29–46)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 29.** Tomas and the miners just standing there. "We're already dispersed." / "This is just where we stand." Good, quiet and stubborn. Then I never find out what happened to them this issue.
- **Page 30, panel 3.** "It is a route. The destination objected." B11 is still the funniest thing in the book.
- **Page 33, panel 4.** "Too much glass." Ada's best line. It told me everything about Halyard in three words.
- **Page 34.** `CREATOR: UNLISTED` / `CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS` and the worker saying "My correction is in there." That was clear and it stung. The company owns what workers make. Got it.
- **Page 35.** Lock's offer: sign it over and your debt goes away. Real temptation. I wanted to see if Alex would take it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 30–32.** Flood-tunnel escape. Climbing, a rope, a rail breaks, a gate closes. I've seen this a hundred times. Skimmed all three pages. Also, nobody is chasing them, so there's no danger except water.
- **Pages 37–40.** Four pages, four makers, one per page. Walk in, they say a line about their tech, hand over a part, page over. It felt like a video game fetch quest: collect the four pieces. By Ren (p40) I wasn't reading the dialogue anymore.
- **Pages 41–42.** Building the purifier. Then Alex writes a warning label on it: `TESTED PROTOTYPE — SERVICE REQUIRED — TREATMENT LIMITS APPLY`. I get that it's honest. It was also the most boring thing a main character has done so far. It felt like a safety pamphlet.
- **Pages 43–44.** Typing names into a registry. Hands on a screen. A seal. A field. A button. That's the climax of the issue and it's paperwork.

### I was confused
- **What happened to Tomas and the miners?** Page 29 sets up a standoff and the issue leaves.
- **What is a "House"?** Caldera House, Vector House, Orbital House. Companies? Families? Countries? They get dropped like I already know them, one per page from p37.
- **Why is Halyard up?** Alex climbs *up* through a storm drain into it. Is it on top of Keel? Above it? I couldn't picture the world.
- **Page 36.** "I'm not signing people I haven't met." What does signing do to them, exactly? Lock says it'll "protect" them, and Lina later says "Don't sign anything." I sort of get that signing is bad, but not why.
- **Page 38.** Suri doesn't hand anything over, she says "I'll bring it when the build can keep it alive." Then on p41 she's there with it. Fine, but I had to flip back.
- **Page 39.** Jalen is in a *Vector* House corridor. How did Alex get from Caldera to Vector to Orbital? Are these all in Halyard? No idea.
- **Page 44, panel 1.** "The bronze Maker's Seal." Where did this come from? Why does Lina have it? What does it do? First time I've heard of it.
- **Page 44.** "EXCLUSIVE CLAIM: BLOCKED PENDING REVIEW" and "DESIGN: PUBLIC / NONEXCLUSIVE." I think this means they gave the design away free so the House can't own it? I *think*. I'm guessing.
- **Page 45.** Why are shutters coming down? Who ordered it? Because they released the design? I assumed so.
- **Page 46, panel 4.** "CONVENOR SUMMONS: OASIS CIVIC ADMINISTRATION." Convenor of what? Who's summoning him? What's Oasis? Hit with three new words on the cliffhanger panel.
- **What happened to Lock?** He walks in on p43, says "the review begins in one hour," and then the book forgets him. He's the closest thing to a villain and he never gets a scene where he loses.

Not really. Alex and Ada are separated, which I care about a bit. Lina is taken, which I don't, because I barely met her. Then the actual cliffhanger is a summons from a word I don't know ("Convenor") to a place I don't know ("Oasis"). It's a loud noise with nobody I know standing in it. I'd wait a week, or until the teacher made me.

## Issue 03 — Oasis, 2043 (pages 47–64)

### Pulled me forward
- **Pages 49–50.** Ada and Leona. "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" That's the strongest scene in the back half. I figured out on my own that Leona is probably Ada's mother who left, and the book never says it outright. That part I liked. "Did you know where I was?" / "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." That hurt.
- **Page 54.** The recording of Adrian in rising water. "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." The bulkhead closes on his hand. First time the dad felt like a person.
- **Page 55.** "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Good beat. B11 earned it.
- **Page 58, panel 2.** `DEBT: CLEARANCE AVAILABLE`. The temptation is back, and this time it's real. I wanted to see if he'd crack.
- **Page 62.** Tomas checking the gauge, still dry, while the paper says 420 delivered. "Owned units aren't delivered water." That woke me up. It's the 62/18 trick all over again and it means they didn't fix it. Good.
- **Page 63, panel 3.** `MEDICINE ADVANCE: PAID`. "Took you long enough." / "You kept counting." That was the most satisfying moment in the book. A 45-credit loan from page 15 got paid back. I felt more about that than about the whole civic hall.
- **Page 63, panel 4.** The debt is still 402,110. He's barely touched it. I respected that. It didn't pretend.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 47–48.** Getting to Oasis. Drone passes, "Not today." Skimmed.
- **Pages 51–52.** Leona talking about "standing" and "public challenge" and putting a band on a table. The mother scene turned into a procedure, and I skimmed.
- **Pages 56–60.** The civic hall. Records, seals, bands, fields, a lever, instruction strips, signatures, `DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL`. This is the climax of the whole book and I was speed-reading it to get to the end. It's a meeting. The final boss is a form.
- **Page 61.** Tables with three representatives each. Felt like the end of a civics worksheet.

### I was confused
- **Is Leona Ada's mother?** I think so. I'm not sure. If she isn't, I've misread the only emotional scene in the issue.
- **Leona Veyra / Lina Vey / Mera Vale.** Three women with names that start the same and arrive within a few pages of each other. On page 52 I thought Leona *was* Lina for a second.
- **The bands.** "A dark metal band" in Adrian's workshop (p17). "A dark metal band" in Leona's box (p49). Leona's band "warms and links to Adrian's band" (p52), then "a separate dark band remains untouched." Whose is that one? What does linking do? On page 56 they're suddenly called "the Covenant bands." I've never heard the word Covenant. I have no idea what the bands do, and the ending seems to depend on them.
- **Page 51.** "If I leave, they can remove my standing." What standing? Why does it matter?
- **Mera.** She shows up on page 53: "I was Mera Vale. I had executive access." Who is she? Why is she with Alex and B11? How did they meet? She's suddenly important and I got no introduction at all.
- **Page 53.** So Alpha was chasing Adrian, and that's why his records were restricted? Was the debt Alpha's doing? I think that's what it's saying, but I'm not sure.
- **Page 54.** "B11, transmit the record." / B11: "I may have mistaken that for an instruction." What? He *was* given an instruction. Did B11 not send it? Was the whole book happening because B11 sat in a box instead of sending the record? I read it four times and gave up.
- **Is Adrian dead?** "Presumed dead," "legal status: missing." I still don't know.
- **Page 57.** "Alpha has no face or body." So I finally see Alpha, and it's lights. I still don't know what Alpha *is*. A government? A program? Who built it?
- **Page 58.** "Don't make two people the answer for everybody." Which two people? The lever says SINGLE authority. Alex and Ada? Alex and Mera? Alex and his dad? I didn't get it.
- **Page 59.** Mera: "Refusal leaves all of it in place." Then he refuses, and on p60 the "emergency authority" is "rescinded." So did refusing fix things or not? And what *did* fix it, the signing? What did he sign?
- **Page 60.** I couldn't explain what happened on this page to someone. Strips go into a record, people sign a field, a structure gets registered. Something changed. I don't know what.
- **Page 63.** "MORROW & SONS." Who is Morrow? That name has never appeared. Is it Ada's family? Tomas's? A dead guy? Why are five makers' names on a sign called Morrow & Sons?
- **The 2099 prologue.** In 2099 Alex is "the old man with everything," with a lake, and he lets thieves go. The book ends in 2043 with Alex 402,110 credits in debt, waiting in a travel queue. How does he get from here to there? The thieves "come back" on page 10 and never do. The brass eye ring on TJ is never explained. Issue 00 asked the only big question I had, and the book never goes back to it.

The book never goes back to it. If the next book promised that answer, I might pick it up. On this last page alone, no.

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 60.** Strips fed into a record, people signing a "blank process field," `THREE TEMPORARY REPRESENTATIVES / EACH AFFECTED PLACE`, `DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL`, `EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED`. This is the big win of the book, and nobody in my class could say what happened. It reads like the end of a government handout. Runner-up: **pages 37–40**, the four-maker fetch quest, where half the room would have stopped reading dialogue.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid gets his dad's giant debt, finds a robot and a gadget that proves the water company is lying, then it turns into a lot of meetings about who owns stuff. The robot dog is cool and there's a good bit with a loan shark who isn't really a loan shark."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 00:** A cool robot dog coming apart and a rich old man with a secret, and the book never comes back to either.
- **Issue 01:** The best issue: a birthday debt, a funny robot, and a clear, angry 62%-vs-18% reveal, buried under words like Alpha and node.
- **Issue 02:** Collect four inventors, build a box, give it away. A fetch quest where I couldn't tell the inventors apart.
- **Issue 03:** One real mother-daughter scene and one paid-back loan, inside a climax that's mostly signing forms.
