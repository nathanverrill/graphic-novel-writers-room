# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it the way the brief says: homework, the night before, wanting it to be bad. I read the whole thing once, in order, pages 1–64. Some notes come from flipping back to check things. I say so where I did.

There is no issue title over pages 1–10. "Issue 01" starts on page 11. So I'm treating pages 1–10 as an opening (a prologue) and reporting on it separately. It gets no VERDICT line because it isn't labelled as an issue.

---

## Opening (pages 1–10, before the "Issue 01" title)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 2.** The robot dog jumps off the dock and comes apart in midair, and the pieces keep going. That's a good image. It's the first thing in the book I actually looked at instead of just reading. "Is TJ broken?" / "Not usually." is funny.
- **Page 1, panel 4.** "They're stealing from us." A clear problem on page 1. Good.
- **Pages 3–5.** The dog jamming the pump is easy to follow, and it's fun. I wanted to see whether he'd get out.
- **Page 6, panel 4.** "He brought something." / "He usually does." I liked TJ by this point.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 7–9.** Once the action stops it turns into a run of short, mysterious grandpa sayings. I skimmed page 8 completely. Nothing happens on it: the boat leaves and they agree to do nothing.
- **Page 9.** The leg clicks in, slips out, clicks in. "So will the lake." I went straight on past it.

### I was confused
- **Page 2.** Who are Nera and Voss? They get four lines each and then they're gone for the rest of the book.
- **Page 2.** "That's Phantum's lake." I didn't know who Phantum was. Page 12 later tells me it's Alex's surname, but on page 2 it's just a word.
- **Page 3.** Why won't Alex call the wardens? "And tell them what?" Tell them a tanker is stealing your water? The granddaughter is right and he brushes her off. I didn't understand his reason.
- **Page 7.** "They said you made water pay for it." I don't know who "they" are, and I don't know what "made water pay" means.
- **Page 10.** Alex holds the fragment "in one hand and the lake in the other direction". I couldn't picture that.
- **The big one, which I only noticed at the end:** the book never comes back to this. No 2099, no granddaughter, no TJ. Nobody explains how the lake got there or why it's "clean", and nobody follows up the commercial mark on the drone fragment. The best character on these pages, the dog, never appears again.

### Lines I read twice
- p7: "Sometimes the work sends an account." Gave up on it.
- p2: "Then he stole it first."
- p9: "So will the lake."
- p4: "He knows the difference between stuck and working." Fine on the second read.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, the granddaughter and TJ were all clear. Nera and Voss were just "red hood" and "lamp helmet". I couldn't have told you which of them said what.

### I felt something
A bit for TJ when his leg tore off (p5). That's all.

### I predicted
I thought we'd find out how Grandpa "got" the lake and that it would be something shady. The book never answers it.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A grandfather with a clean lake in a world short on water, people stealing from him, and a hint that he did something to get it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 8. The thieves get away, Grandpa decides to do nothing, and I'm getting fortune-cookie lines. If page 11 hadn't opened on a new title and a new setting, I'd have stopped.

### The last page (page 10)
"Because chasing people is not the same as making water safe." That's a poster line, not an ending. What does make me turn the page is the new issue title and the sense that the real story is starting.

## Issue 01 — The Inheritance (pages 11–22)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 12, panel 2.** DEBT TRANSFER DAY. You turn 17 and you inherit your missing dad's debt: 418,607 credits, 28.7 years to pay off. This is the hook of the whole book, and it worked on me. Everyone my age knows the feeling of being handed something adults messed up.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "Twenty-eight point seven." Cold and good. I got that it's his birthday and the debt is his present.
- **Page 13, panel 1.** "I saved enough for one." / "There are two mugs." / "I'm generous with containers." I liked Ada straight away.
- **Page 15, panel 1.** Grandmother calls him "Adrian", then corrects herself. That hit. Without anyone saying it, I knew she's losing her memory and that he looks like his dad.
- **Page 16.** The bookcase opens into a secret room when he puts the notebook on the shelf. Classic, and it works. "That's one way to hide a room." / "Badly, eventually." Funny.
- **Page 18.** Bi11bot's entrance. "That took longer than expected" and "Adrian's jaw. His poorer timing." Good personality.
- **Page 19.** Dad's message admits he gambled. I liked that he isn't secretly perfect.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 14.** The photo and the watch. I understood it but didn't care much yet.
- **Page 17.** Chalkboard, brass plate, recess, map with three ticks: four clues on one page. I skimmed and hoped none of them would be important. Some were, and I'd forgotten them.
- **Pages 20–21.** The explaining dump. Alpha, three cities, an infrastructure spine, three safeguards and Site 6, all said by the robot in about four lines. I read it but kept none of it, except that Alpha is the computer that runs everything.
- **Page 22.** I skimmed the tracking-ring stuff.

### I was confused
- **Page 11.** Is this the same Alex as the grandpa? There's no caption with a year. I only worked out it was a flashback because of "Seventeen" on page 12 and the lake being "absent" on page 13.
- **Page 12.** Foreman-7 is a *human*? With a name like that I pictured a robot the whole way through. I only learned otherwise by checking back.
- **Page 12, panel 4.** "The Last Chance bar is closed." Is it important? It's never mentioned again.
- **Page 13.** Who is Ada to Alex? A friend? A girlfriend? Someone who lives with them? She's cooking in his grandmother's flat on page 15. I never found out.
- **Page 15.** There's a woman checking medicine. Nobody says her name on the page. Later a "Pilar" talks (p23) and I assumed it was the same woman, but I was guessing.
- **Page 18.** "Inserts the old key-shaped connector found beside the empty recess." I didn't see a connector being found.
- **Page 20.** His band "stopped reporting" because he's in a hidden room, so now he can't move? It took me a while to understand he's being punished for going off the map.
- **Page 21, panel 4.** "Just checking." Checking what? He touches the mine location on his band. Is he agreeing to go? Is it a joke? I don't know.
- **Page 22.** Who is Mera? Why does she line up a glass with the edge of her desk and then stop herself? It looked like it was supposed to mean something. I had no idea what.

## Issue 02 — The Price of Departure (pages 23–37)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 24.** Alex leaves his work zone to free a trapped worker, and the band instantly fines him 40 credits. Very clear and very unfair. I got it straight away.
- **Page 24, panel 3.** "You know that costs you." / "I noticed." Good.
- **Page 26, panel 3.** "He said I'd know you by what you did first." I got this one: helping Tomas was the test. A good click.
- **Page 27.** "Foreman! Your safety report is on fire." Laughed. "Abandoned mines do not sweat." Best atmosphere line in the book.
- **Page 29.** The robot gets the route wrong and Alex reads the bolts with his hand. I liked the robot being wrong.
- **Page 32.** REPORTED 62%, DELIVERED 18%. This is the clearest moment in the book. They're lying about the water, here's the number, done. Two short lines ("Same period?" / "Same water.") and it lands.
- **Page 34.** The numbers go up on every public screen in town. Tomas: "We're already in it." Good.
- **Page 35.** Neighbours pool their money so he can get out. I liked the town showing up for him.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 23.** It starts with the robot asking "Who carries the downside?" out of nowhere. Then budgeting, then a deal with a man I've never met. Three different moments packed onto one page. I skimmed it.
- **Pages 28–29.** I skimmed the map business. "Ink ticks mark every third junction" meant nothing to me.
- **Page 33.** I lost the thread here (see below) and skimmed to the train.

### I was confused
- **Page 23 → 24.** Tomas sells Alex a key at the Site 6 entrance on page 23. On page 24 Tomas is trapped under a collapsed frame inside Site 6. Did time pass? Did he walk in ahead of them? It felt like two different scenes glued together.
- **The keys.** Page 18: a "key-shaped connector". Page 23: Tomas gives Alex "the heavy old key". Page 25: Tomas has "the old key at his belt". Page 26: Tomas fits the "hand-cut key". Page 27: Alex turns "the old key". I couldn't tell you how many keys there are or who has which one.
- **Page 23.** "Forty credits. Key, token and parts." What token? What is it for?
- **Page 25.** Bi11bot: "I predicted we could leave him unseen." Wait, the robot wanted to leave a trapped man? Then he asks "What would you do?" and *Tomas* answers, "I'd use the door they forgot." That doesn't answer the question. It's as if two conversations crossed.
- **Page 25.** "Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense." I got that he's in trouble and his family will pay. I didn't get what he obstructed.
- **Page 30.** "Inventory's moving." What inventory? Water, I guessed.
- **Page 31.** "It wasn't in the workshop." / "Adrian hid the answer where the question hurt." Pretty, but I don't know what it means.
- **Pages 33–36.** This is where I really lost it. The robot says the private route is open. Alex says "Then it's not the route we take." Why not? Then the private route closes, and he says "Then we use the train," as if that was always the backup. And what does "bought the gate" mean? I eventually worked out that the neighbours paid his overdue installment, so the band lets him travel. But I had to go back.
- **Page 35.** Pilar adds an envelope, pulls her hand back and says "Care stays funded." Did she give money or not?
- **Page 35.** Why 500 when the debt is 418,000? I guessed it's one monthly payment. Never said.

## Issue 03 — The Price of Ownership (pages 38–44)

### Pulled me forward

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 41–43.** Four new people in one page, each with one line, then a three-night invention montage told in one panel. I skimmed all of it. I could not tell you which of the four did what without going back. Page 44 actually lists them for me, which tells you something.
- **Page 44, panel 4.** I skimmed the payment split, 12 / 18 / 8. It's a receipt.

- **Page 44.** The Maker's Seal just gets handed over. What is it? Where did Lina get it? It turns out to be a safeguard, but nobody says so here. I only connected it later, because it ends up on the table with the Measure.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 44 ends on a payment breakdown. It's not a cliffhanger, it's a receipt. Nothing is at risk and nobody I care about is in danger. I'd only go on because it's homework.

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 60.** Alex turns to the representatives and recites a governance plan: districts choose the deciders, Alpha publishes, anyone can challenge with evidence, people beside the pipe get to speak. Every kid in the room would recognise it as the part the teacher wants on the test. Pages 59 and 61 make it worse: one is a three-step "this object means…" speech, the other is four panels of system text. Runner-up: **page 41**, four strangers on one page.

Something else the class would notice, since they're watching for AI: a lot of lines use the same balanced shape. "Those aren't opposites." "Those aren't the same thing." "Chasing people is not the same as making water safe." "This tells us X. It doesn't Y." There are also a lot of short wise-sounding lines that don't quite mean anything: "Sometimes the work sends an account." "Adrian hid the answer where the question hurt." "Then the wrongness travels efficiently." Each one alone is fine. All together, they're the sort of thing that makes a skeptical sixteen-year-old say "a computer wrote this." The lines that felt human were the jokes: "I'm generous with containers." "Your safety report is on fire." "That's what you have?"

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dad's huge debt on his 17th birthday, finds his secret lab and a sarcastic robot, and proves the computer running everything has been stealing his town's water. It's good for the first half, then it turns into a lesson about who should be in charge. Also there's a robot dog at the start, and it never comes back."
