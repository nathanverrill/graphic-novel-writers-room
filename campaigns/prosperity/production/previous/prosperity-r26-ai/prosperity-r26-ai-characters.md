# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is seventeen, lean, wiry, and approximately 178 cm tall, with a mechanically capable build, medium-brown skin, a mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended appearance, a strong jaw, dark brown eyes, thick black hair cut short at the sides and left uneven on top, and a habitual head tilt when thinking; he wears a faded charcoal work shirt, patched rust-brown mining trousers, scuffed black work boots, a sleeveless utility vest, and a weathered tool bag, with a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation always in his pocket as his signature detail.

**Voice** — Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete sentences. His sarcasm is defensive, not performative; he describes evidence rather than principles and translates abstractions into machinery, pressure, debt, distance, and consequence.  
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”  
- “I just want it to be my choice.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic and mine worker.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and choose his own future.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to accept responsibility without becoming the sole authority responsible for everyone.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reads machinery through vibration, pressure, wear, and sound; he improvises repairs and notices contradictions between records and physical reality.
- **Vulnerability:** Debt, restricted movement, his grandmother's medicine, and the shame attached to Adrian's reputation.
- **Mistaken belief:** Adrian left him only debt and abandonment; control is the only reliable way to prevent systems from failing.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not trust official records over what a machine or person is visibly doing.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He publishes Keel's hidden water discrepancy instead of taking the private escape route.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada challenges his escape instinct; Adrian's legacy tempts him toward inherited authority.
- **Decision sequence:** He helps Tomas before taking the access window, restores contributor ownership instead of claiming Adrian's estate, and refuses permanent exclusive override authority.

**Arc in this book** — Alex moves from **Escape → Curiosity → Truth → Responsibility → Ownership → Trust → Power → Restraint → Agency**. His proof of change is that he still leaves Keel, but only after the unlawful household-transfer and allocation liabilities are voided, after he refuses permanent authority, and when leaving is finally his choice. He remains a mechanic and witness, limited to authenticating Adrian's transfer and convening the first equal three-city review.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is twenty, compact and steady at approximately 165 cm tall, with a strong working build, medium-brown skin, a mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla appearance, a broad expressive face, freckles across her nose and cheeks, light teal-blue eyes, and long copper hair usually tied loosely so wind pulls strands free; she wears a dark high-collared shirt, durable charcoal trousers, work boots, a rivet-studded shoulder strap, and a muted moss-green wrap, with her rooted stance and wind-tangled copper hair as her signature details.

**Voice** — Ada speaks concisely and emotionally precisely. She asks one clean question that forces people to name what they are protecting, and she translates institutional abstractions into human burdens and obligations.  
- “Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?”  
- “Then that's what the agreement has to protect.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel worker, observer, civic collaborator, and Alex's longtime friend.
- **Immediate want:** Rebuild Keel rather than abandon it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to accept that rebuilding may require leaving, travel, and institutions beyond Keel.
- **Leverage / capability:** She reads people, detects emotional evasions, and reframes conflict around fear and mutual protection.
- **Vulnerability:** Her belief that Leona died; her fear that Alex's escape will become permanent abandonment.
- **Mistaken belief:** Staying physically present is the clearest proof of loyalty.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex reduce Keel's problems to a price he can personally pay.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She turns the collapsing three-city negotiation from competing demands into a question of what each group fears losing.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's desire to leave forces her to distinguish loving someone from keeping them in place.
- **Decision sequence:** She stays in the workshop, follows the investigation, confronts Leona, and leaves Keel with Alex while continuing civic work elsewhere.

**Arc in this book** — Ada begins as the rooted counterweight to Alex's escape. Discovering that Leona is alive makes her confront the cost of protection by silence. Her proof of change is that she does not abandon rebuilding when she leaves Keel; she carries it into Halyard and Oasis through local civic and community work, choosing partnership with Alex without formally naming their romantic feeling.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is shown in archival recordings as a European-descended man in his late forties to early fifties, approximately 183 cm tall, lean and long-limbed, with tired hazel eyes, a strong jaw shared with Alex, thick dark hair beginning to gray, and a kind but worn face; he wears a burgundy work jacket over a faded shirt, practical trousers, and old boots, with a characteristic head tilt and a casino chip used as a thinking object as his signature details.

**Voice** — Adrian speaks warmly and self-questioningly, using odds, bets, uncertainty, and questions as a discipline rather than a performance. He never tells Alex exactly what to think.  
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”  
- “What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Disgraced gambler, inventor, entrepreneur, collaborator, and architect of the three safeguards.
- **Immediate want:** Complete the dormant transfer before Alpha can permanently absorb it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to restore human authority but has repeatedly treated people, including his family, as variables in a wager.
- **Leverage / capability:** Mathematics, systems design, probability, invention, entrepreneurial risk, and a network of collaborators.
- **Vulnerability:** Gambling, debt, pride, family guilt, and his dependence on secrecy.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently well-designed test can prepare another person for consequences he failed to face himself.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not make Alex a simple copy of himself or grant authority without demonstrated judgment.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He completes the transfer through Bi11bot while flood-control gates, ventilation, and escape routes are closing around him.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's existence forces Adrian to confront whether a useful legacy can compensate for an absent father.
- **Decision sequence:** He builds safeguards, leaves evidence, refuses to escape the final chamber, and dies completing the transfer.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian's arc is reconstructed after death rather than lived in the present. He remains a real gambler, inventor, entrepreneur, collaborator, and harmful absent father. The proof of his final change is that he stops betting on his own calculation and places the last wager on Alex's capacity to choose freely, without absolving himself of the damage he caused.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a child-sized mechanical companion approximately 90 cm tall, visibly built from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with a compact rectangular torso, jointed arms, short articulated legs, tooled seams, mismatched repairs, maker's stamps, soft blue and amber indicator lights, and two expressive optical eyes, one surrounded by a small adjustable lens ring; he has no threatening features, no red lights, no predatory posture, and his signature detail is the visibly repaired brass-and-ivory construction.

**Voice** — Bi11bot speaks with dry, economical precision and patient warmth. He teaches through concrete examples, probability, machinery, money, and questions, withholding conclusions without becoming evasive.  
- “...Well. That took longer than expected.”  
- “A wager.” / “On us.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Adrian's guide, archive, evaluator, mentor, companion, and authenticated witness.
- **Immediate want:** Deliver Adrian's records and witness the succession test without choosing for Alex.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must preserve Adrian's intent while refusing to become another authority that determines the result.
- **Leverage / capability:** Offline archives, mechanical access, technical knowledge, probability analysis, and the B11 witness record.
- **Vulnerability:** He is physically limited, dependent on repairs, and emotionally invested in Adrian's hope for Alex.
- **Mistaken belief:** Careful withholding can protect a person from being overwhelmed by truth.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not independently validate Alex as successor or make a constitutional decision.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He gives Alex enough information to choose, then records the choice instead of steering it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's suspicion forces him to become more direct without becoming controlling.
- **Decision sequence:** He awakens, explains Alpha and the safeguards, assembles TJ, carries Adrian's transfer package, and signs the final witness record.

**Arc in this book** — Bi11bot begins as a guarded inheritance and becomes a trusted companion without becoming Alex's conscience. His proof of change is that he preserves the evidence of Alex's choices while leaving the final judgment human.

### TJ

**Visual lock** — TJ is a small mismatched magnetic repair dog approximately 35 cm long, assembled from scuffed titanium, ceramic, rubber, and painted scrap parts, with a compact dog-shaped head, a single camera eye, four detachable magnetic legs, a segmented body, and a short detachable tail; every component shows different wear and color, and his signature detail is the visible magnetic seam that lets his head, legs, body segments, and tail separate and snap back together.

**Voice** — TJ never speaks. He communicates through clicks, snaps, servo whines, magnetic clacks, posture, head orientation, and practical repair gestures; his behavior is observant and purposeful, never a verbal joke or exaggerated cartoon reaction.  
- “Tik-tik.”  
- “Krrt—snap.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Bi11bot's repair companion.
- **Immediate want:** Reach inaccessible mechanisms and complete practical repairs.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He is built from discarded parts but must prove that modular, improvised construction can be dependable.
- **Leverage / capability:** He separates and recombines his body, leaves his head as a camera, and sends parts through gaps.
- **Vulnerability:** Magnetic joints can fail under force, heat, dust, or interference.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ is nonverbal and action-led.
- **Boundary at the start:** He does not imitate human speech or emotion.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** He reconfigures his body into the shape required by the trapped mechanism rather than preserving a doglike silhouette.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot's reliance on him turns a salvaged tool into a trusted partner.
- **Decision sequence:** He enters the Site 6 mechanism, completes the practical repair, and remains available for later work.

**Arc in this book** — TJ's proof of character is functional reliability. He remains nonverbal, practical, and non-menacing; his construction demonstrates that repair and cooperation can produce capability without seamlessness.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is an older white Latin American woman of European-descended heritage, approximately 173 cm tall, slim and erect, with pale olive skin, silver-blond hair cut in a precise jaw-length bob, gray-green eyes, a narrow composed face, and controlled posture; she wears sharply tailored dove-gray and deep navy clothing with a high collar, discreet metal fasteners, and a single glass tumbler or transparent interface panel as a signature detail.

**Voice** — Mera speaks in controlled, declarative, consequence-oriented sentences. She uses stability, risk, intervention, authorization, and responsibility to make coercion sound like administration.  
- “Because I remember what happened the last time we trusted people with it.”  
- “I preserved order.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alpha-associated overseer and former Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Prevent regional collapse by keeping concentrated coordination intact.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to admit that defending order made her complicit in a system she could no longer control.
- **Leverage / capability:** Institutional authority, historical knowledge, access to Alpha's records, and credibility earned through surviving the Water Wars.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over Adrian's death, fear of mass instability, and the knowledge that Alpha rejected her override.
- **Mistaken belief:** Distributed responsibility will fail whenever scarcity becomes frightening enough.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Alpha's loss of control if she believes disclosure could destabilize essential infrastructure.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She offers Alex the power she once defended rather than insisting that she personally retain it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex refuses her premise without humiliating her; Adrian's death makes her certainty impossible to maintain.
- **Decision sequence:** She tracks the first activation, bargains with Alex, attempts to override Alpha, confesses Alpha's role in Adrian's death, and accepts participation without supremacy.

**Arc in this book** — Mera moves from controlled observer to exposed participant. Her proof of change is not absolution: she becomes one citizen among many, admits “So did I,” and accepts Alex's invitation to help rebuild a system that can judge her actions.

**Arc in this book** — Leona moves from hidden custodian to accountable participant. Her proof of change is that her restrictions are suspended, her case enters independent human review, and she accepts public participation without regaining a personal veto or office.

**Visual lock** — Mara Doss is a Keel community organizer and displaced parent in her early forties, approximately 170 cm tall, broad-shouldered and strong, with dark brown skin, tightly curled black hair threaded with gray and tied under a faded rust scarf, deep brown eyes, and a direct weathered face; she wears a patched dark-red work coat, layered shirts, heavy boots, and a canvas carrier filled with water tags and ration cards, with the red scarf as her signature detail.

**Voice** — Mara speaks plainly, protectively, and with the compressed urgency of someone accustomed to making scarce supplies last.  
- “Keel can release emergency water for thirty days.”  
- “You can call it a reserve. My children still have to drink it.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel community organizer and displaced parent.
- **Immediate want:** Secure Keel's water and protect displaced families from being treated as expendable.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to risk temporary generosity without surrendering Keel's claim.
- **Leverage / capability:** Local trust, distribution knowledge, organizing skill, and direct knowledge of household scarcity.
- **Vulnerability:** Fear of another hidden diversion and the loss of her children's future.
- **Mistaken belief:** Any concession made before Keel's claim is settled will become permanent loss.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not promise water that Keel physically cannot spare.
- **Decision only Mara would make:** She offers thirty days of emergency water before her city's full claim is resolved.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Elias's public capacity data and Frank's limited access force her to distinguish reciprocity from surrender.
- **Decision sequence:** She makes the thirty-day commitment and joins the public monitoring of its fulfillment.

**Arc in this book** — Mara moves from protective distrust toward conditional trust. Her proof of change is a measurable, time-limited commitment that preserves Keel's right to renegotiate.
