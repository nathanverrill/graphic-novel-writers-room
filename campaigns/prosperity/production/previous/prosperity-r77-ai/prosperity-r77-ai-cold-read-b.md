# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, pages only, night before it's due. One pass, reactions as they happened.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
Page 2 did it. Not page 1 — page 1 is a fine cold open (kid doing an unapproved repair while a node watches) but page 2 is the hook: **DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS** against **EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**, then **PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS**, and the guard's "Happy birthday, Phantum." I did the math in my head and got angry on his behalf. The system hands a seventeen-year-old half a million in debt as a birthday present. That's the moment the book had me.

Pages 9–16 I read fast: crossing the yellow line and losing the day's pay (p10), the dog assembling itself out of B11's chest (p11), Tomas's key (p12), the two pipes — one silent, one vibrating (p13), and then **PHYSICAL FLOW: 18% / REPORTED FLOW: 62%** (p15). The whole issue builds to two numbers and the numbers deliver.

Page 16: Alex presses SEND with the wristband literally warning him **CONSEQUENCE: ROUTE EXPOSURE / ACCESS CLOSURE** — he burns his own escape route to publish the truth. I noticed. Respected it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 4–5, the bookcase. The book fits a hidden catch *exactly* and a secret workshop opens. I felt the plot handing him things. Didn't skim, but rolled my eyes.

Page 8, Mera's control room. The book cuts away from Alex right when I'd settled into him, to a woman I don't know in a place (Oasis) I can't picture, doing something technical with routes. Not dense, just disconnected. I read it slightly detached.

Pages 17–18: pressure, a relay tearing loose, climbing toward light. Action mush. I skimmed the splash page.

### I was confused
- Page 4: "Site 6 took the shift." Site 6 doesn't exist for me yet. Then page 8 Mera sends him to Site 6 like it's a new development. So was he already working Site 6 shifts? Small trip.
- Page 14–15: "Alpha's data line," "Alpha's node." Who or what is Alpha? I kept waiting for the book to say. It never does. (See ledger.)
- Page 17: Mera opens a valve to help them escape, and the pressure from her valve tears Tomas's relay loose. Did she just hurt Tomas? Nobody ever says, and the book walks past it. I noticed the cause-and-effect even if the book didn't want me to.
- Page 18: is Tomas dead? "Telemetry interrupted." Confused but *intrigued* — this is the good kind, I wanted issue 2 to answer it.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **"fitch"** (p3): "Five fitch each shift." What is a fitch? Never explained, never used again. I decided it was slang or a typo and moved on, but I stumbled on it.
- **Site 6** (p4), used before it's introduced (p8–9).
- **Keel** — a town? a company? a city? I inferred "company town" by page 19 but the book never says.
- **Alpha** (p14–15) — two mentions, zero explanation. The biggest hole in the book.
- **The debt itself** — 418,607 credits of "parental household liability." Liability *for what*? Gambling? Fines? Never said what Adrian's debt actually is.
- **Uplink Industrial** (p13) — a name on a pipe. Later there's an "Orbital Industrial Branch" (p37). Same thing? The book never ties them.
- **Grandmother** — whose mother is she? And I noticed by page 5 that Alex's mother simply does not exist in this book. Not mentioned once.

### Lines I read twice
- "Same hand. Different damage." (p5) — read twice, never solved it. Kept the mood, dropped the meaning.
- "He didn't ask enough." (p12) — Tomas. Read twice. A riddle that doesn't want to be solved.
- "The bolts say somebody turned." (p13) — read twice and *liked* it. This one earns the reread.
- "A fact with a poor record." (p6) — read twice, smiled. B11's best early line.
- "Would you like the route, or the question first?" (p6) — I waited for "the question." I think it finally arrives on page 29 ("What would you do?"), twenty-three pages later. If that's the payoff, it's a long fuse.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, B11, Tomas, Inez — yes. B11 has the only real voice in the book (dry, tired, funny). Ada is "the one who knows routes," Inez is "ledger woman," Tomas is "pipe guy" — thin but functional. Mera is a name and a node, no person yet. Foreman-7 got a little personality from "So does the gate" (p9). The supervisor from page 1 vanishes forever — and the **REVIEW REQUIRED** stamp from page 1 never comes back on-panel, unless "REVIEW PENDING" (p19) is meant to be it, which is very quiet if so.

### I felt something
- Page 2: the 28.7 years. Genuine gut punch.
- Page 10: **WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR** for crossing a line to help a trapped man. Angry. This is the book at its best — the system punishing decency in one line of screen text.
- Page 12: Tomas kicking the cart and taking the blame. Warmth.
- Page 15: 18% vs 62%. Satisfaction.

### I predicted
- Dad's dead or vanished and the debt is the trap — called it at page 2.
- The workshop contains a map to proof — right.
- Tomas gets hurt for helping — half-right.
- The debt would get cleared by the ending. (The book later proved me wrong. Credit for that.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid born into his missing father's debt discovers the water company is lying about how much water his town actually gets, and his dad left him the tools to prove it.

### Where I would have put it down
I wouldn't have, in this issue. Page 2 got me and nothing in eighteen pages lost me long enough. Closest call is page 8 (the Mera cutaway) — if page 2 hadn't landed, page 8 would have been the exit.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but softly. The full-page climb-toward-light splash is pretty and generic — the pull isn't the image, it's Tomas's dark relay light and wanting to see what the 18/62 reveal does. I'm turning the page for people, not for the cliffhanger.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 19: "It was a route. The destination objected." And Tomas's status: **UNRESOLVED** — "Not dead. Not safe." The book answered my issue-1 question fast. Good.
- Page 20, panel 2: the worker's hand staying on the component after the kiosk takes it. Best panel in the issue. No dialogue, and it says everything the next ten pages say with dialogue.
- Pages 21–22: Lock's offer. "It is value with a lock on it." / "I can make it spendable tonight." Clear deal, clear villain. I wanted the refusal.
- Pages 33–34: the refusal and the naming. "Their work stays named. Mine does too." Read it straight through, no skimming.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 23–28, the assembly stretch. Membrane, biofilter, node logic, recovery unit, eleven seconds, connector edges. It blurs into competent technical noise. I skimmed page 26 entirely.
- Pages 27–28: the machine goes from **11%** to **94%** because Alex adds a salvaged sleeve. Four actual inventors can't make their own parts meet, and the pipe kid fixes it in one page. I said "come on" out loud. This is where the book feels least like people and most like a diagram of a point.
- Page 31: shrinking the unit into "the one people can own." I got the idea and still skimmed. It's a product-design meeting.
- Page 32: Morrow & Sons. A whole page about posted margins and "We fix what we sold." This is where the homework smell got strongest. I read it the way I read a textbook sidebar.

### I was confused
- The book changed subjects. Issue 1 was stolen water; issue 2 is stolen credit for inventions. Around page 20–22 I kept waiting for the water plot and it took me a while to accept we'd switched. Confused but not detached — the dad thread carried me through the swerve.
- Pages 24/34: the Maker's Seal. "It checks who is standing behind the name." Then Alex holds it, says "Public, nonexclusive license," and the registry corrects itself. Why does that *work*? Is the Seal a key, a vote, a microphone? I never learned the mechanism. Confused, but I took the emotional point (the name appears) and kept going.
- Page 22: **LINA VEY**. Page 38: **ADA VEYRA**. Are Lina and Ada related? Those names are too close for the book to never say a word about it. I waited the whole rest of the book for that shoe. It never drops.
- Page 30: Lock on a wall speaker. How is he watching? What is Lock director *of*? All three Houses send notices at once and he comments — so he's above the Houses? Never pinned down.
- Page 36: **SUPPRESSION: CONTRADICTORY**. Read it twice. Don't know what it means. Moved on.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Mara** (p19): "Mara's still shipping." First mention, said like I know her.
- **Houses** (Caldera, Vector, Orbital) — used from page 20 like I know the structure. A House is… a company that owns the permission to make things? I assembled it from context; never told.
- **The Covenant band** (p36): "Her dark Covenant band rests beside the controls." Capital letters, zero setup. Ledger item that pays off late and thin.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then I become a pattern." (p25) — got it on the reread (the patrol catches a repeated signal, and the signal is him). Riddle line.
- "It tells you what it can hold." (p28) — read twice. Sounds wise. Not sure it says anything.
- "Thin still carries." (p30) — read twice, gave up.
- "I can ask. I can't make the answer clean." (p36) — read twice. Mera talks like a caption.
- Pattern notice: in Halyard, *everyone* speaks in the same clipped aphorism — Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren, Lock, even B11 half the time. Same rhythm, same wise-and-compressed shape. If I were the suspicious kid in class, this is where I'd say the book talks like a quote generator. That's the AI-smell moment.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lock: yes — the stylus, the calm offer. Generic menace but a clear silhouette. The four collaborators: **no**. Lina stands out only because she carries the Seal and gets the jaw-tighten panel. Suri is "gentle one with the vial." Jalen and Ren blur completely — both talk in clipped tech-riddles, and I kept having to check the name tags. By page 34 I could only match names to components because the script lists them in order. And Ada disappears this issue — she opens one hatch and says "Thin still carries," otherwise she's luggage. I noticed, because she was half the engine of issue 1.

### I felt something
- Page 20: the hand on the component. Small ache.
- Page 24: **CREATOR: UNLISTED** and Lina's jaw. Small anger.
- Page 34: the registry correcting to **LINA VEY**. Satisfaction — I didn't understand the mechanics and the name appearing still got me.
- Page 29: "I counted the record and missed the people." I didn't feel emotion; I felt *instructed*. The book pointing at its own theme with a laser pointer.

### I predicted
- Lock's deal refused — called it the moment he offered.
- Alex fixes the machine the experts can't — called it, hated being right.
- The dad's estate matters beyond money — right.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Records versus reality — who the system says made something, owns something, deserves something, versus who actually did. The water was just the first example.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 32. The Morrow & Sons page. Reading at 11pm for a class, that's where the book goes face-down. It's a page about ethical retail.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Weakly yes — and only because of Adrian. The last page is a summons: **APPEAR AT OASIS LEGACY NODE**. That's a calendar invite, not a cliffhanger. What pulls me is the estate, the recording that "stops before the outcome," and how Adrian actually ended. The hook is a dead man, not the meeting.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 32; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 37: Tomas alive, gauge in hand, "Pipe first." The book pays off its best side character immediately.
- Pages 39–40: Ada and Leona. "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" The most human scene in the book. I read it slowly on purpose.
- Page 45: Adrian's last recording. I've wanted this since page 7 and the book made me wait thirty-eight pages. Water rising, "If I stay, the record stays with the machine." It lands, mostly.
- Page 51: "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." The callback to page 15, with Tomas holding the gauge this time. Earned.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 41–44: the Covenant exposition. Bands, two-of-three, **EXPIRATION: NONE**, "We kept waiting for 'later.'" I read page 41 twice and still only got the shape of it. This is the homework chapter of the homework book.
- Pages 47–50: the lever scene. Four pages of a hand hovering over a lever while people say thesis lines. I knew he wouldn't pull it from page 48, panel 3, so the hovering felt long.
- Pages 52–53: the review procedure. Challenge accepted, approval paused, next review date recorded *by hand*. I skimmed. The book ends its plot with a meeting adjourning.

### I was confused
- Page 41: what the Covenant bands physically *do*. They warm, they link, they "illuminate obligations." Technology or a mood ring with legal force? Never explained. Confused and a little detached — the book's most important object runs on vibes.
- Pages 44–45: page 44 says the station is sealed and "the record ends inside." Page 45 plays the recording from inside the station. So how does anyone have this recording? I eventually did the math myself — B11 was there at the cradle and carried it out when Adrian deployed him — but the book made me assemble that alone, and I still can't explain how B11 got from a flooding sealed station into the grandmother's bookcase. Confused but intrigued, because it's Adrian.
- Pages 47–48: the designation lever. What does it *designate*? Mera says "Take it. You can change those tonight" — debt, travel, water. So it makes him… the boss? The successor? I never learned what the lever actually confers, which is a strange thing not to know about the object the whole climax is about. The dilemma is clear even though the object isn't.
- Page 50: **EMERGENCY AUTHORITY — RESCINDED**. Rescinded *how*? Because Alex refused and Mera surrendered her credential? The mechanics of the fix are as vague as the mechanics of the power.
- Page 54: the debt went from 418,607 to 402,110. That's 16,497 credits paid down. At 40 credits a day that's over a year of entire wages; at Mara's 20 a repair it's 800+ repairs. How much time passed? The book never says. A numbers kid will catch this.
- Page 54: Grandmother's cloth, "no longer being folded." I never saw her fold it. Read twice, gave up. If it means she's at peace, or declining, I couldn't tell which. (Her "blue cup" line from page 4 never comes back either.)

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The Water Wars** (p41) — first and only explanation, page 41 of 54, and it's one line: "During the Water Wars, three people held this agreement." That's all the Water Wars ever get.
- **"Safeguards"** — the word never appears in the book at all. If the two-of-three Covenant thing is the safeguards, the book never calls them that.
- **Leona** (p38–39) — "I know someone who made it hard to leave." The book never says "mother." I inferred it from the photograph and "You're taller." I'm fairly confident, but the book makes you do the work and some kids won't.
- **Nadi and Olani** (p37) — two more names introduced mid-meeting. The name count in this issue is high.
- **What Oasis is** — the water authority's city? I'd assembled it by page 37. Never told.

### Lines I read twice
- "I saw days I could prevent. I didn't see the years I was making." (p40) — read twice. This one earns it. Best line in the issue.
- "Don't make that an answer for everybody." (p48) — read twice. Compressed, but I got it: taking power for my sake is still taking it over me.
- "I may have mistaken that for an instruction." (p46) — read twice, liked it. B11 admitting it steered him.
- "It costs you control." (p50) — landed clean, no reread. Noting it because it's the rare thesis line that doesn't make me solve it.
- "I'll decide before it leaves." (p54) — read twice. Good closing line.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona: immediately — the quietest person in the book, and the only one who apologizes without a slogan. Mera finally becomes a person on page 44 ("After the door was closing"), but it's late. Nadi and Olani are role-clear (corporate rep / clerk) but they're functions. The four collaborators vanish after page 35 — they don't appear in issue 3 at all, and I didn't miss Jalen or Ren, which tells me they never became people. I half-missed Lina. Lock vanishes entirely after page 35 — the villain gets no ending. I noticed his absence more than I noticed Jalen's presence.

### I felt something
- Pages 39–40: Ada and Leona. "You chose safety instead of me." / "Don't call that care." Real ache — the only relationship in the book that hurts like life.
- Page 43: "First people stopped selling to him. Then people stopped standing near him." A person's erasure in two steps. Felt it.
- Pages 45–46: Adrian's death. Mild feeling, and the chip set beside the recorder is the right kind of quiet. "The rest is yours to choose" is too speechy for a dying man and it cost the scene some of me.
- Page 50: **EXISTING DEBT — UNCHANGED**. I respected this. The book refuses the magic fix.
- Page 54: the two routes on Ada's map. Warm, small. "Neither moves for the other" is a good last image.

### I predicted
- Leona is Ada's mother — called it at page 38.
- Alex refuses the lever — called it before the scene started. The book telegraphs its answer from page 29 onward. The refusal is the right ending and I still felt little when it happened, because I'd been waiting for it for twenty pages.
- The diversion gets stopped — half-right. It's *paused*, not stopped. The book's refusal to hand out a clean win surprised me. Credit.
- Adrian's body: predicted "whereabouts unrecorded" stays unrecorded. It does. The book doesn't fake a rescue. Respect.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Power that can't be answered by the people it affects is the villain — and the hero's job is to refuse it in public and build a table where the affected people answer back. (Yes, I can hear how much that sounds like the worksheet. The book earned it and also sounds like it.)

### Where I would have put it down
I wouldn't have — by page 37 I owed Tomas and Adrian the ending. Closest call is page 41, the Covenant lecture; if this issue had been issue 1, I'd have been gone there.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the last page of the book. Ada's two routes, the token in Alex's hand, "Neither moves for the other." Quiet and honest — the debt stands, the workshop's sealed, the villain's free, and two people have choices they didn't have on page 1. Would I buy the next book? No. Not because it's bad — because it's finished, and it never gives me a reason to come back. Lock is out there, the diversion is only paused, and the book doesn't seem to care. It closes the door instead of leaving it open.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 32. The Morrow & Sons page — posted margins, "We fix what we sold," "A House builds faster. I can tell you what it costs." Thirty teenagers check out on the same panel. It's the page where the book stops pretending it isn't a lesson. (Runner-up: page 41, the Covenant exposition — but being confused is survivable; being lectured about ethical retail is not.)

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a company town inherits his missing dad's massive debt, finds a robot his dad hid, and proves the water company is lying about the water — and then at the end, instead of taking the magic power that could fix everything, he refuses it and makes everyone sit through a public review. It's better than that sounds in issue 1, and more like homework than that sounds by issue 3."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Fast, angry, and clean — a debt, a dog robot, and two numbers (18 and 62) that do more work than any speech in the book.
- **Issue 02:** The book changes subjects from stolen water to stolen credit, and sags in the middle into assembly scenes and a page about honest shopkeeping.
- **Issue 03:** The best scene in the book (Ada and her mother) and its worst instinct (four pages of not pulling a lever) — an honest, talky ending that respects me and doesn't make me want more.
