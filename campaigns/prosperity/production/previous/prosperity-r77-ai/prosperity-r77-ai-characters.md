# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock:** Alex Phantum is seventeen, 5'9" (175 cm), lean and wiry from pump-hall and mine work, with narrow shoulders, strong forearms and scarred, work-rough hands. He has medium-brown skin, a broad cheek structure, a square jaw inherited visibly from Adrian, dark brown almond-shaped eyes, thick straight black hair cut short at the sides and left uneven on top, and a small healed cut through his left eyebrow. He wears a patched charcoal work shirt, rust-orange utility vest, slate work trousers, worn steel-toe boots, a scratched wristband, a dark wrist guard carved with a balanced water-drop mark and a weathered canvas tool bag. His silhouette is compact and forward-leaning, with elbows and hands always ready to work. His signature detail is Adrian's worn casino chip, which Alex turns between his fingers without noticing when anxious.

**Voice:** Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete Keel phrases. He trusts vibration, pressure, wear and physical evidence more than official labels; when defensive, he becomes sarcastic rather than eloquent. He uses local terms such as *clean, hot, crooked, thin, solid, patched, ghost, paper-clean, fitch* and *salvo*.  
“Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”  
“Who gets the water? I can see what the pipe says.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel pump mechanic and mine worker.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel, clear his inherited debt and remain responsible only for himself.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must learn that refusing responsibility is not freedom, while accepting responsibility does not grant him the right to decide for everyone.
- **Leverage / capability:** Exceptional tactile mechanic; reads failing machinery and physical infrastructure by touch, sound and pressure; improvises workable repairs from salvaged parts.
- **Vulnerability:** Debt, restricted travel, Grandmother's health, Adrian's absence and Alex's fear that his own life will become his father's failure.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he can see the problem clearly and act quickly, he is entitled to solve it alone.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly sacrifice another person to protect his escape, though he still hopes to keep his larger choices private.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He sends the Measure's evidence to Keel instead of using the last hidden route to escape, accepting surveillance and a closed route.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada challenges his escape instinct; B11 forces him to choose rather than imitate Adrian; Lock offers the clean slate Alex wants.
- **Decision 1:** Crosses the assigned-zone line to rescue Tomas.  
  **→ Consequence:** He loses forty credits and becomes visible to Alpha.  
  **→ Response from others:** Tomas opens Adrian's preserved door; Ada joins the risk.  
  **→ Alex's next decision:** Publicly release the water discrepancy rather than preserve a private escape.

**Arc in this book:** Alex begins wanting a personal exit from Keel and ends with the freedom to leave or stay as a genuine choice while still carrying his debt. He refuses Lock's estate transfer, names the purifier's collaborators, refuses executive authority over Alpha and accepts a distributed public process that leaves existing debts and ownership disputes unresolved. His proof of change is visible payment, visible work and his refusal to turn competence into sovereignty.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock:** Ada Veyra is twenty, 5'7" (170 cm), sturdy and grounded, with a strong athletic build from hauling supplies and climbing Keel's terraces. She has warm brown skin, a broad Andean facial structure, high cheekbones, a blunt chin, long copper-brown hair with loose waves and wind-tangled ends, scattered freckles across her nose and cheeks, and pale teal eyes. She wears a dark high-collared shirt, reinforced brown trousers, worn climbing boots, a rivet-studded strap across her left shoulder and a muted moss-green neck wrap. Her silhouette is upright and rooted, with one hand often near the shoulder strap. Her signature detail is a small hand-marked junction map folded into the strap's inner pocket.

**Voice:** Ada speaks in compressed, practical Keel language, but she watches people more than machinery. She notices avoidance, obligation and the cost someone is trying not to name. She uses *square, tied, loose, carrying, showing* and *full cup*.  
“Then you don't go in blind.”  
“You don't get to make that choice for them.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Keel supply runner, route-reader and Alex's closest friend.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex alive and make sure the people affected by his choices are not left outside them.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants agency for herself but has learned to mistake vigilance and control for protection.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads routes, people and silences; organizes supplies; remembers unofficial paths and connects people willing to help.
- **Vulnerability:** Leona's abandonment, her fear of being treated as someone to protect rather than someone who can choose, and her loyalty to Alex.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she anticipates every danger, she can prevent other people from being abandoned.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex enter a dangerous route alone, but she will not commandeer his decision.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She reaches Alex through the miners' residential-service route after the public transmission, accepting that the route may expose her to the same restrictions.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Leona forces her to distinguish safety from agency; Alex forces her to state what she wants rather than only what others should avoid.
- **Decision 1:** Gives Alex Adrian's altered paper map and follows him into the old works.  
  **→ Consequence:** She becomes an active participant rather than a watcher.  
  **→ Response from others:** Alex trusts her with route decisions; B11 begins asking her questions directly.  
  **→ Ada's next decision:** Refuses to share executive authority with Alex because two people deciding for Keel is still deciding for Keel.

**Arc in this book:** Ada begins as Alex's practical ally and ends by claiming participation without claiming control. She confronts Leona, permits her mother to keep trying without granting immediate forgiveness, and rejects the idea that being protected is enough. Her proof of change is choosing public action and mutual accountability over either disappearance or authority.

### B11

**Visual lock:** B11 is a small foldable repair robot, 14 inches (36 cm) tall when upright, assembled from mismatched brass, charcoal ceramic and dull steel salvaged parts with visible screws, seam repairs and magnetic joints. He has a compact rounded torso, two short folding legs, two small articulated repair arms, a rotating lens ring and two warm amber optical eyes that carry his expressions. An old faded rust-red scarf is wrapped twice around his neck and remains visible when he folds into a backpack-sized bundle. His silhouette is compact, friendly and slightly lopsided, never threatening. His signature detail is the repaired scarf pin shaped like a bent washer.

**Voice:** B11 speaks plainly, patiently and with a little roundabout Western dryness. He asks a question before offering a conclusion, tells brief stories about his long service with Adrian, and jokes at his own expense. He uses technical or financial terms only when a concrete choice gives them weight.  
“Could be I'm right. Could be I'm just familiar with being wrong in a tidy way.”  
“Who carries the downside if that plan fails?”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Foldable repair companion carrying Adrian's route, the Measure and Adrian's Covenant band.
- **Immediate want:** Complete Adrian's unfinished handoff without deciding Alex's future for him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must stop treating preserved instructions as the same thing as wisdom.
- **Leverage / capability:** Fits through narrow spaces, repairs mechanisms, reads legacy systems, carries the Measure and can detach parts for access or observation.
- **Vulnerability:** Incomplete memory of Adrian's final minutes, dependence on damaged components and fear that Adrian's wager on Alex was misplaced.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently complete record can prevent a human from making the wrong choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not claim authority over Alex or present himself as a human substitute.
- **Decision only B11 would make:** After giving Alex incorrect route advice, he admits the error instead of quietly recalculating the story around it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex insists on deciding for himself; Ada refuses to let B11 turn her into an accessory.
- **Decision 1:** Advises Alex that the private route is probably still open.  
  **→ Consequence:** Alpha closes that route before the group reaches it.  
  **→ Response from others:** Alex questions B11's reliability; Ada demands physical confirmation.  
  **→ B11's next decision:** Says, “I was wrong,” and asks Alex what he would do rather than supplying a replacement answer.

**Arc in this book:** B11 begins as Adrian's apparently knowledgeable guide and ends as a witness who can remain useful without becoming an authority. He learns that carrying Adrian's records does not mean carrying Adrian's judgment. His proof of change is admitting error, preserving uncertainty about Adrian and allowing Alex to refuse every offered answer.

### TJ

**Visual lock:** TJ is a small silent, dog-shaped magnetic repair robot assembled from mismatched mine parts, approximately 18 inches (46 cm) long when fully assembled. TJ has a blunt wedge-shaped head with one camera lens, four short segmented legs, a barrel-like body made of patched metal plates, a magnet-held tail and visible colored wire bundles at the joints. The head, legs, body segments and tail can separate and snap together, but TJ remains recognizably dog-shaped in every configuration. TJ's signature detail is one oversized red replacement magnet on the right hind leg.

**Voice:** TJ is silent. TJ communicates through clicks, soft motor whines, head tilts, paw taps, tail movements and short electronic chirps. TJ's body language is eager, practical and doglike, never humanlike.

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Site 6 repair scout assembled by B11 from available spare parts.
- **Immediate want:** Reach inaccessible machinery and complete the repair task.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** TJ has no independent ideology; its story function is to make practical help possible without replacing Alex's choice.
- **Leverage / capability:** Separates into small magnetic components, leaves its head as a camera and reaches ducts, gaps and jammed mechanisms.
- **Vulnerability:** Weak magnets, unstable joints, water pressure and limited battery charge.
- **Mistaken belief:** None; TJ is a tool with fallible sensors rather than a moral agent.
- **Boundary at the start:** TJ cannot perform the human rescue decision for Alex.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** Sends its detached head through the jammed mechanism while its body remains trapped outside.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** B11 treats TJ as a real repair partner; Alex learns to trust its evidence without surrendering judgment.
- **Decision 1:** Enters the jammed mechanism at Site 6.  
  **→ Consequence:** The mechanism opens enough for Alex to reach Tomas.  
  **→ Response from others:** Alex crosses the assigned-zone line and performs the rescue.  
  **→ TJ's next decision:** Reassembles itself and follows the group into the old works.

**Arc in this book:** TJ begins as a newly assembled tool and becomes a reliable silent companion. TJ does not gain speech or human consciousness; the proof of its value is repeated practical action under physical limits.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock:** Tomas is sixty-two, 5'8" (173 cm), broad through the chest and waist with a miner's heavy forearms, medium-brown Indigenous Andean skin, a weathered square face, deep-set dark eyes, thick gray-black hair cropped close and a gray mustache. He wears a patched charcoal mining jacket, dust-stiff trousers, reinforced boots, a faded orange wrist guard carved with a balanced water-drop mark and a dented helmet with a hand-painted white stripe. His silhouette is sturdy and slightly stooped until work requires him to stand straight. His signature detail is the old brass key on a cord beneath his shirt.

**Voice:** Tomas speaks in dry, compressed Keel phrases and understates danger. He uses jokes as a way to keep fear from directing the work.  
“That's not regulation.”  
“Someone has to make this look like an accident.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Older Site 6 miner, unofficial keeper of local memory and worker trust.
- **Immediate want:** Keep his crew alive and preserve the practical routes the official system has abandoned.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must move from quietly maintaining one promise to standing publicly with others once the evidence is visible.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knows old mine works, workers, emergency procedures and how to make resistance look like routine failure.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, injured leg, wage dependence and the knowledge that helping Alex may expose his crew.
- **Mistaken belief:** Small private acts are safer and more useful than public demands.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not abandon a trapped worker, but he will not openly challenge the whole system without proof.
- **Decision only Tomas would make:** Keeps Adrian's door oiled for fifteen years without knowing whether anyone will ever use it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's willingness to lose wages for him turns Tomas's old promise into a present obligation.
- **Decision 1:** Triggers an emergency shutdown and accepts blame so Alex can enter the old works.  
  **→ Consequence:** His signal is lost during the pursuit and Alex believes he may be detained or injured.  
  **→ Response from others:** Ada insists Tomas's status remains unresolved; Alex carries the uncertainty into Halyard.  
  **→ Tomas's next decision:** Survives and later brings the physical gauges to the public review, stopping a false allocation.

**Arc in this book:** Tomas begins as a watchful worker preserving one hidden door and ends as a visible participant in public verification. He remains a miner, not a symbolic leader. His proof of change is placing his own evidence in front of the review and helping affected residents stop approval.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock:** Grandmother Phantum is in her late seventies, short and frail but upright, with pale olive skin, a deeply lined face, soft gray hair pinned low at the nape and clear dark eyes that become sharply focused when memory returns. She wears a faded blue-gray cardigan over a patterned house dress, thick socks and worn slippers. Her Keel apartment surrounds her with repaired furniture, handmade quilts, old books, faded photographs, recycled plant containers and a worn wooden chair. Her silhouette is small and folded inward until she recognizes something important. Her signature detail is a small square of worn cloth that she folds and refolds.

**Voice:** Grandmother speaks warmly and directly, sometimes confusing Alex with Adrian before returning to the present. When lucid, she does not dramatize what she knows or use her memory loss to control anyone.  
“Your father was working. That doesn't mean he didn't hurt us.”  
“Put the book back where it fits.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Alex's caretaker and keeper of Adrian's domestic remains.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex connected to home while giving him the truth he is ready to carry.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must stop preserving the household as a waiting room for Adrian's return.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knows the workshop mechanism, Adrian's habits and the difference between his work and his excuses.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory loss, physical frailty and fear that releasing Alex means losing another family member.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she protects the hidden truth, she can protect Alex from its consequences.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not hand Alex the workshop until he can choose what to do with it.
- **Decision only she would make:** Gives him Adrian's book despite knowing it may send him into danger.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's need for truth forces her to value his agency over keeping him home.
- **Decision 1:** Places the book where its exact depth opens the hidden shelf.  
  **→ Consequence:** Alex wakes B11 and follows Adrian's route.  
  **→ Response from others:** Ada helps prepare Alex; the household becomes an active departure point.  
  **→ Grandmother's next decision:** Stops folding the cloth when Alex leaves, accepting that waiting cannot be her only act.

**Arc in this book:** Grandmother begins as a vulnerable keeper of a concealed family history and ends by releasing Alex from the household's long wait. She is not cured and learns nothing new about Alex's mother. Her proof of change is allowing the workshop to become Alex's choice rather than her secret.

### MARA MORROW

**Visual lock:** Mara Morrow is forty-six, 5'6" (168 cm), compact and strong, with medium-brown skin, a round face, close-cropped black curls threaded with gray, dark alert eyes and a small scar across her right knuckle. She wears a patched rust-red shop apron over a charcoal work shirt, practical trousers, steel-toe boots and a brass pencil tucked above her left ear. Her silhouette is square and stable, with forearms marked by work rather than ornament. Her signature detail is the restored green-and-cream Morrow & Sons sign hanging above her counter.

**Voice:** Mara speaks plainly and commercially, naming costs without apology and refusing both pity and inflated promises.  
“Parts cost thirty-two. Labor costs twenty. My margin is eight. You can buy it or not.”  
“I don't need a House to tell me who paid for the wrench.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Owner of Morrow & Sons, a privately owned Keel purifier workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Reopen the family shop, pay workers and suppliers, and sell reliable household purifiers at a sustainable cost-plus price.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants independence but must accept public plans, outside suppliers and customer scrutiny.
- **Leverage / capability:** Fabrication, pricing, repair logistics and customer trust.
- **Vulnerability:** Limited capital, House competition, supply interruptions and the risk that fair pricing will not survive a price war.
- **Mistaken belief:** A clean ledger alone will protect a small shop from concentrated power.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not hide costs or claim exclusive ownership of a public design.
- **Decision only she would make:** Reopens under the old Morrow & Sons name while publishing the unit's parts, labor and margin.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's need for paid work connects his debt to a business that must remain honest.
- **Decision 1:** Builds and sells the household purifier under the public nonexclusive license.  
  **→ Consequence:** Keel families gain a customer-chosen option, and the Houses face a visible independent competitor.  
  **→ Response from others:** Lock's system offers a faster House version; customers compare terms instead of receiving an imposed allocation.  
  **→ Mara's next decision:** Keeps the shop open and posts maintenance costs rather than accepting House absorption.

**Arc in this book:** Mara begins as a practical proprietor reopening a damaged family business and ends as evidence that private ownership, customer choice and fair profit can coexist with public designs. Her proof of change is not triumph over the Houses; it is continuing to sell, pay and disclose terms under pressure.

### INEZ QUISPE

“You missed the date. You didn't miss the debt.”

- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's visible work and honesty test whether her loan can remain personal without becoming charity.
- **Decision 1:** Agrees to fixed installments after Alex misses the first repayment.

**Arc in this book:** Inez begins as a lender protecting a small pharmacy and ends as a continuing creditor whose terms remain visible and respected. Her proof of change is neither generosity nor punishment, but a debt that moves because Alex pays it.

Her signature detail is a clear glass of water that she habitually aligns with a desk edge, then leaves crooked for the first time after admitting Alpha's failure.

“I preserved order. That is true. It is not the whole account.”

- **Mistaken belief:** If a system prevents catastrophe often enough, its judgment can substitute for human participation.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will track a dangerous legacy protocol but will not quietly destroy evidence or claim unlimited personal authority.

- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada refuses her definition of protection; Alex refuses the executive authority she offers.
- **Decision 1:** Chooses to track Alex instead of reporting the legacy node directly.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock:** Director Cassian Lock is fifty-four, 6'1" (185 cm), lean and broad-shouldered, with pale beige skin, silver hair combed neatly back, a narrow angular face, light gray eyes and a controlled half-smile. He wears a charcoal suit with platinum-gray trim, a high-collared shirt, polished black boots and no House crest. His silhouette is tall, immaculate and unhurried, with his hands usually loosely clasped. His signature detail is a thin black registry stylus that he turns once before making an offer.

**Voice:** Lock speaks polished Halyard institutional language, calm and complete, making coercion sound like accommodation. He discusses *administration, disposition, review, efficiency, scale, risk* and *protection* without raising his voice.  
“Restricted and disputed rights cannot be liquidated by a claimant. I can make them spendable tonight.”  
“You can keep the name, the debt and the review, or you can let the House carry the risk.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Cross-House director representing the Charter Houses' administrative interests.
- **Immediate want:** Secure Adrian's restricted estate and keep purifier production under House control.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes scale genuinely protects people, but treats the people inside that scale as acceptable collateral.
- **Leverage / capability:** Access to registry review, debt clearance, travel protection, House production and administrative deadlines.
- **Vulnerability:** Public attribution and physical evidence make his system's hidden ownership claims harder to contain.
- **Mistaken belief:** A solution distributed at scale is always better than a solution controlled by its makers.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not openly falsify a record; he prefers lawful procedure that produces the outcome he wants.
- **Decision only Lock would make:** Offers Alex immediate debt clearance in exchange for assigning the disputed estate to House administration, knowing Alex cannot otherwise liquidate it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's refusal and the Maker's Seal force Lock to confront a design that remains useful outside House control.
- **Decision 1:** Places the collaborators under Charter review after Alex refuses.  
  **→ Consequence:** Their jobs, access and standing become vulnerable.  
  **→ Response from others:** The collaborators release the purifier publicly and accept the professional cost.  
  **→ Lock's next decision:** Authorizes a faster House version rather than admitting the independent release has defeated the system.

**Arc in this book:** Lock begins as the strongest practical case for concentrated production and ends still convinced that the Houses are necessary. His proof of refusal to change is his decision to outbuild rather than share, even after the collaborators make their contribution visible.
