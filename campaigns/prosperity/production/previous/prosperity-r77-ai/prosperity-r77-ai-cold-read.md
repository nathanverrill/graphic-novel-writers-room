# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it straight through, all three issues in one sitting, the night before, from the seat in `rules/reader.md`: 16, assigned this, have read graphic novels before, expecting it to be bad. I only had the pages.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, the whole page.** This is what got me. It's his birthday, and the gate tells him he's inherited his missing dad's debt: 418,607 credits, 40 earned today, 28.7 years to clear it. Then the guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum." I put my phone down here. I understood the problem right away and it felt unfair in a way I recognised.
- **Page 2, panel 3.** "WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED" and the casino chip in his pocket. Good. Now I want to know where the dad went.
- **Page 4, panel 4 to page 5.** A secret bookcase in Grandma's apartment. Cheap, but it works. I turned the page.
- **Page 6.** B11 is the first character I actually liked. "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." and "A fact with a poor record." made me laugh a bit. Reading him made me decide the book wasn't fully AI sludge.
- **Page 10.** He steps over the line to save Tomas and loses his whole day's pay, after page 2 told me what 40 credits means to him. "That is today's pay." / "I know." That landed.
- **Page 11.** The dog made of mine parts, TJ, that sends its own head through a gap. "That dog yours?" / "Recently." Fun.
- **Page 16, panel 4.** The public screens all over town switch to "DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%." That felt like a real move. He chose to expose it over escaping clean.
- **Page 18, panel 3.** Tomas's relay light goes dark. That's the thing that made me want Issue 2.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1.** I didn't know who the supervisor was, what a "node" was, or why the repair matters. I got through it, but slowly.
- **Pages 13–15.** Pipes, percentages, the Measure, three arms, "SENSE," "COMPARE — TRACE." I got that 62% is the lie and 18% is the truth. I skimmed the rest of the gadget steps.
- **Page 17.** I skimmed it and then had to go back.

### I was confused
- **Page 3.** Who is Ada? She's just there, checking labels. Is she Inez's assistant, his girlfriend, his sister? I never really found out in this issue.
- **Page 3.** Does he buy the token or not? He has 52, the medicine gets fronted, so... yes? It's in his pocket later, so I guess he did. I had to work that out myself.
- **Page 4, panel 1.** "Site 6 took the shift." But he doesn't get reassigned to Site 6 until page 8. Did Site 6 happen twice? I thought I'd skipped a page.
- **Page 8.** Mera. Who is she, what is Oasis, and is she helping him or hunting him? She picks "TRACK" and then sends him somewhere. "Mera does not touch the assignment field" means nothing to me as a reader. I couldn't tell if this was a villain scene or a friend scene.
- **Page 12, panel 4.** "The shutdown. Make it look like my fault." Which shutdown? Did they shut something down? I thought they pulled a release.
- **Page 15, panel 4 to page 16.** Three routes: private, local, pressure. Then a hatch, a channel and a conduit. I lost track of which door was which and which one they ended up taking.
- **Page 17.** Mera opens a pressure valve: "Open the segment. Not the homes." Is she flooding them? Saving them? Pushing them up to Halyard on purpose? And did that pressure hurt Tomas? I honestly can't tell whether she's the reason Tomas might be dead.
- **Page 18.** Are they climbing out of a water pipe? Into where? I only learned it was Halyard on the next page.

### Lines I read twice
- p3: "I'm coming to the route. Not because you bought a token. Because you don't know one." (I read it three times.)
- p5: "Same hand. Different damage."
- p5: "He used gambling for everything." Meaning what? That he gambled with his inventions?
- p6: "Would you like the route, or the question first?"
- p12: "He ask you to keep him out?" / "He didn't ask enough."
- p12: "The shutdown. Make it look like my fault."
- p15: "Three routes. One is private. One is local. One carries pressure." I gave up on this one.
- p17: "Open the segment. Not the homes."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, B11, TJ, Tomas and Grandma: clear.
- Inez: clear for her two pages, then gone.
- Ada: clear voice (blunt), no identity. I don't know what she is to him.
- Mera: no idea which side she's on.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the birthday debt. Page 10, giving up the pay. Page 18, Tomas's light going out.
- Page 4, Grandma: "I'll remember the blue cup." Small and sad. I liked it.

### I predicted
- Dad isn't dead, he's hiding, and B11 knows where he is.
- The 62% / 18% gap is the whole conspiracy, and Mera is behind it.
- Tomas is alive.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid handed his vanished father's debt finds out his town's water is being secretly stolen and decides to expose it instead of running.

### Where I would have put it down
I didn't. Page 2 and B11 bought me through the confusing middle. The closest I came was **page 15**: three routes and a riddle.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mostly yes, because of Tomas, not because of the splash. Kids climbing while water rises is a loud image, but the real hook is panel 3, Tomas's light going dark. That's someone I'd just met and liked. I'd turn the page tonight.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19, panel 1.** "That was not a route." / "It was a route. The destination objected." B11 is still the best thing in the book.
- **Page 19, panel 3.** "Not dead. Not safe." about Tomas. Good. It kept me hanging on.
- **Page 21.** Dad had 312 patents. Wait, he was an inventor, not just a gambler? That flipped what I thought about him, in a good way.
- **Page 22, panel 4.** Lock's offer: sign it away and the debt clears tonight. That's a real temptation, because I remember page 2.
- **Page 28.** Alex fixing it by touch, 11% to 94%. His hands thing from page 1 finally pays off. Satisfying.
- **Page 29.** B11: "I counted the record and missed the people. What would you do?" That was the best moment of this issue. The robot getting something wrong and asking. I liked B11 more after it.
- **Page 30, panel 4.** Lock coming out of a wall speaker: "And one unanswered offer." Creepy, good.
- **Page 33, panel 3.** "No." Clean.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 20.** Order sheets, ownership kiosks and a slogan display. It reads like a worksheet. I skimmed.
- **Page 22, panel 2.** Four new names with job titles in a list. I didn't learn them and I skimmed past them.
- **Pages 23–26.** Sneaking around a factory with four people I just met, swapping cartridges, vials, power reroutes and an eleven-second timer. I skimmed most of it and couldn't have told you what they were stealing or why.
- **Page 31.** Shrinking the purifier, "HOUSEHOLD CONFIGURATION," "CLEANING REQUIRED." Skimmed.
- **Page 32.** The whole page. More on this below.
- **Pages 34–35.** "Public, nonexclusive license. Limits included." "SAFETY DOCUMENTATION: REQUIRED." This is legal paperwork. I skimmed and only caught that the names came back.

### I was confused
- **Page 19, panel 3.** How did a packet about Tomas get "pushed through the damaged Site 6 channel" to exactly where they landed in a different city?
- **Page 19, panel 4.** "Mara's still shipping." Mara? I thought Mera was the woman from Oasis. **Mera and Mara are two different people.** I didn't figure that out until page 32, and even then I had to flip back and check the spelling. For most of this issue I thought the Oasis lady also ran a parts shop.
- **What is Alex doing in Halyard?** In Issue 1 he wanted "somewhere that isn't here." Then he got flushed up a pipe. Now he's at the registry, then on a heist, then building a purifier. Nobody said the goal out loud. "We know he's in Halyard and his dad had patents, but what's he trying to do?"
- **Page 21.** How did Lock know he was there? Why does Alex trust him enough to follow him around?
- **Page 23.** "You're late." Lina was expecting them? Since when? Who set that up?
- **Page 25.** "Patrol has the clean corridor." / "Suri's power line runs through the clean corridor." I have no idea why that answers "Why this one?"
- **Page 32.** Suddenly we're in Keel with Mara under a "restored sign." Is Alex back home? Did time pass? Did the purifier get built and sold already? I thought it was a flashback at first. It's the most confusing jump in the book.
- **Page 34, panel 4.** The Seal "warms" and fixes the registry. So it's magic? Earlier it "checks who is standing behind the name." Is it tech or magic? The book doesn't seem to care, so I stopped caring too.
- **Page 35.** He said no, so is he still in debt? I assumed yes, but the issue never says it, and that was the biggest stake from page 2.
- **Page 36.** "SUPPRESSION: CONTRADICTORY." What does that mean?

### Lines I read twice
- p20: "Morrow sells the thing. The House controls the permission to make it."
- p21: "It is value with a lock on it." His name is Lock. I groaned.
- p25: "Eleven seconds. Then I become a pattern."
- p26: "House standard rejects the Caldera cycle." / "The edges are wrong."
- p28: "The edge is lying." / "Metal doesn't lie." / "It tells you what it can hold." I sort of got it the second time.
- p29: "The lowest-risk exit uses six recorded bodies."
- p32: "A House builds faster. I can tell you what it costs."
- p36: "I can ask. I can't make the answer clean."

I also noticed a tic. "Same hand. Different damage." (p5), "Same part. Different downside." (p20), "Same shortage. Different problem." (p23). By the third one I was rolling my eyes. It felt generated, and this is exactly the kind of thing my class would screenshot to prove the book is AI.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lock: clear. Corporate creep with a stylus.
- B11: clear.
- **Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren:** I could tell them apart only by their gadget (membrane, bacteria, power, heat). If you covered the names I couldn't tell you who's talking. "They come off as a list."
- **Mera / Mara:** couldn't tell apart. Big problem.
- Ada mostly disappears in this issue. She opens doors and says "They built it." I forgot about her.

### I felt something
- Page 20, panel 2: the worker's hand staying on the part after the machine takes it. Small, but I noticed it.
- Page 29, B11 asking what Alex would do.
- Page 33, "No." I felt it, but less than I wanted, because I wasn't sure what he was giving up.

### I predicted
- He'd refuse Lock. Called it on page 22.
- The purifier would work after one failure. Called it.
- Lock would come back as the main villain. Didn't happen.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it seemed to be about who gets credit and ownership for inventions, and the water story from Issue 1 felt like it had been parked.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 22.** A name dump, a corporate tour and a contract, while Tomas is "unresolved" and nobody mentions him again. If I'd been reading for fun, this is where it goes back on the shelf. Page 32 would have been the second place: it reads like the part of the textbook with the discussion questions at the bottom.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Not really. A lady I still can't place touches SUMMON and says something vague. Nobody I care about is in danger on this page. The thing I did care about, Tomas, got one line on page 19 and then vanished. I'd read Issue 3 because it's assigned, not because I need to.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 22; next issue: no

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Pages 38–40, Ada and Leona.** This is the first time Ada is a person. "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" Ouch. "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." That hit. It's the best dialogue in the book.
- **Page 43.** How Adrian got cut off: first he couldn't buy parts, then "people stopped standing near him." That's chilling, and it explains the dad better than anything else did.
- **Page 45.** Adrian in the rising water: "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." Then the bulkhead closes. I felt that.
- **Page 46.** "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Good. B11 again.
- **Page 51.** "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." The callback worked, and Tomas being alive was a relief.
- **Page 54, panel 2.** "INEZ QUISPE — MEDICINE ADVANCE: PAID." I smiled. That's a promise from page 3, kept.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37.** Three new people (Nadi Ruiz, Olani, and a live wall) plus forms in the first panel of the last issue. I skimmed.
- **Pages 41–42.** Bands, two-of-three authorization, partial circles, "obligations written into the records." I skimmed hard.
- **Pages 47–50.** Verify, verify, lever, credential, rescinded. I followed it was a big choice. I skimmed the mechanics.
- **Pages 52–53.** The climax is a hearing that gets paused. I skimmed it like a council meeting.

### I was confused
- **Page 37 vs page 51.** Tomas is on the wall on page 37 when Alex walks in. Then on page 51 Alex "sees him clearly" and says "You made it," like it's the first time. So when did Alex find out Tomas was alive? The reunion didn't land because I already knew on page 37.
- **And how did Tomas survive?** His light went dark at the end of Issue 1. That was my reason to keep reading. It got resolved off-page with no scene.
- **Page 38.** Ada is "VEYRA." Leona is "VEYRA." Is Leona her mother? Her aunt? Nobody ever says. I'm guessing mother. Also, nothing in Issues 1–2 told me Ada was from Oasis. It came out of nowhere. "It's only mentioned in passing, so I don't fully get it when it matters."
- **Page 41.** "The Water Wars" shows up for the first time on page 41 of 54, as a passing line. That sounds like the most important event in this world's history and I got one sentence.
- **Page 41.** Three people held the agreement. I worked out that it's Mera, Leona and Adrian, but only by counting bands.
- **Page 42.** What happened? Bands link, glow, and "Nothing here approves an allocation." Is that good? I didn't know what I was supposed to want.
- **Page 45.** Is Adrian dead? "No evacuation route appears." I think so. The book never says it, and I can't tell if that's on purpose.
- **Page 45.** B11's deployment cradle is in the flooding station, but in Issue 1 B11 was in a box in Grandma's apartment. How did he get there?
- **Page 48, panel 3.** "Don't give me that much power." / "I didn't offer it to you." / "You brought me here." Is Ada talking about herself? About Alex? I read it three times and still don't know.
- **Page 49.** "CONVENOR OF PROPOSAL." So what is he now? A chairman? Of what?
- **Page 54.** The debt went from 418,607 to 402,110. At 40 credits a shift that's over a year of work. How much time passed? Nothing told me. Grandma's cloth is "no longer being folded," but I never saw her folding it.
- **Page 54, last panel.** Is Ada leaving? Is Alex? Are they together? "Neither moves for the other." I don't know what I'm supposed to feel.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Ada finally became a real person here, but only on pages 38–40.
- Leona: clear, and the best new character.
- Mera: I finally understood her (guilty founder, not a villain). That came in Issue 3, about 35 pages after she was introduced, so I'd spent two issues reading her wrong.
- Nadi and Olani: functions, not people.
- The Issue 2 crew (Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren) is gone. I didn't miss them, and that's kind of the problem.
- Lock never comes back. The only real villain I had just stops being in the book.

### I felt something
- Pages 39–40, Ada and Leona. The strongest scene in the book.
- Page 45, Adrian's message.
- Page 51, "Pipe says eighteen."
- Page 54, the Inez debt paid.

### I predicted
- That Alex wouldn't pull the lever. I knew by the time Ada stepped in. The whole book had been teaching me he'd refuse the shortcut, so there was no tension.
- I expected the dad to be alive. He wasn't (I think). That surprise was real.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
It's about refusing power that nobody can check, even when grabbing it would fix your own life. That's a different book from the "kid exposes the water theft" one I thought I was reading after Issue 1.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 42.** Bands linking into a "partial circle," a clerk saying "Nothing here approves an allocation," and three plotlines I'd lost hold of. I kept going only because Adrian's recording was coming.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
This is the last issue, so the question is whether I'd buy a next book. No. The ending is two people standing at an overlook not deciding anything. I get that it's meant to be open. But the debt is still 402,000, the water is still "paused," and the big fight was a hearing that set another hearing. Honestly it felt like the book ended on the moral instead of on a person. Inez getting paid moved me more than the climax did.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 42; next issue: no

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 22.** Four names and job titles in a list, a factory tour with "Materials. Testing. Distribution. Protection from retaliation," a worker's name literally disappearing under a corporate line, and a contract. It's where the book stops being a story about Alex and starts being a lesson. Everybody in my class would be on their phones by the bottom of it. Runner-up: Issue 02, page 32 (Mara's shop). It reads like an example from an economics worksheet.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dead dad's insane debt, finds out the rich city is stealing his town's water, and spends the rest of the book saying no to shortcuts. The robot's funny and the dog takes its head off. The middle is a lot of paperwork."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A real hook, a robot I liked and a guy I wanted saved, with a confusing middle about pipes and routes.
- **Issue 02:** A crowded, preachy heist about patents, where I lost the reason I was reading (Tomas) and mixed up Mera and Mara.
- **Issue 03:** One great mother-daughter scene and one great dead-dad message, inside a climax that's a hearing getting paused.
