# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a rethink.** The opening earns Alex’s debt, tactile competence and first public choice, but both cold readers identify the same structural failure: Issue 02 becomes an abstract ownership detour and Issue 03 becomes a difficult-to-read civic procedure rather than escalating Alex’s immediate danger. The script also contains a direct execution contradiction on Page 46, a Covenant activation error, unresolved House-name canon, and an ending whose Ada question is not yet legible enough to carry the book forward.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Page 46 execution is a different scene and introduces forbidden material

- **Location:** `script.md` Page 46 versus `layouts.md`/`thumbnails.md` Page 46.
- **Observation:** The script shows Alex and B11 processing Adrian’s recording and casino chip. The layout shows Mera, Leona and Ada in Leona’s apartment discussing an Alpha association-risk record, surrendered Covenant authority and contact restrictions.
- **Problem:** The production artifact has replaced the approved beat and introduces unsupported history about Ada. It also conflicts with the standing rule that nothing more about Alex’s mother is learned; any drift toward family-association exposition risks violating that boundary.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Layout Agent:** discard the current Page 46 layout and rebuild it from the scripted Alex/B11 scene. **Character Designer/Director:** remove the apartment, association-risk record and contact-restriction material from production references. Do not turn that discarded material into canon.
- **Role:** layout, character_designer, director.

#### 2. Covenant activation occurs before the required action

- **Location:** Script Page 40, Panel 3.
- **Observation:** Leona clips on the band, states that she will enter the review room, and the band immediately warms. Canon requires each custodian to state a specific obligation, accept a consequence, **and perform the promised action** before activation.
- **Problem:** The script makes the Covenant activate on declaration rather than accountable action. Page 42 then repeats the performance in the review room, making the rule unclear and weakening the payoff.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** keep the band inert on Page 40 after Leona states the promise. On Page 42, show her entering the public review and accepting a visible consequence; activate the band only then. Make B11’s band follow the same sequence when he releases Adrian’s records.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 3. Charter House names remain contradictory across canon and production

- **Location:** `brief.md`/`script.md` versus `world.md`.
- **Observation:** The brief and script use **Caldera House, Vector House and Orbital House**. `world.md` still marks **Veyr House, Kestrel House and Orison House** as fixed.
- **Problem:** House names appear in dialogue, kiosks, records and image prompts. This will generate contradictory lettering and visual text.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Director:** normalize `world.md`, `characters.md`, `facts.md` and all production references to the brief’s approved names before the writer’s revision is assembled into prompts. Do not leave this to the Letterer or image model.
- **Role:** director.

### Major

#### 4. Issue 02 drops the active water/Tomas problem

- **Location:** Pages 19–36.
- **Observation:** Page 19 marks Tomas’s status unresolved, then the issue spends nearly all of its dramatic energy on patents, collaborators and purifier construction. The 62%/18% evidence returns only in Issue 03.
- **Problem:** The reader experiences a genre and objective shift: survival mystery becomes a patent dispute. Both cold readers identified this middle as a put-down zone.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** carry one concrete consequence of the Keel evidence through Halyard. For example, the transmitted discrepancy triggers a House containment order, threatens Tomas’s crew, or makes the collaborators’ purifier a proposed alternative to the diverted supply. Tie the water pressure directly to Alex’s choice in Halyard, rather than explaining the connection in dialogue.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 5. Alex’s Halyard objective is not chosen clearly

- **Location:** Pages 19–23.
- **Observation:** Alex arrives, meets Lock and then begins helping the collaborators. Lina’s “Take it” and the sequence of component recoveries make the collaborators feel preassembled by the outline.
- **Problem:** Alex is not visibly choosing the harder path after Lock’s offer. The story says his objective is to refuse estate transfer and release the work, but the pages show him being recruited into a sequence.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** make Lock’s offer the immediate fork. Alex should visibly refuse or delay signing because doing so commits him to getting the collaborators’ work into a public, usable form before House review closes. Lina’s contact should result from that choice, not precede it.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 6. The collaborator sequence is still a technical list, not four consequential characters

- **Location:** Pages 23–31.
- **Observation:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren arrive through separate component-recovery beats and are mostly distinguished by job. The cold readers could not retain them as people.
- **Problem:** The sequence overloads the reader with names, Houses and technical nouns while giving each collaborator too little independent desire or cost.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** combine at least two recoveries into one pressure scene. Give each contributor one immediate loss: Lina risks professional attribution, Suri risks the living culture, Jalen risks surveillance exposure and Ren risks equipment or wages. Let one collaborator refuse Alex until he accepts a concrete maintenance or safety responsibility. Preserve the four names for the Seal, but reduce the number of introduction beats.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 7. The Maker’s Seal appears to rewrite the registry

- **Location:** Pages 34–35.
- **Observation:** The Seal projects a mismatch and the registry appears to update around it, but the precise preexisting attribution and the human action that publishes the correction are not sufficiently clear.
- **Problem:** The Seal risks reading as magical ownership correction. Canon limits it to verifying contribution and exposing changes; it cannot transfer property or compel agreement.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** establish one exact House attribution entry before activation, including the unlisted creator. After all contributors accept attribution and Alex accepts responsibility, let the Seal expose the mismatch. A clerk or public publication process must make the correction visible; the Seal itself must not alter ownership.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 8. Mera’s role and motive remain unreadable

- **Location:** Pages 8, 17, 36 and 43–50.
- **Observation:** Mera selects TRACK, opens a conduit valve, summons Alex and later offers him executive authority. The script does not make her immediate objective legible.
- **Problem:** Both readers read her as possibly villainous or irrelevant. The book needs the reader to understand that she is trying to contain a dangerous legacy protocol while remaining trapped inside the authority structure she helped create.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** give Mera one concrete constraint and one concrete cost per intervention. Page 8 should show what TRACK preserves that REPORT would destroy; Page 17 should show that opening the conduit segment is the only available ascent route and risks exposing her action; Page 36 should show that SUMMON creates a public record she cannot retract. Do this through action and consequences, not a Mera monologue.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 9. The Page 15–18 crossing remains geographically unclear

- **Location:** Pages 15–18.
- **Observation:** The private maintenance channel, residential-service hatch and main conduit are introduced in quick succession. Mera’s separate valve then creates pressure, but the reader cannot reliably map the routes or understand whether she is helping or attacking the group.
- **Problem:** Both cold readers lost the geography and the causal relation between valve, pressure surge and Tomas’s lost signal.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer/Layout Agent:** keep one chamber orientation across Pages 15–18. Establish the three routes visually before Alex transmits: private exit, residential-service hatch and upward conduit. Show the private route physically sealing, then show Mera opening the separately labeled conduit-segment valve, creating a dangerous current only after the group has a viable ascent route. Establish the Site 6 relay before it fails.
- **Role:** writer, layout.

#### 10. Tomas’s survival is spoiled and delayed

- **Location:** Page 37 and Pages 51–53.
- **Observation:** Page 37’s live wall shows Tomas alive, but Page 51 treats him as a reveal. There is no visible survival action between the signal loss and his later appearance.
- **Problem:** The Issue 01 question is answered off-page, and the later “You made it” cannot land.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** replace the Page 37 image of Tomas with an unresolved gauge signal, a worker-status fragment or an empty Site 6 feed. Reveal Tomas only when he physically brings the manual gauge into the review, after showing one action that explains his survival: holding a regulator, helping workers clear the tunnel or preserving the gauge. Do not reveal him in a way that makes the later entrance redundant.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 11. The Covenant’s function is not legible through action

- **Location:** Pages 41–42 and 47–50.
- **Observation:** The bands link, an old two-of-three authorization appears, and later a designation lever rises. The reader is left unsure whether the bands approve, prevent or merely record policy.
- **Problem:** Cold readers described the Covenant as magical bracelets or a voting mechanism and identified Pages 41–42 as a stopping point.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** have someone attempt to use the linked bands to approve a specific allocation, then show the attempt fail because the Covenant can expose and bind promises but cannot choose policy. After that failure, Alex’s refusal of designation becomes a response to a clearly understood limit rather than another abstract lesson.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 12. The public-review climax is procedural rather than dramatic

- **Location:** Pages 37 and 47–53.
- **Observation:** The climax relies on records, a lever and an approval stamp. Alex’s refusal is predictable, and the allocation is ultimately paused for a later review.
- **Problem:** The central conflict is not changing in the room. The reader watches a committee discover a discrepancy rather than watching Alex make a consequential intervention under pressure.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** put a physical consequence in motion while the designation decision is pending: a valve begins diverting flow, a board stamp will authorize it, or the industrial branch starts losing pressure. Alex must choose between taking command and moving the Measure’s evidence into the affected participants’ hands. Let his action interrupt the immediate harm while preserving the unresolved remedy.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 13. The ending does not make Ada’s question sufficiently active

- **Location:** Page 54, Panel 4.
- **Observation:** Ada tears the map, gives Alex one half and says “The one I choose,” but her actual movement and destination remain unclear. B11 and TJ also disappear from the final panel.
- **Problem:** The final question is technically open but not dramatically legible. The reader does not know what Ada is choosing or why Alex does not follow.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer/Layout Agent:** show Ada make one unmistakable first move—joining the departure queue or stepping toward a departure route—while retaining a visible route back to Keel. Alex should remain behind or choose not to follow, preserving her agency. Keep B11 and TJ present in the final sequence unless their absence is motivated.
- **Role:** writer, layout.

#### 16. Dialogue remains too uniformly aphoristic

## Plausibility ledger

- **Debt transfer — HOLDS:** Page 2 matches the established figures. Page 54 does not yet demonstrate how the balance fell.
- **Inez loan — HOLDS WITH CONTINUITY REPAIR:** The 30-credit medicine advance must remain separate from Adrian’s household liability and must be repaid through visible terms.
- **Travel token — NEEDS CLARIFICATION:** Page 3 currently leaves it unclear whether Alex buys, borrows or merely retains the token.
- **Tomas telemetry — HOLDS IF PLANTED:** A pressure surge can interrupt a relay, but Alex’s access to the status feed and the relay’s location need to be shown before failure.
- **Conduit crossing — NEEDS REPAIR:** Mera’s conduit-segment valve and the miners’ residential regulator are separate systems. The page sequence must show the distinction and the causal chain.
- **Measure — HOLDS:** The physical comparison instrument fits the grounded setting. Its custody, readout and route tracing need clearer visual continuity.
- **Maker’s Seal — NEEDS EVIDENCE:** It may expose attribution changes but cannot silently rewrite ownership, compel agreement or transfer property.
- **Covenant — CONTRADICTED ON PAGE 40:** The current script activates Leona’s band before she performs the promised action. Repair the order.
- **Purifier — HOLDS WITH SCALE DISTINCTION:** The 94% industrial and 91% household configurations are plausible if the larger unit visibly demands industrial staffing and the smaller unit visibly trades output for maintainability. Neither ends scarcity.
- **Public review — NEEDS GEOGRAPHIC CLARITY:** The review must have a stable room, visible affected parties and a concrete physical consequence.
- **Adrian’s fate — HOLDS IF BOUNDED:** The record may end at the sealed station and show B11’s deployment state, but may not show a recovered body, evacuation or later survival.
- **Alex’s mother — HOLDS ONLY UNDER GUARDRAIL:** The only permitted information is that she left Adrian after he lost everything. No name, image, community, location or additional cause may appear.
- **Page 46 execution — FAILS:** The layout is not an execution of the script.
- **Final debt — NEEDS SUPPORT:** The fourteen-month interval is plausible, but cumulative paid work must be visible.
