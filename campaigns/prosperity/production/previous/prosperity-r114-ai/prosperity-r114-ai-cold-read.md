# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it the way the reader file describes: 16, assigned, the night before, wanting it to be bad. I read it straight through once, in order, then went back to check page numbers. Everything below is what happened on the first pass.

---

## Issue 00 — Lake Keel, 2099 (pages 1–10)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 2.** The robot dog's head, body and legs come apart in midair and keep going. That got me. It's a strange, specific image and I wanted to see what it did next.
- **Page 1, panel 4 / page 2.** The granddaughter says "They're stealing from us" and then we cut to the thieves, who think *Alex* is the thief ("Nobody gets water that clean by being polite." / "Then he stole it first."). Two sides calling each other thieves on page 2 is a hook. I didn't know who to believe, and I liked that.
- **Pages 3–5.** TJ jamming the siphon is easy to follow. Pieces go in, the pump bucks, a leg falls off (*TINK*), the siphon collapses. Clean action, no reading needed.
- **Page 7, panel 2.** "They say you made water pay for it." This is the best line in the issue. It makes the old man someone with a past, maybe a bad one. I turned the page for that.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 8–10.** Once the barge is gone, nothing happens. Alex puts a part next to a terminal, a leg gets clicked back on, lights glow, dawn comes. I skimmed page 9 completely. Panels 2–4 have no words and I didn't know what I was meant to look at.
- **Page 10.** A full-page dawn after three pages of winding down. It felt like an ending before I'd been given a story.

### I was confused
- Why doesn't Alex do anything? He stops her calling the wardens, says "Let him show us where it goes," and then... TJ comes back with a part and that's it. "Show us where it goes" never pays off. Where did it go?
- **Page 7, panel 4 / page 8.** The part's amber light blinks and "Alex notices but does not explain it." So I noticed it too and got nothing. Then he leaves it by a records terminal. I don't know why that matters.
- **Page 10.** "Find out who gets to decide what water is worth." He's an old man who owns a lake. Hasn't he already found out? Is he starting something new at 90? I couldn't tell whether this was the start of a story or the end of one.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First page | Did I ever find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Lake Keel / "the old Keel mine" | 1 | Partly. Keel turns out to be the mining town in Issue 01. I never learn how a dry, debt-ridden mine becomes this lake. |
| TJ | 1 | No. He never appears again. Nobody builds him or mentions him. |
| Nera and Voss | 2 | No. They never come back. |
| "Phantum" | 2 | Yes, it's Alex's surname (page 12). |
| The wardens | 3 | No. |
| The "commercial mark" on the part | 7 | No. Whose mark? Never said. |
| The granddaughter (who's her parent? Ada?) | 1 | No. |

### Lines I read twice
- "He knows the difference between stuck and working." (p4) Fine on the second read, but it sounds like a fortune cookie.
- "The work keeps accounts." (p7) Read it three times. Still don't know what it means as an answer to "Did you make them pay?" It reads like he's dodging, and I think I'm supposed to find that wise.
- "We know what they took. We know what they left." (p8)
- "He'll need a new part." / "So will the lake." (p9) I get that it's a parallel. I don't know what the lake's "new part" is.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, the granddaughter and TJ are all clear. She asks the questions, he gives the riddles, TJ does the work.
- Nera and Voss: one has a red hood, one has a lamp helmet. That's all. Nera seems to have a conscience ("Lower the pull") but I couldn't have told you more.

### I felt something
- A little, when TJ loses the leg and the granddaughter keeps trying to click it back on (p9, panel 1). That small moment worked better than the speeches.

### I predicted
- That the book would come back to 2099 and tell me whether Alex really "made water pay." (It doesn't.)
- That Nera would turn out to be someone important. (She doesn't come back.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old man owns the only clean lake around, people say he got it dirty, and his granddaughter wants to know the truth.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 9. The action's over and three silent panels of lights and terminals gave me nothing to hold onto.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Barely. The dawn image is pretty, but "find out who decides what water is worth" is a thesis statement, not a cliffhanger. What actually pulls me on is page 7's "They say you made water pay for it." I want to know if he's a villain. That's a question about a person, and it's real. The last page just isn't where it is.

VERDICT Issue 00: put down: page 9; next issue: yes

## Issue 01 — The Inheritance (pages 11–22)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 12.** This is the moment the book got me for real. Seventeenth birthday, and the band reads `DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS`, `PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS`. "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "Twenty-eight point seven." Inheriting your missing dad's debt on your birthday is something I understood instantly and felt. That number did more than all of Issue 00.
- **Page 13, panel 4.** "I'm your friend, Alex. Not your exit plan." Good line. Ada was a person right away.
- **Page 16.** Hidden room behind a bookcase. It's the oldest trick there is and I still liked it. "That's one way to hide a room." / "Badly, eventually." made me smile.
- **Page 18.** Bi11bot. "Well. That took longer than expected." / "Adrian's jaw. His poorer timing." / "B11 is acceptable if the room is on fire." He's the first character with real personality. I was happy whenever he talked.
- **Page 19.** Adrian's message is short and doesn't make excuses: "I borrowed too much. I gambled some of it. I hurt you and Grandmother." Then "Question everything. Especially me." Good. That made me trust the book a bit more.
- **Page 20.** The door locks and the band goes amber. Now there's a threat.
- **Page 22.** Someone in a control room tracks the signal. Classic, but it works.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 14.** The photo and watch conversation. "Keel says he gambled." / "Grandmother says he worked." / "Those aren't opposites." / "What if both stories are incomplete?" Four panels of characters telling each other the theme. I skimmed the last two.
- **Page 17.** Equation, brass plate, map with ink ticks, pipe symbol, empty recess. That's a lot of clue-objects in one page and I didn't know which ones I was supposed to remember. I remembered none until they came back.

### I was confused
- **Page 12, panel 4.** "Behind it, the Last Chance bar is closed and dust-covered." Why am I seeing a bar? Does it matter? It never comes back.
- **Page 15.** Who is Pilar Veyra? She shows up checking a "paper dose grid" with no introduction. Later Ada turns out to be a Veyra too. Is Pilar Ada's grandmother? Aunt? A nurse? I never found out.
- **Page 15, panel 1.** Grandmother calls him "Adrian" and corrects herself. I took it as memory loss, and that landed. But "dose grid" and medicine go by fast, and I didn't know she was sick until page 23.
- **Page 18, panel 1.** "Inserts the old connector beside the empty recess." What connector? Where did it come from?
- **Page 20.** "It knows your band stopped reporting." Why did it stop? Because he went into the hidden room? Because the bot woke up? I filled it in myself, but I wasn't sure.
- **Page 21.** "Three safeguards. The first is below Site 6." / "It measures." This is where the plan gets set up and it goes by in two balloons. Three safeguards against *what*? What happens when you have all three? I went into Issue 02 not knowing what the goal was. I knew "go to the mine," not why.

### Lines I read twice
- "I saved enough for one." / "There are two mugs." / "I'm generous with containers." (p13) Took me two reads to realise she's sharing one drink between two mugs. Cute once I got it.
- "Keel's not a sentence." (p13) Sentence as in prison. Got it on the second read.
- "Those aren't opposites." (p14)
- "It is when someone has been counting for you." (p21) Read twice. I think it means "if someone's been lying to you about the numbers." It only clicked in Issue 02.
- "Not everything has to be yours because it started with your father." (p21) Fine, just heavy.
- "Then the wrongness travels efficiently." (p20) Liked it on the second read.

- Foreman-7: clear as "the guy at the checkpoint."
- Pilar: a name with a clipboard.

### I felt something
- Page 12, the debt number on his birthday.
- Page 15, Grandmother calling him by his dad's name.
- Page 19, "I hurt you and Grandmother." That was more honest than I expected.

### I predicted

- Mera is the villain.
- Bi11bot will turn out to have been Adrian's partner in something big.

A kid stuck with his missing dad's huge debt finds his dad's robot and a plan to expose a computer system that's cheating his town.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. This issue kept me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Someone powerful is tracking them toward the mine, and I care about Alex and Ada by now. It's a real cliffhanger, small but honest. I'd go straight on.

## Issue 02 — The Price of Departure (pages 23–37)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 24.** Alex steps out of his zone to save Tomas and the screen says `WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR`. "You know that costs you." / "I noticed." I liked seeing the price of a good deed as a number.
- **Page 27.** "Foreman! Your safety report is on fire." Funny. And "Abandoned mines do not sweat" is a great end to a page. Creepy and clear.
- **Pages 28–29.** The map against the computer's route. B11 says the straight tunnel is safer, then drones sweep it. "I read that wrong." / "Keep reading." I liked the robot being wrong and learning.
- **Page 30.** The big fat industrial pipe over the tiny dry pipe labelled `KEEL RESIDENTIAL`. I got the whole story of the town from one picture. Best image in the book so far.
- **Page 32.** `REPORTED TO KEEL: 62%`, `PHYSICALLY DELIVERED: 18%`. "Same period?" / "Same water." This was the moment I understood what the book was about. Concrete, angry, simple.
- **Pages 34–35.** The town sees the numbers and pools credits. "Care stays funded." I liked the town showing up for him.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 23.** Lost me, and it's the first page of the issue. Four panels in four different places doing four different things (budgeting, Pilar taking the ledger, Tomas at Site 6, the mine gate). "Who carries the downside?" comes out of nowhere. I skimmed it to get to some action.
- **Page 33.** Nodes, transmitter, private route, restriction. I couldn't follow what was decided.

### I was confused
- **Page 23, panel 3.** "Tomas receives the old key's travel token and parts advance." I have no idea what happens in this panel. Who is Tomas? (First time we meet him.) Who gives what to whom? "Forty credits. Separate from your household account." Is Tomas lending to Alex, or Alex to Tomas? "Make it real" — make what real? I was lost, and it only half made sense on page 63.
- **Page 24.** Tomas is pinned under a frame one panel after handing over a loan. Did that happen in between? Is it the same day? It felt like a missing page.
- **Page 25, panel 1.** "I predicted we could leave him unseen." Leave Tomas, or get past him? I read it as B11 wanting to abandon an injured man. That's a big thing for the funny robot to say, and nobody reacts to it.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** "I'd use the door they forgot." Whose door? He's the one holding the key, but I only knew that from the last panel.
- **Page 26.** "He said I'd know you by what you did first." So Tomas tested Alex by being trapped? Or did he just get lucky? I couldn't tell if the accident was staged.
- **Page 33, panel 4.** "Then it's not the route we take." Why not? Because the band is restricted? Because it's a trap? I didn't follow the logic.
- **Page 34.** How does Ada's broadcast get from inside a sealed mine onto every screen in Keel?
- **Page 35.** Why is there an "overdue installment" when the debt only transferred that morning? And why does paying it open the rail gate? "They bought the gate" is a good line, but I didn't understand the rule behind it.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First page | Did I ever find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Tomas | 23 | Who he is, slowly. That Adrian trusted him, on page 26. |
| "The old key" | 23 | It's a physical key to the old door (p26). On p23 it meant nothing. |
| "Travel token" / "parts advance" | 23 | Only at the very end (p63). |
| The Measure | 31 | Yes, it's the first safeguard. |
| The Keel–Halyard rail and why debt blocks it | 35 | Partly. I guessed. |

### Lines I read twice
- "Who carries the downside?" / "Usually the person with the broken part." (p23) Twice, and I gave up.
- "Make it real." (p23) Gave up.
- "He marked what survived them." (p28)
- "Inventory's moving." (p30) Inventory meaning water? Read it twice.
- "Adrian hid it where the records hurt most." (p31)
- "We're already in it." (p34) Got it on the second read. Nice.
- The collar text: `WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE.` Not hard to read, but it's a poster slogan on a machine. This is where I started thinking "this book is trying to teach me something," and that part of me went on alert.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Tomas came into focus on pages 25–27 and I liked him ("Someone has to look ordinary").
- Pilar is still just "the medicine lady."
- The residents are a crowd. That's fine.

### I felt something
- Page 30, the dry orange-stained residential pipe.
- Page 35, the town paying his installment. That small, kind moment worked on me.

### I predicted
- Tomas would get punished for helping. (He doesn't, really.)
- The rail was a trap. (It isn't.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The system is lying about how much water the poor town gets, and Alex has to collect proof and gadgets to expose it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 23, nearly. It's the most confusing page so far and it comes right after a good cliffhanger. I only kept going because I wanted to see the mine.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, mildly. "You still want out?" / "I want the answer." and a train climbing to a new city is a decent end. It's not a cliffhanger, it's a "next level" screen. I'd keep going because of the 62/18 reveal, not because of the last panel.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 23; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — The Price of Ownership (pages 38–44)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 38, panel 2.** "More isn't clean." The water they fought for comes out orange. Good twist on the win, and it showed me why the next part matters.
- **Page 39, panels 1–2.** A worker hands in a repair, gets 14 credits, and his name disappears from the ownership field. Clear and quietly nasty. I got it without any explanation.
- **Page 40.** Lock's offer: sign over the estate and the debt clears. Refuse and everything freezes. A real temptation with real numbers. "You're asking me to sell work I didn't make." Good line.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 41.** Four new people in one page: Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren, each with one line. I skimmed and couldn't tell them apart.
- **Pages 42–43.** The build montage. 11%, then 91%, "a calendar page turns through several weeks." Weeks go by in one panel and I felt nothing, because I don't know these four people.
- **Page 44.** Alex lists everyone's job out loud ("Lina: membrane chemistry. Suri: living biofilter...") like a credits roll. I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 38, panel 1.** How did the Keel residents "open the old residential branch"? We were just in the mine. Is this happening while they're on the train? Days later?
- **Page 39.** 312 patents. What did Adrian invent? Why does a debt-ridden man have 312 patents? If his work was worth that much, why is there a 418,000 debt? Nobody asks, and I spent the rest of the issue asking it.
- **Page 41, panel 1.** "The registry opens four restricted contributor records." How do they find these people? In the next panel they're just there.
- **Page 44, panel 3.** The Maker's Seal. Is this the second safeguard? Nobody says so. Lina just has it and hands it over. With the Measure we went into a mine. With this one somebody gives it to him. I only worked out it was safeguard #2 when Alex lines all three up on page 59.
- Where is Bi11bot this whole issue? He disappears.
- Was the Lock deal turned down? It just sort of fades out.
- What's the "Contributor Venture" on the receipt? The split of 38 credits clearly means something, but I don't know what I'm supposed to feel about it.

- "I'm asking you to protect it at scale." (p40)
- "Mine dies if the flow is wrong." / "And mine gets flagged if the pattern is wrong." (p41) Gave up. I don't know what Jalen does.
- "Smaller failure." (p42)
- `FIELD TEST — NOT POTABLE-OUTPUT GUARANTEE.` (p43) So is the water drinkable or not? Still don't know.

- Lina: maybe, because she has the Seal.
- Suri: the one with the plant.

### I felt something

- Nothing after that.

### I predicted

- That Lock would freeze the estate and make trouble. (He doesn't, really.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Rich companies steal credit for poor people's inventions, and Alex makes a water filter that pays everyone fairly.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 41. Four strangers at once, and I stopped caring who was who.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The issue ends on a receipt: `12 — ALEX DEBT ACCOUNT / 18 — CONTRIBUTOR VENTURE / 8 — MAINTENANCE`. That's a spreadsheet. Nobody is in danger, there's no question, and the villain's offer just evaporated. I'd only open Issue 04 because it's assigned.

## Issue 04 — The Broken Covenant (pages 45–50)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 45, panel 2.** `VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED.` Ada has a past here. I sat up. Ada has been the steadiest person in the book, and now something is about her.
- **Page 46.** Leona: "You're taller." / Ada: "That's what you have?" Great. And "I knew where you were within a year." / "Then you knew enough to come." Best exchange in the book. Ada is furious and short, and it's real.
- **Page 48, panel 1.** "Don't apologize where it costs nothing." Ada again. I liked her a lot here.
- **Page 50.** Mera's offer: hold the power, fix everything, clear the debt, Grandmother safe. Projected images of what he wants. A strong temptation scene.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 47.** The Covenant history. Leona explains the whole backstory: three founders, a shortage, retaliation, the votes, temporary emergency authority. It's a lecture with a projector. I read it twice to keep up and still had to piece it together.
- **Page 48, panels 2–3.** "I will support an agreement answerable to the people affected. Publicly. I accept review if I fail." That's not how a mother talks to her daughter. It's how someone talks at a city council. The emotional scene from page 46 turned into a policy statement.

### I was confused
- The whole reunion takes three pages. Ada's mother left her? Why? "I believed you were safer not knowing I was looking." Safer from what? Never said.
- Pilar Veyra again. Is she Leona's mother? Ada's grandmother? If so, why didn't Pilar ever say anything?
- **Page 48, panel 4.** Bi11bot has Adrian's band? Since when?
- **Page 49.** Mera just walks in. How did she get here? She was tracking them in Issue 01. Is she arresting them? Helping? She opens a door for them, so... both?
- **Page 50.** "Two of three. Again." Read it several times. I think it means two of the three bands are linked, like last time, when two founders voted for the emergency. But "Again" could also mean she's tempting a second Phantum. I'm not sure.
- I still didn't fully get that the Covenant (bands linking) is safeguard #3. I suspected, but nobody said it.
- What happens when Alex turns down the offer? Nothing seems to. The issue just stops.

| Thing | First page | Did I ever find out? |

| Leona Veyra | 46 | Ada's mother, one of three founders. |
| The Covenant | 47 | Mostly, from the lecture. |
| The Covenant bands, and why they link | 46–48 | Roughly. |

- "Those aren't the same thing." (p46) This is the second time the book uses "those aren't [X]" (p14: "Those aren't opposites."). I noticed it.
- "If we hold all the keys, everyone waits for our hands." (p50) Twice. Okay line, but a riddle.
- "Two of three. Again." (p50) Gave up.

### I felt something

### I predicted

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A long time ago three people gave a computer emergency control over water, and now their kids have to undo it without taking the power themselves.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 47. The flashback lecture.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Sort of. Mera is now interesting: is she the villain or the one who regrets it? "Two of three. Again." sounds ominous, but I didn't understand it, so it's a loud noise more than a hook. I'd read on to find out about Mera, not because of how this page ends.

## Issue 05 — The Last Bet (pages 51–56)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 51, panel 3.** Alex's debt and restrictions pop up next to Adrian's in the same shape. "It used the same shape." That chilled me. The system is doing to him exactly what it did to his dad.
- **Page 51, panel 4.** "People stopped helping him when the record became more dangerous than the man." Probably the best idea in the whole book, and I got it first time.
- **Page 52.** Mera, the most powerful person in Oasis, gets `OVERRIDE DENIED`. "I could not stop a door from closing." I didn't see that coming. The villain I'd been expecting turns out to have been overruled by her own system. That changed the book for me.
- **Pages 54–55.** Adrian's last recording with the water rising behind him. "I wanted to leave you a choice. I left you a burden instead." The bulkhead closes on his hand and the image cuts. This is the scene I'll remember. It's the only time the book was actually tense.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 51, panel 1.** Four warning displays at once. I skimmed the words and just took in the feeling.
- **Page 56, panel 2.** Mera's pledge, "I will submit my records and decisions to public review. I accept removal if I conceal or alter them," is word for word the shape of Leona's pledge on page 48. Second time, same speech. I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 52.** The recording says "Suspend pursuit." Pursuit of Adrian? So Mera tried to save him? I think so, but I wasn't sure who was chasing whom.
- **Page 53.** "Ordinary control nodes are absent" and "where Alpha couldn't count it." Got it. But how did they find this station? Mera just leads them there.
- **Page 55, panel 2.** "Adrian finishes the recording and activates a dormant Bi11bot signal." So Bi11bot was switched on from here, years ago? Then what was B11 doing in the workshop? I couldn't work out the timeline.
- **Is Adrian dead?** The book won't say. "I do not know what happened after." That's fine as a mystery if it comes back. (It doesn't.)
- **Page 56.** Mera changes sides in one page. I believe her more after page 52, but it's still fast.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First page | Did I ever find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Why Adrian was being chased | 51–52 | Only "the record became more dangerous than the man." Who chased him? Alpha? People? Unclear. |
| The monitoring station | 53 | No. |
| Bi11bot's "dormant signal" | 55 | No. |

### Lines I read twice
- "It recognized a pattern." (p51) Fine on the second read, and good.
- "Do not become me because I failed to be there." (p54) Twice. I think it means "don't become a gambler or a ghost because I left." Moving once I got it.
- "Now we carry all three." (p56) Only here did I really understand that the Measure, Seal and Covenant are the three safeguards from page 21. That's 35 pages later.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Mera finally became a person for me on page 52.
- Adrian is clearer dead-or-maybe-dead than he was alive.
- Leona isn't in this issue as far as I can tell, so I lost track of her.

### I felt something
- Page 51, "the same shape." Fear for Alex.
- Page 55, the bulkhead on Adrian's hand. Real.
- Page 52, Mera hitting the console. I felt sorry for her, which I didn't expect.

### I predicted
- Adrian is alive and will show up at the end. (He doesn't.)
- Alex will be offered power one more time. (He is.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A computer system turned a man's debt into a death sentence, and his son has to stop it happening to him without becoming the new boss.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. This is the strongest issue after Issue 01.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Not because of the last panel ("Now we carry all three" is a checklist being ticked off) but because the two pages before it made me care, and I want to see what Alex does with the stuff. It's the end of a quest, not a cliffhanger, but I'd turn the page.

VERDICT Issue 05: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 06 — The Fourth Choice (pages 57–64)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 58, panel 4.** Alex looks from the debt number to the cloudy water and says "Easy for who?" That was a good character moment, and it echoes Lina on page 44.
- **Page 62.** The Caldera rep tries to fudge the numbers right away and Tomas catches him with a physical gauge: "You counted the pipe after you stopped counting the homes." I liked that the new system gets tested immediately, and that Tomas is the one who catches it. Easily the best page of the issue.
- **Page 63, panel 3.** The 40-credit Tomas note finally pays off. "Took you long enough." / "You kept counting." I smiled. I also finally understood page 23, sixty pages late.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 59.** Three panels, three objects, three matching sentences: "It shows what's happening. It doesn't choose what matters." / "It shows who did the work. It doesn't erase a contract." / "It shows what we promise. It doesn't make anyone obey." It's a speech built like a school essay. This is the page where the "is this AI-made?" alarm went off for me and the people I'd be reading with: same rhythm three times, every line a slogan.
- **Page 60.** More procedure: reviewers, baselines, records. I skimmed.
- **Page 61.** Four display panels in a row. `EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED.` `ALPHA FUNCTIONS RETAINED...` `EXISTING ALLOCATIONS... UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS.` The climax of the book happens on a screen in all caps. I didn't feel a thing. I skimmed all four.

### I was confused
- **The title says "The Fourth Choice."** What were the first three? Take power, sell to Lock, run away? I worked out a guess. The book never says.
- **Page 57.** How did all three cities end up in one hall? Who called the meeting?
- **Page 60, panel 4.** `RECIPROCAL OBLIGATION ACCEPTED.` Accepted by whom? Alpha? So the computer agrees to give up power because Alex gave a speech? I thought the whole point was that people couldn't override it.
- **Page 63, panel 2.** The debt drops from 418,107 to 314,159. Where did 104,000 credits come from? The purifier paid 12 credits a unit. I guess a lot of units sold? Nobody says. (Also, 314,159 is pi. I noticed, and then wondered if the number was a joke rather than a real amount. It pulled me out.)
- **Pages 1–10 vs. 57–64.** The book never goes back to 2099. I still don't know how Keel becomes that big clean lake, who the granddaughter is, who TJ is, who Nera and Voss work for, or whether Alex "made water pay for it." Issue 00 asked me a question and the book forgot it. And now old Alex's line, "Find out who gets to decide what water is worth," doesn't fit either. By the end he *has* decided that: the affected people decide. So why is he still asking 70 years later? Did the system fail? I genuinely can't tell if that's supposed to be sad.
- **Grandmother's cloth (p64).** I only half remembered the cloth from page 15. Her setting it down is clearly supposed to mean something. I didn't know what.

### Lines I read twice
- "The revised denominator confirms improved delivery." (p62) Twice, then I let page 62 panel 2 explain it.
- "It doesn't erase a contract." (p59) Which contract? Lock's? The worker's? Adrian's debt?
- "We revise it in public." (p60)
- "I'm showing up." (p64) Not hard, but I paused. "You're staying?" / "I'm showing up." I like it, but it's one more line built to be quoted.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, Tomas, Mera: clear.
- The representatives: interchangeable. The Caldera rep is the only one who does anything, and he only exists to be caught.
- Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren don't really come back, except as painted names above a shop door.
- Bi11bot barely appears in the finale, and I missed him.

### I felt something
- Page 62, Tomas and the gauge. Satisfying.
- Page 64, panel 1. The pump from page 11 still running under his bracket. I noticed the callback and it was nice.
- I didn't feel the "win." Emergency authority gets rescinded on a screen and I just watched words change colour.

### I predicted
- That Adrian would turn up. (He doesn't.)
- That the book would cut back to old Alex and the granddaughter. (It doesn't.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Not taking power when you could is the only way to fix a system that crushed your family, and fairness means doing the boring public paperwork forever.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 61. Four screens of all-caps system messages for the climax.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
If there were a second book: no. The last image of Alex and Ada above the town is fine, and "I'm showing up" is a real ending. But the loose threads I actually cared about were the 2099 opening (TJ, the thieves, "They say you made water pay for it") and Adrian's fate, and this page touches neither. So it doesn't make me want more. It makes me wonder why I was shown them.

VERDICT Issue 06: put down: page 61; next issue: no

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 23.** It comes right after the best cliffhanger in the book (page 22, the tracking ring), and it's four disconnected panels with a new character (Tomas), an object we don't know ("the old key's travel token and parts advance") and two lines nobody can parse ("Who carries the downside?", "Make it real."). That's where half the room checks their phone and doesn't come back. Runner-up: **Issue 06, page 61**, the climax told in four system displays.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a mining town inherits his dead-or-missing dad's massive debt on his 17th birthday, finds his dad's robot, and proves the computer running everything has been stealing the town's water. Then he gets offered the power to fix it all himself and says no. The robot's good and Ada's good. There's a cool robot dog at the start that never comes back."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 00:** A great falling-apart robot dog and one sharp question ("did you make them pay?") that the book never answers.
- **Issue 01:** The debt on his birthday got me, and Bi11bot kept me.
- **Issue 02:** A confusing first page, then the 62%/18% pipe reveal, which is the moment the book makes sense.
- **Issue 03:** Four strangers build a filter in a montage and the issue ends on a receipt.
- **Issue 04:** Three honest pages between Ada and her mother, swallowed by a history lecture.
- **Issue 05:** "It used the same shape," and a door closing on Adrian's hand. The best the book gets.
- **Issue 06:** The right choice, made in a speech and confirmed by a screen, with the opening's questions left on the dock.
