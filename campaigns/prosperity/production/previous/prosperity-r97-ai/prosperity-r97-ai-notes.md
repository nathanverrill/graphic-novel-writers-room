# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a pass.** The script’s major causal spine is present, but both cold reads identify repeated failures of reader orientation, especially around TJ, Alpha, the safeguards, the maker ensemble and the Issue 03 governance climax. The execution package also contains a Blocker: the Page 46 layout is an entirely different scene from the approved script, so the book is not ready for production.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Page 46 layout and thumbnail are the wrong scene

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Page 46; `layouts.md`, Page 46; `thumbnails.md`, Page 46.
- **Observation:** The script ends Issue 02 with Alex separated from Ada, Lina taken behind a service shutter and an Oasis summons. The layout and thumbnail instead depict Leona, Mera and Ada in Leona’s apartment.
- **Problem:** This is a complete scene substitution, not a layout interpretation. It removes the approved cliffhanger and duplicates Issue 03 material.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Layout Agent:** discard the existing Page 46 layout and thumbnail and rebuild the barrier-separation page from the script. **Letterer:** do not letter the apartment dialogue on this page.
- **Role:** layout, letterer.

#### 2. Alpha is essential to the climax but is not introduced clearly enough

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 20; Issue 03 Pages 53, 57–60; cold reads’ Issue 03 responses.
- **Observation:** Alpha appears as `LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED` on Page 20, but the script never establishes that Alpha is the system governing water, labor, travel, debt and records. Page 57 suddenly asks for a “designated human authority,” and Page 58 offers Alex control over “Debt, travel, water, House review.”
- **Problem:** The central climax depends on a system the reader may understand only as a few glowing nodes. The offer of authority and the rescinding of emergency authority therefore lack dramatic context.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** plant Alpha’s operational reach before Page 57 through a concrete consequence, not exposition—for example, show the Page 20 trace altering Alex’s travel status or assigning Tomas, and let the display identify Alpha as the authority performing it. At Page 57, make the transfer’s scope visually legible through the existing records rather than adding a speech.
- **Role:** writer.

### Major

#### 3. The Issue 00 hook is disconnected from the principal story

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 00 Pages 8–10; Issue 03 Page 64; `brief.md`, Issue 00 canon.
- **Observation:** The prologue introduces a water theft, a drone component with a white-and-amber node and older Alex’s unexplained wealth. The main story never returns to the thieves, the component, or the question of how Alex became prosperous.
- **Problem:** Both readers report that the opening question is abandoned. The final page leaves the reader with an unrelated travel question, so the book’s first and last narrative promises do not connect.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Director:** decide whether the prologue is intended as a self-contained thematic echo or a plot setup. If self-contained, **Writer:** remove the apparent Alpha-specific node and make the final exchange explicitly about older Alex’s continuing responsibility to protect shared water, without promising an explanation later. If it is a setup, it needs a concrete payoff before Page 64; do not leave the current node as unexplained importance.
- **Role:** director, writer.

#### 4. TJ appears to be introduced twice, and his 2099 identifier is inconsistent

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 00 Pages 1–3 and Page 7; Issue 01 Page 17; `characters.md`, TJ visual lock.
- **Observation:** Issue 00 is written as if TJ is already familiar to older Alex and the granddaughter, but Issue 01 Page 17 presents TJ as a newly discovered folded robot dog. The fixed visual identifier also requires the brass ring in 2099 and plain ceramic lenses in 2043; the prologue does not consistently show the ring.
- **Problem:** The reader cannot confidently tell that the two robots are the same TJ or understand whether Page 17 is his construction, discovery or reactivation.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** make Page 17 establish that B11 is assembling TJ from the dog chassis, rather than presenting TJ as an unexplained finished object; use one unmistakable visual callback to the 2099 dog. **Character Designer:** show the brass right-eye ring in 2099 and plain scratched ceramic lenses in 2043. Preserve the unexplained transfer of the ring.
- **Role:** writer, character_designer.

#### 5. Page 25’s B11 mistake is not literally intelligible

- **Observation:** B11 says, “I predicted we could leave him and remain unseen,” but the script never shows or states that B11 advised leaving Tomas. “Concealment route” and “What would you do?” are not anchored to a visible choice.

- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** establish B11’s concrete recommendation before the rescue—e.g. a visible route that bypasses Tomas with a stated prediction that the search beam will miss them—then show Alex rejecting it. Keep B11’s admission short after the rescue.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 6. The Page 15 clinic sequence contributes to the Issue 01 pacing drop without sufficiently defining Tomas

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Pages 13–15; cold-read reports.
- **Observation:** Three consecutive pages cover the casino, Ada’s food and the clinic before the hidden workshop. Tomas is introduced as the person fronting medicine, but his role as an older miner and Adrian connection is deferred until Pages 24–26.
- **Problem:** Both readers identify Pages 13–15 as the first likely put-down point. The personal debt is important, but the current sequence feels like cryptic setup rather than a choice under pressure.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Plotter:** compress or combine the casino/food/clinic beats while preserving the 45-credit antibiotic cost and 10-credit terms. **Writer:** identify Tomas through a concrete miner’s action or object at the clinic, and make Alex visibly choose medicine over lamp fuel or travel before accepting the loan.
- **Role:** plotter, writer.

#### 7. The Measure appears without adequate setup

- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** plant the Measure in the workshop or B11’s stored equipment on Page 17 or 18, with one visible physical test or limitation. At Page 27, show it clamping separately to pipe and data line before the comparison resolves.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 8. The four Halyard makers remain a fetch-quest sequence

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Pages 37–44; cold reads; `story.md` maker risk.
- **Observation:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren each appear in a short handoff scene, then the purifier is assembled and released within eight pages. The readers report that the makers blur together and their exposure does not yet matter emotionally.
- **Problem:** The story needs the makers to function as people making consequential choices, not as component dispensers. Lina’s Page 46 capture is especially weak because the reader has had little time to invest in her.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Plotter:** collapse redundant retrieval beats and give each remaining maker a distinct choice that changes the build or the risk. **Writer:** make the choice visible: Suri risks the living culture, Jalen chooses to enter the build despite surveillance, Ren commits the repairable connector, and Lina chooses public attribution despite capture risk.
- **Role:** plotter, writer.

#### 9. The Maker’s Seal’s function and the five-maker structure are unclear

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Pages 43–44; Page 63; cold reads.
- **Observation:** Alex names four collaborators on Page 43, then Page 44 lists five contributors without explicitly naming Alex. The Seal appears for the first time on Page 44 and seems to block an exclusive claim, but the relation between attribution, House ownership and public release is not shown.
- **Problem:** The reader cannot tell whether the Seal transfers ownership, blocks a corporation, verifies contribution or simply makes the design free. The five-way Morrow & Sons ownership is then asserted only on Page 63.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** name all five makers—including Alex—in the public record and show the Seal verifying contribution rather than transferring property. **Plotter:** plant the cooperative business as the intended destination of the design before the release, even if the shop opens later. **Letterer:** keep the five names legible without turning the panel into an exposition screen.
- **Role:** plotter, writer, letterer.

#### 10. The Page 46 danger does not pay off in Issue 03

Give Leona the distinct action of choosing public exposure to Ada’s challenge. Preserve both women’s accountability without adding biography.

#### 12. The Covenant activation remains visually and causally under-explained

#### 13. The distributed protocol reads as a civics diagram rather than a process under pressure

Keep the representative ensemble visually simple and name only the people needed for action.

#### 15. The ending’s debt reduction and Morrow & Sons operation are asserted, not earned

## Plausibility ledger

- **64-page structure — HOLDS:** The script contains Pages 1–64 with the required 10-page prologue and three 18-page issues.

- **House names — CONFLICTED:** Brief/script use Caldera, Vector and Orbital; facts/world retain Veyr, Cairn and Ravel. Normalize before production.
- **Alpha’s scope — UNDER-SUPPORTED:** The ending depends on Alpha’s emergency authority, but the script never concretely establishes its governing reach before the transfer offer.
- **Measure — UNDER-SUPPORTED:** The tool’s appearance and operation are plausible within the hard-SF baseline, but it is introduced too late and its physical limitations are not planted.
- **Maker’s Seal — PARTIALLY HOLDS:** It can verify named contribution and block a single exclusive claim, but the script risks implying that it itself changes property law or makes the design public.
- **Purifier — HOLDS WITH LIMITATION:** The 91% figure is correctly labeled an efficiency simulation and not potable output. No unresolved customer specification should be invented.
- **Covenant — PARTIALLY HOLDS:** Leona states an obligation, accepts a consequence and performs the public action before activation. The band continuity and physical mechanism remain under-supported.
- **Distributed protocol — UNDER-SUPPORTED:** The script registers the protocol and stages the first review, but selection, mandate, recall and representatives’ authority are not shown sufficiently.
- **First review — UNDER-SUPPORTED:** The House inventory discrepancy is visible but needs a clearer record connecting owned units to the false “community supply” figure.
- **Debt reduction — UNDER-SUPPORTED:** The final 402,110-credit balance is canonically required but the script does not show the visible work and payments producing it.
- **Morrow & Sons — UNDER-SUPPORTED:** The shop appears only as an ending status image. Joint ownership, wages, equal profit shares and customer-serving operation need a concrete visual bridge.
- **Adrian’s fate — HOLDS:** The station record supports presumed death and legal missing status without recovering a body.
- **Alex’s mother — HOLDS:** No prohibited information is added. Preserve the standing rule that she left Adrian after he lost everything and nothing else is learned.
- **Page 46 execution — CONTRADICTED:** The layout and thumbnail depict the wrong scene. This is an execution Blocker.
- **Hard-SF baseline — GENERALLY HOLDS:** Pipes, gauges, physical keys, data shards and manual repairs support the setting. The Covenant must remain staged as a bounded physical safeguard rather than unexplained magic.
