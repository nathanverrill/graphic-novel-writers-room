# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it straight through in one sitting, the night before, as homework. I read only the pages. A few artist notes leaked through ("No triumphal chime," "the cloth she has folded throughout the book"). I ignored them, because a reader never sees them.

---

## Issue 00 — Lake Keel, 2099 (pages 1–10)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 2.** TJ flies apart in midair, KLAK-KLAK-KLAK. A robot dog that comes apart into magnetic pieces is the first thing that made me look up from my phone. "Is TJ broken?" / "Not usually." is a good exchange.
- **Pages 3–5.** I could follow the dog jamming the pump as a little action sequence. The leg tearing off and dropping into the lake (p5 p1, TINK) actually worried me.
- **Page 6, panel 4.** "He brought something." / "He usually does." I liked the grandpa here.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 8–9.** The barge is gone and we're looking at a records terminal. "They left less than they planned." / "Good." / "Keep that with the record." Nothing is happening. I skimmed page 9 completely: lights, a boot, Alex looking at walls.
- **Page 10.** It's a full-page image for a conversation that mostly says nothing.

### I was confused
- **Page 3.** Why won't he call the wardens? "After the water's gone" sounds like he *wants* them to steal it. Then "Let him show us where it goes," but TJ doesn't follow it anywhere. He breaks the pump and comes home.
- **Page 7.** "They say you made water pay for it." I don't know what that means. Did Grandpa do something bad to get this lake? The book raises it and then drops it.
- I couldn't tell whether the thieves are villains or poor people. "Then he took it first" makes it sound like Alex is the bad guy. I was intrigued, but nobody ever answers it.
- **Page 10.** "Find out who gets to decide what stays ours." Who is he going to ask? Is he doing something tomorrow? It's an ending line about nothing specific.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Phantum** (p2): I worked out it's Alex's last name around p12.
- **Keel** (title, p2): a lake here, a mine town later. Nobody explains how one became the other.
- **Wardens** (p3): never explained.
- **The "dry channels" on the far shore** (p3): why is everyone else dry? Never explained.
- **TJ:** never appears again in the whole book.
- **Nera and Voss:** never appear again.
- **The granddaughter:** no name, and she never comes back.
- **The drone component with the amber light** (p7): it gets a close-up and is kept "with the record." It never matters again.

### Lines I read twice
- "After the water's gone." (p3)
- "They say you made water pay for it." (p7). I gave up on this one.
- "Find out who gets to decide what stays ours." (p10)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, the granddaughter and TJ were all clear. Nera (red hood) and Voss (lamp helmet) were clear too, but they're there for five pages and then gone.

### I felt something
Small worry when TJ's leg fell off. That's about it.

### I predicted
I expected the book to come back to old Alex at the lake and tell me how he got it. It doesn't (see Issue 06).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old guy owns the only clean lake, people are stealing from it, and his robot dog defends it.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 9.** It's after the action, and nothing happens.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. "Find out who gets to decide" isn't a cliffhanger. It's a theme statement. Nobody is in danger and nobody wants anything I can name. The dog was the hook and the dog is just sitting there.

VERDICT Issue 00: put down: page 9; next issue: no

## Issue 01 — The Inheritance (pages 11–22)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 12.** The debt reveal works: "418,607 CREDITS / 28.7 YEARS." Then "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "Twenty-eight point seven." That's the first time the book felt like it had a real problem. Your dad's debt lands on you at seventeen. I got it right away.
- **Page 13.** Ada and the mugs ("I'm generous with containers.") and "I'm your friend, Alex. Not your exit plan." I liked her immediately.
- **Page 16.** A secret door behind the bookcase. Cheap, but it works.
- **Page 18.** Bi11bot. "B11 if the room is on fire" is the best line in the issue. "Adrian's jaw. His poorer timing." is good too.
- **Page 20.** The door locks him in. Finally something pushes back.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 14.** The photograph conversation. "Both can be true." / "Then what are we missing?" feels like a summary of a scene, not the scene itself.
- **Page 15.** Grandmother and Pilar are over in four panels. I skimmed it.
- **Page 17.** Chalkboard, equation, brass plate, map, three ticks. It's a pile of clues I'm told to look at with no reason why.

### I was confused
- **Page 12.** Is Foreman-7 a robot? The name says robot. I guess the art says human. It doesn't matter much, but I paused.
- **Page 12, panel 4.** What's the Last Chance bar? Why are we shown it closed?
- **Page 17.** An "empty recess" next to the water-drop plate. Empty of what? I assume the Measure, but I only guessed that much later.
- **Page 18.** "Inserts the old connector." What connector? Where did he get it?
- **Page 20.** Why does the door lock? "Because the band stopped reporting?" Which band? Stopped when? I didn't see that happen.
- **Page 21.** "Three safeguards." Safeguards against what? "It measures what the records claim" is a riddle. I didn't know what Alex was supposed to *do* with it, or why he decides to go to the mine.
- **Page 21, panel 4.** "Just checking." Checking what?
- Adrian's recording (p19) says "If you're seeing this, I failed to come back." So is Dad dead or missing? I wanted to know, and that was "intrigued," not lost.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Adrian:** the band says "Guarantor: Adrian Phantum." I worked out on p14 that he's the dad.
- **"The looping equation"** (p16): it's treated like I've seen it before. I haven't. It shows up on the watch, the chalkboard, a pipe node and the projectors, and nobody ever says what it is or what it does.
- **Balanced water-drop mark** (p15, 17, 26): a symbol of something. Never explained.
- **Last Chance bar** (p12): never explained.
- **Halyard, Oasis** (p20): just names on a projection until Issues 02 and 04.
- **Alpha** (p20): explained in one line ("routes water, assigns work and records permission"). I got that, barely. Is it a computer? A government? I assumed AI.
- **Site 6** (p21): never explained, though I figured it's part of the mine.
- **Mera** (p22): a woman in a control room with a glass of water. No idea who she is.
- **"Legacy protocol"** (p22): never explained.

### Lines I read twice
- "Badly, eventually." (p16)
- "It measures what the records claim." (p21)
- "The door knows what it was told." (p20). I got it on the second read.
- "Just checking." (p21). I gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, B11, Grandmother and Foreman-7 were all clear. Pilar was "Ada's aunt," and that's all I know about her for the whole book. Mera is a face with no context.

### I felt something
Page 12, the birthday line, and a little on page 19 ("I hurt you and Grandmother"). The dad admitting he gambled was more honest than I expected.

### I predicted
Dad is alive somewhere and the robot will lead to him. B11 will be the funny one. Mera is the villain.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his dad's debt finds the dad's robot and a treasure hunt for three "safeguards" that might fix the system that owns him.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. The debt and the robot carried me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, mildly. Someone is now tracking him, and I care about him because of the debt. But Mera is a stranger, so the threat is a ring on a screen, not a person.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — The Price of Departure (pages 23–37)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 24.** "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR" pops up the second he steps over to help Tomas. "You know that costs you." / "I noticed." This was the first time the money thing felt like a story and not a lecture.
- **Page 27.** "Foreman! Your safety report is on fire." That was funny. "Abandoned mines do not sweat." is a good creepy line.
- **Pages 28–29.** Paper map vs. computer map, with drones on the straight route. It was clear, and I liked B11 saying "I read that wrong."
- **Page 32.** "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% / PHYSICALLY DELIVERED: 18%." This is the clearest, angriest moment in the book. "Same period?" / "Same water." I understood exactly what was stolen.
- **Pages 34–35.** The whole town sees it, and the neighbors put credits on the table. I actually felt something here.
- **Page 36.** Bulkhead, BOOM, running. Good.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 23.** Budgeting food and medicine, a ledger handoff, a key, a token, forty credits. That's a lot of bookkeeping before anything happens.
- **Page 30.** Lithium workings, industrial uplink, residential pipe, control node, analog gauge. I had to slow down to picture which pipe is which.

### I was confused
- **Page 23, panel 1.** "Who carries the downside?" Out of nowhere. Is B11 quizzing him?
- **Pages 23–24.** Tomas hands Alex the key at Site 6, and on the very next page Tomas is pinned under a hauling frame. Did I miss a jump? It read like the same moment.
- **Page 25.** "My route assumed we left Tomas where he fell." So the robot planned to abandon a man? Then "Yes. What would you do?" Was it a test? It's never followed up.
- **Page 26.** "From anyone who came for the work." What work?
- **Page 30.** "Inventory's moving." I didn't know who says "inventory" about water, or why.
- **Page 33.** Why does Alex refuse the private route? "Then it's not the route we take" sounds noble, but I don't know what was wrong with it.
- **Page 35.** Why does paying his debt installment open the train gate? I guess the overdue debt was what had him locked in? I had to piece that together. "They bought the gate" only half-landed.
- How did Ada get onto the town speakers from inside a mine?

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Tomas** (p23): he just appears and hands over a key. Who is he to Alex? I learn he knew Adrian on p26.
- **"The Measure"** (p31): B11 names it like I should already know it. The collar line explains it a bit.
- **"Household offense"** (p25).
- **Lithium workings** (p30): so Keel was a lithium mine? It's said once.
- **Rail authorization** (p35): never set up as the thing he needed.

### Lines I read twice
- "He marked what survived them." (p28)
- "Inventory's moving." (p30)
- "Adrian hid it where the records hurt most." (p31)
- "We're already in it." (p34). I got it after the second read, and liked it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, B11, Tomas and Foreman-7 were all clear. Pilar shows up again with a ledger. She's "the medicine lady."

### I felt something
Page 35, the neighbors paying. It was the first time Keel felt like people instead of a setting.

### I predicted
That the 62/18 number would come back and decide the ending. It sort of does.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The system has been lying about how much water the poor town gets, and Alex has to collect three devices that prove it.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. This is the best issue.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. "You still want out?" / "I want the answer." Then he's on a train to a new city. It isn't a cliffhanger, but I wanted to see Halyard.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — The Price of Ownership (pages 38–44)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 38.** "More isn't clean." They won the water back and it's orange. Good gut punch.
- **Page 39, panels 1–2.** The worker's name disappears from the ownership field while he gets 14 credits. I understood that instantly, and it made me mad.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 39, panel 3.** "312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED." Numbers I didn't need.
- **Page 40.** It felt like a slide deck: three Houses and what each one owns, plus a legal offer. This is where I checked my phone.
- **Page 43.** A montage of people doing technical things with sleeves and bypasses. "91% SIMULATED EFFICIENCY / FIELD TEST — NOT POTABLE-OUTPUT GUARANTEE" reads like fine print.
- **Page 44, panel 4.** A pie chart of 38 credits. This is where it felt like an economics worksheet.

### I was confused
- **Page 40.** Lock says "Refuse, and rights freeze," then "Then find the people who made it." Is he a villain or helping? I couldn't tell what he wanted.
- **Page 41.** Four new people in one page. I don't know how Alex found them, why they'd trust him, or why they were "restricted."
- **Page 41, panel 3.** "Jalen watches them in his collar mirror." What is a collar mirror?
- **Page 44.** Lina hands Alex "the Maker's Seal." Wait, is this the second safeguard? Nobody said so. I only figured it out in Issue 05 when they "carry all three."
- **Page 44.** "Competing industrial unit in development." Is that a threat? It never comes back.
- The whole issue is seven pages and covers weeks. It felt rushed and slow at once.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The Houses** (p38 "House checkpoint arches"): explained on p40, but as a list.
- **Caldera, Vector, Orbital** (p40): only Caldera ever matters.
- **Director Cassian Lock** (p40): no introduction.
- **"Intellectual estate"** (p39).
- **Maker's Seal** (p44): never named as a safeguard before it shows up.

### Lines I read twice
- "I'm asking you to protect it at scale." (p40)
- "Smaller failure." (p42)
- "House review shelves it or owns it." (p41)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
**Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren: I could not tell them apart.** Each gets one line. If you'd asked me after page 44 who did the routing, I couldn't have told you without p44's list. Lock was clear enough as "suit guy."

### I felt something
Page 38, the orange water. After that, nothing.

### I predicted
Lock betrays them, or the "competing unit" crushes their purifier. Neither happens.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Rich companies steal credit for poor people's inventions, and the fix is a fair contract.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 40.** The House list and the patent deal.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The ending is a payment split and a notice in the background. Nobody I care about is in trouble. It reads like a lesson's conclusion.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 40; next issue: no

## Issue 04 — The Broken Covenant (pages 45–50)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 45.** "VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED." Ada has a secret? That woke me up.
- **Page 46.** "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" This is the best dialogue in the book. "I knew where you were within a year." / "Then you knew enough to come." Ouch. I actually felt for Ada.
- **Page 48.** "Don't apologize where it costs nothing." Good, and Ada finally gets to *do* something.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 47.** A flashback compressed into four panels: agreements, a shortage, a dark band, a deciding vote. I read it twice and still only half have it.
- **Page 50.** The temptation scene is over before it starts. He sees the dream and turns away in the same page.

### I was confused
- **Page 46.** Ada's mom is alive and in Oasis? Ada never mentioned her once in 45 pages. Is this why she came? Why is Pilar raising her? "The child's object inside the box" — what object?
- **Page 47.** What are Covenant bands? What did they do? Who voted on what? I got "Leona voted to give someone emergency power, and it went bad." I think that's how Alpha took over? I'm guessing.
- **Page 48.** Where did B11 get Adrian's band? Why do two bands linking matter?
- **Page 49.** Mera was tracking them in Issue 01. Now she walks in and leads them somewhere. Is she the enemy or not?
- **Page 50.** Mera is offering Alex the dictator job? Why him? Why now?

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Leona Veyra** (p46): never set up.
- **The Covenant / Covenant bands** (p46): the issue title uses the word before the book explains it, and it's never fully explained.
- **"Temporary emergency authority"** (p47): I think this is the whole villain of the book. It's one caption.
- **Mera's role**: "the most powerful person in Oasis" only arrives on p52.

### Lines I read twice
- "We built agreements that required all three of us to answer for them." (p47)
- "If we hold all the keys, everyone waits for our hands." (p50). I gave up on it the first time.
- "Two linked. One answer still missing." (p50)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Ada and Leona were clear. **Leona and Mera blurred**: two older Oasis women, both with bands, both speaking in pledges.

### I felt something
Page 46. Ada and her mother. That's the realest scene in the book, and it's over in a page.

### I predicted
Mera will join them and put her band in. I was right, one issue early.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Three adults broke an agreement that let a machine take over, and the kids have to make them own up.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. Ada's mom kept me going.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, weakly. "One answer still missing" makes me want Mera's side. But it's a question about a woman I don't care about.

VERDICT Issue 04: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 05 — The Last Bet (pages 51–56)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 51.** Adrian's record shrinks under warning markers, then Alex's appears "in the same shape." That's creepy and clear. "People stopped helping him when the record became more dangerous than the man" is a good line.
- **Page 52.** The most powerful woman in the city gets "OVERRIDE DENIED." "I could not stop a door from closing." That's when I finally got that Alpha is the real problem.
- **Pages 54–55.** Dad's last recording with the water rising. "Do not become me because I failed to be there." The bulkhead closes on his hand. That got me a little.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 53.** Two projectors and the equation again. I still don't know what the equation is.
- **Page 56.** The second pledge word for word: "submit my records… If I conceal one, remove me." I already read this from Leona. Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 55.** So did Dad die? "The image cuts before showing what happens." Fine, that's on purpose. But the book never comes back to it, so it felt like a dodge rather than a mystery.
- **Page 56.** The bands make a circle and "no command display activates." Is that good? Bad? What did linking them actually do?
- Why is this issue called "The Last Bet"? Whose bet?

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The equation**: still unexplained.
- **What the three safeguards do together**: still unexplained.

### Lines I read twice
- "It recognized a pattern." (p51)
- "He left this where Alpha couldn't count it." (p53)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Everyone present was clear. Mera became a person here, finally, too late for me to have feelings about her.

### I felt something
Page 54, panel 3. The dad line.

### I predicted
Alex will refuse the power in the finale, because he already did in Issue 04. I knew the ending here.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex's dad got crushed by a system that treats people as records, and Alex is getting the same treatment.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. It's short.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mildly. "Now we carry all three" is a checklist being completed, not a cliffhanger. I'd turn the page because it's the last issue and I'm almost done.

VERDICT Issue 05: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 06 — The Fourth Choice (pages 57–64)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 62.** The Caldera guy tries to cheat by leaving the homes off the diagram, and Tomas catches him with a gauge: "You counted the pipe after you stopped counting the homes." That's the only moment in the finale with any fight in it. Tomas is great.
- **Page 63, panel 3.** "TOMAS NOTE: 40 CREDITS." / "Took you long enough." / "You kept counting." That was a sweet callback.
- **Page 64.** "You're staying?" / "I'm showing up." Fine last line.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 57–61.** A civic hall, objects placed on a table, representatives placing records, "PUBLIC REVIEW ACCEPTED," "EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED." It's a meeting. The climax of the book is a meeting. I skimmed 59–61.
- **Page 63, panel 2.** The payment ledger list. I noticed the debt is 314,159, which is pi. I rolled my eyes a bit. It felt cute in a way that made me suspicious of the whole thing.

### I was confused
- **Page 57.** How did everyone get to this hall? Who called the meeting? Why is Tomas suddenly in Oasis?
- **Page 59, panel 4.** "Removes the executive authorization from the table." What is that physically? A card?
- **Page 61.** Alpha just… stands down because a display changed? There's no fight with the machine that's been the enemy since Issue 01. I'm not sure what Alpha is now.
- **What was the "fourth choice"?** I didn't know the first three.
- **What happened to the lake?** Issue 00 showed old Alex with a huge clean lake where the mine was, and someone saying "That's Phantum's lake" and "Nobody gets water that clean by being polite." This book ends with cloudy water and a debt of 314,159. I never learn how he got the lake, or whether he "made water pay for it." The opening asked a question the ending never touches.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **"Designated human authority"** (p57).
- **"Revised denominator"** (p62): I had to stop and think about this one.
- **The old ledgers "remain on the wall"** (p61): so nothing was erased? Is that the point?

### Lines I read twice
- "The revised denominator confirms improved delivery." (p62)
- "Nobody gets the whole room." (p59)
- "Easy for who?" (p58). This is the third time the book uses this line: "Easy for whom?" (p44), "Safe for whom?" (p46), "Easy for who?" (p58). By the third time it felt like a template, and that's exactly the thing that makes people in my class say "AI wrote this."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
The main cast was clear. "A Keel representative," "a Halyard representative," "an Oasis representative" are not characters. The four inventors only come back as names on a sign.

### I felt something
Page 63, Tomas's 40 credits. Small. Page 64, panel 3, Grandmother setting down the cloth. Maybe, if I'd noticed her folding it before. I'd only seen it once.

### I predicted
That Alex refuses the power. Correct, since p50. No surprise left.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Nobody should hold all the power, and you fix a rigged system by measuring honestly, crediting makers and making leaders answer in public.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 60.** The representatives placing records on a table.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Would I buy a next book? No. The ending is calm and decent, but the thing I'd want to know is how 17-year-old Alex becomes the old man with the stolen-from lake and the dog, and the book had that the whole time and didn't use it. Nothing is left open that I care about, except that, and the book acts like it forgot.

VERDICT Issue 06: put down: page 60; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 40.** Three Houses with their industries in a list, a patent-assignment offer, "immediate commercial rights freeze pending review." It comes right after the best issue (02), and from here the book reads like a lesson plan with robots. Second place: **pages 59–61**, where the climax is a public meeting.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid gets stuck with his dad's debt in a mining town, finds the dad's robot, and proves the city's AI has been lying about the water. Then it turns into civics class about patents and voting. The robot dog in the first ten pages is the best part, and it never comes back."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 00:** Cool exploding dog, then a grandpa staring at a terminal and talking in riddles, then I never see any of them again.
- **Issue 01:** The debt-on-your-birthday page and B11 made me care, even though I didn't know what a "safeguard" was.
- **Issue 02:** The best issue: the 62% vs. 18% reveal and the neighbors paying his bill actually hit.
- **Issue 03:** Four interchangeable inventors and a royalty pie chart; homework.
- **Issue 04:** Ada's mom shows up and it's the most real page in the book, then it's flashback captions about bands.
- **Issue 05:** The dad's last message landed; the second identical pledge didn't.
- **Issue 06:** Tomas catching the cheat was good, but the climax is a meeting and the opening's lake and dog are just forgotten.
