# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it in one sitting, Issue 01 through Issue 03, as homework I'd already argued about. I started out wanting it to be bad. Below is what actually happened, page by page.

One thing first, because it shaped the whole read: almost everybody in this book talks the same way. They speak in short, balanced, two-part lines like "Need is not a balance." "Tied, then. Not square." "Convening isn't owning." Inez, Ada, B11, Lina, Ren, Mera and Leona all sound like the same person. My class is primed to call things AI, and this is the first thing they'd point at. By Issue 02 I was rolling my eyes at every one of those lines, even the good ones.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2.** This is the hook, and it works. It's his seventeenth birthday, his dad's debt lands on him, and the screen says **418,607 CREDITS**, **EARNED TODAY: 40**, **28.7 YEARS**. The guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum" and the workers go quiet. I did the math in my head and felt sick for him. Best page of the issue.
- **Page 1.** Not much, but I got that this kid can feel when a machine is working and he doesn't care about permission. "Just notice it works" told me who he is.
- **Page 6.** B11. "I was stored. Sleep is more comfortable." "It is a fact with a poor record." The robot is funny. This page pulled me back after I'd started drifting on page 5.
- **Page 10.** He steps over the line and loses the day's pay, **−40 CR**, the exact number from page 2. I knew what that cost him. That's a real choice.
- **Page 11.** TJ, the dog built out of magnet parts that sends its head off on its own. Cute. I'd have looked at that panel for a while.
- **Pages 14–15.** **PHYSICAL FLOW: 18% / REPORTED FLOW: 62%.** Finally a plain fact: someone is stealing Keel's water and lying about it. That made the earlier confusion feel like it had been going somewhere.
- **Page 16, panel 4.** The public screens across Keel change to show both numbers. That felt big.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 4, panels 1–3.** The second Inez scene is the first scene over again (owes money, will pay five a shift, "tell me before"). I skimmed it.
- **Page 5.** The equation, the chip that "matches the loop," Grandmother saying two versions of the same line about the dad. I slid over this whole page.
- **Page 8.** The control room with Mera. I looked at the screen text and moved on. "Mera's hand remains on the control node, not the screen" meant nothing to me.
- **Page 17.** The cutting between Tomas, Ada, Mera and Site 6 was too fast to follow. I just looked at the pictures.

### I was confused
- **Page 3.** Who is Ada? Sister? Neighbor? Girlfriend? Grandmother's helper? It never says, and it still doesn't say by the end of the book.
- **Page 3.** Why does Alex want to go to Halyard? He wants a travel token, but I never find out what's there for him or why he needs to leave now.
- **Page 4.** "Site 6 took the shift." On page 4 I had no idea what Site 6 was. Then on pages 8–9 Mera reassigns him to Site 6 like it's new. Did that already happen, or is page 4 before page 8? The timeline lost me.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** He puts a book on a shelf and a secret door opens. Did he know? Was it an accident? Grandmother answers a question nobody asked: "Your father was working. That doesn't mean he didn't hurt us." It felt like I'd missed a page.
- **Page 5.** "This isn't a route." Why would a key be a route? I didn't know he was looking for one.
- **Page 6.** "Would you like the route, or the question first?" What question? I don't think anyone ever asks it.
- **Page 8.** Is Mera a bad guy? She tracks him and sends him to Site 6, and that's bad. Then on page 17 she opens a valve to help him escape. I couldn't tell whose side she's on, and not in an interesting way. I just didn't know.
- **Page 9.** The Foreman says clearance denied, Ada says "He's a tool," the Foreman says "So is the gate," and then the gate opens anyway. Why?
- **Pages 9–13.** What's the plan? He has a map and a key. Is he going to Halyard through the mine? Looking for his dad? Fixing water? I couldn't tell what he was trying to do until page 14 told me what he'd found.
- **Page 12.** "Someone has to make this look like an accident." Make what look like an accident? The cart?
- **Page 13.** "The survey says straight. The bolts say left." No idea.
- **Page 16.** Three exits, a side channel, a service hatch, a conduit. I gave up tracking which was which. I only knew one closed.
- **Page 17, panel 3.** "Give them a route that exists." So Mera is helping? Why did she send him into a mine collapse then?

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 5.** The equation, the chip and the riddle lines from Grandmother had me reaching for my phone. B11 on page 6 is the only reason I turned the page.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Weak yes. The splash of them climbing with water rising looks cool. But **TOMAS — STATUS UNAVAILABLE** is meant to scare me about a guy I met three pages ago. That's a loud noise with a near-stranger standing in it. I'd keep going because of the water theft and B11, not the cliffhanger. I'd read the next issue, but not tonight if I had a choice.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 5; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19, panel 1.** "It was a route. The destination objected." That got a laugh.
- **Page 22, panel 4.** Lock's offer: sign and your debt clears tonight. After page 2 that's a real temptation. For a second I wanted him to take it.
- **Pages 27–28.** The machine fails at 11%, then Alex runs his thumb over the edges, fits the sleeve, and it jumps to 94%. That paid off page 1, where he feels machines with his hands. "It tells you what it can hold." This is the one sequence in the issue that felt like his story.
- **Page 33, panel 3.** "No." I knew it was coming, but it still landed because of the debt.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 20.** Parts 32, labor 20, margin 8, a creator bonus of 14, and a slogan on a screen. This felt like the econ worksheet that came with the book. I skimmed it.
- **Pages 23–26.** Four new people in four pages, running down corridors with patrol lights, a reroute with eleven seconds and a heat loop. I looked at pictures and stopped reading the balloons around page 25.
- **Page 31.** Shrinking the machine to fit a household. I got the point ("the one people can own") and skimmed the how.
- **Page 32.** Mara's shop video repeats the page 20 numbers. Skimmed. It felt like a commercial.
- **Page 35.** **CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION: VERIFIED / LICENSE: PUBLIC / NONEXCLUSIVE / SAFETY DOCUMENTATION: REQUIRED.** That's legal language. My eyes slid right off it.

### I was confused
- **Page 19.** "Mara's still shipping." Who is Mara? I'd never met her. I also thought she might be Mera from page 8. **Mara and Mera are nearly the same name**, and I mixed them up for half the issue.
- **Page 19.** Ada says Tomas is "the one we don't know," and then the issue never comes back to him. The cliffhanger from Issue 01 just goes away.
- **Page 23.** "You're late." Who arranged this meeting? How does Lina know Alex is coming? I felt like a scene was missing.
- **Page 23.** "This is contaminated feedwater. Not your Keel routing failure. / Two problems. / Two pipes." So Issue 02 is about a different problem than Issue 01? Keel's water isn't dirty, it's being stolen. Why are we building a purifier? I never connected the purifier back to the 18%.
- **Pages 24–26.** Are they stealing their own inventions back from the Houses? Is this a heist? Is it legal? Why are patrols chasing them? I didn't know the stakes of getting caught.
- **Page 24, panel 3.** "It belongs to whoever can name the work without hiding the failure." I don't know what the Seal is or does.
- **Page 30, panel 4.** "They chose visibility." Visibility of what? When did they choose it?
- **Page 32.** Mara's sign is "restored." When? How? Did something happen in Keel while we were gone?
- **Page 34, panel 4.** The Seal "warms" and the false entry "burns away." So the medallion is magic? Or tech? Either way it rewrote a government registry by being set down next to a machine. That's where I stopped believing the rules of this world.
- **Page 36.** Covenant band and **SUMMON**, both new. And why does Mera have the power to summon him?

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Clear: Alex, Ada, B11, TJ, and Lock with his stylus.
- **Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren: I could not tell them apart** except by job. Jalen is the eleven-seconds guy. Ren cuts metal. Lina has the membrane. Suri I couldn't have told you anything about. None of them has a want of their own. They were a team, not people.
- **Mara and Mera: mixed up.** Half the issue, I wasn't sure which one was in the scene.

### I felt something
- p22, the temptation of the debt clearing.
- p28, the sleeve fix. That's the only time this issue felt like Alex's story.
- p20, panel 2, the worker whose hand stays on the part after the machine takes it. That got me a little, quietly.

### I predicted
- That he'd refuse Lock's deal. Called it on page 22.
- That he'd say all four names out loud at the end. Called it.
- That B11's "efficient" route would leave people behind and Alex would correct it. The setup was obvious.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now I think it's about who gets credit for inventions, which is a different book from the stolen-water one I started.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 24.** It's the third new name in two pages, the patrol lights, and the seal that "belongs to whoever can name the work." That's where I'd have quit in a shop. Page 20 had already made me suspicious that this was a lesson dressed up as a comic.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. Mera presses SUMMON and says something wise. I didn't understand what she wanted, I don't know what a "legacy node" is, and nobody I care about is in danger. Tomas, who I was supposed to be worried about, hasn't come up in seventeen pages. I'd only open Issue 03 because it's assigned.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: no

## Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
- **Pages 39–40.** Ada and Leona. "Did you know where I was?" "Within a year." "And you didn't come." That's the best exchange in the book. It's the first time anyone talks like a person who is hurt. "Don't call that care" is a good line, and a teenager says it to a parent. I believed it.
- **Page 43, panels 3–4.** "First people stopped selling to him. Then people stopped standing near him." That made the dad's debt feel like something done to him, not just something he did. It chilled me.
- **Page 46.** "He was still a gambler." "Yes." "He built something worth finding." "Also yes." It's corny but I liked it. B11 earns it.
- **Pages 51–52.** Tomas with the gauge: "Paper says sixty-two." "Pipe says eighteen." Short, clear, and it brought back the one thing from Issue 01 I cared about.
- **Page 50.** The debt is still there, the workshop is sealed, and refusing costs him. I respected that it didn't just reward him.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37.** A review room, two new people (Nadi and Olani), an allocation request and "declared interest." It opens the final issue like a city council meeting. I skimmed the whole page.
- **Pages 41–42.** The Covenant bands, two of three, emergency authorization, bands linking into "a partial circle." I read the words without understanding them.
- **Page 47.** VERIFY, VERIFY, the bands "hold the records." Skimmed.
- **Page 53.** More council meeting.

### I was confused
- **Page 37.** Tomas is just... fine? On a video feed holding a gauge? The Issue 01 cliffhanger is answered by him being in the background of a meeting. I felt cheated.
- **Page 38.** Ada's last name is Veyra and she's from Oasis? That's new, and so is the fact that she ran away. Is Leona her mom? I assumed so, but the book never says "mother." It says "one person who made it hard to leave."
- **Page 40.** "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." Traded what? What terrible days? I never found out what Leona actually did or why Ada left.
- **Page 40, panel 4.** She makes a promise and the band warms. **TING.** These bands are magic too?
- **Page 41.** "During the Water Wars, three people held the Covenant." This is page 41 of 54, and it's the first time anyone tells me what the bands are and that there was a war. It's one line and it explains half the book. It came too late for me to care.
- **Page 41.** Mera carries a "pearl-gray band" in panel 2, then puts down her "dark band" in panel 4. Are those different bands? It was dark on page 36 too. I got lost on whose band was whose.
- **Page 42.** "Two can expose an obligation. Two cannot restore the old permission." No idea.
- **Page 44.** "I gave it authority to move faster than I could stop." "It" is Alpha, I think? Alpha came up once on page 8 and once on page 14. This seems to be the villain and I never saw it.
- **Pages 44–45.** Why was Adrian in the monitoring station? Did he die? "The image cuts before evacuation or death is shown." I get not showing it, but I don't know what I'm supposed to believe.
- **Page 48.** "Tonight you could correct all three." All three what? Then Ada says "Don't give me that much power" and Alex says he didn't offer it to her. That's three lines that each seemed to answer a different conversation.
- **Page 49.** "Convenor only." I had to guess what that word means.
- **Page 51.** Alex sent the true numbers to every screen in Keel on page 16. Why does the public board still say 62% here, as if nobody knows? Did the send fail? That page-16 moment now feels like it didn't count.
- **Page 54, panel 3.** Grandmother "sets the worn cloth down instead of refolding it." What cloth? I've never seen a cloth.
- **Page 54.** Halyard and the travel token are back at the end, and I still don't know why he wanted to go.

### I predicted

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
It's about refusing to take power even when it would fix things, and letting people argue it out in public instead. That's a third book from the one I started (stolen water) and the second one (credit for inventions).

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 42.** The bands link into a partial circle and Mera says "Two can expose an obligation." If I'd reached Issue 03 on my own, this is where I'd quit. Page 37 nearly did it first.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's a quiet ending: "I'm thinking." "Good." The debt went from 418,607 to 402,110, the medicine is paid off, and he's holding the token. I respected that it didn't pretend everything was fixed, and the debt number got a reaction out of me. But I wouldn't buy a next book. The only open question is whether they'll leave Keel, and I never knew why they wanted to in the first place.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 42; next issue: no

## The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 42.** The bands, the partial circle, "two can expose an obligation." The few who made it that far would close the book there. Honestly, most would already be gone by **page 20**, the price-breakdown page with a corporate slogan at the bottom. That's the moment the book stops feeling like a story and starts feeling like the lesson plan it came with. And the matching, clipped, fortune-cookie dialogue across every character is what would make them say "AI wrote this" out loud.

## What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid gets stuck with his dead-ish dad's huge debt, finds out a computer is stealing his town's water, and then it turns into a book about patents and council meetings. The robot and the magnet dog are good, and there's one really good scene with a girl and her mom."

## One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A strong hook (the birthday debt, the robot, the stolen-water numbers) buried under riddles, and I never learned what Alex was actually trying to do.
- **Issue 02:** Four strangers I couldn't tell apart, an econ lesson, and a magic stamp. The one good moment is Alex's hands fixing the machine.
- **Issue 03:** One real scene between Ada and her mother and one great line from Tomas, surrounded by a government meeting about bands and a war I was told about on page 41.
