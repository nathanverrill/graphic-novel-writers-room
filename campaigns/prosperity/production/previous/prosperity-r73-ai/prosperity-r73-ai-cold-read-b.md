# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, pages only, the night before. One pass, issue by issue. I did not want to be here.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
Page 1 got me against my will. "That isn't the approved repair." / "It's holding." / "Then don't approve it." Three lines and I know the kid and the world. Page 2 is the punch: **AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN**, **DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS**, **EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**, "Happy birthday, Phantum." I did the math — 418,607 at 40 a day is 28.7 years, and the screen says 28.7 years. The book checked its own homework. I noticed and it bought trust.

Page 3's pharmacy scene is the best kind of scene: 30 for the medicine, 42 for the token, he has 52. "Which buys one. Not both." I understood the stakes instantly, no explaining.

Page 10: Alex steps over the yellow line to save Tomas and the wristband goes **WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR**. "That is today's pay." / "I know." That's the whole system in one beep. This is where the world clicked for me — every decent act costs money.

Page 15: **PHYSICAL FLOW: 18% / REPORTED FLOW: 62%**. There's the crime, in two numbers. Best moment in the issue.

Page 16: he presses SEND knowing it seals his own exit. A real choice with a real price. I turned the page fast.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 5–7 sagged. The workshop is a lot of pointing at things — the loop equation, the chip, the key, the recording — and everyone talks in hints. Page 13's "The survey says straight. / The bolts say left." I got it only because page 15 explained it later.

### I was confused
- Page 5: "The casino chip matches the loop, but no slot accepts it." Matches *how*? A chip matches an equation? I still can't picture this.
- Page 6: B11 offers "the route, or the question first?" Nobody ever asks what the question is. It never comes back. Dangling.
- Page 8: did Mera reassign Alex to Site 6, or did the system and she just watched? Her hand is "on the control node, not the screen," which I think is meant to answer that and doesn't.
- Page 12: Tomas — a random trapped miner — pulls Adrian's key from around his neck. How did Adrian know Tomas? How did Tomas know Alex would ever come? "He didn't ask enough" explains nothing. Big coincidence, no payoff.
- Page 9, panel 2: "His unused travel token remains in his pocket." Page 3 left that token on Inez's counter — "The token waits" — and page 4 he shows up empty-handed and behind on payments. I flipped back to check. When did he buy it?

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Control node** (page 1): white-and-amber, everywhere, watches things. Never physically described. I pictured a camera-screen box and went with it.
- **Alpha** (page 8): "REPORT TO ALPHA." Then "Alpha's data line" (page 14), "Alpha's node flashes cold white" (page 15). By the end of the whole book I still do not know what Alpha is. The system? An AI? The water company? The book talks about it like I know. I don't.
- **Mera** (page 8): appears with authority and no introduction. A hand and a mood.
- **Legacy node** (page 8): used like a known term. Fuzzy until issue 3.

### Lines I read twice
- "The casino chip matches the loop, but no slot accepts it." (p5) — gave up.
- "Trust the machine when it checks the pipe. Question me when I tell you why." (p7) — solved it eventually, but it's a riddle doing the work of a sentence.
- "The survey says straight. / The bolts say left." (p13)
- "Tied, then. Not square." (p4) — this one I liked.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Inez, Ada, Grandmother, B11, Tomas, TJ — all distinct. Foreman-7 gets a personality in two lines ("He's a tool." / "So is the gate."). Mera is nobody yet. The supervisor and guard are furniture, which is fine.

### I felt something
Page 2, the birthday debt. Page 10, the forfeited wage. Page 16, pressing SEND as the channel slams shut behind him. Page 4, Grandmother: "Your father was working. That doesn't mean he didn't hurt us." That line is doing more than the whole workshop sequence.

### I predicted
Dad is dead (looks right by page 45). The route leads to proof the water numbers are cooked (right, fast). Ada knows more than she says (right). Tomas might not make it (wrong, and see issue 3 for how quietly that got resolved).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid crushed by inherited debt finds proof his city's water numbers are a lie and chooses to publish it even though it costs him his escape.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 5. "The casino chip matches the loop" is the first riddle I couldn't even picture, and the workshop sequence is where the book starts talking to itself. Pages 1–2 had already sold me, so it's a soft one — but in a shop, that's the page I'd have hesitated on.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Not because of the splash — the climb was always going to succeed, so the rising water isn't the hook. The hook is panel 3, **TOMAS — STATUS UNAVAILABLE**, and wanting to see what Keel does now that the 62/18 numbers are public. Tomas is someone I know a little, so it's a real question, not a loud noise. But it's a medium hook, not a great one.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 5; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
Page 19: "That was not a route." / "It was a route. The destination objected." B11 is the best voice in the book. Page 21–22: Lock and the estate — 312 patents, 47 restricted, 19 disputed, "I can open the lock tonight." A real temptation, clearly a trap, and I wanted to see which. Lock turning the stylus, and Alex pushing it back on page 33 — small prop, paid off. Page 33: "No." He refuses 418,607 credits of debt clearance to keep four people's names on their work. Because page 2 made the debt hurt, this lands. Page 35: "They can see us now." / "Then make them look." Good closer.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 20–22 are where the book becomes a lesson — margins, charters, estates, propaganda screens. Clear, but I felt the worksheet underneath. Pages 23–28 are the worst stretch in the book so far: four new people diagnosing a machine in fragments. Page 27 is four panels of people saying pieces of sentences — "The living cycle never starts." / "The node sends it." / "The standards don't meet." / "They were never designed to." I skimmed here, then went back because I felt guilty. The idea underneath (the parts were designed to be incompatible, so the simple adapter on page 28 is the point) is actually sharp — but I had to assemble it myself, and a tired reader won't.

### I was confused
- Page 23: "Two problems." / "Two pipes." Took me two reads to get: Keel's failure was routing, Halyard's is contamination.
- Page 25: "Because the other corridor has Suri's power line." Still not sure why that makes opening *this* door the good option.
- Page 34: Alex places the Seal, says the words, and the false registry entry "burns away," replaced by **LINA VEY**. How? The Seal was introduced as a symbolic medallion ("belongs to whoever can name the work"). Now it edits House-controlled registries. This read as magic because the book needed it to work.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Mara** (page 19): "Mara's still shipping." Ada knows her; I don't. She shows up page 32, fine, but the name-drop assumed recognition.
- **The Houses / charter** (pages 20–22): company? guild? government? I decided "corporations with charters" and moved on. Never nailed down.
- **The four collaborators** (page 22): four names on one screen, each with a component. The book does attach a thing to each name, which is the only reason I kept them straight.
- **Maker's Seal** (page 24): powers grow as needed (see above).
- **Covenant** (page 36): "Mera's dark Covenant band." First mention, zero explanation, issue ends.

### Lines I read twice
- "The edge is lying." / "Metal doesn't lie." / "It tells you what it can hold." (p28)
- "This way is thin." / "Thin still carries." (p30)
- "I counted the record and missed the people. What would you do?" (p29) — and B11 asking "what would you do?" out loud made me feel the quiz question hiding in the book. Small eye-roll.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lock: distinct, the stylus helps. Lina: distinct immediately ("That explains the smell."). Mara: distinct. Suri, Jalen, Ren: I can match each to a gimmick — Suri/living filter, Jalen/"eleven seconds," Ren/heat and edges — but not to a voice. If you quizzed me on which one said what, I'd fail. Also Jalen holding the reroute and Ren "making the heat look accidental" are the same self-sacrifice beat on consecutive pages, and it's the same beat Tomas already did in issue 1. Third time I've watched someone cover Alex's exit.

### I felt something
Page 20, panel 2: the worker's hand staying on the component after the kiosk takes it. Quiet, no dialogue, best panel in the issue. Page 24: Lina's jaw tight at **MEMBRANE ATTRIBUTION: CALDERA HOUSE**. Page 33: the "No."

### I predicted
He refuses Lock's deal (right). The debt gets wiped by the end of the book anyway (wrong — and I want to report being wrong, because the book surprised me by keeping the debt; I respect it). One of the four collaborators betrays them (wrong — this book doesn't do betrayal; everyone is decent, which is starting to feel like a law of the world).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The same kid refuses to sell his father's collaborators to erase his debt, and instead makes their work impossible to steal.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 27. Four people I just met, talking in fragments about why a machine fails, and the fix two pages later is instant. If I weren't assigned, that's where the book goes back on the shelf.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but it's the softest hook of the three. A summons to Oasis is a "go to the next location" hook, not a question I need answered. What actually pulls me is the Covenant band tease and Mera — two issues of her being portentous, and I want the book to finally say what she is.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 27; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
Page 38: the gate knows Ada. **VEYRA, ADA — RESIDENT RECORD: UNRESOLVED.** First time the book surprised me about a character — Ada was "girl with map" for two issues, and now she has a history. "You can wait." Good. Pages 39–40: the Leona scene is the emotional best thing in the book. "Did you know where I was?" / "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." Restrained, and it hurts. Page 46: "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Alex gets to hold both things about his father, and the book earned it. Page 50: **EXISTING DEBT — UNCHANGED**. "So the refusal has a price." / "It should." The book doesn't cheat, and I noticed. Page 51: "Paper says sixty-two. / Pipe says eighteen." The central image, deployed at the climax, given to Tomas. Works. Page 54: "Neither moves for the other." I liked the last page of the book more than I expected to.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 37 opens the final issue with a city council meeting — a representative, a clerk, a declared-interest form. The homework feeling peaked right there. Pages 41–42 are the Covenant rules delivered as wall text. Page 47 is three artifacts on a table being explained. Pages 51–53 are a procedural. I read all of it, but I felt myself reading *for the test*, not because I couldn't stop.

### I was confused
- Page 37, panel 1: Tomas is alive, on a wall feed, holding a gauge. Issue 1 ended on **TOMAS — STATUS UNAVAILABLE** and the resolution is him standing in the background of a meeting with nobody reacting. The book shrugged off its own cliffhanger. I felt cheated out of a reunion I was owed.
- Page 40: Leona traded "a few terrible days" for "fifteen years." If Ada left Oasis fifteen years ago, she was a small child — but she talks about being chosen *for* like she was old enough to feel it. How old is Ada? The book never says, and the math made me stop.
- Page 44: "I gave it authority to move faster than I could stop." *It.* This is the closest the book ever comes to explaining Alpha, and it's a pronoun. Mera's whole confession — what she built, what she tried to stop, whether she tried to save Adrian — is three vague lines.
- Page 48: "You offered it to us. That's still two people deciding for everyone else." He didn't offer anything; he was reaching for a lever. I get what she means after a second read, but the logic wobbles.
- Page 54: "the worn cloth" Grandmother sets down. What cloth? It appears from nowhere to carry a symbolic beat, so the beat doesn't land. Also the Inez money wobbles again: he says "Five goes where? Inez," and the ledger already shows the advance **PAID**, and then he "makes another payment." I couldn't tell if this was the last payment or a continuing one.
- The big one: does Keel ever get its water back? The bypass from page 15 is exposed, Nadi's *new* diversion is paused — but no page shows the old theft corrected. The pipe still says eighteen when the book closes. I kept waiting for the restored-flow beat and it never came. The book ends before the ending, on purpose, and I felt the gap.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The Water Wars** (page 41): first and only mention, 41 pages in, as backstory for the Covenant. Never described. The founding event of this world is a name.
- **The Covenant** (pages 41–42): actually explained — three holders, two-of-three, no expiration — but explained as forms on a wall.
- **Adrian as one of the three** (pages 41–45): I assembled it across five pages. Mera, Leona, Adrian. It clicks, but I did the clicking.
- **Alpha**: still nothing. Finished the book not knowing what the antagonist system is.

### Lines I read twice
- "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." (p40) — dense, but the good kind; a confession, not a riddle.
- "You offered it to us." (p48)
- "Convening isn't owning." (p49) — the thesis as a bumper sticker. By this point every character speaks in epigrams at the big moments, and the sameness of register flattened the climax for me. Real people do not talk like this, and in this book everyone does.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona: distinct in one scene, which is impressive. Tomas: gets the best line in the issue. Nadi: he *is* his conflict of interest, nothing more. Olani: a stamp. Mera: after three issues I still couldn't tell you what she wants. She tracks Alex, maybe-reassigns him, saves him, summons him, offers him the lever, then renounces her own power. Was the lever a test? The book never says. She reads as a plot device in a coat.

### I felt something
Pages 39–40, Ada and Leona — the one scene that got past my guard completely. Page 46, the gambler exchange. Page 50, the refusal having a price. Page 54, the overlook.

### I predicted
Alex refuses the lever (right — the book signaled its thesis loudly by page 47, so the climax was visible from a distance; the refusal felt respectable rather than thrilling). Mera was testing him (unresolved). The water gets restored in the finale (wrong — paused, not fixed, and the book calls that "Good").

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Offered the chance to become the new unaccountable authority, the kid refuses and forces the system to argue in public instead — and the argument, still running, is the ending.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 41. The whole mystery — the Water Wars, the Covenant, what happened to the dad — pays off as a record panel reading **TWO OF THREE — EMERGENCY AUTHORIZATION / EXPIRATION: NONE**. The moment the book should be most thrilling is the moment it most feels like a worksheet.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No — and it's an honest no, not an angry one. The overlook scene is a real ending and I don't regret the book. But Tomas is fine, the truth is out, the debt is accepted, and the one open material question — does Keel get its water — is one the book has told me not to worry about because "the argument is still running" and that's "Good." The book closed its own loop and framed the open part as settled. I don't know what a next book would be about, so I wouldn't buy it.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 41; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 41. The class has been promised answers about the dad, the machine, and the war since page 5, and the answers arrive as a form on a wall. That's the page where thirty kids simultaneously feel the homework-ness of the book, at the exact moment it needed to feel like a story. Runner-up: page 27, four strangers diagnosing a machine in fragments.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a city where water is metered and debt is inherited finds out his missing dad hid proof the city is being robbed — the paper says 62%, the pipe says 18%. He spends three issues refusing every shortcut: the bribe that would clear his debt, the safe route that would leave people behind, and finally the chance to run the whole system himself. It ends with a public meeting, on purpose. First issue is genuinely good. Last third is a civics class with a funny robot dog."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: the birthday checkpoint and two numbers that prove a whole city is being lied to.
- Issue 02: four people insisting their names stay on their work, and a kid saying no to 418,607 credits.
- Issue 03: the hero refuses the lever, and his reward is a meeting that is still running when the book ends.
