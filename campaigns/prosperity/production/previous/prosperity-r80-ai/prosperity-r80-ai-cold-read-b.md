# Second Cold Reader Report

## Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
Page 2. The wristband scanning his debt—418,607 credits inherited from his dad, and he only earned 40 today. That is a massive, crushing problem that I instantly understood. It made me want to see how he gets out of it. 

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 14 and 15. The moment they find the "Measure" and start plugging arms into pipes and talking about "physical flow" versus "reported flow" and "traces." It felt like reading a manual for a plumbing inspection.

### I was confused
Page 8. Mera in the Oasis control room. Who is she? Why is she tracking this "Legacy Node"? And why does she reassign Alex to Site 6? I couldn't tell if she was trying to help him or trap him.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel
- Travel Status / Clearance
- Halyard
- Oasis
- What a "node" actually is (it seems to be a camera, a computer, and a stamp all at once?)

### Lines I read twice
"The record says one thing. The pipe may say another." (Page 7)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex and Ada are clear. B11 (the robot) and TJ (the robot dog) are cool and distinct. Tomas is just "some guy in a mine." Foreman-7 is a generic robot cop.

### I felt something
Page 10, when Alex steps over the line to save Tomas and instantly loses his 40 credits for the day. That stung, especially after seeing how much he owes and how he couldn't even pay for his grandma's medicine.

### I predicted
Alex is going to find a secret stash of water his dad hid, sell it, and pay off his massive debt.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid trying to survive a crushing inherited debt by following a treasure map left by his dead dad.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 14. The pacing dies as soon as they start doing diagnostic tests on the water pipes.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. They are climbing up a pipe while a flood of water rushes up behind them. It's a real cliffhanger.

`VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 14; next issue: yes`

***

## Issue 02

### Pulled me forward
Page 26, when Ren rips the overheating module out and says "Take the part. I take the heat." It was the only moment of actual tension in this whole middle section.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Basically from Page 21 to Page 35. The entire issue is about intellectual property, patent disputes, "collaborator ledgers," and public licensing. I am reading this for homework, and suddenly the characters are doing homework too. I skimmed heavily through the maker room scenes.

### I was confused
How did they get from drowning in a pipe to a rich city (Halyard) so easily? Also, what exactly are Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren building? They just stand around talking about membranes, biofilters, and heat loops. I didn't understand the machine they were making until Page 31 when it was suddenly a "household purifier."

### Things I was expected to already know
- Caldera House (and Houses in general)
- Intellectual Estate rules
- Maker's Seal
- Covenant band

### Lines I read twice
"Not until someone carries the risk." (Page 21)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I completely lost track of Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren. They are introduced all at once, they all wear uniforms, and they just exist to shout tech jargon at each other. They blurred into one collective "mechanic" character. Lock is just a generic evil corporate guy in a suit.

### I felt something
Annoyance. We ended Issue 1 running from a flood, and Issue 2 is entirely about contract negotiation and copyright law. 

### I predicted
Alex would sign the contract with Lock to clear his 400k debt, but then feel guilty about it later. (I was wrong, he just refused it).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid fighting a corporation over who owns the copyright to a water filter.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 22. The moment Lock says "Assign the disputed estate to House administration." My eyes glazed over.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The cliffhanger is literally just a text message summoning him to a meeting. 

`VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 22; next issue: no`

***

## Issue 03

### Pulled me forward
Page 45. The flashback of Adrian (the dad) locking himself in the room to drown so the recording could survive. Finally, some actual stakes and emotion.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 37, 42, 51, 52, 53. Any time we are in the "Oasis review room" with Nadi and Olani stamping paperwork and talking about "temporary cross-city diversions." It is literally a committee meeting about plumbing.

### I was confused
The "Covenant bands" on Pages 40-42. Are they magic? Are they USB drives? Why do Leona, Mera, and Adrian have them? They put them on a table to unlock an "Emergency Authorization," but I have no idea what that authorization actually does or why Mera feels so guilty about it. 

### Things I was expected to already know
- Covenant band (still don't really get it)
- Civic Administration
- Emergency Authorization

### Lines I read twice
"I saw days I could prevent. I didn't see the years I was making." (Page 40)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona and Mera completely blended together for me. They are both older women in charge who feel guilty about a decision they made years ago, and they both have these weird bracelets. Nadi and Olani are just talking heads at a desk.

### I felt something
Massive disappointment on Page 54. Fourteen months later and Alex is still just working a normal job, and his debt only went down from 418k to 402k? What was the point of the whole adventure? 

### I predicted
Alex would use the legacy node to magically erase his debt and fix the water supply for everyone. 

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A bunch of people having a committee meeting about water distribution while a kid decides he doesn't want to be in charge.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 41. Three people putting bracelets on a table to unlock a voting system. I would have closed the book and checked my phone.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The story just fizzles out. He's still in debt, Ada leaves, and nothing really changed for him personally. I wouldn't buy the next book.

`VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 41; next issue: no`

***

## End of Book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 22. The second Lock starts talking about "Intellectual Estate" and "Charter review," every high schooler reading this is going to zone out. We thought we were reading a sci-fi survival story, not a textbook on patent law.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
It starts out really cool with a kid who inherits a massive, impossible debt and finds a secret robot, but then it turns into a really boring story about copyright law and water committee meetings.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Cool setup, crushing debt, and a great escape sequence.
- **Issue 02:** A really boring side quest about who owns the patent to a water filter.
- **Issue 03:** A boardroom meeting where nobody actually fixes the main problem and the kid stays in debt.
