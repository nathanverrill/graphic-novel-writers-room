# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a rethink.** The approved 54-page structure is present, but the current script still violates the opening requirement, the supplied Page 46 execution is unrelated to its script, and Issue 01’s route geography undermines its cliffhanger. The strongest material—Alex’s physical evidence ethic, the 62/18 discrepancy, TJ’s rescue, Lock’s offer, B11’s error, Ada and Leona’s confrontation, Adrian’s final choice, and Tomas’s “pipe says eighteen”—should be preserved while the book receives a causal and production pass.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Page 1 does not begin with Alex at work

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 1; `brief.md`, Non-negotiables.
- **Observation:** Page 1 is a full-page establishing image of Keel, Halyard and Oasis. Alex first appears on Page 2. The page also retains the discarded captions about humanity reaching the stars and the gravity of inequality.
- **Problem:** This contradicts the fixed opening requirement and makes the book begin with an abstract statement rather than a person under pressure.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Rebuild Page 1 around Alex’s pump repair. Use the pump hall to establish Keel, the node, patched infrastructure and the physical gap between records and reality. Remove the abstract theme captions. Do not expand the page count.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 2. Page 46 execution is the wrong scene

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 03 Page 46; `layouts.md`, Page 46; `thumbnails.md`, Page 46.
- **Observation:** The script requires Adrian’s flooded monitoring-station recording and B11’s deployment-state activation. The layout and thumbnail instead depict Leona and Mera in an apartment discussing association risk.
- **Problem:** Execution omits the approved Adrian reveal and supplies unrelated dialogue. This is a direct script-to-page contradiction.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Discard the current Page 46 layout and thumbnail. Rebuild the flooded monitoring-station sequence in no more than four panels, including Adrian activating B11’s recording/deployment state and the cut before evacuation. Replace the apartment lettering.
- **Role:** Layout, Letterer.

#### 3. Pages 16–18 do not establish readable route causality

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Pages 16–18.
- **Observation:** Page 16 frames Alex’s choice as either taking a private side passage or transmitting the evidence and returning through Tomas’s door. Page 17 then introduces the miners’ residential route, Mera’s separate conduit valve, Ada’s arrival, and the ascent route. Page 18 floods the conduit without making the direction or purpose of the water clear.
- **Problem:** The reader cannot tell which route each character uses, why Ada is present, or whether the water is helping or threatening the group. The stated cost of Alex’s public transmission appears to vanish.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Show three distinct routes in order: the sealed private side passage; the miners’ residential-service route, through which Ada reaches the group; and Mera’s separate conduit-segment valve, which creates the viable ascent route. Show the water entering from the conduit segment rather than the residential regulator. Keep Tomas at Site 6 and make his signal loss a communications uncertainty, not evidence that he entered the conduit.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

### Major

#### 4. Tomas’s cliffhanger is dropped in Issue 02

- **Location:** Issue 01 Page 18; Issue 02 Pages 19–36; Issue 03 Pages 52–53.
- **Observation:** Issue 01 ends with Tomas’s signal lost. Issue 02 never asks whether he survived. Issue 03 simply reveals him alive at the review.
- **Problem:** The book promises a person-centered question, then behaves as though the question does not matter.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Add one brief Issue 02 acknowledgment from Alex or Ada that Tomas’s status remains unknown. Seed a physical or administrative hint that he may have survived before Page 52, then let Page 52 reveal him using the gauges.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 5. Remove the unsupported Tomas status term

- **Location:** Issue 01 Page 18, Panel 3, and any lettering generated from it.
- **Observation:** The status is rendered as `TOMAS PHANTUM-LINK — SIGNAL LOST`.
- **Problem:** Canon establishes no Phantum link between Tomas and Alex. The term implies a family or technical relationship that does not exist.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Use `TOMAS SITE 6 SIGNAL — LOST` or `TOMAS — SIGNAL LOST`.
- **Role:** Writer, Letterer.

#### 6. Mera’s Issue 02 decision arrives before she is legible

- **Location:** Issue 01 Page 8; Issue 02 Page 36.
- **Observation:** Mera is initially a back, hand and interface. Page 36 ends on her choosing `SUMMON`.
- **Problem:** The reader does not yet know that she is a senior Oasis administrator, former Covenant custodian or limited human authority-holder. The cliffhanger is therefore a menu selection rather than a character decision.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Identify Mera’s role and scope before Page 36 through action, not explanation. At Page 36, show that she summons Alex because the Seal event exceeds her ability to suppress while escalation remains unavailable. Preserve the deeper Ada/Leona history for Issue 03.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 7. Alpha’s governing premise is delayed too long

- **Location:** Issue 01 Pages 8–17; Issue 02 Page 36; Issue 03 Pages 41–52.
- **Observation:** Alpha is named and active early, but the Water War, Covenant custodians, two-of-three authorization and lack of expiration/revocation are not concretely established until Page 41.
- **Problem:** The reader must guess what Alpha is and why Mera matters for most of the book. The restoration sequence then explains the premise instead of paying it off.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Seed one usable fact per issue. In Issue 01, attach a visible standing emergency order to Alpha. In Issue 02, show the three-custodian structure or an unreturned authorization in a registry trace. Keep the fuller human explanation on Pages 41–42.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 8. Issue 02 repeats the same collaborator encounter

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 23–27.
- **Observation:** Each collaborator introduces a component, explains a constraint, hands it over and is separated by an access obstacle.
- **Problem:** The sequence becomes a predictable fetch quest, and the collaborators are remembered as technical functions rather than people.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Combine at least two encounters into causal action. Suri’s biofilter extraction should depend on Jalen’s monitored power reroute; Ren’s component should create the next escape or stabilization problem. Each collaborator must make a choice that changes the following beat.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer.

#### 9. The contaminated feeder displaces the 62/18 thread

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 19–33.
- **Observation:** The story moves from the routing discrepancy to contamination, then concentrates on purifier construction and ownership.
- **Problem:** The purifier can read as a replacement plot, while the original water crisis disappears.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Keep the problems distinct but visibly related: routing determines where water goes; contamination determines whether that water is usable. Return the 62/18 consequence during the prototype or Lock sequence so the purifier remains a bounded response rather than the new central crisis.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 10. The 94% and 91% results need a visible distinction

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 28 and 32–33.
- **Observation:** The industrial configuration reaches 94% simulated efficiency; the household configuration later reaches 91%.
- **Problem:** The lower number reads as unexplained failure or inconsistency.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Label the first result `INDUSTRIAL CONFIGURATION — SIMULATED RECOVERY: 94%` and the second `HOUSEHOLD CONFIGURATION — SIMULATED RECOVERY: 91%`. Show that 91% is the deliberate cost of shrinking the system while preserving maintenance and feedwater requirements.
- **Role:** Writer, Letterer.

#### 11. The Maker’s Seal’s function remains unclear

- **Location:** Issue 02 Pages 24, 34–35; Issue 03 Page 48.
- **Observation:** Lina gives Alex the physical Seal after the collaborators confirm the build. It warms and registers attribution.
- **Problem:** The reader cannot tell what changed or why the object matters. It risks reading as a chosen-one trigger rather than a safeguard tied to contribution and responsibility.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Make the collaborators’ confirmation visibly update the working purifier and public contributor record. The Seal should register attribution and maintenance responsibility, not ownership, sovereignty or automatic authority.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 12. Adrian’s Covenant band appears without setup

- **Location:** Issue 03 Page 41.
- **Observation:** B11 reveals Adrian’s band from his chest compartment, although earlier pages establish only the Measure in that compartment.
- **Problem:** The third safeguard appears assembled by plot convenience.

- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 13. Mera’s Page 40 wording creates an unintended second mother

#### 14. The restoration climax is too screen-led

- **Location:** Issue 03 Pages 47–53, especially Pages 50–51.
- **Observation:** The climax relies on `CONVENOR / AUTHENTICATOR`, `DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED`, `EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED` and caretaker-function lists.

Make the climax a chain of visible actions: Leona supports the proposal publicly; participants publish interests and evidence; Mera loses override access; the House representative submits the false allocation; Tomas and Keel residents stop approval.

## Plausibility ledger

- **Format:** The book is exactly 54 pages in three 18-page issues. Any chapter headings, chapter end cards or inherited 15-page structures are superseded.
- **Opening:** Page 1 must begin with Alex at work. The abstract inequality captions are explicitly discarded.
- **Alpha:** Alpha is a distributed network operating through nodes, records and delegated interfaces. It has no face, body or consciousness. Mera’s actions remain within human scope; independent reports continue.
- **Tomas:** Tomas survives Site 6. No Phantum link or family link exists. His signal may disappear, but the status must not invent a relationship.
- **Ada’s route:** Ada needs a visible physical route into the old works. The residential regulator, Mera’s conduit valve and the sealed private passage are separate systems.
- **Measure:** The Measure senses, compares, traces and verifies evidence. It does not move water, grant authority or choose an outcome.
- **Maker’s Seal:** One physical Seal exists. Projections and diagrams must not read as a second object. It registers contribution and responsibility; it does not grant sovereignty.
- **Covenant:** Adrian’s band is carried by B11; Leona’s is in the stone-grey box; Mera’s remains dark. Linking Adrian’s and Leona’s bands does not restore the old emergency authority.
- **B11:** Adrian activates a recording/deployment state in the flooded station. Alex later activates the dormant workshop body. No physical handoff or evacuation is shown.
- **Purifier:** 94% and 91% are different simulated configurations. The household purifier remains feedwater-dependent, requires cleaning and paid replacement parts, and does not end scarcity.
- **Houses:** Only Caldera House, Vector House and Orbital House are canon. Aqualis, Helion, Astra and “five Houses” are inherited-draft conflicts.
- **Morrow & Sons:** The shop remains in Keel. A Halyard page may show its product or service record, not a second location.
- **Debt:** Alex’s balance remains after restoration. The transition from 418,607 to 402,110 must be supported by visible time, service income and payments.
- **Alex’s mother:** No information may be added beyond her leaving Adrian after he lost everything.
- **Public review:** The process can pause or challenge an allocation. It does not instantly equalize water, erase debt or transfer ownership.
- **Hard-SF bounds:** The restoration node and Distributed Verification Protocol act through evidence, attribution and reciprocal agreement. No safeguard performs another safeguard’s job.
