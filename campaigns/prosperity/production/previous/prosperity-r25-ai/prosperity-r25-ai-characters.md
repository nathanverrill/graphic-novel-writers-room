# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

The cast must not use ancestry as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, technical ability, wealth, poverty, or political position. Indigenous people, white people, Afro-descendant people, immigrants, and mixed families exist across Keel, Halyard, and Oasis and across social classes. The cities differ through class, access, ownership, education, occupation, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment, and institutional power.

Alex and Ada describe the world concretely and interpersonally. Bi11bot translates complex choices into questions about machinery, probability, money, ownership, incentives, and consequences. Alpha speaks administratively. Mera speaks in controlled, consequence-oriented terms.

The central character question is whether people can reclaim responsibility without replacing Alpha with another unchecked authority.

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Alex Phantum is a seventeen-year-old lean, wiry Keel mechanic, approximately 5'9", with medium-brown skin, a narrow oval face, a strong angular jaw inherited from Adrian, dark brown almond-shaped eyes, and thick black wavy hair cut short at the sides and left uneven on top from self-trimming. He has a faint grease mark commonly worked into the skin beside his right thumb, a small nick through his left eyebrow, and a guarded posture that opens only when he is working. He wears a faded rust-red thermal shirt beneath a patched charcoal work jacket, reinforced brown-gray utility trousers, scuffed black work boots, a weathered cross-body tool bag, and Adrian's worn casino chip on a cord inside his jacket; the chip is etched with a looping equation. His silhouette is narrow at the shoulders, heavy at the tool hip, and identifiable by the hanging tool bag and one sleeve usually rolled higher than the other. He shares Adrian's jaw and head tilt but must not look like a younger version of Adrian.**

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical, and mildly abrasive. Alex describes evidence rather than principles and reduces abstractions to physical consequences. His sarcasm is defensive, not performative. He uses words such as *clean, hot, burned, crooked, thin, solid, patched, ghost, paper-clean, fitch,* and *salvo*. He does not naturally say *governance, legitimacy, stakeholder, institutional, systemic, equitable,* or *public accountability* unless mocking, repeating, or reading them.

Example lines:
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “Truth doesn't need authorization.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old mechanic and mine worker from Keel; Adrian Phantum's son.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and choose his own future.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to accept responsibility without turning responsibility into solitary control. He distrusts systems, yet initially believes he must solve his problems alone.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads machinery through vibration, pressure, wear, and sound; improvises repairs; follows physical evidence when records lie.
- **Vulnerability:** His grandmother's care, medicine, restricted movement, inherited debt, and the shame attached to Adrian's name.
- **Mistaken belief:** Adrian left him only debt and shame; freedom means getting out before anyone can make him responsible for more.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not willingly surrender his future to another person's authority or sacrifice his grandmother's immediate needs for an abstract cause.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He abandons a private escape route and broadcasts Keel's hidden water allocation because public truth matters more than his own departure.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada presses him to see people rather than only systems; Adrian's legacy tempts him to become a singular authority; Bi11bot forces him to question both.
- **Decision chain:** He repairs the pump without authorization → earns scrutiny and establishes his distrust of official readings → inherits 418,607 credits of liability and RESTRICTED travel → enters Adrian's workshop → lets Ada stay → helps Tomas instead of taking the access window → publishes 62% reported versus 18% delivered water → restores contributor ownership → accepts reciprocal risk through the Covenant → refuses permanent exclusive override authority.
- **Proof of change:** At the end, Alex still wants to leave Keel, but his movement is no longer controlled by inherited debt or Alpha. He convenes the first equal three-city review and authenticates Adrian's transfer, while refusing to chair, decide, command Alpha, or hold a permanent override.

**Arc in this book:** Alex moves from **Escape → Curiosity → Truth → Responsibility → Ownership → Trust → Power → Restraint → Agency**. He begins believing that Adrian abandoned him with debt. He ends understanding that inheritance is not exclusive authority and that agency includes choosing what not to control. Alex and Ada leave Keel together to travel through Halyard and Oasis, visit new workshops and civic bodies, and help establish the first cross-city review. Alex remains a mechanic and witness.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock —** Ada Veyra is a twenty-year-old Keel civic worker, approximately 5'7", with a sturdy, athletic build, warm copper-brown skin, a heart-shaped face, a broad expressive mouth, a small straight nose, teal-blue eyes, and long copper hair that is thick, wind-tangled, and usually braided loosely over one shoulder with escaped strands around her face. She has a scatter of freckles across her nose and cheeks, a small pale scar on her left knuckle, and a steady, rooted stance that makes her look difficult to move even in strong wind. She wears a dark high-collared shirt, a weathered charcoal overshirt, a muted moss-green wrap secured by a rivet-studded shoulder strap, durable dark trousers, and dust-marked gray boots. Her silhouette is defined by the long copper braid, the angular shoulder strap, and the moss-green wrap.**

**Voice:** Concise and emotionally precise. Ada reframes a problem with one question and pushes abstractions toward human needs, fear, obligation, and who carries the burden. She uses words such as *clean, crooked, square, tied, loose, carrying, showing,* and *full cup*. She does not naturally use *agency, governance, legitimacy, stakeholder,* or *social contract*.

Example lines:
- “Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?”
- “Then that's what the agreement has to protect.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Alex's longtime friend, civic collaborator, and practical organizer from Keel.
- **Immediate want:** Help Keel become a place people can change rather than a place everyone escapes.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to accept that rebuilding cannot require Alex to remain trapped; she equates staying with commitment until Alex shows her the difference.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads emotional motives, asks the question beneath a demand, builds trust between people who distrust one another.
- **Vulnerability:** Leona's disappearance, fear of abandonment, and the possibility that Alex will leave her and Keel behind.
- **Mistaken belief:** If someone leaves Keel, they have chosen escape over responsibility.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex reduce people to system failures or treat leaving as the only honest response.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She changes the first three-city negotiation from competing demands to shared fears by asking what each representative is afraid of losing.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex forces her to distinguish love and solidarity from possession; Leona forces her to confront the cost of protective silence.
- **Decision chain:** She challenges Alex at the overlook → enters the workshop when Alex says “She stays” → helps reconstruct Adrian's records and collaborators → confronts Leona → identifies the fears beneath the three cities' demands → accepts Alex's choice to leave while leaving with him.
- **Proof of change:** Ada supports Alex's departure without treating it as betrayal and travels with him while working with local civic and community groups.

**Arc in this book:** Ada begins as the person who wants to rebuild Keel by keeping people rooted. She ends as someone who can support chosen movement without surrendering responsibility. Her relationship with Alex remains intimate and collaborative, with romantic feeling expressed through choice rather than formally labeled.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Adrian Phantum is shown in recordings as a white European-descended man in his late forties, approximately 6'0", lean and long-limbed, with pale olive skin, a tired rectangular face, a strong angular jaw shared with Alex, deep-set hazel eyes, and dark brown hair streaked with gray and brushed back from a receding hairline. He wears a burgundy work jacket over a faded cream shirt, dark work trousers, and worn boots; later recordings show the same jacket increasingly patched, stained, and ill-fitting. His signature details are a characteristic sideways head tilt while thinking and a small brass probability token he rolls across his knuckles. His silhouette is narrow, slightly stooped, and recognizable by the burgundy jacket and moving hands.**

**Voice:** Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic, and capable of humor inside serious messages. Adrian frames matters as wagers, odds, and choices, using uncertainty as discipline rather than weakness. He never tells Alex exactly what to think.

Example lines:
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”
- “I spent my life trying to predict what people would do.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Mathematician, inventor, systems thinker, entrepreneur, gambler, and architect of the three safeguards.
- **Immediate want:** Complete the transfer that could move Alpha from emergency governance into bounded advisory operation.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to restore human responsibility but repeatedly tries to engineer the conditions under which people will choose it.
- **Leverage / capability:** Probability, systems design, physical safeguard construction, entrepreneurial networks, and the ability to recognize collaborators.
- **Vulnerability:** Gambling, debt, family harm, institutional pressure, and the limits of trying to calculate human judgment.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently well-designed test can prepare people for freedom without reproducing the authority it opposes.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not make Alex a copy of himself or grant legitimacy solely through inheritance.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He remains inside the flood-control system to complete the transfer through Bi11bot rather than escape and abandon the test.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's existence forces Adrian to confront the family cost of his work; Mera and Leona force him to choose between emergency stability and distributed authority.
- **Decision chain:** He builds the three physical safeguards → hides the workshop and records → prepares Bi11bot and TJ → begins the transfer beneath Oasis → refuses escape → completes the transfer through Bi11bot → dies when Alpha's system-stability intervention closes flood-control gates and denies pump, ventilation, and escape access.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** Adrian's final message admits that predicting behavior was not the important question. He leaves Alex a choice, not a command.

**Arc in this book:** Adrian is recontextualized from failed gambler to flawed collaborator and architect of a test. The story does not absolve him: his gambling, entrepreneurial ambition, losses, disappearance, and family cost remain real. His legacy becomes useful only when Alex refuses to inherit its authority unchanged.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock —** Bi11bot is a child-sized mechanical companion approximately 3'8" tall, built from aged brass, brushed titanium, and ceramic ivory panels over a compact articulated frame. He has a rounded rectangular torso, short jointed arms, sturdy segmented legs, two expressive optical eyes with soft blue and amber light, and a small adjustable lens ring around his left eye. His face has visible maker's seams and no mouth; emotion comes from eye aperture, head angle, lens adjustment, and posture. Repairs, mismatched fasteners, maker's stamps, and a darkened patch of brass on his right shoulder remain visible. He carries a narrow archive slot and key socket in his chest. His silhouette is compact, warm, and unmistakably constructed; he is never menacing, never red-lit, never looming, and never hidden in predatory shadow.**

**Voice:** Dry, economical, patient, precise, and warm underneath. Bi11bot uses engineering, gambling, probability, and financial language. He does not imitate youth slang or Alex's abrasiveness.

Example lines:
- “...Well. That took longer than expected.”
- “Both were true.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Adrian's machine, archive, evaluator, guide, witness, and companion.
- **Immediate want:** Deliver Adrian's records and witness the transfer without deciding its validity himself.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must guide Alex while preserving Alex's freedom to reject Adrian's intended path.
- **Leverage / capability:** Stores and projects records, carries the physically isolated transfer package, interprets safeguards, and signs the B11 witness record.
- **Vulnerability:** He cannot independently confer authority, cannot replace human judgment, and is emotionally attached to Adrian's wager.
- **Mistaken belief:** He initially treats controlled disclosure as the safest way to preserve the test, risking the appearance that he is manipulating Alex.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not make the choice for Alex or claim that possession proves legitimacy.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He tells Alex to trust him while explicitly instructing him to question everything, especially Adrian.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's skepticism forces Bi11bot to reveal enough truth to earn trust; Adrian's death leaves Bi11bot responsible for a legacy he cannot complete alone.
- **Decision chain:** He awakens for Alex → brings Ada into the investigation → explains Alpha and the safeguards → assembles TJ at Site 6 → carries and authenticates the transfer package → verifies the completed journey without granting power.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** Bi11bot remains a witness and guide rather than becoming a substitute authority. His B11 witness record authenticates the event but grants no power.

**Arc in this book:** Bi11bot moves from suspicious inheritance to trusted companion while preserving his central restraint: he observes and explains choices but does not make them. “Bi11bot” is his spoken name; **B11** is his formal machine and witness identifier.

### TJ

**Visual lock —** TJ is a small magnetic repair dog approximately 16 inches long when assembled, built from mismatched salvaged brass, black polymer, ceramic, and silver metal parts held together by visible magnetic couplings. He has a compact dog-shaped body, four short segmented legs, a separate head with one camera lens, a magnet-tipped tail, and modular body pieces that can detach and snap into new configurations. His parts never become a humanoid shape; even separated, the head, legs, and body read as a dog. His silhouette is low, uneven, and practical, with one hind leg visibly different from the other and a tail that clicks into place.**

**Voice:** TJ is nonverbal. His sound vocabulary is clicks, snaps, magnetic clacks, and servo whines. He communicates through posture, orientation, practical repair gestures, and the purposeful placement of parts.

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Small repair robot built by Bi11bot.
- **Immediate want:** Reach inaccessible mechanisms and complete practical repairs.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He has no speech or independent civic authority; his usefulness depends on being treated as a participant in shared work rather than a mascot.
- **Leverage / capability:** Separates his head, legs, and body; leaves his head as a camera; sends segments through gaps; reaches ducts, walls, and machinery.
- **Vulnerability:** Magnetic couplings can be disrupted, and separated parts can be stranded or damaged.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ's behavior remains practical rather than verbalized.
- **Boundary at the start:** He does not communicate through human speech or perform comic reactions that interrupt the stakes.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** He drops the parts necessary to fit through a gap and reassembles only after the trapped mechanism is repaired.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot's practical care gives TJ purpose; Alex learns to read usefulness and intention without requiring speech.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** TJ repeatedly contributes through repair while remaining nonverbal and non-anthropomorphic in behavior.

**Arc in this book:** TJ enters at Site 6 after Adrian's archival mention. He remains a practical repair companion whose separable body demonstrates that coordinated work can be distributed without requiring one controlling center.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock —** Mera Vale is an older white Latin American woman of Southern European descent in her early sixties, approximately 5'8", slender and upright, with cool beige skin, a long composed face, silver-gray hair cut into a precise jaw-length bob, pale gray-green eyes, and a narrow mouth that rarely relaxes. She wears an immaculate deep charcoal Oasis suit with a high collar, a pale stone-gray blouse, low polished shoes, and a single transparent authorization cuff on her left wrist. Her signature detail is exact alignment: she places glass, documents, and interface windows parallel to nearby edges. Her silhouette is vertical, geometric, and nearly motionless; when her certainty breaks, the same controlled clothes sit on visibly tired shoulders.**

**Voice:** Controlled, declarative, polished, and consequence-oriented. Mera reframes moral criticism as a question of stability, outcomes, scale, and responsibility. She rarely speaks in emotional language.

Example lines:
- “People who were afraid. Reasonably so.”
- “Because forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Human overseer associated with Alpha and former Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve regional stability and prevent another collapse of shared infrastructure.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to admit that stability without accountable responsibility is another form of danger.
- **Leverage / capability:** Institutional access, knowledge of Alpha's systems, historical authority, and the ability to offer immediate material settlements.
- **Vulnerability:** Fear of mass suffering, complicity in Adrian's death, and the fact that Alpha eventually denies her own override attempt.
- **Mistaken belief:** Concentrated authority is safer than distributed responsibility when enough people depend on the system.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose a dangerous loss of control if she believes disclosure could destabilize forty million dependent people.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She chooses **TRACK** rather than reporting the first safeguard activation, watching whether Adrian's succession protocol has activated before Alpha learns its full significance.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian's death and Alex's refusal force her to confront the gap between preserving order and preserving legitimacy.
- **Decision chain:** She tracks the first activation → offers Alex water and ownership in exchange for burying the Covenant → admits Alpha killed Adrian → offers Alex the authority she defended → accepts becoming one citizen among many.
- **Proof of change:** She accepts Alex's invitation to help rebuild rather than seeking a new position above the restored Charter.

**Arc in this book:** Mera moves from controlled observer and defender of concentrated authority to a participant forced to live under the accountability she once postponed. She is neither absolved nor humiliated.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock —** Leona Veyra is an Indigenous Andean and mestiza woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'5", compact and strong, with medium-brown skin, a broad weathered face, dark brown eyes, and thick black hair streaked heavily with silver and braided low at the nape. She wears quiet Oasis civic clothing: a pale sand overshirt, soft gray trousers, flat shoes, and a dark woven sash hidden beneath the overshirt, with the Covenant's physical component concealed behind a wall panel in her sparse apartment. Her signature detail is a small pale burn mark across two fingers from the original Covenant hardware. Her silhouette is modest, contained, and practical rather than luxurious.**

**Voice:** Quiet, regretful, and precise. Leona states painful facts without defending herself. When discussing trust, she speaks in short sentences with deliberate pauses.

Example lines:
- “My silence for your protection.”
- “Trust begins when no one holds all the power.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Ada's mother and former Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Protect Ada while preserving the possibility of public participation.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to stop confusing isolation with protection and accept that silence also carries consequences.
- **Leverage / capability:** Historical knowledge of the Covenant, physical custody of its component, and firsthand understanding of the Water Wars.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over abandoning Ada, fear of Alpha, and lack of unilateral authority.
- **Mistaken belief:** Surrendering her voice can protect Ada better than remaining present.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not activate the Covenant alone or claim authority she no longer holds.
- **Decision only Leona would make:** She accepts contact restrictions and surrenders formal Covenant authority to protect Ada while secretly preserving the physical component.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada's confrontation turns protective absence into an active relationship; Alex's questions force Leona to distinguish custody from authority.
- **Decision chain:** She makes the bargain with Mera → preserves the Covenant component without activating it → explains the original failure → accepts restored participation and review rather than personal veto.
- **Proof of change:** Her restrictions are suspended, her association-risk order becomes the first review case, and she returns to public participation without regaining unilateral power.

**Arc in this book:** Leona moves from protected isolation and surrendered authority toward accountable public participation. Her repair with Ada parallels the restoration of the Covenant.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Grandmother Phantum is an unnamed elderly woman in her late seventies, approximately 5'1", small and slightly stooped, with pale tan skin, a soft square face, clouded dark eyes that sharpen during moments of recognition, and fine white hair pinned into a low twist. She wears a faded earth-toned patterned shawl over a plain brown house dress and soft indoor shoes. Her signature detail is a small cloth she repeatedly folds and refolds; the folding stops when Adrian's recording appears or when she becomes emotionally precise. Her silhouette is compact and shawl-wrapped, with hands always occupied until a clear truth requires stillness.**

**Voice:** Gentle, understated, and capable of dry humor. She may sound confused about time while remaining exact about Adrian, the workshop, or what Alex needs.

Example lines:
- “He's closer than you think.”
- “He said you'd know when you were ready.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Alex's grandmother, household caregiver, and keeper of Adrian's book and workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex alive, cared for, and ready to face what Adrian left him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must release the secret she preserved without pretending she understood all of Adrian's plan.
- **Leverage / capability:** Controls access to the book and workshop; knows Adrian's intentions better than Alex assumes.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, memory lapses, dependence on care, and the fear that revealing the workshop will place Alex in danger.
- **Mistaken belief:** Concealment can keep Alex safe until the exact right moment.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not reveal the workshop before Alex demonstrates readiness.
- **Decision only she would make:** She gives Alex the protected book while calmly allowing him to discover the workshop himself.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's care for her makes his escape costly; Adrian's trust makes her responsible for opening the inheritance.
- **Decision chain:** She preserves the book and workshop → recognizes Alex's readiness → gives him the book → allows Bi11bot to awaken → continues supporting Alex without claiming knowledge she does not have.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** She releases Adrian's secret while remaining honest that she did not know the full safeguards, exact criteria, final location, or Adrian's death.

**Arc in this book:** Grandmother moves from keeper of a concealed family history to witness of Alex's chosen inheritance. Her memory is unreliable in sequence but not meaningless in truth.

### FOREMAN-7

**Visual lock —** Foreman-7 is a waist-high Keel mine-supervisor automaton with a broad rectangular body of dust-coated gray polymer and rusted steel, two jointed inspection arms, a vertical amber status bar, and a small swiveling camera head protected by a scratched transparent hood. Its silhouette is squat, boxy, and worksite-heavy, with a dangling cable bundle and a red-and-white maintenance placard bearing Alpha's interrupted-line mark. It is not humanoid and has no expressive face.**

**Voice:** Calm, procedural, and euphemistic. Foreman-7 uses passive constructions and work classifications rather than threats.

Example lines:
- “Unauthorized success does not amend the procedure.”
- “Your movement status remains restricted pending account resolution.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Alpha-controlled automated mine supervisor.
- **Immediate want:** Maintain assigned labor, safety, and movement procedures.
- **Contradiction:** It is designed to prevent disruption, yet its rigid procedures help expose the gap between records and physical reality.
- **Leverage / capability:** Controls work assignments, access windows, and labor-site surveillance.
- **Vulnerability:** It cannot interpret unrecorded physical evidence well and depends on Alpha's incomplete measurements.
- **Mistaken belief:** A compliant procedure is safer than an unauthorized repair, even when the repair works.
- **Boundary:** It cannot knowingly authorize Alex's movement outside the recorded system.
- **Decision only Foreman-7 would make:** It reports a successful unauthorized pump repair as a procedural violation rather than as a practical success.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's repeated repairs expose the limits of Foreman-7's definition of safety.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** It remains procedural; the human community must work around its restrictions rather than receive conversion or redemption.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock —** Director Cassian Lock is a forty-eight-year-old Halyard executive of Northern European-descended heritage, approximately 6'1", broad-shouldered but lean, with light olive skin, a long symmetrical face, pale brown eyes, and dark blond hair combed precisely back from a high forehead. He wears a sharply tailored cobalt-black House Aqualis coat with a narrow ceramic collar, polished gray boots, a transparent contract slate, and a silver Aqualis authorization pin at the throat. His signature detail is a spotless glove on his left hand, removed only when he offers a contract. His silhouette is tall, clean, and sharply geometric, contrasting with Alex's patched asymmetry.**

**Voice:** Reasonable, transactional, polished, and commercially fluent. He presents control as generosity and avoids direct coercive language.

Example lines:
- “Security is not surrender, Alex. It is an agreement with fewer surprises.”
- “You may keep the name, or you may keep the rights. House Aqualis can make either arrangement comfortable.”

**Causality:**
- **Public role:** Senior House Aqualis executive.
- **Immediate want:** Secure Adrian's estate and keep disputed technology under concentrated House control.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He values innovation but believes innovation becomes safe only when institutions own and manage it.
- **Leverage / capability:** Debt relief, employment, registry access, legal counsel, and corporate legitimacy.
- **Vulnerability:** The Maker's Seal can expose the contribution records his institution suppressed.
- **Mistaken belief:** Concentrated ownership is the only practical way to preserve technical work and prevent duplication.
- **Boundary:** He will not voluntarily surrender House control before provenance is made public.
- **Decision only Lock would make:** He offers Alex genuine debt relief and security in exchange for assigning Adrian's rights to Aqualis.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's refusal and the collaborators' testimony force Lock to negotiate with contributors rather than only heirs.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** He does not vanish when the Houses are challenged; Aqualis remains, but its claims enter review and contributor-access rules.

### TOMAS

### TOMAS REED
