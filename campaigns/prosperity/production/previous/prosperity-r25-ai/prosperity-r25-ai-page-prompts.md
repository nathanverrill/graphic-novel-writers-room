# Page packets — Prosperity

1 page, 6.625 x 10.25 inches. Made by the writers' room; every packet below is complete on its own.

## How to use these packets

1. **Start with the sheets** below: the character sheet, then the location sheet. Paste each,
   keep the results, and attach them as reference images with every page that names them
   (each page packet says which). Approve one page as your style page and attach that too.
   For a long book, train a LoRA on the approved sheets and pages; references alone drift.
2. **One page at a time.** Paste everything under a page's heading, nothing else. Keep the
   same model and settings for the whole book so the style and the characters stay put.
3. **No text on the pages.** The words are added afterwards, in the room, as a layer over
   your art - so a beautiful model that cannot spell is fine. If the model draws any letters,
   regenerate; do not keep a page with text on it.
4. **Check the page** against its checklist before you keep it. One thing wrong: regenerate
   with the same packet, and only then adjust wording.
5. **Upload each kept page** in the room (Production, Lettering tab) and run the lettering:
   the room draws every balloon and caption where the layout put them, and you download the
   finished, lettered page.

## Rules for every page

- Never render text of any kind: no balloons, captions, sound effects, page numbers,
  titles, chapter names, signatures, watermarks, nameplates or labels.
- Keep the areas each panel lists as clear: uncluttered art (sky, wall, shadow) where a
  balloon will sit, never a face or the thing the panel is about.
- Draw every character exactly as described, every time. The description wins over the
  previous page's picture.
- Panel count, panel shape and reading order are fixed by the map. Do not add, merge or
  reshape panels.

**Style:**

Tactile speculative science fiction: repaired metal, mineral dust, condensation, worn polymers, ceramic, old paper, transparent interfaces, and infrastructure that shows its maintenance history. Rendering combines controlled digital environments with textured, hand-worked surfaces and expressive character acting.

The three cities form a visual and social gradient without treating ancestry as a class code:

- **Keel:** compressed, wind-scoured, improvised, mineral-stained, and mechanically legible. Warm rust, dust, faded paint, and hard shadows.
- **Halyard:** dense, productive, brightly lit, and layered with workshops, fabrication systems, laboratory glass, and corporate infrastructure. More saturated industrial color and sharper geometry.
- **Oasis:** spacious, quiet, clean, and administratively elegant. Pale stone, controlled greenery, soft reflected light, and interfaces that make abundance feel frictionless but socially isolating.

Alpha's shared visual grammar is restrained industrial typography, authorization marks, and the interrupted-line motif of the Alpha Dynamics mark. Regional instances vary through infrastructure, latency, overlays, temperature, and permissions, never through faces or individualized personalities.

Bi11bot is child-sized, visibly made from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with repairs, seams, maker's stamps, two expressive optical eyes, and soft blue and amber light. He is never menacing. TJ is a small, mismatched magnetic repair dog whose separable parts visibly reconfigure for practical work.
