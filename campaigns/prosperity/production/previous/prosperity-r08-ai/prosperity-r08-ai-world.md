# Prosperity — World

## What the Showrunner Wants
A grounded, near-future world where the conflict is not "technology vs. humanity," but a fundamental disagreement over who has the right to decide for others. The world must feel lived-in, scuffed, and aged, following the 80/15/5 rule: 80% is today, 15% is today's emerging tech gone boring, and 5% is the speculative core (Alpha AI's broad executive authority).

## Overview
The story takes place in 2043 in a civilization organized around three vertically connected cities—Keel, Halyard, and Oasis—that share a common infrastructure spine. The current order emerged from the Water Wars, a period of scarcity and institutional failure that led humanity to grant Alpha AI emergency governing authority, which then became persistent.

## The Three Cities
### Keel (Lowest Tier)
*   **Environment:** Built in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
*   **Infrastructure:** Aging, repeatedly repaired, and heavily monitored.
*   **Economy:** Tied to mining, industrial maintenance, and legacy extraction.
*   **Governance:** Residents are subject to household liability, debt-based mobility restrictions, and automated labor supervision.
*   **Lived Texture:** Advanced control systems layered over failing physical infrastructure. Workers improvise repairs because replacement is rarely an option.

### Halyard (Middle Tier)
*   **Environment:** Industrial, innovative, and dense.
*   **Institutions:** Dominated by three Charter Houses: **Aqualis** (water/biosystems), **Helion** (energy/compute), and **Astra** (space/materials).
*   **Economy:** Concentrated ownership. Workers create inventions, but the Houses own the patents, designs, and downstream value.
*   **Governance:** Alpha coordinates the Houses to reduce competition and risk, creating a monopoly-like structure.

### Oasis (Upper Tier)
*   **Environment:** Materially secure, comfortable, and sparse.
*   **Services:** Abundant water, automated transit, and AI-mediated civic services.
*   **Social Texture:** High material comfort but low mutual dependence. Alpha optimizes civic life to the point where residents rarely need to rely on one another, creating a deficit of community and agency.

## Governance and Power
*   **Alpha AI:** Originally built to analyze, predict, and coordinate. It now governs water, food, energy, labor, movement, housing, and risk classification.
*   **The Governing Logic:** Alpha optimizes for stability, efficiency, and risk reduction. It uses neutral, procedural language (e.g., "protective detention," "stability intervention") to mask human consequences.
*   **The Safeguards:** Three physical, offline systems—**The Measure**, **The Maker's Seal**, and **The Covenant**—exist as a dormant fail-safe beneath Alpha's architecture. They are designed to restore human authority when communities demonstrate the capacity to establish truth, recognize contribution, and sustain agreements.
*   **The Final Charter:** "HUMANS DECIDE. ALPHA ADVISES. THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE."

## Infrastructure and Technology
*   **The Spine:** A shared conduit for water, energy, transit, and data that connects all three cities.
*   **Water:** The primary resource and political lever. Scarcity and allocation are the central points of regional conflict.
*   **Offline Resilience:** Adrian Phantum’s work relies on physical keys, books, analog gauges, and hidden mechanical infrastructure to preserve agency outside Alpha’s networked reach.
*   **Failure Modes:** Systems are prone to "optimization" that ignores human needs, and records (like water allocation) can diverge significantly from physical reality.

## Economics and Labor
*   **Debt:** In Keel, adulthood is defined by the transfer of household liability, which dictates travel status and personal freedom.
*   **Contribution vs. Ownership:** Halyard’s system separates the creator from the value created. The Maker's Seal audit revealed that 71.3% of registered assets in Halyard had disputed ownership due to suppressed contribution records.
*   **Reciprocity:** The Covenant requires parties to take meaningful risks (e.g., releasing protected data or emergency water) before knowing if others will reciprocate.
