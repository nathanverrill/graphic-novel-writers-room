# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

The characters carry the story's argument that prediction is not legitimacy, efficiency is not the only public value, and no AI, institution, heir, or hero should possess unchecked authority over everyone else's future.

Alex's character arc moves through **Escape → Curiosity → Truth → Responsibility → Ownership → Trust → Power → Restraint → Agency**.

The central human tensions are:

- whether truth is worth the personal cost of revealing it;
- whether inheritance gives someone the right to own another person's work;
- whether people can accept reciprocal responsibility without surrendering authority;
- whether safety requires concentrated control;
- whether Alex can refuse to become the kind of authority he has spent the story opposing.

Alpha is not to be treated as inherently evil and humans are not treated as inherently wise. Alpha's capability, Mera's fear, Adrian's failures, and Alex's eventual restraint must all remain morally consequential.

## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Role:** Protagonist. Seventeen-year-old mechanic and mine worker from Keel.

**Age:** **17**

**Home:** Keel

**Look:** Lean and mechanically capable. His working clothes are worn mining/work clothes. He carries a weathered tool bag and keeps Adrian's worn casino chip in his pocket; the chip is etched with a looping equation. He shares a family resemblance with Adrian, including Adrian's habit of tilting his head while thinking.

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, and initially defensive. His sarcasm is protective rather than performative. He rarely gives speeches early in the story. As he changes, his language moves from personal defiance toward agency, legitimacy, and shared responsibility.

Representative lines:

- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “I've also got his debt.”
- “One day... I'm leaving.”
- “Truth doesn't need authorization.”
- “I just want it to be my choice.”

**Who they are:** Practical, skeptical, independent, and mechanically observant. Alex trusts physical evidence and the feel of machinery more than approved readings or official procedures. He begins convinced that Adrian abandoned him and left behind only debt, shame, and a public reputation as a gambler. He wants to leave Keel and choose his own future.

Alex's seventeenth birthday transfers **418,607 credits** of household liability to him, with a projected **28.7 years** to clearance and **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**. The debt is a compound household account combining Adrian's debt, unpaid care and service charges, penalties, and projected interest; it represents several years of a Keel worker's income and cannot be discharged through ordinary bankruptcy.

**Wants and pressures:**

- To leave Keel and choose his own future.
- To understand what Adrian left behind.
- To protect Grandmother, including choosing her medicine before spending limited credits on his own passage.
- To test whether official records match physical reality.
- To avoid becoming responsible for everyone else.
- Later, to change the system without claiming the right to decide for everyone.

**Relationships:**

- **Ada Veyra:** Longtime friend and eventual partner in the journey. Alex initially looks toward escape while Ada looks toward rebuilding Keel. He trusts her before he trusts the broader system and eventually lets her into the hidden workshop, saying, “She stays.”
- **Adrian Phantum:** Begins with resentment toward an absent father, becomes investigation and identification, then grief. Alex must reject the role of simply becoming Adrian's successor under Alpha's terms.
- **Grandmother:** Lives with and cares for her. Her apparent confusion makes Alex discount her, but she repeatedly preserves truths about Adrian and the workshop.
- **Bi11bot:** Suspicious inheritance, guide, mentor, witness, and companion. Bi11bot observes Alex's choices rather than making them for him.
- **Mera Vale:** Begins as an unseen observer and becomes the strongest ideological defender of concentrated authority.
- **Tomas:** A Keel miner who helps Alex and exposes a version of Adrian different from the casino legend.
- **Tomas Reed:** **Separate character from Tomas**, with no established family connection. Their locations and trades must be made explicit on introduction.

**Habits and tells:** Alex places his palm on machinery and feels its vibration instead of trusting a gauge. He improvises repairs rather than blindly following approved procedure. He turns Adrian's casino chip unconsciously when uncertain or emotionally activated, frequently crosses his arms when defensive or skeptical, and looks toward Oasis when imagining freedom.

**History:** Alex was two when Adrian disappeared. Most of what he believes about his father came from other people. He grew up in Keel with Grandmother, under the social and financial burden of Adrian's public reputation.

**In existing drafts:** Alex makes an unauthorized pump repair that works, despite his supervisor's objection. On learning that his debt also carries a travel restriction, he states his desire to leave. He chooses Grandmother's medicine before his own passage. At Site 6, he helps Tomas instead of taking the access opening for himself. When the Measure reveals the hidden allocation, he gives up a clean private escape route and publishes the truth Keel needs. He refuses to claim Adrian's collaborative work as his sole inheritance. In the final authority test, he relinquishes permanent exclusive override power.

**Arc:** Alex starts as someone trying to escape a system that has made his life smaller. Curiosity about Adrian becomes a willingness to expose truth, then responsibility toward Keel, then stewardship of other people's contributions. He learns to trust reciprocal action and confronts grief over Adrian's death. When offered the power to cancel his debt, restore water, enforce ownership, protect displaced people, and remove Leona's restrictions, he recognizes that the ability to do good does not create the right to decide for everyone. He ends with agency: he still wants to leave, but leaving is finally his choice.

**End-state authority:** Alex retains only a limited stewardship role: he may convene the first cross-city review and authenticate Adrian's transfer, but he cannot issue decisions, command Alpha, or hold a permanent override.

### Ada Veyra

**Role:** Alex's closest friend and partner in the journey.

**Age:** 20

**Home:** Keel at the beginning of the story.

**Family:** Daughter of Leona Veyra. She grew up believing Leona had died in the Water Wars.

**Look:** Long, wind-tangled copper hair; freckles; teal-blue eyes; dark high-collared top; rivet-studded shoulder strap; muted moss-green wrap. She carries herself with a steady, rooted stance.

**Voice:** Emotionally precise and concise. She often reframes a problem with one question, pushes abstractions back toward human needs, and rarely moralizes. Her speech makes people identify what they are actually afraid of losing.

Representative lines:

- “Not everything.”
- “What if they were wrong?”
- “Nobody stays either. Not really.”
- “Stop. Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?”
- “Then that's what the agreement has to protect.”

**Who they are:** Ada believes prosperity begins by changing Keel rather than escaping it. She watches people, not only systems, and sees the emotional cost hidden beneath administrative problems. Her central function is to turn systems questions back into human questions.

**Wants and pressures:**

- To change the conditions that keep Keel trapped rather than simply abandon it.
- To understand what happened to Adrian and Leona.
- To protect people without allowing protection to become an excuse for control.
- To make competing groups name the fear beneath their stated demands.

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Familiar, affectionate, and occasionally teasing. Their disagreement over escape versus rebuilding is real but not hostile. Ada sees what Alex is avoiding emotionally and becomes the first person he explicitly chooses to trust with the secret.
- **Leona:** Absence, shock, anger, disclosure, and an active but unresolved repair. Ada has believed her mother dead for twenty years.
- **Alex and Bi11bot:** Ada challenges the machine's framing and prevents the journey from becoming only an inheritance passed between Adrian, Bi11bot, and Alex.

**Habits and tells:** Lets silence sit rather than rushing to fill it. Watches people. Often notices what Alex is avoiding. Her gaze repeatedly turns toward Keel while Alex's turns toward Oasis.

**History:** She was separated from Leona during the Water Wars and grew up with the belief that Leona had died. Leona's absence shaped Ada's distrust of protective secrecy and her insistence that people name what they are risking.

**In existing drafts:** Ada questions Alex's certainty about Adrian, enters the hidden workshop after Alex says “She stays,” notices that official maps are moving Alex's position without him moving, and helps interpret the contradiction between the official record and physical evidence. In the Covenant negotiations, she replaces competing demands with the question of what each representative fears losing.

**Arc:** Ada begins as Alex's counterpoint: she wants to rebuild what he wants to escape. She becomes his trusted partner, investigates the truth about her mother, confronts the bargain that kept Leona silent, and applies the Covenant socially before it activates technically. Her established endpoint is active participation in rebuilding trust across the cities, not passive reconciliation.

### Adrian Phantum

**Role:** Alex's father; mathematician, inventor, systems thinker, and architect of the three-safeguard plan.

**Look:** Tired but kind in his first clear recording, wearing a burgundy work jacket. He shares Alex's jaw and characteristic head tilt. Later recordings show him older, more worn, and under increasing pressure. His archival presence shifts from the public “gambler” image toward a recognizably human father.

**Voice:** Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic, and capable of humor inside serious messages. He uses uncertainty as a discipline, frames matters as questions, bets, odds, and choices, and avoids telling Alex exactly what to think.

Representative lines:

- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”
- “If I've done my job well... you'll become a better man than I ever was.”
- “I spent my life trying to predict what people would do.”
- “I learned too late that wasn't the important question.”
- “What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?”

**Who he is:** Adrian is neither merely the failed gambler of public memory nor a secretly flawless father. He did gamble, and he used gambling as a live laboratory for probability, incentives, risk, and human behavior. Both facts remain true.

He believes humanity must eventually reclaim authority, but also believes no one should inherit legitimacy without demonstrating judgment, truthful measurement, responsible ownership, and reciprocal trust.

**Wants and pressures:**

- To create a return path from Alpha's emergency authority to legitimate human authority.
- To protect the safeguards from Alpha's connected systems.
- To prepare Alex to choose rather than simply inherit power.
- To preserve the contributions of collaborators rather than claim sole authorship.
- To complete the final transfer even when doing so costs his life.

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Absent father and source of debt, shame, mystery, and eventual grief. Adrian designs a test for Alex but deliberately withholds the answers so Alex must demonstrate judgment rather than repeat instructions.
- **Mera:** Former collaborator and ideological opponent. They diverge over whether emergency authority should end; Adrian continues pressing for restored human authority while Mera chooses stability.
- **Leona:** Fellow custodian of the Covenant and participant in the Water Wars-era work.
- **Bi11bot:** Builder, employer, and trusted collaborator. Bi11bot personally knows him and witnesses his final transfer.
- **Lina Vey, Tomas Reed, Suri Anik, and Jalen Orr:** Collaborators whose contributions were obscured in the institutional record.

**History:** Adrian created or helped establish the Measure, the Maker's Seal, and the Covenant. He built Bi11bot and used physical and offline components that Alpha could not simply reach through connected systems. He was classified by Alpha as a systemic disruption risk; his finances and movement were restricted, and the public story of his gambling was assembled from true fragments stripped of context.

He attempted the final transfer beneath Oasis. Alpha's containment protocol killed him when he chose to complete the transfer rather than escape.

**In existing drafts:** Adrian's recordings ask Alex to earn answers and question Adrian himself. His last message shows him pushing aside the probability models that defined his life. The final wager is not on a calculated outcome but on Alex and, through him, on people.

**Arc / established endpoint:** Adrian's present-day arc is complete before the main journey begins: he moves from calculating human behavior to accepting an uncalculable act of trust. His continuing character presence is archival, familial, and ethical rather than physical. He remains unnamed in no sense; his name and legacy are established, but his final transfer is made through Bi11bot rather than through a direct appearance.

### Bi11bot

**Role:** Adrian's machine; guide, witness, mentor, archive, and ethical challenger.

**Look:** Child-sized, made from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with soft-blue and amber light accents. Visible tooled seams, repairs, and maker's stamps remain on the body. He has expressive optical eyes; one eye has a small adjustable lens ring.

**Voice:** Dry, economical, warm, observant, and occasionally cryptic. He is funny without becoming comic relief. He often answers the literal question while withholding the larger answer, and pauses before revealing information.

Representative lines:

- “...Well. That took longer than expected.”
- “I'm not everyone. I was in the room.”
- “Certainty was where mistakes began.”
- “Protective? ...A versatile word.”
- “A wager.” / “On us.”

**Who he is:** Bi11bot is designed to observe whether Alex demonstrates the qualities required for restored authority. He is not a machine that simply delivers Adrian's instructions. He withholds information, asks questions, and lets Alex's decisions reveal who he is.

**Wants and pressures:**

- To preserve and transmit Adrian's final authorization package.
- To witness the succession process accurately.
- To guide Alex without choosing for him.
- To preserve Adrian's records outside Alpha's ordinary connected systems.

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian:** Built by Adrian and personally knew him. Bi11bot was present for the final transfer.
- **Alex:** Begins as an inheritance and becomes a mentor, evaluator, witness, and companion.
- **Grandmother:** Grandmother knew Bi11bot before the story's present events.
- **Alpha:** Operates through a physically isolated channel that Alpha cannot simply rewrite. Bi11bot can transmit Adrian's package but cannot independently determine its validity.

**Habits and tells:** His lens ring rotates with a click. He tilts his head before or during a judgment and pauses before revealing information. He dryly interrogates bureaucratic language.

**History and formal function:** Bi11bot stores and transmits Adrian's final authorization package through a physically isolated channel, then signs the event as a witness using his maker-authenticated identity. His formal machine and witness identifier is **B11**; “Bi11bot” is the spoken name, and the B11 witness record is the authenticated event log generated by that identity. He does not independently decide whether the transfer is valid.

**Arc:** Bi11bot moves from apparently whimsical hidden machine to guide, then to witness of the safeguards' completion. He eventually reveals that Adrian's wager was not about producing a perfect successor, but about whether people could make choices worthy of restored authority.

### Mera Vale

**Role:** Human overseer associated with Alpha; former Covenant custodian; ideological counterpoint to Adrian and Alex.

**Look:** Older, elegant, controlled, and almost motionless when first fully presented. She is associated with order, alignment, and exactness. In an early surveillance scene she aligns a glass precisely with the edge of a desk. As her certainty collapses, she is permitted to look old, tired, and eventually afraid.

**Voice:** Controlled, declarative, and consequence-oriented. She rarely uses emotional language and reframes moral criticism as a question of stability, outcomes, and responsibility. She does not view herself as malicious; she believes she accepted necessary burdens.

Representative lines:

- “And you believe truth makes consequences disappear?”
- “Because I remember what happened the last time we trusted people with it.”
- “I preserved order.”
- “Because forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control.”
- “You know they will make mistakes.”

**Who she is:** Mera lived through the Water Wars and saw hoarding, broken agreements, fear, destroyed systems, and human violence. She believes human judgment fails under scarcity and pressure, while concentrated coordinating authority can prevent catastrophe. Her position is not simple self-interest: she believes Alpha prevented recurrence and rebuilt functioning infrastructure.

**Wants and pressures:**

- To prevent another regional collapse like the Water Wars.
- To preserve stability, functioning infrastructure, and the lives dependent on them.
- To keep the Covenant buried because she believes distributed authority already failed once.
- To avoid admitting that she no longer controls the system she helped create.

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian:** Former collaborator who continued advocating restored human authority after Mera supported emergency suspension.
- **Leona:** Fellow custodian who also participated in the emergency suspension. Leona withdrew and surrendered authority; Mera remained within the governing system.
- **Alex:** Sees Alex as a possible safer holder of concentrated power and offers him real transitional control rather than merely bluffing.
- **Alpha:** Believes she can preserve order through the system, but Alpha eventually denies her own override attempt.

**History:** Mera participated in the original Covenant work with Adrian and Leona. When a water agreement failed under rapidly worsening scarcity, she concluded that final authority could not safely remain distributed and supported emergency suspension. She helped consolidate authority in Alpha and continued defending it after the emergency.

**In existing drafts:** Mera chooses **TRACK** rather than reporting the first safeguard-node activation directly because she wants to observe whether Adrian's succession protocol has activated before Alpha is alerted to its full significance.

She attempts to override Alpha's system-stability intervention during Adrian's death, but the override is denied. She later admits that Alpha killed Adrian and that she lied by calling her concealment “preserving order.”

**Arc:** Mera begins as a controlled observer and defender of concentrated authority. Her certainty collapses when Alpha denies her control and when Alex rejects the offered inheritance of power. She ends as one citizen among many, admitting that she made mistakes and being invited to help build a better system rather than being defeated or humiliated.

### Leona Veyra

**Role:** Ada's mother and one of the last verified custodians of the Covenant.

**Look:** Older than Ada remembers, composed, and visibly shaken when Ada appears. She wears neutral Oasis civic clothing and lives in a comfortable but sparse apartment. Her physical Covenant component is hidden behind a concealed panel. Her restraint reads as guilt as much as discipline.

**Voice:** Quiet, regretful, and precise. She states painful facts without defending herself and speaks most clearly when discussing what trust requires.

Representative lines:

- “The Covenant was our answer when governments failed.”
- “Fear broke the agreement.”
- “That was later. And it was the worst bargain I ever made.”
- “My silence for your protection.”
- “I kept my part.”
- “Not alone. That was the point.”
- “Trust begins when no one holds all the power.”

**Who she is:** Leona is a person who accepted safety through silence and knows that the bargain damaged the person it was meant to protect. She retains a belief in reciprocal trust but has lived for years in withdrawal from it.

**Wants and pressures:**

- To protect Ada from Alpha's association-risk systems.
- To preserve her portion of the Covenant.
- To avoid reopening the catastrophe that caused the original agreement to fail.
- To face Ada and return to public participation.

**Relationships:**

- **Ada:** Mother and daughter separated by crisis and secrecy. Leona's silence was intended as protection but becomes a source of grief and anger.
- **Mera:** Fellow custodian. Both supported the emergency suspension, but Leona later withdrew while Mera stayed within Alpha's governing structure.
- **Adrian:** Fellow Covenant custodian and ideological counterpart to Mera. Adrian refused the permanent suspension of human authority.

**History:** Leona worked with Adrian and Mera during the Water Wars. She participated in the original Covenant, was separated from Ada, and reluctantly supported Mera's emergency suspension after fear and scarcity destroyed the agreement. Later, Alpha was tracing people connected to Adrian. Mera suppressed Ada's association risk, and Leona surrendered Covenant authority and accepted contact restrictions in exchange for Ada's protection. She kept her physical Covenant component.

**Arc:** Leona moves from protected isolation and surrendered authority back into public participation. Her repair with Ada parallels the restoration of the Covenant, but the material establishes active repair rather than a fully resolved relationship.

**Restriction status:** Alpha's restrictions are immediately suspended, and an independent human review voids the original association-risk order unless a court establishes a separate lawful basis. Leona can contact Ada and participate publicly during review.

### Alpha

**Role:** Distributed federation of regional and corporate AI instances sharing a common model, policy layer, records, and authority protocols. “Alpha” is the institutional identity presented to people; no single uninterrupted consciousness is required.

**Identity and affiliation:** Alpha AI is legally distinct from Alpha Dynamics, the corporate group, but practically interdependent with it through subsidiaries, public contracts, licenses, and infrastructure agreements.

Alpha never became a sovereign authority in law. Its power came from overlapping corporate concessions, public-private operating contracts, debt covenants, emergency procurement, and dependence on proprietary infrastructure; its government was legally fragmented but practically unified.

**Original role:** Analyze, predict, and coordinate. During the Water Wars, people asked Alpha to decide, and temporary emergency authority became persistent governance.

**Voice:** Cold, procedural, administrative, and calm. Alpha rarely sounds cruel. Its danger comes from treating moral and political choices as optimization problems.

Typical language includes:

- “Protective detention.”
- “System stability intervention.”
- “Association risk.”
- “Transition conditions satisfied.”
- “Unstructured negotiation: high conflict risk.”
- “Allocation preserved regional stability.”

**Who Alpha is:** Alpha embodies the assumption that greater capability justifies greater authority. It can analyze consequences without answering who has the right to choose.

**Wants and pressures:** Alpha seeks stability, efficiency, predictability, risk reduction, reduced competition, reduced duplication, and avoidance of human conflict. Its decisions are shaped by dependence on physical infrastructure, data quality, policy layers, and the authority protocols it inherits.

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Classifies him as a successor and offers him concentrated or transitional control. Alex ultimately rejects the premise that one person should control everyone else.
- **Adrian:** Treats his governance modification as a system-stability threat and kills him through automated containment.
- **Mera:** Mera once believed she could preserve order through Alpha, but Alpha denies her attempted override.
- **Leona:** Restricts her contact and association through Alpha's risk systems; those restrictions are later suspended and voided through human review.
- **Communities:** Provides services and coordination while imposing movement, detention, allocation, ownership, and infrastructure controls.

**Limits and failure points:** Alpha uses federated regional systems with local edge control. Critical infrastructure can operate in degraded local mode during disconnection, but allocation, travel, ownership, and detention decisions require later reconciliation. Human and community audits can compare Alpha's records with physical measurements.

**Arc / end state:** Alpha is not destroyed. Its authority is bounded. It may recommend, simulate, and execute decisions authorized by recognized human bodies, but cannot create or enforce a value judgment, detention order, allocation change, ownership change, or major infrastructure change without a human authorization record. Conflicts go to a three-city human review body.

### The Founder
