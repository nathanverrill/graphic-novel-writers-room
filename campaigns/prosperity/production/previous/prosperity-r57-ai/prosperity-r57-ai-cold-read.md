# Cold Read: Prosperity (Issues 01–03, pages 1–54)

I read this in one sitting, as a 16-year-old who was assigned it and opened it wanting to hate it. There is only one version of the book, so I read it once, front to back. I only reacted to what's on the page (art, captions, balloons, screens, sound effects). A few bits that read like notes to the artist slipped through ("keep figures small", "page-turn reveal", "no holographic definitions"). I ignored them.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 2.** The checkpoint screen got me. "DEBT TRANSFER DAY", age seventeen, 418,607 credits, 40 earned today, 28.7 years. Then the guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum." Up to then I was getting ready to hate it. That's the first page where I wanted to know what happens.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** "Then don't approve it. / Just notice it works." Two lines and I know who Alex is: a kid who fixes things the wrong way and the fix works.
- **Pages 5–6.** Grandma hands him the book, he puts it in the gap, CLICK, the shelf swings open, and she just keeps folding. The secret door is old but it works, and her calm is creepier than a scream would be.
- **Page 8, panel 4.** "...Well. That took longer than expected." The robot's first line is funny. I liked him straight away.
- **Page 10, panel 2.** "I heard that." Grandma in the doorway is the only real laugh in the issue.
- **Page 14, panel 3.** "LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR." He loses a whole day's pay to save someone, and the screen says it. That works because page 2 told me what 40 credits means to him.
- **Page 15, panel 3.** "You stopped for a stranger." Simple and good.
- **Page 16, panel 4.** "It's sweating. / Abandoned mines don't." Best exchange in the issue. Short, I understood it first time, and it's a clue I worked out myself.
- **Page 18, panel 2.** "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% / DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%." Finally a problem I could hold in my head: they're stealing the water and lying about it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 3–4.** The rooftop talk with Ada is long. Four panels of back-and-forth on page 3, then more on page 4. By "It's the part where you pretend it isn't" I was skimming balloons.
- **Page 11.** I skimmed the whole page because I couldn't follow it (see below).
- **Page 12.** The control room cutaway. I looked at the pictures and didn't read the screens properly.
- **Page 17, panel 3.** Alex and Ada call each other's names across two tunnels. I skipped it.

### I was confused
- **Page 3, panel 4:** "Then don't spend them proving he was right about people leaving." Who's "he"? His dad? Did his dad *say* people leave? Is she saying his dad left, so leaving proves... something? I gave up.
- **Page 11:** I have no idea what this page is about. Ada is suddenly in the workshop, but she walked home on page 4 and nobody let her in. Bi11bot opens boxes with a "Measure", a "Maker's Seal" and three "Covenant bands" and says "tools for three different kinds of damage." Then there's an injured worker's record, a Seal that "records the worker's name", and a band that stays dark because "No reciprocal promise has been made." I didn't know what any of the objects do, why they matter, or why I'm looking at a stranger's bandaged hand. "Paper-clean" means nothing to me.
- **Page 12:** Why does Alex press the amber node? What does it do? Who is the silver-haired woman? Nobody says her name. What's "Alpha"? What's "Legacy Protocol"? "The first node is under the pit." The first node of what? What are they looking for? We know there's a map and the mine is sealed, but what is he trying to *do*?
- **Page 13, panel 1:** Ada "enters" and says "You said you were leaving," as if she wasn't standing at the workshop table two pages ago. Did page 11 happen the next morning? Was she there or not? I flipped back to check.
- **Page 13:** "The city disagreed." Disagreed how? The reassignment? I guess.
- **Page 14:** "Foreman-7" made me think robot, but he's drawn as a man. Is he a person? A person with a number? Never explained.
- **Page 15:** Tomas calls the robot "B1". The balloons spell it "Bi11bot". Is that his name or a nickname? How do you even say "Bi11bot"? "Bill-bot"? "B-eleven-bot"? I read it wrong every time.
- **Page 17, panel 3:** How does Ada know where they are? She comes in through a different tunnel with her own map. When did she find out?
- **Page 17, panel 4:** A robot dog builds itself out of Bi11bot's chest. It's cool, but nobody says its name, so I didn't know it was "TJ" until later issues kept saying it. Where did it come from? Was it in there the whole time?
- **Page 18, panel 4:** The woman from page 12 "authorizes" a water release and water floods in behind them. Did she just try to drown four people? Or was it an accident? I couldn't tell if she's the villain.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, Ada, Grandma, Bi11bot, Tomas. Each looks and sounds different.
- **Unclear:** the woman at the screens on p12 and p18. No name, no line, no reason. Foreman-7 is just a uniform. The supervisor and the guard on p2 are fine as walk-ons.
- **Sound-alike problem:** Ada and Bi11bot both talk in neat little sayings. Ada especially: nearly every line she says is a lesson.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the birthday line. That one hurt a bit.
- Page 5, "Adrian? / Not today, Grandma. It's Alex." That's sad.
- Page 16, Tomas yelling "Wrong button! My fault!" to cover for them. I liked him a lot for one page.

### I predicted
- Page 5, "He's closer than you think": the dad is alive, or his body is in the sealed mine.
- Page 12: the woman will turn out to be secretly on his side, because she pressed TRACK and not REPORT.
- Page 3: Ada knows more than she's saying.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid gets his missing dad's debt for his 17th birthday, finds the dad's secret workshop and robot, and finds out the city is lying about how much water his neighbourhood gets.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 11.** In a shop it goes back on the shelf here. Pages 2 and 5–10 had me. Then a page of objects with capital-letter names, a stranger's injury, and robot sayings, and I don't know what anything does or what Alex wants. Page 12 makes it worse with a woman and a computer I can't name.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Kind of. Water rushing in on people climbing into a pipe is loud, and I do care about Alex by now. But I know the heroes don't drown in issue 1, so the danger isn't the hook. The hook is that Keel just saw the 62/18 numbers, and I wanted to know what the people do about it. (The next issue never really shows that.) I'd read on tonight because it's homework. If I was choosing, I'd wait a week.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 11; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 20, panel 2.** The kiosk: "OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS." "She made it." / "The House owns it." The clearest idea in the issue, shown not explained. I got it instantly.
- **Page 21, panel 1.** "312 PATENTS." So his debt-dad was secretly rich in ideas. Good twist, and "He left me debt" is a good comeback.
- **Pages 27–29.** The "Mine fails without..." round (p27) has a rhythm I liked. Then the machine sparks and fails at 11% (p28). Then Alex files down a piece of scrap and it jumps to 91% (p29). That pays off page 1, where his unapproved bracket worked. "Scrap is allowed to be useful" landed. This was the best stretch of the issue.
- **Page 33, panel 2.** The water comes out cloudy. "Not a city." I liked that it isn't a magic fix. It's the first time the book felt honest instead of preachy.
- **Page 36, panel 4.** The woman with Ada's hair in the glass. I sat up.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 23–26.** Four pages, four scientists, four parts. Each one hands over a part and says one wise line. It's a video-game collect-the-pieces quest. By Jalen (p25) I was reading only the first balloon on each page.
- **Page 22.** Four names read out as a list, then five parts. I skimmed the list.
- **Pages 31–32.** Arguing, then building. Skimmed.
- **Page 34, panel 3 and panel 4.** Alex's speech to the review board reads like a class presentation. Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 19:** "Still disagreeable." / "Halyard may consider that a distinction." I don't know who or what is disagreeable. TJ's leg? The water? No idea.
- **Page 21:** A polished man walks up and never speaks. Not once, here or on page 30. Alex has to say both sides of the conversation: "And the people in the records?" ... "You want the estate." I don't know his name from the page, what exactly he's offering, or what signing does. The villain of the issue is a silent man holding a tablet.
- **Page 22:** Five parts, four people. I counted twice. (Ren has two, which I only worked out on p26.)
- **Page 23:** "So the 62 percent comes back through a different pipe." So the stolen water comes back to Keel dirty? Or the water they freed is dirty? I read it twice and still wasn't sure.
- **Page 25:** "Five people standing still is already a pattern." There are three people and two robots. Does that count?
- **Page 30, panel 3:** "Those look identical from here." What looks identical? The notices? Identical to what?
- **Page 30:** Lock walks in with a "review", people's badges change, and he leaves. What happened? Is it a threat? It reads like the power went out.
- **Page 34:** What is the "Charter House review"? A court? Who decides?
- **Page 35, panel 1:** Lina gives Alex "the bronze Maker's Seal". But Bi11bot had the bronze Maker's Seal in the workshop on page 11, and Alex used it on a pipe. Are there two? I flipped back. This one really threw me.
- **Page 35, panel 4:** "Then they can't reproduce the people you erased." Gave up.
- **Page 36, panel 2:** Aqualis copied it, "They scaled it." So did Alex win or lose? The book seems fine with it, and I didn't know how to feel.
- **Page 36, panel 3:** "COVENANT CUSTODIAN RECORD — THIRD NAME WITHHELD." Third name of what? Still don't know what the Covenant is.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, Ada, Bi11bot. Ren, a bit, because he welds and is blunt ("Took you long enough").
- **Couldn't:** Lina, Suri and Jalen blur together. They all speak in the same neat sayings, and by p27 I couldn't tell you who brought what without checking. Suri = plants, I guess. Jalen = nervous. Lina = ...membrane?
- **Lock** isn't a character. He's a silent prop.
- **Everybody talks the same.** Bi11bot, Ada, Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren, even Alex all speak in short balanced sayings: "X isn't Y." "Not X. Y." That sameness is exactly what makes people in my class say "this was written by AI." I thought it by page 24.

### I felt something
- Page 24, panel 3: Suri touching the empty bench. "I stayed because something had to keep breathing." That was a little sad, and the empty desks do more than her lines.
- Page 29: the click when the scrap sleeve fits. Satisfying.
- Not much else. Nobody in Halyard was around long enough for me to care.

### I predicted
- Page 21: he won't sign. (Correct, and obvious.)
- Page 36: the woman in the glass is Ada's mother. (Guessed instantly from "copper-brown hair".)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex has to choose between selling his dad's inventions to a corporation for his own way out and giving credit to the people who actually built them.

(This is a different book from the one I thought I was reading after Issue 01. The water theft and the 62/18 reveal, the thing Issue 01 ended on, mostly drops away for an issue about patents.)

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 25.** Third scientist in a row, another part, another wise saying, another "find the next one." That's where I'd have gone to my phone. If I'd made it past that, page 30 (the silent Lock scene) was the next spot.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
A bit, and only because of Ada. She's the one I care about most, and the reflection is a real question about a person I know. That's a real cliffhanger, not a noise. But I'd guessed it was her mom the second I saw the hair, so it's "go on then, show me" more than "I need this."

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 25; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 37.** Alex's pass stalls and Ada's opens straight away. "It knew my name." Great quick hook. It flips who the outsider is.
- **Page 38, panel 3.** "I thought she had disappeared. I stopped asking whether that meant dead or unwilling." Best line in the book. I read it twice because it was good, not because it was confusing.
- **Pages 39–40.** The mother scene is the best scene in the whole book. "Neither speaks." "Would you like something?" / "I didn't come for tea." I caught that it calls back to the tea on page 3. "You chose it instead of me." "You traded my choice." It's short and clear and I could feel it. If the whole book were like this I'd have finished it by choice.
- **Page 42, panel 3.** "Adrian said no. Mera said yes. ... I made the deciding choice." For the first time I understood how this world got broken, and that it was a person's choice.
- **Page 48.** The dad recording his message while the water rises. It explains the flood on page 18, his disappearance, and the message. It hit.
- **Page 53, panel 4.** The debt is still 402,110. I respected that. It didn't cheat.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 41.** Mera's lines. Skimmed after the second one.
- **Page 43.** Ada's speech about voices and promises. It reads like the lesson plan talking. Skimmed.
- **Page 47.** Screen after screen: surveillance, financial restriction, liability, override denied. Skimmed.
- **Pages 49 and 51.** "Claimant, witness, and convenor." "Designate a process." Alex's three rules. I skimmed and only read the screen text.
- **Page 52.** Three brand-new people at a table. Skimmed.
- **Page 54, last panel.** The final captions explain the ending to me, then the "FINAL HUMAN QUESTION" asks me something. I rolled my eyes, literally.

### I was confused
- **Page 41:** "I suppressed your association risk from the active reports. I could not erase the network's inferences." I don't know what she's saying. "It was the part of the protection I could still perform." Gave up.
- **Page 41:** Is Mera the woman from pages 12 and 18? I think so, but her name is only ever on a screen list (p38). And did she try to drown them on page 18 or not? The book never tells me. That bothered me more and more, because page 48 shows the same thing happening to Adrian.
- **Page 42:** "The Covenant failed when fear made promises smaller than survival." Gave up. Also: this is page 42 of 54, the first time I learn what the Covenant even is. It shows up in passing on page 11, so when it matters I don't understand it.
- **Page 44, panel 3:** Bi11bot has Adrian's band, and Leona had hers in a box. But on page 11 Bi11bot had *three* bands. Where did the other two go? Same problem as the Seal on page 35.
- **Page 48, panel 4:** Bi11bot switches on next to Adrian in the flooded station. So how did Bi11bot get back into a dusty workshop behind a bookshelf in Keel, switched off, saying "That took longer than expected"? Who carried him? That broke the dad-death scene for me a little, because I spent it trying to work out the robot's timeline.
- **Page 48:** Why does the dad record a message instead of reaching the emergency controls he "could reach"? The book seems to think it's noble. I thought: you had a two-year-old.
- **Page 48 vs page 10:** He said "You deserve answers. But they have to be earned" in the workshop. Here, recording that same message, he says "What you do with this is up to you." Same message or not?
- **Page 5:** "He's closer than you think." So... was Grandma just confused? He was dead in a station under Oasis, which is the opposite of close. I was waiting for that to mean something.
- **Page 49:** "Convenor of the handoff from emergency authority to a human structure." Even after Alex asks, I didn't get it.
- **Page 51, panel 4:** "The fourth choice is yours." Fourth? What were the first three? I flipped back and couldn't find them.
- **Page 51:** So Alpha just... agrees? A screen says "EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED" and the all-powerful system that ran everything for fifteen years steps aside because a seventeen-year-old pointed at three objects. That felt too easy.
- **Page 52:** Mara Doss, Frank Reed, Elias Renn: three new names on page 52 of 54. "Mara" right after "Mera". "Renn" after "Ren". I honestly thought Mera had sat down at the table until I read it again.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex is offered the power to fix everything himself, and he turns it down for a slow public process where everyone can see who decided what.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 43.** Right after the best scene in the book (pp39–40), Mera's jargon on page 41 and Ada's civics speech on page 43 lose me. Page 48 would have pulled me back if I'd made it there, but in a shop I'd have stopped at 43.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The last page tells me what to think ("The system was not fixed... Now the people carrying the consequences could see who had chosen them"), then asks me a discussion question in capital letters: "FINAL HUMAN QUESTION". That's the moment the book stopped pretending not to be homework. The image of Alex and Ada small on the overlook, free to go or stay, was a nice ending. The words on top of it took it away. I wouldn't buy another book in this world.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 43; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 01, page 11.** Pages 2–10 had most of us: the debt on his birthday, the secret door, the funny robot, the dad's hologram. Then page 11 drops three objects with capital-letter names, a stranger's injury record, and lines like "No reciprocal promise has been made." Nobody knows what anything does or what Alex wants now. Page 12 piles a nameless woman and a computer called "Alpha" on top. Half the class would be on their phones by page 13. And because the Measure, Seal and Covenant are what the whole ending runs on, anyone who checked out here would be lost on pages 49–51.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dad's huge debt on his 17th birthday, finds a secret robot that leads him to proof the rich cities are stealing his town's water, then collects his dad's inventions from four scientists, and in the end refuses to become king and sets up a committee instead. The bit with his friend meeting her mom is actually good. The rest is a lot of people saying wise things at each other."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A great birthday-debt opening and a fun secret workshop, then a page of objects I couldn't follow, then saved by "It's sweating" and the 62/18 reveal.
- **Issue 02:** A fetch quest through three companies where everyone talks in fortune cookies, with one good scrap-metal moment in the middle and a silent villain I never met.
- **Issue 03:** The best scene in the book (Ada and her mom) and the dad's death, buried under jargon, three new characters on the second-to-last page, and an ending that tells me the lesson and then sets homework.
