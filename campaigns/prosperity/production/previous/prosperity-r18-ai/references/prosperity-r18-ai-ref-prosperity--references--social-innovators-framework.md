# The Social Innovators' Framework

Freeman, B. & Hawkins, R. 2016. *Evoke — Developing Skills in Youth to Solve the World's Most
Complex Problems: The Social Innovators' Framework.* World Bank Education, Technology &
Innovation: SABER-ICT Technical Paper Series (#11). Washington, DC: The World Bank.
Available at saber.worldbank.org. Licensed CC BY 3.0 IGO.

This file summarises and quotes that paper, which is the framework the Evoke campaign runs on.
Everything in the numbered sections below is theirs. Where this room adds anything of its own,
it says so.

## What the framework is for

The learning outcome is not knowledge; it is a kind of person. The paper asks what qualities
successful social innovators have used across many settings, names four, then breaks each into
powers and each power into skills that can actually be observed, practised and judged.

Its argument for doing so: many of these words — communication, empathy, systems thinking — are
recognisable but hollow. "There is in fact very little clarity about what many of the terms mean
in practice." A skill that is not rendered observable cannot be taught or assessed. So the
framework names, for every skill, what a person does when they have it.

Four dimensions run through the whole thing, from the technical (operational knowledge), to the
enabling (21st-century skills), to the empowering (socio-emotional skills), to the
transformational (emancipating qualities).

**The shape:** 4 qualities → 16 powers → 48 skills, plus one cross-cutting power, persistence,
with 3 more skills. Skill numbers read `quality.power.skill` — 2.3.1 is the first skill of the
third power of the Deep Collaborator.

A power was only admitted if it met all three tests: cited in the social innovation or social
entrepreneurship literature; cited in the 21st-century or socio-emotional skills literature; and
supported by research in at least two academic disciplines.

## Quality 1 — Creative Visionary (CV)

*Social innovators imagine a better future and a better world.*

### Power 1 · Imagination [1.1] — applies the imagination

- **1.1.1** Produces original and novel ideas through the willingness to take risks and try
  something different. *Thought experiments; tinkering and making; lateral thinking against
  taken-for-granted assumptions.*
- **1.1.2** Views familiar things in a different light. *Challenges ingrained assumptions;
  reframes old problems with people's feelings in view; conceives different purposes for existing
  tools.*
- **1.1.3** Dreams of creative ways to resolve a conflict or problem and initiates forward-looking
  solutions. *Tells stories; learns experientially through small games or scenarios; talks with
  people from different cultures and backgrounds.*

### Power 2 · Ideation [1.2] — commands the world of ideas

- **1.2.1** Generates lots of new and original ideas and a variety of innovative alternatives.
  *Brainstorms; mind maps; goes somewhere new and documents what it taught.*
- **1.2.2** Shows flexibility of mind by exploring ideas in a diversity of ways. *Investigates
  different layers of an issue; draws ideas from multiple domains; explores through research,
  field visits, conversation.*
- **1.2.3** Deals well with complex and nuanced ideas, showing fluency as an integrative thinker.
  *Simplifies the abstract; synthesises a range of ideas; elaborates existing ones.*

### Power 3 · Vision [1.3] — displays clarity of vision

- **1.3.1** Sees the bigger picture and is oriented to a "true north" — even if everyone else does
  not see it. *Marshals resources; writes the plan or canvas; seeks evidence of the right path.*
- **1.3.2** Sees opportunity where others see failure, and inspires others to do so too. *Keeps
  trying when it does not work; identifies potential in every person and every situation.*
- **1.3.3** Motivated to move a concept to reality; it is not enough to have an idea, they are
  compelled to bring it to life. *Works one problem at a time; makes concrete artifacts;
  prototypes.*

### Power 4 · Courage [1.4] — engages to create change

- **1.4.1** Has the courage to build consensus that challenges the status quo to address social
  causes. *Begins even when the path is long or obscured, and finds like-minded people; offers
  imperfect ideas for others to build on; asks for help, knowing it is a collective effort.*
- **1.4.2** Tolerance for ambiguity and uncertainty. *Digs beneath the surface rather than putting
  things in neat boxes; stretches beyond what they know — more important to learn than to prove
  you are smart; recognises doubt and confronts it.*
- **1.4.3** Engages in respectful dialogue with people who see the world differently and who may
  be resistant to change. *Stands up for what is right even when unpopular; assertive without
  aggression; patient with themselves and others.*

## Quality 2 — Deep Collaborator (DC)

*Social innovators understand that they cannot act alone.*

### Power 5 · Communication [2.1] — communicates clearly

- **2.1.1** Listens actively and empathetically to other people; not only attentive but truly
  understanding what they are expressing. *Paraphrases to check understanding; summarises, but
  not prematurely; asks why.*
- **2.1.2** Seeks understanding by presenting ideas in a thoughtful and assertive way. *Tests
  which message resonates; expresses interests clearly and empathetically, respecting cultural
  difference; uses aids — pictures, maps, video.*
- **2.1.3** Reasons persuasively by clearly expressing and supporting a position so other people
  can understand. *Formulates an argument; contextualises it to the audience, local against
  global; responds thoughtfully to questions.*

### Power 6 · Teamwork [2.2] — participates actively in diverse teams

- **2.2.1** Collaborates and works on teams with a diversity of views to resolve conflict and
  solve problems. *Works with people of different gender, ethnicity, discipline, age; locally and
  remotely; across differences of power.*
- **2.2.2** Creates trust as part of an effective team. *Follows through on commitments; agrees
  roles that maximise each member's strength; sets processes and decision-making that keep
  communication open.*
- **2.2.3** Gets things done by actively working with others. *No member dominates and none takes
  a free ride; co-constructs meaning and artifacts; produces workable real-world solutions.*

### Power 7 · Networking [2.3] — leverages the power of network resources

- **2.3.1** Assesses other people's ideas constructively. *Votes thoughtfully on others' posts;
  comments respectfully; reads with a view to understanding.*
- **2.3.2** Makes new contacts and connections with a variety of people. *Asks questions that seek
  a connection; values quality of relationship over quantity; uses existing social networks to
  reach ideas and information.*
- **2.3.3** Seeks and offers advice as needed. *Raises thought-provoking issues and asks the
  network for help; answers other people's requests; follows through on an offer of help.*

### Power 8 · Generosity of Spirit [2.4] — displays a generosity of spirit

- **2.4.1** Nurtures relationships and considers the intentions and purpose of other people's
  comments and actions. *Asks about and listens to another's story and passions; shows sensitivity
  for their well-being; says "we" rather than "I" about the team's work.*
- **2.4.2** Shares time, resources and information freely. *Posts what is useful; makes
  introductions; shares their own expertise.*
- **2.4.3** Shows gratitude and support. *Thanks people for their time and effort; works with and
  encourages even the weakest member of the team; advocates for and actively collaborates with
  other agents.*

## Quality 3 — Systems Thinker (ST)

*Social innovators consider the problem in light of the whole system around it.*

### Power 9 · Problem Solving [3.1] — questions and takes on unfamiliar problems

- **3.1.1** Takes on unfamiliar problems. *Defines the issue and its extent, global against local;
  specifies the human dimension; investigates root cause — it takes five questions, not one.*
- **3.1.2** Analyzes the system. *Articulates a hypothesis; probes parts and whole; draws out
  patterns in data.*
- **3.1.3** Experiments with potential solutions. *Develops criteria for knowing whether it works;
  iterates through trial and error; learns from mistakes and finds the workable constraints.*

### Power 10 · Analysis [3.2] — understands complex problems through analysis

- **3.2.1** Uses structured analysis. *Human-centred design, design thinking, systems thinking or
  their own formal process; concept maps and timelines for causal reasoning; describes dynamic
  flows and dependencies.*
- **3.2.2** Ensures that the people affected are central to understanding the problem and the
  solution. *Seeks their motivations and needs; speaks with them and listens throughout; accounts
  for the partners who would have to implement it.*
- **3.2.3** Tackles the problem and considers possible consequences of alternative plans and
  actions. *Considers effects holistically — people, environment, future generations; breaks the
  problem into manageable pieces; weighs cost against benefit.*

### Power 11 · Aggregation [3.3] — connects to multiple sources

- **3.3.1** Uses many sources of information and research to gain awareness and knowledge about
  the technical issues. *Third-party and first-hand research; learns from experts and mentors;
  checks the accuracy of sources.*
- **3.3.2** Makes connections between multiple sources. *Connects the dots across disciplines;
  connects research to experience; remixes ideas and media.*
- **3.3.3** Models a system. *Visualises it with concept and causal maps; draws feedback loops;
  evaluates prototypes through cycles of abstraction and hands-on work.*

### Power 12 · Critical Reflection [3.4] — reflects critically to build self-awareness

- **3.4.1** Critical reflection. *Sets aside time to reflect honestly on their own actions,
  priorities, biases, decisions and emotions; on how others perceive them; reassesses views in
  light of new evidence.*
- **3.4.2** Task-oriented reflection. *Reflects on experience and lessons learned; debriefs as a
  group on what went well and what could improve; considers how they could have acted differently
  if they could redo the work.*
- **3.4.3** Means of reflection. *A private journal; reflective thoughts posted in shared spaces or
  made as art; peer or expert review.*

## Quality 4 — Empathetic Activist (EA)

*Social innovators are proactive, and believe change is possible.*

### Power 13 · Leadership [4.1] — brings about change as leader of an interdependent team

- **4.1.1** Responsibility. *Binds the team together, inspiring trust, concerned with the morality
  of the goal; orients the team to a common purpose; sustains commitment by example.*
- **4.1.2** Leader-follower relationship. *Power "with" the team, not "over" it; provides identity
  stories that frame the situation and give a roadmap; treats leadership as situational.*
- **4.1.3** Handles challenges for which there is currently no solution. *Adapts through
  experiment and iteration; keeps the human dimension in view; provides consistency through
  disruption.*

### Power 14 · Empathy [4.2] — walks in others' shoes

- **4.2.1** Treats other people with respect. *Looks for commonality and shared values, setting
  preconceptions aside even with those seen as adversaries; puts themselves in another's place
  through experience; opens up first, sharing their own vulnerability.*
- **4.2.2** Tries to understand other people's perspectives, and is passionate about making a
  positive difference. *Listens closely without judgement; recognises what others are feeling;
  works with them on what they are feeling.*
- **4.2.3** Nurtures a sense of inclusiveness and belonging to a shared cause. *Asks the question
  that opens a deeper conversation; creates a safe environment; helps people feel good about what
  they are doing.*

### Power 15 · Transformation [4.3] — engages to create change in the community

- **4.3.1** Inspires and motivates other people to take action. *Shows their own aspirations and a
  visible commitment; contributes actively to the network; tells compelling stories of what things
  could be like.*
- **4.3.2** Proactive in the local community. *Connects global and national issues to local ones;
  builds consensus; helps build a supportive, non-coercive network.*
- **4.3.3** Inhabits a growth mindset. *Embraces challenge, even a small first step; learns from
  feedback and criticism; helps others move when they believe character or events are fixed.*

### Power 16 · Curiosity [4.4] — seeks new knowledge and embraces the uncertain

- **4.4.1** Embraces different experiences and copes with novel situations. *Seeks new information
  without believing they already know the answer; shadows another person and observes without
  judgement; continues despite discomfort.*
- **4.4.2** Asks questions that seek to understand other people's perspectives and context. *Asks
  open questions; is comfortable not knowing where a conversation will lead; receptive without
  predetermining the outcome.*
- **4.4.3** Stretches beyond one's current capabilities. *Immerses themselves in the context; sets
  goals that push understanding; seeks personal growth.*

## Cross-cutting — Power 17 · Persistence [CC], "Grit"

*Never gives up. Stays with an issue, a team, or a mission to the very end.* It runs through all
four qualities rather than sitting under one.

- **CC1** Shows persistence and works relentlessly to stay with an issue and meet project goals.
  *Effort stamina; interest stamina — follows through even when the line is not straight; sticks
  with meaningful challenges while staying flexible.*
- **CC2** Commits to deep practice. *Sees setbacks as a reason to dig in; seeks feedback and uses
  it; believes hard work will find a way.*
- **CC3** Never gives up, and works with others through uncertainty and difficulty.

## The eight missions — the social innovation cycle

The skills are not taught as a list. Each mission is a stage of the cycle, carries six activities
drawn from across the four qualities, and produces content that becomes part of the team's final
project (an "Evokation", organised under community, problem, solution, communication). Eight
missions over 8 to 16 weeks.

| Mission | The stage | The six skills it exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understanding the grand challenge | Curiosity 4.4.1, 4.4.2, 4.4.3 · Aggregation 3.3.1, 3.3.2, 3.3.3 |
| 2 | Understanding the community, with human-centred design | Analysis 3.2.1 · Teamwork 2.2.2 · Empathy 4.2.1 · Courage 1.4.3 · Leadership 4.1.1, 4.1.2 |
| 3 | Defining the problem | Problem Solving 3.1.1 · Teamwork 2.2.1 · Communication 2.1.1 · Critical Reflection 3.4.1 · Courage 1.4.2 · Leadership 4.1.3 |
| 4 | Creating the solution | Vision 1.3.2 · Imagination 1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.1.3 · Networking 2.3.2, 2.3.3 |
| 5 | Communicating the idea | Communication 2.1.2, 2.1.3 · Transformation 4.3.2 · Problem Solving 3.1.2 · Generosity 2.4.1, 2.4.2 |
| 6 | Revisiting how the problem affects the community | Ideation 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3 · Analysis 3.2.2 · Generosity 2.4.3 · Transformation 4.3.3 |
| 7 | Understanding the interdependencies of problem and solution | Problem Solving 3.1.3 · Vision 1.3.1, 1.3.3 · Critical Reflection 3.4.2, 3.4.3 · Analysis 3.2.3 |
| 8 | Engaging the community through communication | Courage 1.4.1 · Teamwork 2.2.3 · Empathy 4.2.2, 4.2.3 · Transformation 4.3.1 · Networking 2.3.1 |

Note what the order says: the cycle starts with curiosity and research, not with an idea. The
solution arrives in Mission 4, after two missions spent with the people affected. Then it goes
back to the community twice more before anyone is asked to persuade anyone.

## How a skill is shown, and judged

- **An activity actualises a skill.** Each of the 48 skills has an activity built for it, set in
  the campaign's fiction and ending in an instruction to the player: *"look around your community.
  Take a picture of an object, a place, or a person and conceive how it could be transformed."*
- **Evidence is what comes back** — text, photo, video, audio. The content itself is the proof
  that the action happened.
- **The rubric is one question.** *"Does this evidence present an original and novel idea?"*
  *"Does the evidence show a willingness to articulate what is right even if it is unpopular?"*
- **Peers and mentors judge it,** not a machine and not only a teacher. Evidence is shared with
  the whole network on submission; reviewers are rewarded far more for a substantive comment than
  for scoring the rubric.

## Using this in a story — this room's note, not the paper's

The framework is a description of behaviour, which makes it usable on a page: every skill above is
already written as something a person visibly does.

- **Dramatise the skill, never the label.** A character does not "demonstrate 4.2.1"; she sits
  through a meeting she would never normally attend. If the caption has to name the skill, the
  panel has not shown it.
- **The cycle is a story structure.** Missions 1-3 are a first act that resists the idea; Mission
  4 is the idea; Missions 5-8 are the world answering back. A story that starts at Mission 4 is
  the story the framework exists to argue against.
- **Failure is in the framework.** Persistence is defined by setbacks; courage by unpopularity;
  growth mindset by criticism. A page where the innovation simply works has dropped the content.
- **Power "with", not "over"** (4.1.2) is a usable dramatic distinction, and the clean opposite of
  a figure who believes his judgment should stand in for other people's.
- **Keep it honest.** The paper is about youth learning to act, not about young people fixing what
  institutions could not. Nothing here licenses a story where the community exists to be helped.
