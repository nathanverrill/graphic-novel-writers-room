# Cold Read: Prosperity

Read in one sitting, Issue 01 through Issue 03, as assigned homework the night before. I wanted it to be bad. Below is what actually happened to me.

---

## Issue 01: Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 3.** "Then don't approve it. Just notice it works." I liked him right away. A kid who fixes things and talks back to the rules is a person I can follow.
- **Page 2, the whole page.** This is the hook, and it got me. It's his birthday, he's seventeen, and the gate screen hands him 418,607 credits of his dad's gambling debt. He earned 40 today, it'll take 28.7 years, and he can't travel. Then "Happy birthday, Phantum." The workers going quiet behind him. I stopped looking for reasons to hate it for a second.
- **Page 6.** B11. "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." / "A fact with a poor record." The robot has a voice, and it's the first character I wanted more of.
- **Page 10, panel 4.** "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR" / "That is today's pay." / "I know." Helping the guy costs him the whole day's money, and I already know what 40 means to him because of page 2. That landed.
- **Page 11.** TJ, the dog built out of mine parts, sending its own head through a gap. "That dog yours?" "Recently." Funny.
- **Page 15, panel 1 and page 16, panel 4.** PHYSICAL FLOW 18%, REPORTED FLOW 62%, then it goes up on public screens across Keel. Finally I knew what the story was. Someone is stealing their water and lying about it. That was satisfying.
- **Page 18.** The climb with water rising behind them is a good image to end on.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3** is a math problem. Thirty, forty-two, fifty-two, fronted, thirty by next shift. I did the sums and still wasn't sure what he bought.
- **Page 4, panel 3.** The grandmother folding and refolding a cloth beside a blue cup. I could tell I was supposed to notice it. I didn't know why, so I moved on.
- **Page 7.** I skimmed the dad's recording. "Bring back the physical reading. Don't trust my conclusion." I didn't know what reading he meant.
- **Pages 15–17.** The hatch, the narrow channel, the conduit, the conduit wall, the ledge, the segment. By the middle of page 16 I'd stopped trying to picture where anyone was standing and just waited for them to get out.

### I was confused
- **Page 3: who is Ada?** She ties a medicine pouch at the pharmacy. Does she work there? Is she his friend, his girlfriend, a cousin? She decides to come with him on panel 4 and I have no idea what they are to each other. I never find out.
- **Page 3: the token.** He leaves it, then reaches for it, then she pushes it toward him. Did he buy it or not? Where does he get the money if the medicine is on credit and he's paying 30 next shift?
- **Page 4: "Site 6 took the shift."** He hasn't been sent to Site 6 yet. That happens on page 8, after the workshop. So either he was already working there or the order is wrong. I flipped back to check, and I hate flipping back.
- **Page 4: "The shift took your wages."** What? Page 2 said he earned 40. Where did it go?
- **Page 4, panel 4.** Grandma hands him the dad's book, he shelves it, and a secret bookcase opens. Why now? Did she know? Why hadn't anyone opened it before?
- **Page 8: Mera.** Who is she? Where is Oasis? She says "track," the system overrides her, and she seems fine with it: "Let the mechanic reach it." Is she the bad guy? Is she helping? By the end of the issue I honestly can't say. **Confused but intrigued**, for now.
- **Page 10, panel 1.** Tomas, under a collapsed frame: "That's not regulation." What isn't? The frame? The dust? That was my first look at this guy and I didn't understand his first line.
- **Page 16.** I didn't get the choice. The screen says CONSEQUENCE: ROUTE EXPOSURE / ACCESS CLOSURE. I only understood after the fact that sending the record shut their way out. **Confused and detached** here.
- **Page 17, panel 2.** Mera: "Open the segment. Not the homes." So she's helping them escape but keeping water from the homes? Or saving the homes? I don't know.

### Lines I read twice
- p1 "Then don't approve it. Just notice it works." (I liked this one, I just had to read it twice.)
- p4 "The shift took your wages. You still missed the date."
- p5 "He built and bet with the same hands."
- p12 "He ask you to keep him out?" / "He didn't ask enough." Read three times. Gave up.
- p13 "The survey says straight." / "The bolts say somebody turned." Got it on the second read: someone rerouted the pipe.
- p15 "One goes out unseen. One reaches the homes. The big pipe carries the pressure." Gave up. It sounds like a riddle from a video game.
- p17 "This gets us to the wall, not up it."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, B11, TJ, the grandmother.
- **Half:** Ada. I can tell she's tough and knows routes. I can't tell who she is to Alex. Inez has one setting, "I need the money," which is fine.
- **Blur:** the supervisor on p1, the guard on p2 and Foreman-7 all feel like the same rule-voice. Mera is a face at a screen.
- Tomas I could pick out, but I'd known him for about two pages.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the birthday. That's the one real gut-punch of the issue.
- Page 10, losing the day's pay to help someone.
- Page 18, Tomas's light going dark. I felt it a little, but I'd met him on page 10. It was more "oh no" than grief.

### I predicted
- Page 5, "Then he stopped coming home": the dad is dead somewhere down in the mine.
- Page 12, Tomas staying behind: he isn't really dead. Sidekicks who say "go" in the first issue come back.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A seventeen-year-old who inherits his gambler dad's huge debt finds the dad's robot and proves the city is stealing his neighborhood's water.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 16.** That's where I lost track of where they were and what they were choosing. Page 2 and page 6 would have got me back to the shop counter, but page 16 is where I'd have picked up my phone.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Somewhat. The climb is a good picture, and I want to know what happened to Tomas and what happened to the dad. It's a real question, but about people I've known for a few pages. I'd read Issue 02 this week. I wouldn't need it tonight.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 16; next issue: yes

## Issue 02: Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19, panel 1.** "That was not a route." / "It was a route. The destination objected." B11 is still the best thing in the book.
- **Page 21.** The dad has **312 patents**. Then the offer: sign them away, debt cleared tonight. That's a real temptation. I wanted to see what he'd do.
- **Page 25, panel 4 / page 26, panel 1.** "Take the part. I take the heat." Then Alex says "No" and goes back. A small moment, but it showed me who he is.
- **Page 27, panel 4.** "They were built not to meet." The parts were made on purpose so they wouldn't fit together. That's a genuinely good idea, and the issue's best moment for me.
- **Page 28, panel 4.** "I expected a longer failure." / "You can have one later." Got a laugh out of me.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 20.** A crate, a kiosk, an ownership label, then B11 says the lesson out loud: "One customer paid for a thing. One worker got paid for losing hers." This is where it started feeling like homework. The robot explained the moral before I'd had time to feel anything about it.
- **Page 22, panel 1.** Lock's "Scale buys materials, testing, distribution and protection from retaliation." That's a slide from a presentation.
- **Page 22, panel 3.** Four names in a list. I knew instantly I wouldn't remember any of them, and I didn't.
- **Pages 33–35.** Registry, record, clerk, attribution, mismatch, publication control, license, safety documentation. I skimmed all of it and only read the dialogue.
- **Page 29.** "Industrial flow. Industrial heat. Industrial staff." Then on page 28: "The node sends." / "The membrane receives." / "The casing chokes." Everyone talks in matching three-part lines. Honestly, this is the kind of thing that makes people in my class say "AI wrote this." It's too tidy.

### I was confused
- **Page 19, panel 4.** A House courier, Lock's seal, Charter review. Three new words in one panel, none explained. "They brought the water up with us." I think it means the evidence followed them, but I'm not sure.
- **Page 21 vs page 22.** If the dad had 312 patents, why is the family 418,000 in debt? Then: how are four strangers' purifier parts part of my dad's estate? "I need to see the people before I sell what they made." Did the dad steal their work? Did the House? I never got it straight.
- **Page 23, panel 1.** "Keel's line was diverted. This feeder is contaminated. Different failure." I don't know what she's explaining or why it matters.
- **Pages 23–26.** I didn't know whether Alex, Ada and these four were breaking in, breaking out, or just doing their jobs during a patrol. Is this a heist? Are they going to get arrested?
- **Page 32, panel 2.** "She paid before seeing it." / "She saw the failure list." **Who is she?** I have no idea. Is this Morrow & Sons? Is Morrow & Sons a person? A shop? Whose?
- **Page 35.** The collaborators get "reassignment notices." Is that a punishment? Did they win or lose? "The Houses will still build it." "Yes." So what was the point? I think the point is that it's free for everyone now, but the book made me work that out myself.
- **Where is Ada?** She's in most of the panels this issue and does almost nothing. One line on p31 and one on p32.
- **Tomas.** Page 19 says UNRESOLVED and then he's dropped for the whole issue.
- **Page 36.** Mera again. Two issues in and I still don't know who she works for or what she wants.

### Lines I read twice
- p19 "They brought the water up with us."
- p20 "One customer paid for a thing. One worker got paid for losing hers."
- p23 "Nobody whose name they kept." Read twice, still unsure.
- p24 "If they take the name, they take the maintenance."
- p24 "We finish before they can bury the correction." What correction?

- p30 "I counted the record and missed the people." I understood it. It's the lesson again, in bold.
- p36 "Let them see who called him." Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, B11, Lock. Lock is a guy in a suit with an offer, which is easy to hold onto.
- **Could not:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. I knew them by their part, not by a personality. Ren maybe edges ahead because of "I take the heat." If you swapped Lina's and Suri's lines I wouldn't notice.
- Ada has faded into the background.

### I felt something
- Page 26, Alex going back for Ren. A little.
- Page 27, panel 4, the parts being designed not to meet. That was more anger than feeling, and it was the closest I got to caring about the House stuff.
- Nothing at the publication scene on pages 34–35. It's a form being filed.

### I predicted
- Page 21: he refuses Lock. Obviously. The only question was when.
- Page 29: they'll make it smaller and it'll work. They did.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex refuses to sell his dad's patents to a corporation and instead helps four workers put their names on a water purifier anyone can build.

(This is a different book from Issue 01. Issue 01 was about stolen water and a missing dad. Issue 02 is about patents and credit. The water and the dad basically vanish.)

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 22.** Between Lock's speech, the list of four names and a plot I couldn't connect to the stolen water, this is where I'd have closed it. If I'd somehow kept going, pages 33–35 would have finished me off.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The cliffhanger is a notification on a wristband from a woman I still don't know. That isn't a cliffhanger, it's a calendar invite. What would actually pull me into Issue 03 is the dad and Tomas, and this issue didn't touch either one.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 22; next issue: no

## Issue 03: Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 38.** Ada finally gets a last name and a past. "I know someone who made it hard to leave." / "Wait here." / "You can wait." For the first time she's a person instead of a guide.
- **Page 39.** The best scene in the book. "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" / "Did you know where I was?" / "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." Short, mean, clear, and it hurt.
- **Page 43.** The dad wasn't just a gambler. The system slowly shut him out. He couldn't buy parts, then people stopped selling to him, then "people stopped standing near him." That changed how I saw him, and it worked.
- **Page 45.** The dad's last recording. "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." I'd been waiting since page 5 and it paid off.
- **Page 46, panel 4.** "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." This was good. It's B11's best line, and it's barely a line.
- **Page 51.** Tomas, alive, with a hand gauge. "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." That called back to Issue 01 and I actually remembered the numbers. "Had work to do." Cheap or not, I was glad.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37.** It opens on a review room, two people I've never met, a "temporary cross-city diversion," a "declared interest," and three physical instruments on a table. I skimmed the whole page.
- **Pages 41–42.** Bands linking, stamps descending, "TWO OF THREE — EMERGENCY AUTHORIZATION." I read the words and didn't take them in.
- **Pages 47–50.** Measure, Seal, bands, a lever, a designation field, "CONVENOR OF PROPOSAL / NO EXECUTIVE DESIGNATION." I skimmed to see whether Alex pulled the lever, and that's all I took from four pages.
- **Pages 52–53.** It's a meeting. Then they schedule another meeting.

### I was confused
- **Page 37.** Who are Nadi Ruiz and Clerk Olani? Why are we in a review room? I just got a summons. Is this the summons?
- **Page 39, panel 2.** "Covenant band." What is it? Why does it warm up on page 40? Is it magic? Tech? A vote?
- **Is Leona Ada's mother?** The book never says the word. I assumed so from the scene, but I'm guessing.
- **Pages 41–42.** I put this together slowly: Adrian, Leona and Mera were three people who voted to give the system emergency powers, forgot to give it an end date, and it turned on them. If that's right, it's a big reveal, and it was delivered through screen text about "independent revocation." I'm still not sure I'm right.
- **Page 42, panel 4.** "Then what does the link permit?" / "A promise to be seen." No idea.
- **Page 45.** "If I stay, the record stays with the machine." Did he stay so the recording would survive? Did he drown? I think he drowned. The book doesn't quite say.
- **Page 48.** What does the lever do? The screen lists DEBT, TRAVEL, KEEL WATER ROUTE, HOUSE REVIEW. Does it mean he could just fix everything? Mera says "Take it." Ada says don't. Then he doesn't, and I don't know what he gave up.
- **Page 50.** Debt unchanged, travel expired, workshop sealed. So after three issues, the main character's situation is worse? Part of me respected that. Part of me felt cheated.
- **Page 54, panel 1. Mara vs Mera.** I thought Mera had shown up in Keel paying for a repair. It took me a second read to see it's a different name. Two characters, one letter apart.
- **Page 54: Morrow & Sons "beneath the restored sign."** Is this Alex's shop now? His family's? His name is Phantum. I don't get it.
- **Page 54, panel 4.** Ada keeps "the route back to Keel," then steps into the *departure* queue. So is she leaving or staying? "Which one are you taking?" / "The one I choose." I read it three times and still don't know where Ada went. Also, "Halyard lights come on above them": they're in Keel, so is Halyard literally up?
- **The cloth.** Folded on p4, unfolded on p54. I can tell it means something. I don't know what.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, B11, Ada (finally), Leona, Tomas.
- **Half:** Mera. I know what she did now. I still don't feel like I know her.
- **Couldn't:** Nadi and Olani are two names at a table. Mera and Mara are the same name to my eyes. TJ disappears into the background; it just taps its magnet on p50. Lock is gone after Issue 02, and I'd assumed he was the villain.

### I felt something
- Page 39, Ada and her mother. Yes.
- Page 45, the dad: "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." Yes.
- Page 46, "Also yes." Yes, the most I felt in the whole book.
- Page 51, Tomas alive. A little, even though I saw it coming.

### I predicted
- Tomas alive (I called it on p12).
- The dad dead in the station (called on p5).
- Alex not pulling the lever. Once Ada said "Don't make two people the answer," it was obvious.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid finds out his dad was shut out by a system his dad helped give too much power to, and he refuses to take that power himself, even though nothing gets fixed quickly.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 37.** I open the last issue, the summons from the end of Issue 02 should pay off, and instead I get two new people and paperwork on a table. If I hadn't been assigned this, I'd have stopped here and never reached page 39, which is the best page in the book.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
This is the end of the book, so the question is whether I'd buy another one. No. The last page is quiet and has some nice callbacks (Inez paid, the token, the cloth), but I don't know where Ada went, the debt is still 402,000, and nobody is in danger. It's an ending, not a reason to come back. I'd want a book about B11. I wouldn't want a book about another review hearing.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 37; next issue: no

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 20.** Up to there it's a survival story about a kid with an impossible debt and a funny robot, and people would have kept going. Page 20 is a crate, a kiosk with OWNERSHIP: CALDERA HOUSE, and then the robot says the lesson out loud. That's the moment my class decides it's an "educational program" book, and maybe an AI one. The matching three-part lines on pages 28–29 would confirm it for them. Anyone still reading after that would be lost on page 22 or 37.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dead dad's gambling debt, finds his robot, and discovers the city's stealing their water. Then it turns into a lot of meetings about patents and voting. The robot's good, and there's one scene with a girl and her mom that's actually good. You can skip to those."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A strong birthday-debt opening and a great robot, and then I lost track of where anyone was standing in the pipes.
- **Issue 02:** A fair point about stolen credit, made by four people I couldn't tell apart, with the robot reading out the lesson.
- **Issue 03:** Two scenes that really hit (Ada's mom, the dad's goodbye) buried in levers, bands and a hearing that ends by scheduling another hearing.
