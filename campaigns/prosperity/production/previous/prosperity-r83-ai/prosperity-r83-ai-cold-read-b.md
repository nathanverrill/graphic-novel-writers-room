### Issue 01

#### Pulled me forward
Page 2 pulled me in immediately. The giant floating debt screen (418,607 credits) and the "28.7 years" clearance is a brutal, clear problem. I also liked Page 5 and 6 when the secret bookcase opened and the little robot (B11) unfolded. The end of the issue (Pages 17-18) with the water rising in the pipe and Tomas's light going dark was a great trap.

#### I slowed down / skimmed
I slowed down around Pages 14 and 15. Alex and B11 start measuring water percentages and talking about bypasses and data lines. It felt like a math word problem.

#### I was confused
I didn't understand Mera's motivations on Page 8. She sees a "legacy pulse," gets denied an override, and then reassigns Alex to Site 6. Did she want him to find the secret pipe, or was she trying to get him killed? 

#### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel (Is it a city? A company?)
- Node (Some kind of AI camera?)
- Oasis (A place? A rival company?)
- Site 6

#### Lines I read twice
- "A fact with a poor record." (B11, Page 6, Panel 3)
- "The bolts say somebody turned." (Ada, Page 13, Panel 4)

#### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I could easily tell Alex (debt kid), Ada (friend), B11 (robot), and Tomas (grumpy miner) apart. Mera was just "lady looking at screens."

#### I felt something
Page 10, Panel 4. Alex steps over the line to save Tomas and instantly loses his 40 credits for the day. That stung.

#### I predicted
I predicted Alex would use his dad's secret map to find a hidden water stash, sell it, and clear his massive debt.

#### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teen trying to clear his dead dad's massive debt by finding a secret underground water source.

#### Where I would have put it down
Page 15. The whole bit with the brass Measure and the "Physical flow: 18% / Reported flow: 62%" felt like a textbook.

#### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. They are trapped in a pipe, water is rushing up, and Tomas's life sign just went out. It's a real cliffhanger.

`VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 15; next issue: yes`

### Issue 02

#### Pulled me forward
Page 21. Lock slides the paper across the table and offers to clear the 400k debt tonight. It was a massive temptation and I wanted to see if Alex would take it.

#### I slowed down / skimmed
I heavily skimmed Pages 23 through 29. The whole sequence of running around the Caldera maintenance floor gathering four random people to build a water filter was exhausting. It felt like a video game fetch quest full of tech jargon.

#### I was confused
I was completely lost on the politics. What is Halyard? What is Caldera House? How does the "Maker's Seal" work? Also, why are we suddenly building a water purifier when the last issue ended with them discovering a massive city-wide water theft? It felt like we changed plots entirely.

#### Things I was expected to already know
- Halyard
- Charter kiosk
- Caldera House
- Maker's Seal

#### Lines I read twice
- "One customer paid for a thing. One worker got paid for losing hers." (B11, Page 20, Panel 4)
- "If it fails, it fails where I can touch it." (Ren, Page 26, Panel 4)

#### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I completely lost track of Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren. They were just "the four tech people" who yelled out different parts of a machine. Lock was easy to spot as the corporate bad guy.

#### I felt something
Page 34, Panel 4. When Alex tells the clerk to just publish the record publicly so everyone can have it, rather than selling out to Lock.

#### I predicted
I predicted the House would just steal their invention anyway and mass-produce it. (Which they did on Page 32).

#### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A group of underground mechanics fighting a mega-corporation for the patents to their inventions.

#### Where I would have put it down
Page 27. Watching four characters I just met stare at a cloudy water vessel while yelling things like "No draw" and "Heat trapped" was incredibly boring.

#### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The last page is just Mera looking at a screen and sending Alex a calendar invite to come to Oasis. It's not a cliffhanger at all.

`VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 27; next issue: no`

### Issue 03

#### Pulled me forward
Pages 45 and 46. The archival recording of Adrian locking himself in the room as the water rises. It finally explained what happened to his dad and why he left B11 behind.

#### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 47 through 50. The entire climax of the book is a bureaucratic meeting about a "designation lever" and an "allocation stamp." I skimmed all the dialogue about physical shifts and public records.

#### I was confused
The entire conversation between Ada and Leona (Pages 38-40) was a giant riddle. What is a Covenant band? Is Leona her mom? What did Leona actually do to her? They just spoke in vague poetry ("I saw days I could prevent. I didn't see the years I was making.") and I had no idea what they were talking about. 
Also, on Page 48, Alex has the chance to wipe his debt and fix the water, but he just... doesn't? I didn't understand why he refused to pull the lever.

#### Things I was expected to already know
- Covenant band
- Orbital Industrial Branch
- Legacy panel

#### Lines I read twice
- "I saw days I could prevent. I didn't see the years I was making." (Leona, Page 40, Panel 1)
- "A promise to be seen." (Mera, Page 42, Panel 4)

#### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Nadi and Olani were just talking heads at a desk. Leona was "Ada's mom?". Tomas coming back was awesome and easy to track.

#### I felt something
Page 51, Panel 4. Tomas showing up alive, walking right up to the camera, and proving the pipe is dry. "Pipe says eighteen." That was a great payoff.

#### I predicted
I predicted Alex would magically wipe his debt and fix the water for everyone using the legacy node. I was wrong.

#### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid realizing that fixing a broken system takes years of boring meetings and slow work, not just one magic button.

#### Where I would have put it down
Page 39. The dialogue between Ada and Leona was too cryptic and annoying. I would have closed the book there.

#### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The story is over. He's just going to keep paying off his debt slowly for the next decade. I wouldn't buy a sequel.

`VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 39; next issue: no`

### Whole Book

#### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 27. Four random characters doing a simulated water recovery test while yelling out tech jargon. We would all be on our phones.

#### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid inherits a massive debt and finds his dad's secret robot, but instead of a cool adventure, they just go to a bunch of city council meetings about water pipes and patents.

#### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: Cool setup with the debt and the robot, ending in a solid trap.
- Issue 02: A boring side-quest to build a water filter with people I don't care about.
- Issue 03: A confusing mix of vague family drama and literal bureaucratic paperwork.
