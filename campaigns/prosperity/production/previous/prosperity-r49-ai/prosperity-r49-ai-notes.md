# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a rethink.** The brief and facts establish a coherent 54-page book, but the current script and development material contradict fixed decisions about the crossing, Alex’s debt, Foreman-7, the safeguard sequence, and the causal order of the final protocol. The strongest spine remains Alex’s debt, public verification, shared ownership, Ada’s confrontation with Leona, and refusal of sole authority; repair the canon-breaking beats before further layout or polish.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. The ending voids Alex’s debt, contradicting fixed canon

- **Location:** Script, Page 54, Panel 3; `story.md`, Page 54; `brief.md`, Canon and Ending State; `facts.md`, “Ending state” and “Economics and labor.”
- **Observation:** The script displays `HOUSEHOLD-TRANSFER LIABILITY: VOIDED` and suspends Alex’s travel restriction. The brief and facts require Alex’s existing debt to remain after registration, with a balance of **402,110 credits** on return to Keel. The Distributed Verification Protocol does not erase debt.
- **Problem:** This reverses the book’s established promise: Alex’s final freedom is the freedom to choose despite still owing the debt, not debt cancellation. It also makes the Page 2 debt hook and Page 53 statement that debt remains internally contradictory.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: restore the 402,110-credit debt as legally owed at the ending. If travel status changes, distinguish that administrative change from debt cancellation and preserve ordinary review of the account. Director: resolve whether any travel suspension is part of the approved ending; do not let the protocol erase the liability.
- **Role:** Writer, Director.

#### 2. Issue 01 uses the forbidden train crossing

- **Location:** Script, Issue 01 Page 15, Panels 1–7; `brief.md`, Format and Non-negotiables; `facts.md`, Transit and movement.
- **Observation:** Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot board an industrial train and ride upward into Halyard. Fixed canon requires the principal Keel-to-Halyard crossing to descend through the old mine works and ascend inside the near-empty water conduit; Issue 01 ends during that climb and Issue 02 opens in a Halyard storm drain.
- **Problem:** This is a direct contradiction of a fixed showrunner rule and removes the physical consequence of Alex’s Page 18 choice. It also makes the conduit cliffhanger impossible as the actual crossing.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: replace the entire Page 15 train sequence with the established conduit climb, preserving the Issue 01 endpoint: the four travelers are inside the conduit and water begins filling behind them. Do not retain the train as an alternate route or imply that the group takes both routes.
- **Role:** Writer, Director.

#### 3. The script’s Issue 01 water cause conflicts with the established Mera intervention

- **Location:** Script, Issue 01 Page 18, Panel 3; Page 18, Panel 4; `story.md`, Page 17–18; `facts.md`, Water infrastructure and Transit.
- **Observation:** The script shows Keel workers physically seizing and opening the regulator, then water rushes into the conduit. Canon requires Mera to knowingly authorize or trigger the conduit’s water release, while the workers respond to the public discrepancy by forcing the Keel response.
- **Problem:** The causal chain makes the Page 18 human question—whether Mera opened or trapped the group—unanswerable for the wrong reason. The reader sees an apparent worker-triggered cause rather than an intentionally ambiguous Mera action.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: separate the actions visually and causally. Keel workers respond to the broadcast at the residential regulator; independently, Mera’s intervention permits or triggers the conduit fill. Preserve ambiguity about whether her intervention is rescue, containment, or testing, but do not attribute the conduit fill to the workers.
- **Role:** Writer, Director.

#### 4. Foreman-7 is rendered as a machine, contradicting the character and world files

- **Location:** `draft-final.md`, Issue 01 Page 6 style block; `draft-edited.md`, Issue 01 Page 6 style block; `characters.md`, Foreman-7; `facts.md`, Tomas and Foreman-7.
- **Observation:** The draft describes Foreman-7 as “an industrial humanoid with a smooth, featureless metal face” and a chest node. Canon defines Foreman-7 as a human Keel extraction foreman: a large, broad-shouldered man with a shaved head, medium-brown skin, dark eyes, and a throat-mounted authority node.
- **Problem:** This changes the social conflict from a human worker enforcing procedure to an embodied robot authority, undermining the fixed character premise that Foreman-7 has no personal ideology beyond executing Site 6 procedure.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Character Designer: restore Foreman-7’s human visual lock word for word. Layout and image prompts must show a human face and body; retain the white-and-amber node as an administrative device, not his identity.
- **Role:** Character Designer, Layout.

### Major

#### 5. The current final-act order does not match the approved causal structure

- **Location:** Script, Pages 44–49 versus `story.md`, Pages 43–53 and `brief.md`, Issue 03 structure.
- **Observation:** The script activates a new Covenant procedure through Mara, Frank, and Elias on Pages 44–48, then reveals Adrian’s death on Pages 50–51, and only afterward has Mera offer Alex executive authority on Pages 52–53. Approved story order is: Mera and Adrian’s station; Leona’s obligation and Adrian–Leona band link; Mera’s executive offer; Alex’s refusal; Distributed Verification Protocol registration; first public review on Pages 52–54.
- **Problem:** The script makes the representatives’ “Charter” the climax before Alex has made the defining choice. It also makes the Covenant appear to restore itself independently of Leona’s public obligation and risks replacing the approved protocol with a different governance mechanism.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter/Writer: restore the causal sequence rather than merely relabeling the current scenes. Leona’s public obligation must precede the Adrian–Leona band link; Mera’s offer must be the immediate temptation; Alex’s refusal must create the distributed protocol; the three-city representatives must appear in the first review as a consequence, not as the mechanism that activates the Covenant earlier.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer, Director.

#### 6. The “Charter” terminology creates a world-rule contradiction

- **Location:** Script, Page 49, Panel 4; Pages 20–24; `world.md`, Charter Houses and Distributed-verification proposal; `facts.md`.
- **Observation:** The script calls the new civic compact “the Charter” after repeatedly using Charter Houses as the major Halyard institutions. The two terms are not distinguished on the page.
- **Problem:** Readers may infer that the Charter Houses and the restored civic compact are the same institution, or that the Houses are being dissolved. Canon requires Charter Houses to remain powerful after the protocol.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director/Writer: choose distinct terminology for the civic process and the Houses, or explicitly establish that “Charter House” refers to the colony’s institutional corporations while the new process has the fixed name **Distributed Verification Protocol**. Do not call the protocol simply “the Charter” unless the Director approves that distinction and the text makes it unambiguous.
- **Role:** Director, Writer.

#### 7. Lock’s offer is missing from its first dramatic appearance

- **Location:** Script, Page 21, Panels 3–5; Page 28.
- **Observation:** Lock appears and calls Alex “an heir,” but the script omits the actual offer before Alex asks “In exchange for what?” The concrete terms—debt clearance, Halyard position, and assignment of Adrian’s estate—arrive only later and remain spatially disconnected.
- **Problem:** The central Issue 02 conflict begins with a dialogue gap. The reader cannot tell what Alex is responding to or what Lock wants, and the ownership dilemma loses causality.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: give Lock one complete, concrete offer at his first approach: assign the estate to House administration in exchange for debt clearance, legal/movement relief, and a Halyard position. Alex’s “In exchange for what?” must directly answer that offer, and Page 28 should escalate the same offer rather than introduce it.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 8. The Measure, Maker’s Seal, and Covenant are introduced as simultaneous definitions

- **Location:** Script, Issue 01 Page 11; `story.md`, Issue 01 Page 7 and Proposal 1; cold reads.
- **Observation:** Bi11bot displays all three objects and gives each a conceptual explanation before any immediate human action demonstrates its limits.
- **Problem:** This is the documented Issue 01 drop-off point. The reader cannot retain three names, three functions, and the “dormant fail-safe” language at once. It also makes the safeguards feel magical and pre-explained rather than physical systems whose consequences emerge through choices.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: replace the lecture with one concrete incident, using the objects only as evidence or tools inside that incident. Give the Measure one clear physical comparison; show the Seal recording a named contribution; leave the Covenant inert because no reciprocal obligation has yet occurred. Do not explain the complete constitutional handoff there.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 9. The script introduces a second water problem without distinguishing it from the first

- **Location:** Script, Issue 01 Pages 14–18 and Issue 02 Page 24.
- **Observation:** Issue 01 exposes a maintained routing/accounting discrepancy: 62% reported to Keel, 18% delivered. Issue 02 then introduces a contaminated feeder channel and says the earlier event bought Keel only “a few months.”
- **Problem:** The relationship between diversion, contamination, and the prototype is not legible. The reader may think the Page 18 discrepancy was solved, contradicted, or replaced.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter/Writer: explicitly separate the problems through consequence. The Measure exposes the accounting/routing discrepancy; restoring flow does not repair contaminated feedwater or guarantee future supply; Lina’s feeder-channel crisis is the next, materially different problem. Use one visual callback to the 62%/18% display and one visible contamination consequence rather than repeating exposition.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer.

#### 10. The Leona/Ada emotional sequence violates the standing mother rule in its draft language

- **Location:** `draft.md`, Issue 03 Page 38; `draft-final.md`, Issue 03 Page 38; `taste-writers.md`, standing rules.
- **Observation:** The draft says Ada believed Leona “died,” and the script says, “You thought she was dead” / “I thought she left after she died.”
- **Problem:** The showrunner’s standing rule says only that Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything and that nothing else about Alex’s mother is learned. This is about Ada’s mother, not Alex’s, so the material is not directly prohibited; however, the line is grammatically and causally confusing, and the draft’s wording makes Ada’s belief unclear.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: state the intended fact plainly without adding new history: Ada believed Leona had disappeared or died during the Water Wars; she now learns Leona survived and knew where she was. Do not use “left after she died,” which makes the event temporally impossible.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 11. Mera’s protection of Ada and tracking of Alex need a clear information hierarchy

- **Location:** Script, Issue 01 Page 9; Issue 03 Page 41; `facts.md`, Mera and Leona; `story.md`, Reader vs character knowledge.
- **Observation:** Mera chooses TRACK on Alex, later says she suppressed Ada’s association risk, and appears to have monitored Ada for years. The script does not distinguish Mera’s purpose, scope, or reporting channel.
- **Problem:** The reader may interpret Mera as both protecting Ada and actively hunting Alex without understanding that Alpha controls reporting and can continue tracking beyond Mera’s direct intervention.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: plant one concrete distinction earlier: Mera tracks Alex to observe the safeguards and withholds immediate escalation; separately, she suppresses Ada’s association risk to honor Leona’s bargain. Later establish that Alpha can still receive or infer reports outside Mera’s control. Preserve Mera’s moral ambiguity; do not make her secretly benevolent.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 12. The executive succession has no established trigger or standing

- **Location:** Script, Issue 03 Pages 50–53; `world.md`, Safeguards; `facts.md`, The Covenant and safeguards.
- **Observation:** Alpha displays `AUTHORIZED HUMAN SUCCESSOR: ALEX PHANTUM` and offers permanent authority, but no preceding page establishes how Alex alone acquires standing or why the system recognizes him.
- **Problem:** The offer reads as an arbitrary authorial reward and conflicts with the canon that a human with standing must designate the next authority or structure. It also makes Adrian appear to have secretly installed Alex as heir to the government without the story confronting that implication.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director/Plotter: establish the standing chain before the offer. The safeguards authenticate Alex as claimant, witness, and convenor, but do not make him sole owner or automatic ruler. Mera, as remaining original custodian, may designate him as a temporary human interlocutor, but the scene must state that he can accept a structure or refuse concentration—not simply inherit Alpha.
- **Role:** Director, Plotter, Writer.

#### 13. The first public review is not yet the approved first human process

The approved first process is a public, appealable review of **Keel’s water allocation**, with Keel, Halyard, and Oasis representatives publishing evidence, hearing affected residents, and issuing a temporary allocation order.

- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter/Writer: make Pages 52–54 a compact water-allocation review. Show the evidence display, named representatives, affected Keel residents, disagreement over a temporary order, Alpha recording/executing the human decision, and the appeal marker. Leave debt, House ownership, and existing allocations pending rather than automatically changing them.

#### 14. Issue 02 compresses away the collaborators’ causal differences

## Plausibility ledger

- **Fixed format:** The brief and facts agree that the book is three issues, exactly 54 pages, with 18 pages per issue. Any inherited “chapter” numbering or chapter-ending language is a production inconsistency, not new canon.
- **Crossing:** The train is available in the world but explicitly prohibited as the principal Issue 01 Keel-to-Halyard crossing. The current script’s Page 15 train scene contradicts a fixed fact and must be removed.
- **Water release:** The Measure reveals and verifies the discrepancy; it does not move water. The workers’ response, Mera’s conduit intervention, and the physical water flow must be separate causal events.
- **Alpha:** Alpha is a distributed administrative network, not a human face, robot body, or single conscious villain. Any depiction of Foreman-7 as a machine risks conflating a human enforcer with Alpha.
- **Safeguards:** Measure, Maker’s Seal, and Covenant are physical, bounded, tamper-evident systems. They respond to demonstrated choices and consequences; they are not magical authorization keys.
- **Covenant activation:** Possession does not activate a band. The original three-custodian Covenant is not restored; Mera’s band remains dark. Adrian’s and Leona’s bands link only after Leona accepts a public obligation.
- **Distributed protocol:** The protocol rescinds Alpha’s emergency authority but does not erase debt, ownership, allocations, House power, or existing contracts.
- **Debt:** 418,607 credits at transfer and 402,110 credits at return are established facts. The script’s `HOUSEHOLD-TRANSFER LIABILITY: VOIDED` contradicts this truth.
- **Prototype:** The 91% number is simulated recovery efficiency, not potable-water output. Any 99.97% result must be scoped to a different controlled test or removed.
- **Hard-SF frame:** The compact unit requires feedwater, power, membrane cleaning, living biofilter maintenance, and mineral-waste handling. It cannot replace Keel’s main infrastructure.
- **Halyard ownership:** The Maker’s Seal restores attribution and opens human review; it does not automatically transfer title or dissolve House ownership.
- **Foreman-7:** The facts and character file establish a human Site 6 foreman. The draft’s humanoid-machine description is an undeclared invention that contradicts the character lock.
- **Adrian’s mother:** The showrunner rule remains intact in the development files: Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything, and nothing else about her is learned. No license or proposal may expand that history.
