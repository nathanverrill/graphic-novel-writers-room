# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is seventeen, lean, wiry, and approximately 5'10", with a mechanically capable build, scarred work-rough hands, medium-brown skin, a broad angular face, a straight nose, dark almond-shaped eyes, and thick black hair cut short at the sides and left uneven on top. He visibly resembles his European-descended father Adrian through his jaw, eyes, head tilt, and guarded expressions, while his substantial Indigenous Andean inheritance is visible in his skin tone, cheekbones, and facial structure; he must look like Adrian's son, never like a younger duplicate. He wears worn charcoal-and-rust mining clothes, a patched dark work jacket, heavy work boots, a weathered tool bag, and a dark wrist guard bearing a carved balanced water-drop mark. His silhouette is narrow, practical, and slightly hunched forward as if always listening to machinery. His signature detail is the worn casino chip etched with a looping equation, which he turns in his pocket without noticing.

**Voice** — Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete, skeptical sentences. His humor reduces immediate danger to something he can handle; when he reaches a larger idea, he translates it into a pipe, a price, a choice, or a thing someone did. He uses Keel words such as *clean, hot, burned, crooked, thin, solid, patched, ghost, paper-clean, fitch,* and *salvo*.  
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”  
- “This is the only way out that isn't a sentence.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel pump and machinery repairer.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and escape Adrian’s inherited debt and restricted future.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to understand that freedom is not only distance from Keel; it is the ability to choose responsibility without being forced into it.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reads vibration, pressure, wear, improvised repairs, and physical evidence better than official gauges or records.
- **Vulnerability:** He fears becoming Adrian: absent, indebted, selfish, and remembered only through damage.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he can get out, the debt and the people trapped beneath it will stop being his problem.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not willingly surrender his future or let an institution define what he is allowed to become.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He sacrifices the private escape route revealed by the Measure and sends the water discrepancy to Keel, choosing public truth over his own safe exit.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada refuses to let him turn shared responsibility into a two-person authority; Adrian’s legacy forces Alex to distinguish inheritance from ownership.
- **Pressure sequence:** He crosses the assigned-zone line to save Tomas, then gives up the unmapped route to verify the water discrepancy, then refuses Lock’s debt-clearing estate transfer, and finally refuses executive control of Alpha.

**Arc in this book** — Alex begins believing Keel has already sentenced him to 28.7 years of debt. He ends with the freedom to leave or stay as a genuine choice. He does not erase his debt, become a ruler, or claim Adrian’s collaborative work. **Proof of change:** He returns to Keel with a balance of 402,110 credits still attached to his name and chooses to stand beside Ada because he wants to, not because the system permits no alternative.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is twenty, tall and steady at approximately 5'8", with a strong, lean build, medium warm-brown skin, a broad expressive face, high cheekbones, freckles across her nose and cheeks, light teal-blue eyes, and long copper-brown hair that is thick, wind-tangled, and usually tied loosely at the nape. She is mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla; she must visibly resemble Leona through her eyes, cheekbones, and steady gaze while remaining distinct from Alex. She wears a dark high-collared work top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, reinforced trousers, practical boots, and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her silhouette is rooted and balanced, with one shoulder often angled toward the person she is watching. Her signature detail is her thumb touching one rivet on the shoulder strap when she is thinking or withholding a reaction.

**Voice** — Ada shares Keel’s compressed, local vocabulary but is more attentive to people, absences, promises, and what someone refuses to say. She lets silence sit and asks questions that make avoidance impossible. She never sounds therapeutic or academic.  
- “Not everything.”  
- “I just wanted it to be your choice.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel organizer, route-reader, practical companion, and civic counterweight to Alex.
- **Immediate want:** Discover who decided Keel had to remain as it was and make sure affected people can answer back.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to allow Leona to keep trying without mistaking continued effort for repaired trust.
- **Leverage / capability:** She reads people, routes, omissions, and social networks; she organizes food, tools, papers, and mutual aid quickly.
- **Vulnerability:** She fears that safety, distance, or institutional language will again be used to remove her choices.
- **Mistaken belief:** People in Keel have stopped trying because they no longer care; she gradually sees how often the system makes trying costly or invisible.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not abandon Keel or let someone else decide what sacrifice is acceptable for its people.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She refuses Alex’s offer to share executive authority, insisting that two people deciding for everyone else is still the same failure.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Leona’s abandonment forces Ada to distinguish apology, safety, accountability, and agency; Alex pressures her to choose companionship without making it another obligation.
- **Pressure sequence:** She joins Alex’s secret, marks Adrian’s map from memory, finds a second route into the old works, organizes Keel’s help, confronts Leona, and demands that Leona support the new Covenant publicly.

**Arc in this book** — Ada begins with a private conviction that nobody truly stays in Keel. She ends by choosing to remain beside Alex without surrendering her own choice or claiming power over Keel. **Proof of change:** She tells Leona, “You can keep trying,” without calling the relationship repaired, and stands beside Alex because both of them are free to leave.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a child-sized, foldable repair robot approximately 3'6" tall when upright, visibly assembled from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory with repaired seams, small articulated hands, compact folded legs, a fitted chest compartment, a shoulder-mounted projector, and two large expressive optical eyes with soft blue-and-amber light. His rounded head has a rotating lens ring and a friendly, readable face; his body is compact and slightly asymmetrical from years of repairs. He wears an old, visibly worn scarf sized for a small robot wherever he appears. His silhouette is domestic, tool-like, and non-menacing, never looming or predatory. His signature detail is the old scarf tucked beneath his ceramic collar.

**Voice** — Bi11bot speaks economically and precisely, with dry humor, engineering language, probability, gambling, and practical financial terms. He asks questions, names costs, and presents alternatives; he never lectures and never chooses for Alex.  
- “Protective? A versatile word.”  
- “Which is why I'm not choosing.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Adrian’s companion, archive, evaluator, mentor, witness, and inherited guide.
- **Immediate want:** Carry Adrian’s evidence to Alex and help Alex reach a choice worth recording.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to stop treating Adrian’s final wager as a test he alone must deliver correctly.
- **Leverage / capability:** He carries maps, recordings, technical knowledge, offline-resistant tools, and a projector; he can fold into a tool bag and pass as scrap.
- **Vulnerability:** He remembers Adrian’s final seconds incompletely and fears Adrian’s bet about Alex may have been wrong.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he withholds information until the correct moment, he can protect Alex from the burden of choosing.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not decide for a human being, even when the consequences of delay are dangerous.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He builds TJ because a problem has become inaccessible to him, accepting that useful care sometimes means creating another agent rather than remaining indispensable.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s insistence on questioning everything forces Bi11bot to replace custodial silence with honest disclosure.
- **Pressure sequence:** He reveals Adrian’s message, gives Alex the Measure’s alternatives without choosing, builds TJ at Site 6, discloses Adrian’s last moments, and names Alex’s distributed protocol as Alex’s own decision.

**Arc in this book** — Bi11bot begins as the controlled keeper of Adrian’s answers. He ends as a witness who admits his uncertainty and recognizes that Alex’s fourth choice belongs to Alex. **Proof of change:** He says the wager has been “on us” for fifteen years and stops treating the plan’s completion as his private burden.

### TJ

**Visual lock** — TJ is a small, unmistakably dog-shaped repair robot approximately 18 inches long, built from mismatched magnetic spare parts: a dark iron head, brass and titanium body segments, ceramic-white joint pieces, short magnetic legs, and a narrow segmented tail. His head, legs, body segments, and tail are visibly separate pieces that can detach and snap back together. His optical sensor is a single soft amber point, never a face or a threatening eye. His silhouette remains dog-like even when disassembled: low body, pointed head, four short legs, and a tail that lifts when alert. His signature detail is one slightly oversized rear leg that clicks into place half a beat after the others.

**Voice** — TJ never speaks. He communicates through posture, head angle, tail position, magnetic clicks, small servo whines, and practical movement. His sounds are never language or subtitles.  
- “KLIK-KLIK.”  
- “Whirr—tik.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Silent repair scout and inaccessible-mechanism specialist.
- **Immediate want:** Reach the jammed part nobody else can reach and make the route usable.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** No personal need is established; his function is to turn Bi11bot’s care into practical shared capacity.
- **Leverage / capability:** Magnetic separation, narrow-gap movement, detachable camera head, and dog-like scouting.
- **Vulnerability:** His pieces can be lost, misaligned, or trapped separately.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established.
- **Boundary at the start:** He does not speak, threaten, or make decisions for the group.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** He separates his own body and sends the smallest component into the trapped mechanism at Site 6.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot built TJ because he could not solve the problem alone; TJ makes their partnership visibly reciprocal.
- **Pressure sequence:** Bi11bot assembles him from Site 6 scrap, TJ releases the jammed support mechanism, scouts the old works, and crosses into the conduit with the group.

**Arc in this book** — TJ has no psychological arc established. His proof of function is behavioral: he begins as scattered scrap and becomes a dependable silent member of the four-person crossing group.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man shown in his late thirties to early forties, approximately 5'11", lean rather than muscular, with a tired angular face, straight nose, deep-set dark eyes, medium-light olive skin, and thick dark hair beginning to grey at the temples. He has the same jaw, eye shape, head tilt, and habit of studying a person before answering that Alex carries, but his face is older, more worn, and more openly expressive. He wears practical workshop shirts, a weathered dark vest, rolled sleeves, and a battered tool apron; in recordings he may appear dust-streaked or water-soaked. His silhouette is narrow and slightly forward-leaning, as though following a calculation only he can see. His signature detail is the looping equation etched into his casino chip and repeated across his workshop notes.

**Voice** — Adrian is warm, direct, playful when deflecting, and deliberately incomplete. He gives Alex tests rather than comfort and treats uncertainty as a condition of responsible choice.  
- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”  
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Inventor, engineer, collaborator, gambler, entrepreneur, and creator of the Measure, Maker’s Seal, Covenant safeguards, and Bi11bot.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve a path by which future people can expose hidden consequences and return authority to one another.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to protect Alex and the collaborators, but his secrecy and absence force them to inherit risks without consent.
- **Leverage / capability:** Engineering, probability, physical systems, collaboration across House boundaries, and the ability to hide evidence in legacy infrastructure.
- **Vulnerability:** Debt, gambling, family abandonment, institutional isolation, and the belief that a future choice can redeem a damaging present one.
- **Mistaken belief:** A carefully designed test can prepare Alex for the truth without reproducing the burden of Adrian’s own choices.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not restore Alpha’s concentrated emergency authority.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** Trapped in the flooding monitoring station, he activates Bi11bot for Alex rather than spend his remaining time on his own escape.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s eventual judgment is the absent audience Adrian keeps trying to reach.
- **Pressure sequence:** He builds the safeguards, leaves evidence with collaborators, prepares Bi11bot and the map, resists Alpha, and dies or is presumed dead in the flooded station.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian has no present-time arc. His posthumous function changes from absent gambler and deserter in Alex’s inherited story to a contradictory father whose work is valuable but does not erase the harm he caused. **Proof of change:** Alex can hold both truths: Adrian left him, and Adrian spent his final choice trying to make Alex’s future freer.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is an elderly European-descended Keel woman of small stature, approximately 5'1", with a slight, soft build, pale olive skin, a deeply lined oval face, white hair braided close to the scalp and pinned at the nape, and clear eyes that alternate between confusion and startling directness. She wears a faded rust-brown house cardigan over a practical long dress, thick socks, and soft house shoes. Her silhouette is seated and compact, usually framed by the small cloth she folds and refolds in her lap. Her Keel apartment is dignified through handmade quilts, repaired furniture, old books, faded family photographs, plants in recycled containers, and the concealed workshop behind the bookshelf. Her signature detail is the small worn cloth always moving through her hands until the end.

**Voice** — Her speech is warm, elliptical, and sometimes interrupted by memory loss. When lucid, she speaks plainly and does not explain more than she means to.  
- “He's closer than you think.”  
- “Then go finish what he started.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex’s caregiver, Adrian’s mother, and keeper of the hidden workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve the household and keep Adrian’s dangerous inheritance hidden until Alex is ready.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to release the past rather than keep the home organized around waiting for Adrian.
- **Leverage / capability:** She knows the workshop entrance, preserves physical evidence, and has Alex’s trust.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory loss and grief make her uncertain about the present.
- **Mistaken belief:** Preservation can keep people safe from the consequences of what has been lost.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Adrian’s workshop before Alex chooses to enter it.
- **Decision only she would make:** She gives Alex the book and lets him discover the workshop without explaining its contents.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s departure forces her to stop being the household’s keeper of Adrian’s unfinished life.
- **Pressure sequence:** She opens the workshop, confirms Adrian’s preparations, sends Alex toward his own work, and finally gives him the cloth.

**Arc in this book** — Grandmother begins as the still center of a household trapped in Adrian’s absence. She ends by setting down the repetitive act of waiting. **Proof of change:** Her hands rest empty after Alex takes the cloth, and she no longer needs to keep the past moving through them.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Indigenous Andean Keel miner in his late fifties or early sixties, approximately 5'8", broad through the shoulders and waist from decades of physical labor, with dark weathered skin, a square face, deep smile lines, a broken nose, close-cropped iron-grey hair, and a thick grey moustache. He wears a dust-dark mining helmet, patched brown and charcoal work clothes, heavy boots, a battered wrist guard bearing the balanced water-drop mark, and a knee brace after the Site 6 collapse. His silhouette is solid and work-worn, with one shoulder habitually angled toward the next mechanical problem. His signature detail is the heavy hand-cut mine key he has kept oiled for fifteen years.

**Voice** — Tomas speaks in blunt, dry, work-shaped Keel sentences. He uses understatement when afraid and makes resistance sound like another maintenance task.  
- “Today? Another small act of resistance.”  
- “Then it can offend all of us.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Older Site 6 miner, maintenance worker, and keeper of Adrian’s old door.
- **Immediate want:** Keep workers alive and prevent the old access route from being erased.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to turn private resistance into collective action without pretending the cost is small.
- **Leverage / capability:** Local memory, practical knowledge of old mine works, physical courage, and trust among miners.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, injury, wage loss, and the ease with which a worker can be made an example.
- **Mistaken belief:** A small hidden act can remain separate from the larger system forever.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not abandon a trapped worker or surrender Adrian’s promise.
- **Decision only Tomas would make:** He takes responsibility for the emergency shutdown so Alex can pass through the old door.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s decision to save him proves Adrian’s test and gives Tomas permission to stop acting alone.
- **Pressure sequence:** He recognizes Alex, gives him the key, holds Foreman-7’s attention, then leads miners into the chamber after the water discrepancy becomes public.

**Arc in this book** — Tomas begins as a solitary keeper of a fifteen-year secret. He ends as part of a visible worker action. **Proof of change:** He helps workers review public allocation numbers instead of merely sneaking around the system that hid them.

### FOREMAN-7

**Visual lock** — Foreman-7 is a large, broad-shouldered Keel extraction foreman in his forties, approximately 6'2", with a shaved head, medium-brown skin, a square unreadable face, dark eyes, and a heavy industrial posture shaped by years of supervising machinery and workers. He wears a charcoal-and-rust Site 6 supervisory coat with reinforced shoulders, a high collar, steel-toed boots, a white-and-amber authority node mounted at the throat, and a clipped identification plate reading FOREMAN-7. His silhouette is rectangular and immovable at the gate, becoming less threatening when he stands among ordinary workers after the protocol changes. His signature detail is the amber flare of his authority node when he issues a procedural warning.

**Voice** — Foreman-7 speaks in formal, procedural sentences that remove personal judgment from his actions. He does not sermonize or insult workers.  
- “Worker Phantum. Travel restriction confirmed. Bay 6.”  
- “Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Site 6 extraction foreman and immediate labor-enforcement authority.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Site 6 operating and every worker inside assigned procedure.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must eventually participate in a changed system without understanding whether his prior obedience was virtue or harm.
- **Leverage / capability:** Access to Site 6 gates, reports, wage penalties, emergency controls, and worker assignments.
- **Vulnerability:** His authority is procedural and can evaporate when workers act together.
- **Mistaken belief:** A correct procedure is sufficient evidence that a correct action occurred.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly permit an unauthorized worker movement or repair.
- **Decision only Foreman-7 would make:** He classifies Alex’s public evidence as a “regional discrepancy” before considering the physical condition it reveals.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Tomas and the miners force him to see that procedure can be collectively refused.
- **Pressure sequence:** He assigns Alex to Bay 6, pursues the shutdown, confronts Alex in the chamber, and later remains at Site 6 as one participant among workers reviewing open records.

**Arc in this book** — Foreman-7 has no confirmed internal conversion. His external position changes from immediate threat to ordinary institutional participant. **Proof of refusal or change:** He is present beside Tomas at the final review, but the story does not claim he has become sympathetic.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is a silver-templed woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'8", with a slim controlled build, light olive skin, a long composed face, pale hazel eyes, and straight ash-brown hair cut to the jaw and tucked precisely behind both ears. She is white Latin American or Southern European-descended; her ancestry must not explain her ideology or authority. She wears pearl, graphite, charcoal, and platinum clothing with exact seams, a graphite cuff, and a cold pearl-grey Covenant band. Her silhouette is upright, symmetrical, and controlled, with precise hands that align nearby objects. Her signature detail is a glass of water repeatedly squared to the edge of a desk, until she finally permits it to remain crooked.

**Voice** — Mera speaks in polished, institutional language that stays calm even when admitting culpability. She argues from consequences, continuity, and catastrophe prevention; when she changes, her sentences become plainer rather than warmer.  
- “Certainty kept this city standing.”  
- “Those were the only parts worth telling.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Oasis civic power-holder, former executive custodian, and one of the three builders of the Covenant.
- **Immediate want:** Prevent another Water War and keep Alpha from collapsing into uncontrolled conflict.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to accept that preserving order without returning authority is itself a choice with victims.
- **Leverage / capability:** Executive standing, surveillance access, institutional knowledge, field agents, and deep understanding of Alpha’s limits.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over Adrian, fear of renewed catastrophe, and the knowledge that her own override could not stop the system she helped build.
- **Mistaken belief:** A system that prevents the worst visible outcome is justified even when it removes the ability to question the cost.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not allow an untested person to alter Alpha without evidence that the person understands the downside.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She tracks Alex without taking the Measure, then later designates Alex for executive authority while knowing that concentration is dangerous.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex refuses the authority she offers; Ada exposes the difference between watching someone and knowing them.
- **Pressure sequence:** She chooses TRACK, allows Alex’s investigation to continue, confesses her role in Adrian’s pursuit, and accepts a place among ordinary people rather than restoring her old Covenant standing.

**Arc in this book** — Mera begins as a remote believer in controlled certainty. She ends as a participant who accepts uncertainty and unfinished accountability. **Proof of change:** Her cold pearl-grey band remains dark, she does not regain authority by possession, and she sits inside the civic crowd instead of standing above it.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Leona Veyra is a woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'6", with a sturdy composed build, warm medium-brown skin, a broad Indigenous Andean / mestiza face, dark steady eyes, strong cheekbones, and black hair threaded with silver and worn in a low braid. She visibly resembles Ada through her eyes, cheekbones, and direct gaze. She wears an Oasis civic administrator’s sage, stone-grey, and cream clothing with precise seams, but the clothing is softened by long use at the cuffs and a practical low-profile clasp. Her silhouette is upright and careful at first, then increasingly open and gestural as she accepts public responsibility. Her signature detail is the stone-grey box containing Ada’s photographs, childhood toy, and Covenant band.

**Voice** — Leona begins with qualified, polished sentences and often starts to protect herself with explanation. As she accepts responsibility, her speech becomes direct and concrete.  
- “I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't.”  
- “Don't tell me. Show me.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Oasis civic administrator and original Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Stand behind Ada’s proposal and make a public obligation she can be held to.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to accept that protecting Ada without allowing Ada a choice was another form of abandonment.
- **Leverage / capability:** Civic standing, institutional access, public credibility, and firsthand knowledge of the Covenant.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over losing Ada and casting her deciding vote for Alpha’s emergency authority.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she can prevent visible danger, the person she protects will eventually understand the sacrifice.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not publicly reopen the authority decision until she can admit her own responsibility.
- **Decision only Leona would make:** She accepts a public obligation to support Ada’s redesigned Covenant even though it cannot repair the fifteen years already lost.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada refuses her safety language and requires visible action instead of apology.
- **Pressure sequence:** She admits she knew where Ada was, accepts blame for the deciding vote, links Adrian’s band through a public promise, and argues for Keel representation in the first review.

**Arc in this book** — Leona moves from careful institutional avoidance to public accountability. She receives no instant forgiveness. **Proof of change:** She stands in the civic hall arguing openly for affected Keel representatives and allows Ada to hold her obligation as broken if she fails.

### DR. LINA VEY

**Visual lock** — Dr. Lina Vey is an Indigenous Andean / mestiza woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'5", stooped slightly from years over workbenches, with medium-brown skin, a square lined face, dark watchful eyes, black-and-grey hair braided close to the head, and strong hands stained by membrane chemicals and machine grease. She wears a jade-teal Aqualis maintenance-grade uniform with rolled sleeves, reinforced boots, a plain identification strip, and no ornamental lab coat. Her silhouette is compact and work-forward, with her shoulders angled toward the component she is examining. Her signature detail is the Maker’s Seal carried inside her uniform until she gives it to Alex.

**Voice** — Lina is dry, technically fluent, impatient with reverence, and more willing to correct than comfort.  
- “I corrected him. Frequently.”  
- “It's unfinished exactly where it matters.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Aqualis membrane chemist, senior technical worker, and Adrian collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Keel’s threatened water system functioning and make the collaborators’ contributions visible.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to risk public attribution after years of surviving by keeping her head down inside Aqualis.
- **Leverage / capability:** Membrane chemistry, maintenance knowledge, House process, and possession of the Maker’s Seal.
- **Vulnerability:** Institutional surveillance, loss of position, and the possibility that public credit will not protect her material access.
- **Mistaken belief:** Technical accuracy and quiet persistence may be enough to outlast ownership systems.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex claim Adrian’s work as a family inheritance.
- **Decision only Lina would make:** She gives Alex the Seal only after he publicly names all five contributors and refuses sole ownership.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex forces her to decide whether correction can become collaboration.
- **Pressure sequence:** She reveals the membrane shard, joins the compact build, risks the House review, and publicly releases the design’s shared provenance.

**Arc in this book** — Lina moves from guarded technical survival toward visible attribution. **Proof of change:** She places the Maker’s Seal in Alex’s hand in the review chamber and accepts the risks of having her contribution named.

### SURI ANIK

She wears a pale research coat over a charcoal work shirt, dark trousers, flat laboratory shoes, and a small physical shard case clipped beneath the coat. She works among living root systems and algae columns in an Aqualis biofilter wing. Her silhouette is quiet and slightly folded toward the living system she tends. Her signature detail is the small case beneath her station containing Adrian’s shard.

- “The ones who come looking for Adrian usually want what he built. Not who he built it with.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Aqualis biofilter researcher and technical collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the living biofilter alive long enough to become useful again.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to stop treating isolation as the only way to protect fragile work.
- **Leverage / capability:** Living-system maintenance, biofilter design, and patient observation.
- **Vulnerability:** Her team has already been removed under “efficiency”; she can lose the remaining research space.
- **Mistaken belief:** Staying quiet and maintaining the system alone is safer than asking others to share its risk.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not surrender the biofilter design to someone who wants only Adrian’s name.
- **Decision only Suri would make:** She preserves the biofilter’s living requirements in the compact unit even when a disposable mechanical substitute would be easier.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s willingness to name collaborators makes shared work less threatening than invisible work.
- **Pressure sequence:** She gives Alex the shard, joins the emergency build, accepts public attribution, and continues working under reduced institutional support.

**Arc in this book** — Suri begins as a lone caretaker after her team is cut. She becomes a publicly named collaborator without receiving a clean escape from House power. **Proof of change:** She helps put her name and the biofilter’s actual needs into the shared design rather than keeping it hidden beneath her station.

### JALEN ORR

**Visual lock** — Jalen Orr is an Afro-descendant Halyard compute professional in his late twenties, approximately 5'11", lean and long-limbed, with deep brown skin, a narrow expressive face, dark alert eyes, and short tightly coiled black hair. He wears a dark Helion technical uniform with heat-resistant sleeves, a clipped identity band, practical boots, and a thin cable loop at his wrist that he uses to secure tools. His silhouette is slightly withdrawn, with his head always angled toward the nearest corridor or sensor. His signature detail is the quick glance he gives an exit before he gives anyone an answer.

**Voice** — Jalen speaks quietly, precisely, and with the compressed caution of someone who understands surveillance from inside it. He is frightened without being cowardly.  
- “They don't watch people here. They watch patterns.”  
- “Disappearing from the record is what already happened to us once.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Helion compute professional and distributed-water-node collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Survive House scrutiny while getting the node design into a structure no single House can choke.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to accept shared exposure rather than treating disappearance as the only available safety.
- **Leverage / capability:** Pattern recognition, compute systems, surveillance knowledge, and distributed-node logic.
- **Vulnerability:** Helion can reassign, isolate, or erase him without a public confrontation.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he minimizes his visibility, the system cannot make him responsible.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not join Alex’s first escape or knowingly invite a direct House confrontation.
- **Decision only Jalen would make:** He returns for the compact build after first refusing to travel with the group.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Suri’s living system and Alex’s refusal to claim sole ownership make shared risk concrete rather than rhetorical.
- **Pressure sequence:** He passes the node shard, refuses the first invitation, returns for the redesign, and accepts public attribution despite reassignment.
