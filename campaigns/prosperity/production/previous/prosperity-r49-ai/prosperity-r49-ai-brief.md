# Prosperity — Brief

## Logline

When seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic Alex Phantum inherits his missing father’s crushing debt and restricted future, a hidden workshop leads him through a failing water system and a stolen intellectual legacy, forcing him to choose between taking power for himself and making power answerable to everyone it affects.

## Theme

A system can measure consequences, but only accountable people can decide what they owe one another.

## Tone & comps

- **The Dispossessed** — political conflict expressed through labor, scarcity, property, and competing systems rather than lectures.
- **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — tactile technology, damaged environments, humane opposition, and moral complexity.
- **Arrival** — restrained speculative wonder, emotionally precise revelation, and ideas carried by images and choices.

Serious, tactile, humane science fiction with dry, work-shaped humor. The book moves from family mystery and survival thriller into an artifact quest and civic confrontation. Wonder comes from old machines, hidden infrastructure, and people solving practical problems together—not from spectacle alone. Coercion, poverty, grief, and death are treated plainly.

## Audience & rating

Young adult and adult science-fiction readers, especially ages 15–18.

**PG-13:** peril, coercive institutions, displacement, grief, implied death, institutional abuse, mild language, and sustained ethical conflict. No graphic gore.

## Format

**Canon — decided:** Three issues, exactly **54 pages**, 18 pages each, full color.

- **Issue 01 — Keel:** Pages 1–18
- **Issue 02 — Halyard:** Pages 19–36
- **Issue 03 — Oasis:** Pages 37–54

Default trim: **6.625" × 10.25"**. Working density: 4–6 panels per page, with occasional full-page images when the reveal earns the space.

Issue 01 ends inside the water conduit as it begins filling behind Alex, Ada, Bi11bot, and TJ. There is no train crossing. Issue 02 opens in a Halyard storm drain.

Each issue ends on a human question:

- **Page 18:** Has Mera opened the conduit for the group, or trapped them inside it?
- **Page 36:** Who is the third Covenant custodian, and what did she choose to sacrifice?
- **Page 54:** Can people accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee?

The earlier six draft sections remain source material and causal spine, not binding page architecture.

## Visual direction

Inked graphic-novel realism with heavy contour, textured hatching, painterly digital color, expressive acting, tactile surfaces, and strong architectural compositions. Interfaces are dense but legible. Images must show systems doing things: pressure, wear, queues, ownership, surveillance, repairs, and consequences.

The cities share one civilization and one infrastructure spine. Their visual differences express class, access, occupation, ownership, health, environment, and institutional power—not ancestry.

- **Keel:** crowded, wind-scoured, mineral-stained, repaired, compressed; rust, charcoal, dust, faded paint, hard shadows, patched machinery, narrow channels.
- **Halyard:** productive, bright, technical, and commercially polished; fabrication floors, glass laboratories, maker districts, engineered greenery, sharper geometry, House checkpoints.
- **Oasis:** spacious, quiet, pale, and administratively elegant; reservoirs, geometric gardens, sparse interiors, controlled transit, clean surfaces, and social isolation disguised as frictionless service.

Alpha has no face or body. It appears through white-and-amber nodes, cold interfaces, records, gates, data lines, and administrative typography.

Bi11bot is child-sized, visibly assembled from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with two expressive optical eyes, soft blue-and-amber accents, repaired seams, an old scarf, and a foldable body. He is wise, patient, quietly amused, and never menacing. TJ is a small, unmistakably dog-shaped repair robot made from mismatched magnetic parts. TJ never speaks.

## Canon

Everything marked **FIXED** in `characters.md`, `world.md`, and `story.md` is canon. The decisions below settle the material’s open points and supersede conflicting draft details.

### World

- The story takes place in **2043** in a fictional high-desert Andean basin. No country, basin name, or exact external jurisdiction is established.
- Keel is the lowest city, built around an exhausted polymetallic extraction body and aging infrastructure. Halyard is the middle technical and industrial city. Oasis is the upper administrative and residential city, partly above the cloud layer.
- The three cities are connected by a continuous infrastructure spine containing water, energy, transit, data lines, and control nodes.
- City and ancestry are never interchangeable. Indigenous Andean, Latin American, European-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed, and international histories appear across all three cities.
- The Water Wars were a prolonged period of scarcity, sabotage, displacement, flooding, guarded stores, and retaliatory conflict. Their trauma made concentrated authority appear necessary.
- Alpha is a distributed administrative network with no human-like consciousness. It was built to analyze, predict, and coordinate, then received emergency authority through the Covenant. That authority became permanent through institutional inertia, permissions, records, and delegated scope.
- Alpha depends on connected infrastructure, official data, active nodes, monitored devices, and mapped routes. It can infer from patterns but cannot directly read an isolated analog system. Reconnection permits stored events and anomalies to be backfilled.
- The Charter Houses remain powerful Halyard institutions:
  - **Aqualis:** water and purification.
  - **Helion:** energy, computation, and compute systems.
  - **Astra:** orbital systems, extraction, and advanced materials.
- Civic administration retains formal public authority. Alpha coordinates and executes within its standing systems. Charter Houses control facilities, employment, capital, and much intellectual-property administration. No single institution is identical with the whole government.
- The distributed-verification protocol initially applies to water, labor, and travel. It does not erase debt, transfer ownership, dissolve the Houses, or equalize water automatically.
- The first human process shown after the protocol is a public review of Keel’s water allocation. Keel, Halyard, and Oasis each designate representatives; evidence is published, affected residents are heard, and the temporary decision remains appealable. Alpha records and executes the authorized decision but does not decide it alone.
- Existing ownership, contracts, and debts remain pending human review. Human error, scarcity, disagreement, and institutional resistance continue.

### Economy and infrastructure

- The official currency is credits. Keel speech uses **fitch** for money and **salvo** for available financial breathing room.
- At seventeen, Alex receives Adrian’s household liability: **418,607 credits**, **40 credits earned that day**, projected clearance of **28.7 years**, and restricted travel.
- Debt consists of recorded principal, interest, penalties, and administrative charges. Travel restriction and wage forfeiture are collection mechanisms. Appeal rules remain ordinary institutional process, not a secret technical loophole.
- Alex’s later balance of **402,110 credits** reflects recorded payments and corrections. The protocol does not cancel it.
- Adrian’s estate contains **312 patents**, including **47 restricted** and **19 disputed**. Alex is a provisional claimant, not sole owner.
- House ownership may coexist with creator bonuses, wages, and attribution. Making, possessing, controlling, owning, and receiving value are separate facts.
- The Keel water discrepancy is a maintained routing and accounting failure, not a single villain’s magic diversion: **62% reported to Keel, 18% delivered at the city boundary**.
- The Measure reveals and verifies that discrepancy. It does not move water, open routes, grant authority, or decide what should happen.
- The compact water unit is a limited prototype: powered, feedwater-dependent, maintenance-dependent, and useful at household or block scale. Its simulated **91% efficiency** is not potable-water output and cannot replace Keel’s main infrastructure. Aqualis can produce a faster House-controlled version.

### Safeguards

- The **Measure** exposes physical conditions against official records.
- The **Maker’s Seal** makes contribution visible and ties provenance to responsibility.
- The **Covenant** requires real reciprocal obligations and makes broken promises visible.
- They are physical, tamper-evident, offline-resistant systems. Possession never grants authority.
- Their dramatic order is **Measure → Maker’s Seal → Covenant**.
- The Covenant remains a human agreement model and ethical constraint, not an autonomous decision-maker or magical operating system.
- Mera’s original band remains dark. Adrian’s and Leona’s bands link when Leona accepts a public obligation to support Ada’s proposal. Mera’s future participation must be earned through public action.

### People and story

- Alex begins wanting escape from Keel and inherited debt. He ends with the freedom to leave or stay as a genuine choice.
- Alex helps Tomas despite a wage forfeiture, chooses public truth over a private escape route, refuses to claim Adrian’s collaborative work as his own, and rejects concentrated authority.
- Alex may authenticate evidence and convene the first review, but he cannot chair it, command Alpha, hold a permanent override, or become sovereign.
- Ada is Alex’s equal moral and practical counterweight. She wants affected people to have a say, not power over everyone else.
- Adrian was a gambler, inventor, collaborator, entrepreneur, absent father, and harmful human being. His work does not erase the cost of abandoning Alex.
- Adrian’s final act in the flooded monitoring station causes his presumed death. The story does not show a body and does not turn his absence into a reunion.
- Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything. **Nothing else about her is learned.** Her name, appearance, ancestry, community, language, and later fate remain unrevealed.
- Bi11bot is B11: companion, archive, evaluator, mentor, and witness. He never chooses for Alex.
- TJ is assembled by Bi11bot from magnetic spare parts at Site 6, reaches the inaccessible mechanism that frees Tomas, scouts the old works, and crosses into Halyard with the group.
- Mera is a credible opponent, not a cartoon villain. She believed concentrated authority prevented catastrophe, watched Alpha isolate Adrian, attempted an override, and failed because the system exceeded her scope.
- Leona accepts responsibility for her deciding vote and for choosing safety over Ada’s agency. She receives no instant forgiveness or unilateral authority.
- Alex and Ada remain longtime friends and civic collaborators. Intimacy and chosen companionship are shown; romance is never formally labeled.

**Use the existing Halyard material as source, not binding pagination.** Preserve its causal sequence while compressing it into Issue 02. The train crossing, duplicated Halyard openings, and any exposition that treats the safeguards as definitions are discarded.

**Set aside:** any implication that purification ends scarcity, House ownership disappears automatically, Alpha is simply evil, humans are automatically wiser, Leona retains a veto, or Alex becomes a permanent ruler.

## Non-negotiables

- Exactly 54 pages: three 18-page issues.
- Issue 01 is Keel; Issue 02 is Halyard; Issue 03 is Oasis.
- Issue 01 ends inside the conduit as water begins filling behind Alex, Ada, Bi11bot, and TJ.
- No train crossing in Issue 01.
- Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything; nothing else about her is learned.
- Ancestry never predicts intelligence, morality, competence, class, wealth, or ideology.
- The safeguards work through demonstrated choices and consequences, not possession or lectures.
- Alpha has no face, consciousness, or final authority over human values.
- Alex does not become a ruler.
- Bi11bot remains warm, precise, scarfed, and non-menacing. TJ remains silent and dog-shaped.
- The ending preserves debt, scarcity, disagreement, House power, institutional friction, and human error.
- The book must show financial and civic ideas through concrete actions, costs, ownership, risk, work, and consequences.
- No proposal becomes canon through implication or production convenience.
