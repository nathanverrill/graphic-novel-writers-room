# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

The cast must not use ancestry as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, technical ability, wealth, poverty, or political position. Indigenous people, white people, Afro-descendant people, immigrants, and mixed families exist across the three cities and across social classes. The cities differ primarily through class, access, ownership, education, occupation, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment, and institutional power. **FIXED** `(rules/ancestry.md)`

The story's character movement travels upward from Keel to Halyard to Oasis, and speech generally becomes more formal, technical, and institutionally fluent along that route. This is a language and class gradient, not an intelligence scale. **FIXED** `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Alex and Ada describe the world concretely and interpersonally rather than through civic or academic abstractions. Bi11bot translates complex choices into questions about machinery, probability, money, ownership, incentives, and consequences. Alpha speaks administratively. Mera speaks in controlled, consequence-oriented terms. **FIXED** `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The central character question is not whether one person can make the correct decision for everyone. It is whether people can reclaim responsibility without replacing Alpha with another unchecked authority. **FIXED** `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

The showrunner's standing rule concerning Alex's mother is absolute: **Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. We never learn anything else about her.** **FIXED** `(taste-writers.md; brief.md)`

## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Visual lock —** Alex Phantum is a lean seventeen-year-old boy of average height with a wiry mechanic's build, narrow shoulders, strong forearms, medium-brown skin, a broad mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended face, dark brown eyes, a blunt dark-brown jaw-length undercut with uneven wind-lifted strands, and a short straight nose; he wears a faded rust-red thermal shirt under a charcoal patched work jacket, dust-stained slate work trousers, scuffed black work boots, a weathered canvas tool bag crossing his torso, and Adrian's worn casino chip etched with a looping equation visible at his right pocket edge; his silhouette is compact, practical, and slightly forward-leaning, and his signature detail is the unconscious head tilt he shares with Adrian when he is testing a machine or doubting a record. **FIXED** `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical, and mildly abrasive. Alex describes evidence rather than principles, translates abstractions into machinery or cost, and uses Keel slang such as *clean, crooked, hot, patched, fitch,* and *salvo*. He does not naturally say *governance, legitimacy, stakeholder,* or *accountability* except when mocking, repeating, or reading them.

Example lines:
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “I just want it to be my choice.”

**Causality:**

- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic and mine worker; Adrian Phantum's son.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and choose a future not controlled by Adrian's debt or Alpha's movement restrictions.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs to accept responsibility without accepting ownership of everyone else. He distrusts systems because records often contradict physical reality, but initially mistakes solitary independence for freedom.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads machinery through vibration, pressure, wear, sound, and improvised repair; follows physical evidence when official records shift; can authenticate and explain Adrian's transfer.
- **Vulnerability:** He is seventeen, financially trapped by **418,607 credits** of inherited liability, responsible for his grandmother's medicine, emotionally starved by Adrian's absence, and easily tempted by any power that promises immediate repair.
- **Mistaken belief:** Adrian left him only debt and shame; if Alex can escape alone, he will be free.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly abandon someone trapped when he can help, even if helping costs him the route out.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He publishes Keel's **62% recorded allocation versus 18% delivered flow** when he could take a private escape route.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to distinguish leaving from escaping and to name who would carry the consequences of his choices.
- **Pressure chain:** Alex repairs the pump despite procedure → the repair proves records can be wrong → the debt transfer makes escape urgent → the hidden workshop turns resentment into investigation → helping Tomas costs him time but opens the mine route → publishing the Measure costs him private departure but enables Keel's collective response → restoring contributor ownership costs him personal security → refusing exclusive override authority preserves other people's right to decide.
- **Proof of change:** At the end, his unlawful household-transfer and allocation liabilities are voided, but he still chooses to leave Keel. He convenes the first cross-city review and authenticates Adrian's transfer without chairing, questioning, deciding, commanding Alpha, or retaining a permanent override. **FIXED** `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc in this book:** Alex moves through **Escape → Curiosity → Truth → Responsibility → Ownership → Trust → Power → Restraint → Agency**. He begins by treating freedom as physical departure. He ends as a mechanic and witness who accepts shared responsibility while refusing sovereignty. His final choice to leave Keel is no longer dictated by debt, Alpha, or shame.

### Ada Veyra

**Visual lock —** Ada Veyra is a twenty-year-old woman of medium height with a sturdy, grounded build, warm brown skin, a strong oval face with high cheekbones, teal-blue eyes, freckles across her nose and cheeks, and long copper hair worn in a thick wind-tangled braid with loose strands around her face; she wears a dark high-collared shirt, a weathered charcoal utility coat, a muted moss-green wrap secured across her torso, reinforced work trousers, dusty brown boots, and a rivet-studded shoulder strap carrying a compact field notebook and tea flask; her silhouette is rooted and balanced, and her signature detail is the copper braid repeatedly catching the high-desert wind while she stands still and watches people rather than machinery. **FIXED** `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** Concise, emotionally precise, and quietly insistent. Ada asks one concrete question that exposes the burden beneath a demand. She avoids civic abstractions and speaks about fear, protection, carrying, showing, and what people owe one another.

Example lines:
- “Not everything.”
- “Then that's what the agreement has to protect.”

**Causality:**

- **Public role:** Keel community-minded worker, Alex's longtime friend, and later civic collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Change Keel rather than abandon it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She believes rebuilding requires staying close to people, but must learn that supporting Alex's departure can also be an act of care rather than betrayal.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads interpersonal motives, notices avoidance, recognizes when a negotiation is framed around demands instead of fear, and connects technical evidence to human consequence.
- **Vulnerability:** Her mother, Leona, is alive after Ada spent twenty years believing she was dead; the discovery threatens her account of both family and protection.
- **Mistaken belief:** Staying is the only meaningful form of commitment; departure usually means surrender.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex reduce people to records, debts, or obstacles.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She interrupts the first three-city negotiation with “What are you afraid of losing?” and turns competing claims into reciprocal commitments.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex makes her confront the difference between rebuilding with someone and deciding where they must belong.
- **Pressure chain:** She challenges Alex at the overlook → earns entry to the workshop when he says “She stays” → catches the false movement on the official map → follows him from Keel to Halyard → identifies Leona → converts the Covenant meeting from demands to fears → accepts Alex's chosen departure while continuing civic work.
- **Proof of change:** Ada leaves Keel with Alex without treating his departure as abandonment, then works with local civic and community groups through Halyard and Oasis. **FIXED** `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc in this book:** Ada begins as Alex's rooted counterpoint, wanting to rebuild what he wants to escape. She becomes his investigative partner, confronts the mother who protected her through silence, and applies the Covenant's principle before the Covenant activates. She ends able to support both local rebuilding and Alex's agency.

### Adrian Phantum

**Visual lock —** Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man shown from middle age into his late years, tall and once lean but increasingly gaunt, with tired kind eyes, a long angular face, Alex's jaw, deep-set dark eyes, thinning dark-brown hair streaked with gray, and a habitual slight head tilt; in recordings he wears a burgundy work jacket over a pale shirt with rolled sleeves, while later fragments show a weathered charcoal coat, an analog watch, and work-worn hands; his silhouette is long, folded, and increasingly burdened, and his signature detail is the careful placement of a casino chip beside technical diagrams as though probability and machinery belong on the same workbench. **FIXED** `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic, and lightly humorous inside serious messages. He frames decisions as bets and questions, uses uncertainty as discipline, and refuses to tell Alex exactly what to think.

Example lines:
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”
- “What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?”

**Causality:**

- **Public role:** Gambler, inventor, entrepreneur, collaborator, and publicly disgraced debtor.
- **Immediate want:** Complete the three-safeguard transfer before Alpha can turn emergency authority into permanent rule.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants people to reclaim authority but knows he cannot manufacture a morally perfect successor. He loves probability while recognizing that people must choose what cannot be calculated.
- **Leverage / capability:** Designs physical, offline-resistant systems; understands probability, incentives, infrastructure, and institutional behavior; builds Bi11bot and the succession test.
- **Vulnerability:** He is a harmful absent father whose gambling, losses, and family costs cannot be erased by the importance of his work.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently well-designed test can prepare another person for every consequence.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not make Alex an obedient copy or grant authority solely through inheritance.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He refuses an available escape beneath Oasis and completes the transfer through Bi11bot while Alpha's containment protocol kills him.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's eventual judgment forces Adrian's hidden plan to matter as a father's incomplete attempt, not merely as an institutional design.
- **Pressure chain:** Gambling funds inventions and restoration → collaboration creates the safeguards → Mera's fear makes emergency authority permanent → Adrian hides the safeguards and prepares Alex → Alpha classifies him as a systemic disruption → he completes the transfer through Bi11bot rather than escape.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** Adrian does not receive a redemption. The archive proves that his public disgrace and private contributions were both real. His final refusal to escape demonstrates commitment to the transfer, not absolution from abandoning Alex.

**Arc in this book:** Adrian's character is reconstructed after death: failed gambler and harmful father, then inventor, collaborator, entrepreneur, and opponent of permanent emergency authority. His final wager is on people rather than calculation.

### Bi11bot

**Visual lock —** Bi11bot is a child-sized repair robot built from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with a compact upright body, rounded shoulders, visible tooled seams, mismatched replacement plates, maker's stamps, small articulated hands, soft blue and amber indicator lights, and two expressive optical eyes, the right eye fitted with a small adjustable lens ring; his silhouette is small, warm, and visibly constructed rather than seamless, and his signature detail is the adjustable lens ring that clicks into a new position whenever he is examining evidence or pretending not to be amused. **FIXED** `(brief.md; rules/characters.md)`

**Voice:** Dry, economical, patient, precise, warm underneath, and occasionally cryptic. He teaches through concrete examples and consequences, using engineering, gambling, probability, debt, ownership, and risk language. He never makes the decision for Alex.

Example lines:
- “...Well. That took longer than expected.”
- “A wager.”

**Causality:**

- **Public role:** Adrian's machine, archive, guide, evaluator, witness, and companion.
- **Immediate want:** Bring Alex through the succession test without deciding the test for him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He was built to preserve Adrian's judgment but must avoid becoming another authority that substitutes answers for choices.
- **Leverage / capability:** Carries offline records and the signed B11 witness record; explains Alpha's history; evaluates choices; assembles TJ; recognizes hidden technical and financial relationships.
- **Vulnerability:** He is physically small, dependent on maintenance and battery power, and cannot authenticate constitutional authority by himself.
- **Mistaken belief:** Early in the journey, he treats withholding information as the safest way to preserve the test.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not conceal a consequence once Alex has made a consequential choice.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He answers Adrian's final transfer through an offline signed witness record rather than treating his own machine judgment as authority.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's skepticism forces him to become a guide who can be trusted without being obeyed.
- **Proof of change:** He witnesses the transfer, explains its limits, and accepts the restored relationship in which humans decide and Alpha advises rather than becoming an alternative sovereign.

**Arc in this book:** Bi11bot awakens as suspicious inheritance, becomes a warm evaluator and mentor, and ends as a witness who proves that guidance can preserve human choice rather than replace it.

### TJ

**Visual lock —** TJ is a small magnetic repair robot dog assembled from mismatched salvaged brass, black polymer, ceramic, and steel parts, with a compact dog-shaped body, a separate wedge-shaped head, four detachable jointed legs, a segmented magnetic tail, visible seams and mismatched fasteners, and a tiny blue inspection light in one eye; his silhouette remains recognizably canine even when reconfigured, and his signature detail is the brief metallic snap as one body part detaches and magnetically reconnects for a practical repair task. **FIXED** `(rules/characters.md)`

**Voice:** TJ is nonverbal. He communicates through clicks, snaps, servo whines, posture, head orientation, and practical repair gestures. He does not make comic reactions to suffering or political stakes.

**Causality:**

- **Public role:** Bi11bot's repair companion and inaccessible-space maintenance robot.
- **Immediate want:** Reach and repair mechanisms Bi11bot and the human crew cannot physically access.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** TJ is built from discarded parts but proves that useful value can emerge from recombination rather than pristine ownership.
- **Leverage / capability:** Detaches his head as a camera, sends body segments through gaps, drops and retrieves limbs, and reconfigures for ducts, walls, and machinery.
- **Vulnerability:** Magnets can fail, parts can be separated, and his small body cannot substitute for human judgment or heavy equipment.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ does not speak.
- **Boundary at the start:** He does not perform expressive behavior that obscures a repair task.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** He separates his own body into independently useful parts to reach the trapped mechanism at Site 6.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot's practical trust in TJ gives TJ purpose without turning him into a mascot.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** TJ remains a practical, nonverbal repair presence; his recurring behavior demonstrates contribution through action rather than explanation.

**Arc in this book:** TJ first appears at Site 6 after Adrian's archival mention, proving that the hidden workshop's value is distributed across makers, machines, and tasks.

### Mera Vale

**Visual lock —** Mera Vale is an older white Latin American woman of medium height with a slim, controlled build, pale olive skin, a narrow oval face, silver-blond hair cut into a precise chin-length bob, cool gray-green eyes, and immaculate posture; she wears a high-collared ivory blouse under a sharply tailored graphite coat, narrow black trousers, polished low boots, and a single thin glass ring that catches interface light; her silhouette is vertical, elegant, and nearly motionless, and her signature detail is the exact alignment of a glass or data slate with the edge of any surface she occupies. **FIXED** `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** Controlled, declarative, polished, and consequence-oriented. Mera reframes moral criticism as stability, risk, intervention, and responsibility. She does not raise her voice to assert power.

Example lines:
- “I preserved order.”
- “Because forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control.”

**Causality:**

- **Public role:** Alpha-associated human overseer and former Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve stable regional operation and prevent another Water Wars collapse.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She needs to admit that preserving order without accountable control became its own danger.
- **Leverage / capability:** Understands Alpha's infrastructure, policy language, emergency protocols, and institutional dependencies; can offer credible material bargains.
- **Vulnerability:** Alpha denies her override attempt, proving that the system she defended does not ultimately answer to her.
- **Mistaken belief:** Concentrated authority is justified whenever the consequences of distributed failure are large enough.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Alpha's loss of control if disclosure might destabilize the region.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She offers Alex the power she once defended for herself, making the argument about authority rather than personal corruption.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex refuses to humiliate her and instead asks her to help rebuild, forcing her to participate without standing above the system.
- **Proof of change:** She becomes one citizen among many and accepts that human decision-making will include mistakes.

**Arc in this book:** Mera moves from controlled observer and defender of concentrated authority to a participant who must live under the shared accountability she once feared.
