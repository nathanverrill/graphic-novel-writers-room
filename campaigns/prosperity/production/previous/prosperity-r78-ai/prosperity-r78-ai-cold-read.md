# Cold Read — Prosperity

Read in one sitting, start to finish, from the seat of a 16-year-old who got this as homework, argued about it, and opened it at 10pm the night before it's due wanting it to be bad. I read straight through, pages 1–54, no going back except where noted.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, the whole page.** This is where the book got me, and I didn't want it to. A kid scans his wristband at the end of a shift and the screen tells him he turned seventeen today and he now owes 418,607 credits because of his dad. "Happy birthday, Phantum" from the guard is mean in the right way. "EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS" next to that number means I don't need anyone to do the maths for me. "PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS" is the gut punch. Then panel 4, him just saying "Twenty-eight point seven." I'd have kept reading in a shop because of this page.
- **Page 1, panel 3.** "Then don't approve it. Just notice it works." Okay, he has an attitude. I liked him a bit.
- **Page 6.** B11 is the first thing I actually enjoyed. "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." / "A fact with a poor record." A sarcastic old robot, fine, I'm in.
- **Page 10.** Clearest moment in the issue. Guy trapped, yellow line, wristband says HOLD POSITION, he steps over it and loses the day's pay, "That is today's pay." / "I know." I understood exactly what it cost him. The book should feel like this more often.
- **Page 11.** TJ, the dog built out of mine parts with a giant red magnet, sending its own head through a gap. "That dog yours?" / "Recently." This was fun. Honestly the dog does more for me than most of the people.
- **Pages 14–16.** 18% delivered vs 62% reported. That's the first time I understood what the "water" stuff was about: someone is lying about how much water the town gets and stealing the rest for industry. Page 16 panel 4, the public screens across Keel flipping to "DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%", was satisfying.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 5.** Hidden workshop behind the bookcase that opens because grandma says "put it where it fits." Very convenient. I skimmed the gauges and plans.
- **Page 7.** Adrian's recording. Every line in it is vague ("the old line may still be carrying water", "The record says one thing. The pipe may say another."). I read it, I didn't take anything in, I moved on.
- **Pages 17–18.** I skimmed the escape. I couldn't follow the hatch/ledge/conduit/segment stuff. I just looked at the splash.

### I was confused
- **Page 3: who is Ada?** She's just there checking labels in the pharmacy. Does she work there? Is she his friend, his girlfriend, his sister? Nobody says. She then announces she's coming with him, and the book acts like that's a big deal to me. It isn't, because I don't know her.
- **Page 3: the token.** He leaves the token on the counter, then reaches for it "anyway", then Ada pushes it to him. Did he pay for it? Did she? Did Inez give it to him free? I couldn't tell who ended up paying for what, and the whole page is about paying for things.
- **Page 4.** He had 52 credits on page 3, then he can't pay 30. "Site 6 took the shift." / "The shift took your wages." I don't get it. What is Site 6 at this point? Then on page 8 his shift gets *reassigned* to Site 6, which seems to happen after page 4. The timeline lost me.
- **Page 8: Mera.** New woman, new place ("Oasis control room"), no idea who she is. She tracks the signal and sends Alex to Site 6. Is she hunting him or helping him? "Put the signal where a mechanic can reach it" — so she's helping? Then "EXECUTIVE SCOPE: ROUTING ONLY" — so she's not allowed to do something? I didn't know whether to be scared of her. Then on page 17 she opens a valve, "Not the homes." Helping again? Hurting? I finished the issue not knowing whose side she's on, and not in the good "ooh mystery" way. It was confused and detached.
- **Page 6, panel 4.** "I have a route. I also have a question." What's the question? I don't think anyone ever asks it.
- **Page 15, panel 4.** "One way gets you out unseen. One gets you to the homes. The third carries pressure." Three ways? I counted two doors in the panel. I didn't understand the choice I was supposed to be weighing.
- **Page 17.** Why does opening pressure in the conduit save them? Why does it hurt Tomas? I just accepted that water was coming.

### Lines I read twice
- p3: "You know a direction." / "I know enough."
- p5: "Same hand. Different damage." Gave up on this one.
- p5: "He was working. He had already lost everything."
- p12: "Your father asked me to keep a door." / "He ask you to keep him out?" / "He didn't ask enough." Read three times. Still not sure.
- p13: "The survey says straight." / "The bolts say somebody turned."
- p15: "The third carries pressure."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: yes. B11: yes, easily the best voice. TJ: yes.
- Grandmother, Inez, Tomas, Ada: they all talk the same way, in short little sayings. Inez "Then one gets paid today." Grandma "Put it where it fits." Tomas "Enough to complain." Ada "I know what people stop repairing." If you swapped their names I wouldn't notice.
- Mera: tellable apart visually, but not as a person. I didn't know what she wanted.

### I felt something
- p2, the debt reveal. Real.
- p10, losing the wage to step over the line.
- p18, Tomas's light going dark. A little, but only a little, because I'd known him for three pages.

### I predicted
- Dad's not just "unrecorded." He's dead, or he's coming back as a twist. (From p2.)
- The robot would be the dad's real legacy. (p5–6.)
- The water numbers were faked. Called it on p13.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his gambling dad's huge debt finds the dad's robot and proves the town is being cheated out of its water.

### Where I would have put it down
I didn't, quite. Page 8 (Mera, Oasis, "EXECUTIVE SCOPE") was the closest. But the debt on page 2 and the dog on page 11 kept me going.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 18 is a cool splash, climbing out with water behind them. The real question is whether Tomas is dead, and I care a bit but not much, because I met him on page 10. The other question, whether the town gets its water now, is one I do care about. I'd read Issue 02 tonight, but more from momentum than need.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19, panel 1.** "That was not a route." / "It was a route. The destination objected." Best joke in the book.
- **Page 20, panels 2–3.** The worker who keeps her hand on the part after the kiosk takes it, then "I made the correction." / "HOUSE PROPERTY — DISPOSITION PENDING." Clear and kind of sick. I got what the Houses are: companies that take credit for workers' stuff.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** Ren: "Take the part. I take the heat." Then page 26 Alex says "No." and goes back. Okay, that's a hero moment, and it worked on me.
- **Pages 27–28.** Puzzle-fixing. 11%, they argue about which piece is broken, "They were designed not to meet", Alex finds the bad edge, Ren's sleeve, 94%. That was satisfying even though I didn't follow the science. "I expected a longer failure." / "You can have one later." Good.
- **Page 30.** B11 picks the escape route using "six recorded bodies" and misses the people who aren't in the system. "I counted the record and missed the people." The robot learning something was the one moment in this issue that felt like the book's actual idea.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 22.** Production floors, four names in a list (LINA VEY — MEMBRANE, SURI ANIK — BIOFILTER, JALEN ORR — NODE LOGIC, REN OKAFOR — RECOVERY / HEAT), and a transfer document, all on one page. My eyes went over the list without reading it. Four people at once, introduced as a spreadsheet.
- **Pages 23–25.** I skimmed most of the Caldera floor sequence. Contaminated feeder, test pipe, culture vial, monitored power line, clean corridor, eleven seconds. I followed it as "they're sneaking out with parts" and nothing more.
- **Page 29.** Too-big machine, steam. Skimmed.
- **Pages 33–35.** This is the climax and it's people reading things into a recorder while a registry screen changes labels. "HOUSE RECORD / CONTRIBUTOR RECORD — MISMATCH", "ALTERED ATTRIBUTION: EXPOSED", "LICENSE: PUBLIC / NONEXCLUSIVE". I skimmed every screen. I know he won something because Lock looks sad. I couldn't tell you what.

### I was confused
- **Page 19.** Is Tomas dead? The answer is "STATUS: UNRESOLVED." / "We don't know." Fine. But then nobody mentions him again for the whole issue, so the cliffhanger just evaporated.
- **Page 19, panel 4: who is Mara?** "Mara's still shipping." I've never met her. Then she pays for things all issue. Is she someone from Keel I should remember? I checked back. She isn't in Issue 01.
- **Mara vs Mera.** I genuinely thought for a few pages that Mera from the control room was running a shipping company. These names are one letter apart.
- **Page 21.** Dad has *312 patents* and an "intellectual estate"? So why does he owe 418,000 credits and why was he gambling? Issue 01 told me he was a broke gambler. Now he's a genius inventor with a fortune that "isn't money." Nobody explains the switch, and I didn't get why patents can't pay off the debt. "Not until someone carries the risk." Meaning?
- **Page 22, panel 4.** "Their reviews stop." Good or bad for them? I think it's a threat. Not sure.
- **Page 23.** "Take this and the House owns the design by morning." If Alex takes the membrane, the House owns it? Or if he signs? I couldn't work out what "this" was.
- **Page 23, panel 1.** "Keel's line was diverted. This feeder is contaminated. Do not combine the failures." No idea why that's being said to me.
- **Page 24.** The Maker's Seal. Another magic object, handed over with "Keep the work attached to the people who can keep it alive."
- **Page 31.** Why does a cracked replacement cartridge plus Mara's recorded voice ("If it needs parts, list the parts.") matter? I think the point is "it's honest that it breaks." That only came to me on the second look.
- **Page 34, panel 4.** The Seal "warms" and projects things. So it's magic? Tech? It just does whatever the scene needs.
- **Page 36.** Mera again, with a "dark Covenant band." What's a Covenant band? "SUMMON." Is she the villain now, summoning him? "Let them see who called him" — so is she on his side? Same confusion as Issue 01, page 8.
- **Where did Ada go?** She has almost nothing to do this issue. A couple of lines ("Her hand is still on it.", "This way is thin."). She said on p3 that she was coming because Alex didn't know the route, and then Halyard happened without her.
- **The water.** Issue 01 was about Keel's water. This issue is about who gets their name on a purifier patent. I kept waiting for it to connect back. I sort of get that a home purifier helps Keel, but nobody says so out loud, and I didn't feel it.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I ever find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Halyard (as a place) | p19 | A rich factory city. Worked out from the art. |
| Mara Morrow / Morrow & Sons | p19 | A shopkeeper in Keel, I guess. Never introduced. |

| Registry / "Charter review" | p21 | Some legal system. Never explained. |
| Cassian Lock | p21 | Introduces himself, which helped. What he wants: to own stuff. |
| Intellectual estate / restricted / disputed patents | p21 | No. |
| Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren | p22 | By job only. |
| Maker's Seal | p24 | Magic name-protecting medallion? Not really. |
| Covenant band | p36 | Not in this issue. |
| SUMMON / Oasis Legacy Node | p36 | No. |

### Lines I read twice
- p21: "Not until someone carries the risk."
- p23: "Do not combine the failures."
- p24: "If they take the name, they take the maintenance." Read three times. I think I get it now, maybe.
- p25: "Eleven seconds. After that, the pattern has my shape." Gave up.
- p25: "This is my unrecorded correction."
- p26: "If it fails, it fails where I can touch it."
- p29: "Less than the House promises. More than Keel gets now." Read twice, then it clicked. That one paid off.
- p31: "Thin still carries."
- p36: "Let them see who called him."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lock: yes, the stylus guy. But he's a suit with a pen. He never felt dangerous.
- Ren: yes, the heat guy who says tough things.
- Lina, Suri, Jalen: no. Membrane lady, plant lady, power guy. They all talk in the same short wise-sounding lines. By p33, when Alex reads all four full names out, I couldn't have matched three of them to a face.
- Mara / Mera: could NOT tell apart.
- Lina / (later) Leona: close too.
- Here's my honest reaction: around this issue I started thinking a machine wrote this. Everyone speaks in the same balanced two-part sayings. "One customer paid for a thing. One worker got paid for losing hers." "A House builds faster." / "Then it can build beside us." "They can see us now." / "Then make them look." Each one alone is fine, but there are so many that it all sounds like one voice. My class would screenshot these.

### I felt something
- p20, the worker's hand on the part.
- p26, "No." and he goes back.
- p30, B11's eyes dimming when it realises it skipped the unrecorded people.
- Nothing at the actual climax, pp33–35.

### I predicted
- Alex would refuse Lock's deal. Knew it from p22. It was never really in doubt, so the "ACCEPT / REFUSE" on p23 had no tension.
- The House would steal the design anyway. Sort of right ("The Houses will still build it." / "Yes.").

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid refuses to sell his dad's inventions to a corporation so the workers who actually built them keep their names on them. I honestly can't say how that connects to the water from Issue 01.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 22.** Four names in a list, three corporations, a transfer document, and a dad who is suddenly rich in patents but still in debt. If this were a shop, this is where it goes back on the shelf. I kept going because I had to.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The cliffhanger is a notification on a wristband: "APPEAR AT OASIS LEGACY NODE." The person sending it, Mera, I still can't place as friend or enemy, so I don't know if this is a threat. And Tomas, the one person I was actually worried about, has gone unmentioned for seventeen pages. Nobody I care about is in danger on page 36.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 22; next issue: no

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Pages 39–40, Ada and Leona.** "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" Sharp. "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." That hurt a bit. This is the first time Ada is a person to me. But see "confused" below.
- **Page 43.** How Adrian got shut out: he couldn't buy parts, "Then people stopped selling to him," "After that, people stopped standing near him." The door locks. Clearest and creepiest page in the issue. It finally explained the dad more than anything in Issue 01 did.
- **Page 45.** Adrian in the rising water choosing to stay. "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." It landed, even though I'd guessed it.
- **Page 46.** "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Best exchange in the book. B11 earns this.
- **Page 51.** "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." I liked the callback, and seeing Tomas alive. "You made it." / "Had work to do."
- **Page 54.** The 14-months-later page. The debt has only gone from 418,607 to 402,110. That's bleak, and I respected that the book didn't magic it away. Inez's medicine marked PAID got me a little: the 30 credits from page 3, finally.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37.** Two new people (Nadi Ruiz, Clerk Olani), an allocation request, "DECLARED INTEREST: ORBITAL INDUSTRIAL BRANCH." It's the opening of the last issue and it's a committee meeting. Skimmed.
- **Pages 41–42.** The bands. I skimmed and then had to go back, and going back didn't help.
- **Pages 47–50.** The Measure, the Seal and the bands on a table, a lever rising, "It wants a human name." It felt like a video game where you collect three artifacts and put them on the altar. I skimmed the screens on p50.
- **Pages 52–53.** A hearing. "Challenge accepted. Approval paused." Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 37 → 38.** On p37 Alex and Ada "enter the room." On p38 they're approaching the service entrance. Did they enter or not? I thought I'd missed a page.
- **Page 37.** Tomas is alive, on a "persistent live wall," and the book doesn't stop to tell me. The Issue 01 cliffhanger gets resolved in a stage direction. Nobody reacts until p51.
- **Who is Leona to Ada?** Mother? Aunt? Guardian? Nobody ever says the word. "I know someone who made it hard to leave" makes it sound like she *trapped* Ada, but then the fight is about Leona *not coming for her*. Those feel like opposite stories. I couldn't work out whether Ada ran away or was sent away.
- **Page 40.** Leona's band "warms." TING. What did that do?
- **Pages 41–42. The bands. I'm lost.** "TWO OF THREE — EMERGENCY AUTHORIZATION." Three bands: Adrian's, Leona's, Mera's. So the dad was on some ruling council with these two? When? That's never been hinted. It's a third version of the dad: gambler, then patent genius, now co-signer of emergency government powers. Then Leona's and Adrian's bands "link into a partial circle," the stamp stays inert, "Then the bands do nothing." / "They did not promise to do that." So what was the point of them? I read pp41–42 twice and still couldn't tell you what happened.
- **Page 44.** "I gave the system authority to move faster than I could stop." So the system — the node? — is the real bad guy? An AI? The book hints at that and never says it.
- **Page 45.** "If I stay, the record stays with the machine." Why does staying keep the record? Why couldn't he leave and take it?
- **Page 48.** "Don't give me that much power." / "I didn't offer it to you." / "You brought me here. Don't make that an answer for everybody." I don't understand this conversation at all. He wasn't giving her anything. Then she steps back "refusing to touch it." Touch what?
- **Page 49.** "CONVENOR OF PROPOSAL." So instead of fixing everything he becomes... the guy who calls the meeting. I get that it's on purpose. It still felt like the ending of a student council election.
- **The water.** Did Keel get its water back? The hearing on p53 is paused, "No instant redistribution occurs," then fourteen months later, and the book never tells me if grandma's tap runs. That was the question from Issue 01 and I don't think it ever got answered.
- **Page 54, last panel.** Ada leaves and Alex doesn't follow. Is this a breakup? Were they together? I never knew what they were to each other, so I can't tell what I'm meant to feel. "The one I choose" — her route, or him? I read it as her leaving. Not sure.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, B11, Tomas: yes.
- Ada: finally has a past, but it shows up three issues in. She mostly comes across as a loner who appears when someone needs a map.
- Leona and Mera: both older women at Oasis with bands, both confessing, both saying "I will not / I will answer for." I kept mixing up who had voted for what and whose band was dark.
- Nadi and Olani: furniture.
- By this issue nearly every adult is giving a speech about responsibility in the same rhythm. Leona, Mera and B11 sound like the same person.

### I felt something
- p39, "And you didn't come."
- p43, people stopped standing near him.
- p45–46, Dad in the water. "Also yes."
- p54, the balance barely moving, and Inez's line marked PAID.
- Nothing at p47–50, the actual climax.

### I predicted
- Dad died down there. Had it since Issue 01.
- Alex wouldn't pull the lever. Once Ada said "don't give me that much power," it was obvious.
- The debt wouldn't go away. Half-guessed it from how the book had been going, and I liked being right.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid finds out his dad died trying to stop a broken system, and he turns down the power to fix everything himself so the decisions go to the people affected, even though that means his own debt stays.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 41/42**, the bands. If I'd somehow got this far on my own, this is where I'd stop: new rules for new objects nobody explained, in the last issue.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the last issue, so: would I buy a next book? Probably not. Page 54 is the best-feeling page in Issue 03, quiet and honest about the debt. But the ending I'm left with is Ada walking off, and I never knew what she was to him, so it didn't hurt the way it wanted to. The question I actually cared about since page 14, whether Keel gets its water, isn't answered. I'd skip a sequel and read the summary someone else posts.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 41; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 22.** Whoever was still with us after the robot dog loses it here: three corporations, four collaborators as a name list, a transfer document, and a dad who was a broke gambler two pages ago now holding 312 patents. Issue 03, pages 41–42 (the bands) would finish off anyone left.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"Kid inherits his dead dad's debt, finds his dad's sarcastic robot and a dog made of magnets, proves the rich city is stealing his town's water, then spends two issues in hearings about patents and emergency powers. The robot's good. Read Issue 1 and the last page."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A strong hook (the birthday debt) and a great dog, but I never knew who Ada or Mera were or which side anyone was on.
- **Issue 02:** A heist about patent paperwork, four interchangeable inventors, and a climax that's a screen changing labels. The water from Issue 1 disappears.
- **Issue 03:** Two really good dad pages (43, 45–46) buried in a committee meeting about magic bands I never understood, and an ending that's honest about the debt but never tells me whether the taps run.
