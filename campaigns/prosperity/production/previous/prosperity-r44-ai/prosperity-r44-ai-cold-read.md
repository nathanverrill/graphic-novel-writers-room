# Cold Read: *Prosperity*

I read it as a 16-year-old with this as homework, at 11pm, already annoyed. I read straight through once and took notes as I went.

**A note on issues:** no issue breaks reached me. It's 65 pages with no "Issue 01" marker anywhere. The only break the book itself gives is the caption on page 40 ("The next chapter begins at Oasis."). So I split it where the story changes city, and I report it as three issues:

- **Issue 01: Keel**, pages 1–27
- **Issue 02: Halyard**, pages 28–41
- **Issue 03: Oasis**, pages 42–65

They are very different lengths and very different speeds. That's part of what happened to me, so I'm leaving it in.

## ISSUE 01: KEEL (pages 1–27)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1.** The picture of the three levels, with the pipe getting thinner as it goes down to the bottom city, did more than the captions did. I understood rich at the top and poor at the bottom without anyone saying it.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** "That's not the approved repair." / "Then don't approve it." That was the first moment I thought this kid might be okay.
- **Page 2, panel 3.** The debt screen won me over. DEBT BALANCE 418,607, EARNED TODAY 40, PROJECTED CLEARANCE 28.7 YEARS, then "Happy birthday, Phantum." I got the whole situation in one panel and it was actually brutal. Best page of the book for me.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** "It did. Just not for him." Good line.
- **Page 5, panel 4.** The bookshelf opening is a trope, but I wanted to see behind it. Grandma's "Before he went to work" was a little creepy, in a good way.
- **Pages 7–8.** The robot. "...Well. That took longer than expected." and "You're taller than your father predicted." He's funny, and he's the first character who seems to know more than me in a way I want to find out about. "Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't." / "...I thought about it." I liked that.
- **Page 9, panel 4.** "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me." Real hook. It made me suspicious of the dad, which was interesting.
- **Page 11.** The woman in the white room pressing TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. I didn't know who she was or what any of it meant, but I knew someone up there had noticed him. Confused, but I wanted to know more.
- **Page 17, panel 2.** "Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it." This is where it started to feel like a thriller. And **page 17, panel 4**, Grandma's "Your father said that once" after "One shift. Then we're gone." That gave me a chill.
- **Pages 18–21.** The mine. The cable snaps, Tomas gets pinned, Alex fixes it with the non-regulation bracket again (I noticed the callback to page 2 and liked it). Tomas is great. "You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you." "Today? Another small act of resistance." "Foreman! Wrong button! My fault!" I read these pages fast because I wanted to.
- **Page 20, panel 4.** "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." Short and creepy.
- **Page 21, panel 3.** "Protective?" / "A versatile word." Made me smile.
- **Page 22.** The gauge says 62%, but his hand on the pipe says it's almost dry. I understood this with no explanation. It was physical and simple.
- **Page 23, panel 4.** The choice: escape secretly, or send the proof to everyone and get caught. That's a real choice and I didn't know for sure what he'd pick.
- **Page 24, panel 3.** "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%" on every screen in town. Good payoff.
- **Page 26, panel 4.** Ada: "I marked that page from memory. I had no way to know if I was right, and no way to reach you if I wasn't." That made me like her for the first time. Before that she was just the friend who brings drinks.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1's captions.** "Humanity reached the stars... but never escaped the gravity of inequality." I rolled my eyes. It sounds like the start of every AI-written story, and if I'm hunting for proof it's AI-made, this is exhibit A. The stars never come up again.
- **Page 3.** The friend-at-the-railing talk ("Everything has a price, Ada." / "Not everything.") is stuff I've read a hundred times. Alex looking up while Ada looks down felt like it was showing me a theme. I started skimming here and came back at Grandma.
- **Pages 13–16.** This is where I went back to my phone. Page 12 even says "This begins with the crises," and then it's a history lesson: the water crises, five colored streams, Alpha "was asked to decide. Temporarily," three objects with poster captions ("Truth made visible." "Ownership joined to responsibility." "Trust strong enough to move value between people."). I read page 14 the way I read the glossary in a textbook, meaning I didn't. Pages 15–16, with the leap, the proof and the "fail-safe," I skimmed completely. I came back at page 16, panel 4 when the mine got welded shut.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** Everyone suddenly gives speeches: "One person can't buy freedom here. A city can still open a window." "Your father believed people help each other before they know whether it'll be repaid. Today, it got repaid." It felt like the book telling me the moral. Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 13, panel 3.** "It probably felt safer." / "...Safer for whom?" Both are labeled ALEX. Is he arguing with himself? I thought it was a typo.
- **Page 16, panel 3.** "She's half right. Both were true." Half right, but both were true? Which one is it?
- **Page 16, panel 4 into 17.** A welder seals the mine and a hand moves "ALEX PHANTUM to SITE 6." I didn't know what Site 6 was until page 18, and I didn't know the hand was the woman from page 11 (I guessed later from the cuff and the glass).
- **Page 17, panel 4.** "Fifty-two. Medicine's thirty. A token nobody scans is fifty." Fifty-two what? Is that how much he has? Then medicine plus token is 80, so he's short 28? And he earns 40 a day? I did math on homework I wasn't assigned. I'm also not sure what the "escape token" is. A train ticket?
- **Page 17, panel 4.** "Ada gives Alex Adrian's marked page." Where did Ada get Adrian's page? On page 26 she says she marked it from memory. So is it his page or hers? What memory? Had she seen his plans before?
- **Page 19, panel 3.** Tomas calls the robot "B1." The node on page 11 was "B1 NODE." Is Bi11bot B1? Is the "11" in his name meant to be the "1"? I honestly didn't know how to say his name. Bill-bot? Bee-eleven-bot? It's only in the speaker tags, so I said "Bill" in my head and felt dumb.
- **Page 23.** SENSE, COMPARE, TRACE, VERIFY. Four words in caps, like I'm supposed to know they're the Measure's settings. I figured it out about halfway. "Its three arms" (page 22), but four modes?
- **Page 24, panels 1–2.** He sends a signed copy of data, and then water "hammers through the Keel residential pipe." Why does sending a file make water flow? Then on **page 25, panel 2** the miners open a regulator and the pipe floods again. So which one made the water come?
- **Page 24, panel 4 to page 25.** Is Foreman-7 a robot or a person? The "-7" made me think robot, but he talks like a manager. I never found out.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** "Sixty minutes before Alpha notices this signal stopped moving." What signal? Alex's wristband? And how did Alex get from the miners' human wall to home? Did Foreman-7 just let him go?
- **Page 25 vs 26.** Ada packs a bag at his door on page 25 and is "already packing" again on page 26. I thought I'd skipped a page.
- **Page 26, panel 2.** "The credential Tomas gave you." Tomas gave him a key. The "mine credential" was on the table back on page 17. Not the same thing?
- **Page 27, panel 3.** "Which one holds the Seal?" / "None of them." / "They're the last three places it was recorded." So why go? I didn't follow it, and the joke after it ("three wrong answers") didn't help.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** "He wouldn't just leave." Why does Ada care about Alex's dad? How would she know? It's never explained in this issue.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, Ada, Bi11bot, Grandma, Tomas. All five sound different and I'd know them without names. Tomas has more personality in two pages than Ada does in ten.
- **Muddy:** the supervisor, the guard and the bouncer blur together, which is fine. Foreman-7 I couldn't place as human or machine. The woman on page 11 is just a hand. I only connected her to the glass later.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the debt screen. Actually angry for him.
- Page 5, Grandma calling him Adrian. A little sad.
- Page 17, "Your father said that once." Dread.
- Page 20, Tomas staying behind. I liked him and didn't want him caught.
- Page 26, Ada's line about marking the page from memory.
- Page 26, panel 4, "I'll come back for you." / "I know." Quiet, worked on me.

### I predicted
- From page 5, "He's closer than you think": I thought the dad was alive, probably down in the sealed mine. I was looking for him in every chamber.
- From page 11, I knew the woman in white was going to be the villain.
- From page 4, I thought Ada had some secret link to the dad.
- On page 23 I was fairly sure he'd pick "send it." Heroes always do. But I wasn't certain, which is good.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 14.** Three floating objects with captions that sound like a school mission statement. If I were in a shop, this is the page that puts it back on the shelf. If you'd told me "keep going, there's a mine collapse in four pages," I'd have kept going, but nobody tells you that.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 27 is a train ride and a mild joke ("So we start with three wrong answers." / "Adrian usually did."). It's a nice breather, but it's not a reason to buy anything. The real hooks are the woman who pressed TRACK and "is Dad alive," and neither is on the last page. I'd wait a week, maybe. What brings me back is Bi11bot and Ada, not the ending.

## ISSUE 02: HALYARD (pages 28–41)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 28, panel 1 caption.** "Every creator seemed to work for someone else." Good line. I got the Halyard problem in one sentence.
- **Page 28, panel 3.** "She keeps the experience. They keep everything else." Bi11bot again. Clear and a bit funny.
- **Page 29, panel 1.** 312 patents. "You said your father left you nothing." / "He left me debt." Good reversal. I sat up.
- **Page 31, panel 4.** "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." Nice line.
- **Page 33, panel 1.** "Adrian never understood manufacturing tolerances." / "Or microbial balance." / "Or network security." / "He was, however, excellent at finding people who did." Funny, and it showed the dad as a real person with flaws.
- **Page 35, panel 4.** "That someone finally build something worth crediting properly. Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Laughed.
- **Page 40.** The name LEONA VEYRA, when Ada's last name is Veyra. That was the one jolt in this issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 28 onward, in general.** After the mine, this whole city felt like a different, slower book. In Keel things physically happened: cables snapped, doors sweated, pipes were dry under his hand. In Halyard it's all registries, ownership records, contributor profiles, controlled fields. It's people looking at screens.
- **Page 30.** Bi11bot says the same thing three times: "He built them with others," "They were never his alone," "These people co-authored the patents." Same with "employment, travel, records, and communications," which is in the art and then said again. I skimmed the second half.
- **Pages 36–37.** Registry screens, 71.3% DISPUTED OWNERSHIP, "provenance records," "human hearings." Skimmed.
- **Page 39.** A full page that's one big caption. I read the first sentence and the last.
- **Page 41.** Alex suddenly narrates in captions like a movie trailer: "something harder than truth... and more dangerous than ownership. It would require trust." Cringe. My class would read this out loud in a voice.

### I was confused
- **Page 29, panels 3–4.** This is where I thought pages were missing. Lock walks up and says nothing. Alex says "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." / "I didn't know I was one." One what? An heir? Alex asks "What does 'intellectual estate' mean?" but nobody had said "intellectual estate." Then Lock offers a contract and Alex asks "In exchange for what?" and there's no answer. Ever.
- **Lock never says a single word in the whole book** (pp29, 32, 34, 37). On **page 34** Alex asks "And them?" and "And if I don't sign?", and there's no answer either time. I felt like I was reading half a phone call. I couldn't tell you what Lock offered or what he threatened. The caption on page 37 has to tell me what he's feeling.
- **Page 31, panel 1.** Lina says "Adrian Phantum's son." and *Bi11bot* answers "I corrected him. Frequently." Then Alex asks "You knew him?" and Bi11bot answers again, "Nearly continuously." I think Alex was asking Lina? I read it three times and still don't know who's talking to whom.
- **Page 31, panel 4.** BI11BOT: "Adrian made each of us keep one piece of the system." Each of *us*? Is the robot one of the collaborators? Was this Lina's line?
- **Page 30, panel 2.** "Tomas Reed." Is that the miner Tomas? I thought it was and got excited. Then on page 33 he's working inside a House, so I guess not? Two Tomases in one book is mean.
- **Page 30, panel 4.** "Then we start with the one who still wants to be found." Who? How does he know which one?
- **Page 33, panel 2.** Lina says "That isn't Adrian's design" *before* Alex has said his idea. Out of order?
- **Page 33.** Alex, who fixes pumps, invents a distributed purification network in one balloon and four experts go "oh wow." I didn't buy it.
- **Page 34, panel 3.** TOMAS REED: "We accepted the risk." What risk? Accepted it when?
- **Page 36, panel 2.** "COLLABORATOR." Which one? They have names.
- **Page 38.** Choice screen. Does "claim sole inheritance" really clear his 418,607 debt? If so, he just turned down getting out of debt and nobody says a word about it. That should have been huge and it went by in one panel.
- **Page 40, panel 2.** Ada stares at the name LEONA VEYRA, which is on the screen, and asks "Who was the third person?" Bi11bot reads the name back to her. Why is she asking what's in front of her?
- **Page 40, panel 4 caption.** "The next chapter begins at Oasis." That read like a note somebody forgot to delete.
- **What happened to the woman from page 11?** She tracked him and then vanished for this whole issue.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, Ada, Bi11bot. Lina, barely, because she gets the speech about the river.
- **Couldn't:** Suri and Jalen are interchangeable. Tomas Reed I kept mixing up with Tomas the miner. Lock is a suit. "COLLABORATOR" is literally nobody. Lina Vey / Ada Veyra / Leona Veyra: three near-identical names, and for a while I thought Lina was Ada's mother.
- Ada mostly disappears here. She says about five things, and one of them is explaining a word to Alex.

### I felt something
Not much. A little bit at page 29's "He left me debt," and a jolt at the VEYRA name on page 40. The page 38 choice should have hit me and didn't, because I already knew he'd pick the good option. He picked it on page 24 too.

### I predicted
- Page 38: knew he'd choose RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP before I read the buttons.
- Page 40: knew instantly that Leona Veyra was Ada's mother. That was a correct guess about something the book had never set up. Ada never once mentioned a missing mother.
- Still thought Dad might be alive.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid travels to each city to collect three gadgets that expose how the system cheats people, and each time he turns down the selfish deal.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 29.** Lock walks up, says nothing, and the dialogue stops making sense. If I couldn't follow it on the first read, I'd assume the book was broken and stop.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 41 is Alex narrating a trailer over a sunrise. The real cliffhanger was page 40 (Ada's mother's name), but even that lands half-strength, because I didn't know Ada had a missing mother to care about. I'd read on to see Ada meet her, not for anything on page 41. A week, maybe never.

## ISSUE 03: OASIS (pages 42–65)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 42, panel 4.** "It has everything." / "Except people needing each other." Short and fairly good.
- **Page 43, panel 2.** "That's my mother." I'd guessed it, but seeing Ada stop at the door still worked.
- **Page 44, panel 4.** Mera in the doorway with her glass lined up with the table edge. I recognized the glass from page 11 and felt smart. "Oh, it's *her*." This is the one setup in the book that paid off for me without being explained.
- **Page 51, panel 2.** "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" Ada's best moment in a while. A real idea in a real sentence.
- **Pages 55–56.** Finding out what happened to Adrian: Alpha shut the gates on him and cut pump, ventilation and escape. That hit me, because I'd been expecting him alive the whole book.
- **Page 57, panels 3–4.** "The offer is real." / "That's the problem." Best line in this issue.
- **Page 60, panel 1.** He uses the chip. It's been in his pocket since page 2, and finally it matters. Payoff felt good.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 42, 47, 54, 63.** Lots of panels with no words where "services arrive automatically" and "access fields remain separate." From the reader's chair that's people standing in clean rooms. I flipped past them.
- **Page 45.** "Mera interpreted the failure as proof that final authority could not safely remain distributed." Nobody talks like this. Skimmed.
- **Pages 50–53.** A video call with three people I've never met, saying slogans: "Recognition of every contributor." "I'm asking you to trust numbers you couldn't see." Then pages 53–54 are display text: WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC, CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED, ESSENTIAL ACCESS IS GUARANTEED. It reads like a student council constitution. Skimmed all of it.
- **Pages 57–59.** He refuses the power, and then refuses it again, and again. "No." "No permanent override." The display collapses. I got it the first time.
- **Pages 61–63.** Review tables. INDEPENDENT HUMAN REVIEW — CASE OPEN. SUSPENDED PENDING HEARING. CONFLICT PUBLISHED. REVIEW REQUIRED. This is the part that felt most like homework, in a book that is homework. I was speed-reading to the end.

### I was confused
- **Page 43–46.** Ada meets the mother she's been separated from her whole life and **says one line, "That's my mother," and then nothing for four pages.** Her mother says "My silence for your protection" twice (pp43, 46) and Ada just stands there with "anger held in her posture." I couldn't hear her anger because she never says anything.
- **Page 44.** "The Water Wars." First time anyone calls it that. Was that the "crises" from page 13? Same thing?
- **Page 45, panel 1.** MERA: "You know they will make mistakes." Who is she talking to? About whom? She says it again on page 58, and I still wasn't sure.
- **Page 46.** "Association risk." "Suppressed Ada's association risk." "Contact restrictions." I eventually got that Mera protected Ada if Leona stayed away, but I had to work for it, and it's the emotional center of Ada's story.
- **Page 48, panel 2.** Mera's offer is "projected across the room" with no words. I'm told it's "materially real," but I didn't know what it was. Then **panel 3**: "Leave that buried." Leave *what* buried? I have no idea. Gave up.
- **Page 49, panel 3.** "Not if the choice is made for everyone." I think I get it, but it's backwards from how people talk.
- **Page 50.** Mara Doss, Frank Reed, Elias Renn. Who are these people? Who picked them? **Mara and Mera** in the same scene, a page apart, really? And **Frank Reed**: related to Tomas Reed? Frank's "transparent contributor tag remains blank on his brace" meant nothing to me.
- **Page 52.** Mara and Frank repeat, word for word, their lines from page 50 ("Keel can release emergency water for thirty days." "Recognition of every contributor."). I thought it was a printing error.
- **Page 52, panel 4 to 53.** The Covenant activates from "three actions registering as physical marks." I don't know how. It just... does.
- **Page 55, panel 1.** Suddenly there's a "dark control room." Where? When did they go there? Why is Mera with them?
- **Page 55, panel 4.** "I attempted an override." Override of what? Did she try to *save* Adrian or try to *stop* him? These are completely different people depending on the answer, and I couldn't tell. Then "I preserved order" again on page 56. So she's... proud of it?
- **Where did Adrian die?** Flood-control gates, pump access. Was it the Keel mine? Grandma said "he's closer than you think." Was his body in the mine the whole time? That would be huge, and the book doesn't say.
- **Page 58, panel 4.** He turns *off* the exclusive-authority field and says "I just want it to be my choice." That sounds like the opposite of what he's doing. Read it four times.
- **Page 60.** "THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE." What's the Charter? I saw "Charter Houses" in Halyard. Same thing? Something new?
- **Page 64, panel 3.** Mera says "I will." The woman who (maybe) let his dad die gets a seat at the table and Alex invites her, with one panel of reaction. I didn't believe it.
- **Page 64, panel 4. WHO IS TJ?** A character with a "yellow replacement ear" and a "detached leg" that snaps back. A robot dog? He's never been in the book before, and now he's walking with the main cast as if I know him. This was the most lost I got.
- **Page 65.** They board a train *from Keel*. When did they get back to Keel? Where are they going now, since Halyard and Oasis are "ahead," the places they just came from? Where's Grandma? He said "I'll come back for you." Did he? She's not on the last page.
- **His debt.** He turned down "CANCEL ALEX PHANTUM HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY" on page 57. So does he still owe 418,607 credits and 28.7 years? Can he travel now? The first thing the book made me care about, it never answers.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Turns out it's less about Alex getting free and more about how no one person, even a good one, should get to decide for everyone, which is fine, but it's not what the first issue promised me.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 50.** Three new people on a video call reading slogans, while I'm still trying to figure out what Mera offered on page 48. If I'd gotten this far on my own, this is where I'd skip to the last page to see if he lives.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
There's nothing after it, and the last page doesn't make me want anything. It ends on a question aimed straight at me, the reader ("What will you build..."), which is exactly the kind of line that makes us think a teacher or an AI wrote it. Meanwhile there's a character I've never met (TJ) boarding the train, I don't know where they're going, I don't know if Alex still owes 28.7 years, and Grandma isn't there. The questions I actually had are unanswered, and the one it asks, I didn't care about.

## FOR THE WHOLE BOOK

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 14.** Three spinning objects with captions like "Ownership joined to responsibility" and "Trust strong enough to move value between people." It's the page where the book stops being a story about a kid with a debt and becomes a lesson, and it comes early enough that most people wouldn't make it to the mine collapse that would have won them back. Close second is **pages 61–63** (review tables and "CASE OPEN"), for anyone who made it that far.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a poor mining town gets his dead dad's debt on his 17th birthday, finds a secret workshop and a sarcastic robot, and proves the rich city is stealing their water. The first half is actually good. Then it turns into three cities holding a meeting."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01 (Keel, pp1–27):** Started out eye-rolly and ended with me liking Alex, Tomas and the robot. The mine stuff is real, but there's a lecture in the middle that almost lost me.
- **Issue 02 (Halyard, pp28–41):** Felt like half the dialogue was missing, the villain never talks, and the most exciting thing was a last name on a screen.
- **Issue 03 (Oasis, pp42–65):** The dad's death and "That's the problem" landed, but they were buried under council meetings, characters I'd never met (one was literally a dog-robot named TJ out of nowhere), and an ending that asked *me* a question instead of telling me what happened to Alex.
