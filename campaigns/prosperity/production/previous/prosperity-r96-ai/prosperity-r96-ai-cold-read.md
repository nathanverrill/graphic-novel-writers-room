# Cold Read: Prosperity

I read it from start to finish in one sitting, pages only, as homework the night before it's due. I went in wanting it to be bad. Everything below is what happened to me, in order.

---

## Issue 00: Lake Keel, 2099 (pages 1–10)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 1.** A robot dog that comes apart mid-step and keeps walking in pieces is a good first image. I didn't go back to my phone on page 1, and this panel is why.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** The head floats across the lake while the legs go along a rail. It's weird and specific, not generic sci-fi. I wanted to see what it would do.
- **Page 7.** The lost leg sits on the bottom, its magnet pulses, then it snaps back onto the head and drags it home. Best page of the issue. Nobody explains it and nobody needed to.
- **Page 8, panel 2.** "You could have stopped them." / "I could have started something." The old man let it happen on purpose, and that made me curious about him.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 4–6.** The drone fight. I followed it, but it's all clamping, buckling and spraying, and I was skimming SFX by page 5.

### I was confused
- **Page 1, panel 1.** "His right eye carries a small adjustable brass ring." Whose eye? Alex's or TJ's? On page 3 it's TJ's eye-ring. On page 18 B11 has the brass ring. By the end of the book I still couldn't tell you if TJ and B11 share a part, or if I misread page 1.
- **Page 4, panel 3.** The granddaughter calls him "Alex", not grandpa. I stopped to work out whether she's really his granddaughter.
- **Page 5, panel 2.** "It has the route." What route? Route to where?
- **Page 9, panel 4.** "No white-and-amber control node." I'm being told something is missing that I've never seen. It meant nothing to me.
- **Page 10.** "That isn't why they came here." / "No. It isn't." OK, so why? I'm confused but curious. This is the hook, and it mostly works. But see the end of the book.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Alex Phantum (as a name I'd know) | p1 | Yes, issue 01 |
| TJ | p1 | Sort of: Adrian's robot dog, p17 |
| Siphon drones / "the route" | p2, p5 | No |
| Why water is worth stealing in 2099 | p2–3 | Partly (issue 01 water shortage) |
| "The old man with everything": why he's rich | p3 | **No. Never.** |
| Adrian (the casino chip) | p9 | Dad, issue 01. Never really known as a person |
| White-and-amber control node | p9 | Shows up p19, still not explained |
| Lake terraces / old mine rails | p7–8 | Barely |

### Lines I read twice
- p3: "Water paid for it?" / "Or water made him rich."
- p6: "He always sees the part that costs." I gave up on this one.
- p5: "It knows where home is." That one's fine, actually. I just checked whether it meant TJ or the drone.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, the girl and TJ are clear. Thief 1 and Thief 2 are interchangeable, and that's fine.

### I felt something
A small thing on page 8, panel 4, when the girl kneels for the broken dog. Nothing bigger.

### I predicted
The drone part would turn out to be evidence that someone (not random thieves) was after Alex. I also predicted the book would come back to this night at the end.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old rich man who owns a lake lets water thieves go because something bigger is behind them, and he's hiding a past.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. It's short and visual and I got through it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mild yes. "No. It isn't." is a real question, but it's about a man I've known for ten pages. I'd turn the page tonight because it's short. I wasn't hooked.

VERDICT Issue 00: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 01: Keel, 2043 (pages 11–28)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 11, panel 3.** The gauge says `FLOW: NOMINAL` while water drips on his hand. Instantly clear: the system lies.
- **Page 12.** This is the page that got me. You turn 17 and a scanner hands you your missing dad's debt: 418,607 credits, 28.7 years, travel restricted. Then "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "Twenty-eight point seven." This is the book's best idea and I'm the exact age for it.
- **Page 16, panel 3, to page 17.** The chip lines up with the notebook, then "That isn't a bookcase," then a hidden workshop. Puzzle-box stuff. I liked it.
- **Page 17, panel 3.** It's TJ. Connecting the prologue dog to this kid felt good.
- **Page 18.** B11 is the best character in the book from here on. "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." "A fact with a poor record." He's dry and funny, and he's what kept me reading.
- **Page 26.** Tomas has a key from Adrian, and "Go. I'm about to become paperwork." Good surprise, good line.
- **Page 28, panel 1.** `REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% / DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%`. Finally a problem I understand and care about: they're stealing the city's water and faking the numbers.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 13–14.** The walk with Ada. Short lines, none of which I understood. I skimmed.
- **Page 15.** The clinic loan. I followed the math but didn't know who Tomas was, so I didn't care about the deal yet.
- **Page 22.** The shift reassignment. I skimmed the display.

### I was confused
- **Who is Ada?** Sister? Girlfriend? Friend? Neighbour? She brings him food, knows about Alpha, packs rope, refuses to leave. Nobody ever says what she is to him. I needed this on page 12 and never got it.
- **Page 14, panel 4.** She touches a rivet on her shoulder strap and says "Not everything." No idea.
- **Page 19.** "The exit." Then he looks at the medicine and says "Answers first." Why does Grandma's medicine make him pick answers over escape? I didn't get the logic.
- **Page 20–21.** He plugs in a node, displays across the city flicker, and Ada says "You woke Alpha." What is Alpha? Ada knows. I don't. It's said like I should be scared and I wasn't, because I didn't know what it was.
- **Page 22.** He gets reassigned to the exact site his route ends at. "Convenient." Who did it? Alpha? Ada? B11? Never said.
- **Pages 23–24, the order of events.** Friday evening, he meets Tomas at the clinic and has lost the money. Next page, Tomas is pinned in the mine at Site 6. Is Tomas a miner? Is this the same day? Did they escape on Friday? I lost the timeline here.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** B11: "Yes. What would you do?" I read it twice. Is he asking Alex to teach him? It felt like a line meant for someone else.
- **Page 27, panel 4.** Where did Adrian's case with the Measure come from? Is it the "sealed metal case" B11 came out of? I assumed so, but I wasn't sure.

### Lines I read twice
- p13: "He built and bet with the same hands." / "One of them paid better." / "Which one?" / "Not ours." I read it three times. Which "one of them": building or betting? "Not ours" answers which question?
- p14: "Not everything." I gave up.
- p15: "I need the money. Not a story." / "Make it real."
- p21: "It was sleeping badly."
- p25: "Yes. What would you do?"

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, B11, TJ, Foreman-7 and Tomas are all distinct. Grandmother is a prop who exists to need medicine. Ada I could pick out of a panel, but I couldn't tell you who she is.

### I felt something
- Page 12, the birthday. Real.
- Page 24, when he steps over the line and loses 40 credits to lift Tomas out. Small but real, because I'd seen him count every credit.

### I predicted
That Tomas would come back. That the water numbers were rigged (page 11 told me, so page 28 confirmed it rather than surprising me). That Adrian is dead.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A 17-year-old saddled with his vanished father's debt finds his dad's hidden robots and proves the city is being fed fake water numbers.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 14.** Two pages of riddle dialogue between a boy and a girl I can't place. If page 12 hadn't been so good I'd have stopped there.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. The evidence is public, the gate slams, and the beams close in on people I now actually know: B11, Tomas, sort of Alex. That's a real cliffhanger. I'd turn the page.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 14; next issue: yes

## Issue 02: Halyard, 2043 (pages 29–46)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 29.** Tomas and the miners just standing there. "This is just where we stand." Quiet and good.
- **Page 30, panel 3.** B11: "It is a route. The destination objected." I laughed.
- **Page 34.** `CREATOR: UNLISTED / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS` / "My correction is in there." That's clear and unfair in a way I instantly got. Then 312 patents in Adrian's name. So his dad left him debt and a fortune he can't touch? That's interesting.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 30–32.** The flood escape. It's water, rope, a rail that breaks, more water. Generic action, and I skimmed it.
- **Pages 37–40.** Four pages, four strangers, each hands Alex one part. It's a fetch quest. Each person gets one page and about one line of personality. By Ren I was flipping.
- **Page 42.** `NOT A POTABLE-OUTPUT GUARANTEE` and `TESTED PROTOTYPE — SERVICE REQUIRED — TREATMENT LIMITS APPLY`. This read like a legal disclaimer. Honestly, this is where I first thought "did an AI write this," because a person wouldn't hang a safety label on the big triumphant moment.
- **Page 43, panel 4.** Alex reads out all five names and says "No one gets to turn five into one." It's a speech, and I could feel it being a speech.

### I was confused
- **Page 33.** "Caldera House." Then p39 "Vector House," then p40 "Orbital House." What is a House? A company? A family? A government? Never explained. I guessed corporations.
- **Page 35.** Lock appears out of nowhere: "Director." Director of what? How did he know Alex was here?
- **Page 35, panel 3.** `COLLABORATOR PROTECTION: PENDING`. I didn't know what the threat to the collaborators was. Protected from what?
- **Page 39.** Jalen "remains behind," then on page 41 he's in the doorway. Fine, but I wasn't sure he'd changed his mind or was just watching.
- **Page 44, panel 1.** Where did the Maker's Seal come from? Lina just has it. What does it do that a registry button doesn't?
- **Page 44, panel 4.** He releases the design for free. OK. Does that mean his debt stays? Does he lose the patents? Did he just give up the thing that could clear his debt? If so, that's a big choice and the page doesn't make me feel it, because I'm not sure it's what happened.
- **Pages 45–46.** Why are shutters coming down? Who ordered it? Why is Lina dragged away? Is she arrested?
- **Page 46, panel 4.** `CONVENOR SUMMONS: OASIS CIVIC ADMINISTRATION`. Convenor? Oasis? Two new words on the cliffhanger panel.

### Lines I read twice
- p31: "I am carrying less than I appear to be." / "Keep appearing." I gave up.
- p37: "Then make it function where the House can't erase you."
- p38: "It's slow. It's alive. It doesn't fail quietly."
- p39: "That's not a plan. It's a death wish with extra steps." Not confusing, but I've heard it in a hundred movies.
- p40: "I'm avoiding a worse failure."
- p41: "Then we give it a smaller failure."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
B11 and Tomas are still the clearest. Lock is a suit. **I could not tell Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren apart as people**, only by which part they handed over: membrane girl, bug-tank girl, data-shard guy, heat girl. If you asked me tomorrow which one said "build it so a customer can repair it," I'd guess. Ada mostly stands next to things this issue.

### I felt something
Page 34's 14-credit worker, briefly. Page 46, panel 2, Lina's "Don't sign anything" as she's shoved through. A little.

### I predicted
That Alex would refuse Lock's deal (obvious from p35). That the purifier would get built. That Lock would retaliate.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex goes to the rich city to claim his dad's inventions, but chooses to credit the people who actually made them and give the design away.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 38.** Second stranger in a row handing over a part. I'd figured out the pattern and knew there were two more.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Kind of. Ada and Alex being split up works a little. But the summons is a word I don't know, from a place I don't know, and I wasn't scared of it. It's more of a "next location" sign than a cliffhanger. I'd wait.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 38; next issue: yes

## Issue 03: Oasis, 2043 (pages 47–64)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 49.** "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" Strongest scene in the book. I figured out Leona is Ada's mother who left, and nobody had to say it. Ada finally becomes a person here, on page 49 of 64.
- **Page 50.** "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." Hit hard.
- **Page 54.** Adrian in rising water, "Alex. The rest is yours to choose," the bulkhead closes, his hand disappears. Effective, though it would have hurt more if I'd ever seen Adrian alive.
- **Page 63, panel 3.** `MEDICINE ADVANCE: PAID` / "You kept counting." The Tomas loan paying off was the one ending that felt earned.
- **Page 63, panel 4.** Debt still 402,110. I liked that the book didn't magically clear it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 51.** "I will remain where affected people can answer me." The emotional mother scene turns into a civics oath. I felt the scene go cold.
- **Page 52.** Recording, challenge and recall, bands warming. Skimmed.
- **Page 56.** "Evidence. Attribution. Obligation." This is where the book told me what the three objects symbolised. It felt like the lesson plan showing through.
- **Pages 57–60.** Authority transfer, the lever, the refusal, the representatives structure. I skimmed most of the displays. `EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS`: I did not read that whole thing.
- **Page 61.** Three tables with three reps each, paper strips, physical markers. Student-council meeting. Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 50.** "Covenant band." New thing, on top of the "dark metal band" from p17. Are these the same kind of object?
- **Page 52, panel 4.** Leona's band links to Adrian's band "carried by B11," and "a separate dark band remains untouched." Whose? Three bands: Adrian's, Leona's and…? I never found out.
- **Page 53.** Mera. "I was Mera Vale. I had executive access." Who is she? Why is Alex with her? How did he get to Oasis? The last I saw, he was at a Halyard junction.
- **Page 53, panel 2.** She sets a water glass straight, then leaves it crooked. Clearly meant to mean something. I don't know what.
- **Page 54, panel 4.** B11: "I may have mistaken that for an instruction." Mistaken what? Was he not supposed to transmit? Did B11 fail Adrian? I couldn't work it out, and it sounds like it matters.
- **Page 56.** Ada is suddenly at the civic hall table. Fine, but when did she and Alex meet back up? The split on page 46 was the cliffhanger, and the reunion happened off-page.
- **Page 57.** "Alpha has no face or body." This is the first time the book tells me anything about Alpha, and it's on page 57. I still don't know what Alpha did all these years, why it chased Adrian, or who built it. It's "mentioned casually in passing, so I didn't understand it when it mattered."
- **Page 58, panel 3.** "Don't make two people the answer for everybody." Two? Alex and who? Mera? I read it three times.
- **Page 62.** I thought Alex released the purifier design for free on page 44. So why does Caldera have sealed crates of them? I don't get how this connects.
- **Page 63.** **Morrow & Sons.** Who is Morrow? There's no Morrow in this book. There are no sons. I genuinely thought I'd missed a page.
- **Page 64.** What gate? Where's he going, and why does he need an application? Is he leaving Keel for good?
- **The whole book vs. Issue 00.** The prologue asked why the thieves came, and the book never answers it. It also never explains how a man with 402,110 debt who refused all power ends up "the old man with everything" with his own lake. Who is the granddaughter's grandmother? Is it Ada? I wanted the ending to come back to the prologue and it never did.

### Lines I read twice
- p50: "I traded days I could prevent for years I couldn't see." I gave up. I think it means "I stayed away to protect you," but I'm not sure.
- p53: "I preserved order. That is true. It was not enough."
- p54: "I may have mistaken that for an instruction."
- p58: "Don't make two people the answer for everybody."
- p59: "And who sees you?"
- p60: "Three temporary representatives from each affected place. Public records. Recall if they stop answering." Not a riddle. I read it twice because it's a policy, not a line of dialogue.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Ada finally comes alive with Leona. Tomas is still great. B11 goes quiet this issue, which is a loss. **Leona and Mera blurred**: two older Oasis women with bands and regrets about the past. I had to check which one was the mom. Alex gets flatter as the book goes on. By page 59 he's mostly saying no to things.

### I felt something
- Pages 49–50, Ada and her mother. Yes.
- Page 54, Adrian's hand behind the bulkhead. Yes, a bit.
- Page 63, "You kept counting." Yes.
- Pages 56–62, the big climax: nothing.

### I predicted
That he'd refuse the lever, from the moment it was put on the table. That Adrian was dead. That Ada wouldn't answer at the end.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who could take total power to fix a rigged water system refuses it and sets up a public, checkable system instead. By the end that's clearly the point, and I could feel it being the point.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 60.** The hero ends the climax by proposing a committee. I'd already been drifting on page 56.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The ending is a quiet "Ada does not answer" next to a pending application. It's not a cliffhanger, and it's not a full ending either. The only real reason to want more is the 2099 prologue, which this book set up and then forgot. If I'm honest, I wouldn't go looking for a sequel to find out.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 60; next issue: no

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 56.** "Evidence. Attribution. Obligation." That's where the book stops being a story and turns into the worksheet, and everyone in class would see it. Pages 60–61, with temporary representatives at three tables, would finish off anyone still reading. Runner-up: page 42's warning label, which is where some of us would start saying "AI wrote this."

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid gets his missing dad's debt for his 17th birthday, finds his dad's robot dog and a sarcastic robot, and proves the city's stealing its water. Then it turns into a civics lesson about not taking power. The robot's good, the mom scene's good, and you never find out what Alpha is."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 00:** A cool robot dog that falls apart and a question the book never answers.
- **Issue 01:** The birthday-debt page and B11 hooked me. Ada and Alpha were blanks I was supposed to fill in myself.
- **Issue 02:** One clear injustice (the 14-credit bonus), then a four-stop fetch quest with people I couldn't tell apart.
- **Issue 03:** One real scene between Ada and her mother, then a climax made of saying no and setting up a committee, and a "Morrow & Sons" sign for a Morrow I never met.
