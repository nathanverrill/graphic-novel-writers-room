# Cold Read B — Prosperity

One version, read once, in order, night-before-homework conditions. I did not want to open it. Page 2 got me anyway.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2, the whole checkpoint display. 418,607 credits against 40 earned today, "PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS," and the guard's "Happy birthday, Phantum." I did the math myself — that's the hook. Inheriting your father's debt on your birthday is a rule I immediately wanted to see broken.
- Page 1 held me before that: the bracket, "Then don't approve it," and the warning icon that stays lit even after the gauge goes amber. The system never fully forgives. One panel, understood.
- Page 9: "He's a tool." / "So is the gate." I laughed. First time the book made me laugh on purpose.
- Page 10, stepping over the yellow line: "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR." Because page 2 taught me what 40 credits is, this actually hurt.
- Page 15: PHYSICAL FLOW 18% sitting under REPORTED FLOW 62%. Cleanest moment in the issue. I understood it instantly and it re-explained every dusty page before it.
- Page 16, pressing SEND — giving up the secret route to make the number public. A real choice with a real price. I turned the page fast.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 5–6, the workshop. A lot of objects at once — chip, chalkboard equation, labeled key, machine under cloth — and the dialogue goes riddle-y right when I wanted to look around. I didn't skim, but I caught myself waiting for the robot to wake up so someone would talk normally.
- Page 17: four locations in four panels (Tomas at the cart, Ada at a hatch, Mera at a valve, the conduit). I read it twice to sort out who was where.

### I was confused
- Page 8: Mera picks TRACK, says "Don't make me choose blind," and the system answers by reassigning Alex's shift to Site 6 at 02:14. Did she do that? Did Alpha? Why does the system want Alex at Site 6 at all? Never cleared up. Confused but intrigued.
- Page 11: TJ. Parts levitate out of B11's bag and click into a dog. B11 says "Recently." So B11 built a dog robot inside the bag at some point? I accepted it because the dog is fun, but I have no idea where TJ came from.
- Page 12: "Someone has to make this look like an accident." Make *what* look like an accident? I decided Tomas means he'll cover their escape, but the line sits wrong — it almost reads as a threat.
- Page 17: Mera turns "ASCENT PRESSURE" and then the conduit they're inside floods behind them. Was she helping them or flushing them? I think the pressure blows the hatch they escape through, but on first read it looked like she tried to drown them.
- Page 18: "TOMAS PHANTUM?" — question mark in the display. Is Tomas related to Alex? The book raises it and never touches it again. I checked all the way through issue 3. Nothing.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel (page 1) — fine, the dust tells me.
- Alpha (page 8, "REPORT TO ALPHA") — never explained anywhere in the book. I inferred it's the AI running everything. Nobody ever says so.
- Houses (page 8, "No House crest") — first mention, no explanation. Had to wait for issue 2 to learn Caldera is one.
- Ada (page 3) — just standing by the door with a surname. Friend? Sister? The book never says what she is to Alex. I ran with "friend."
- The casino chip's "looping equation" (page 2) — presented like a loaded gun, matches the chalkboard on page 5, and by page 46 it's just set down beside a recorder. It never does anything. I kept waiting for it to matter mechanically.
- Travel token (page 3) — clear from context. Good.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 4: "Square." / "Tied, then. Not square." Still not sure what "tied" means here. Bound to each other? A bet? I gave up and moved on.
- Page 5: "That's not a route." / "No." What's not a route — the key? The label? Felt written to be quoted, not understood.
- Page 12: "Someone has to make this look like an accident." (above)
- Page 13: "The survey says straight. / The bolts say left." Got it on the second pass — paper versus pipe. It's good once it lands, but it made me work for it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Clear: Alex (fixes things his own way), Inez (numbers, no mercy, fronts the money anyway), Grandmother (the cloth, the two-sided verdict on Adrian), B11 (polite machine with a mouth on him — "It is a fact with a poor record"), Tomas (old miner, talks in riddles).
- Ada is the blur. Three pages in she's just there, being wry. I couldn't have told you one thing she wants until issue 3.
- "Phantum." I rolled my eyes at the surname. It sounds like a gamertag.

### I felt something
- Page 2: the debt display. Trapped.
- Page 4: "Your father was working. / That doesn't mean he didn't hurt us." Both things at once. The line I believed most in the issue.
- Page 10: the −40 CR. Genuine wince.
- Page 16: the SEND. The whole issue exhaled.

### I predicted
- The dad was dead and would turn up in recordings — confirmed page 7.
- The chip would unlock something — wrong. It's a keepsake.
- "Someone noticed" (page 7) meant trouble at Site 6 — sort of, via Foreman-7.
- Tomas's "signal unavailable" was a fake-out — confirmed page 37, and I'm glad it was fast.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A debt-slave kid in a dried-out town finds proof the water numbers are lies and has to decide what exposing them will cost.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue. Page 2 had me. First eye-roll was page 5's riddle-talk, but the secret room carried me over it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 18: yes. Not because of "signal unavailable" — that felt like a trick and I didn't believe it — but because 62/18 is now public and I want to see what the system does about it, and because "TOMAS PHANTUM?" is a real question. (A question the book never answers, it turns out.)

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
- Page 20: the order sheet (parts 32 / labor 20 / margin 8 / maintenance NO) against the kiosk taking the girl's component for a 14-credit "creator bonus," her hand staying on it after the kiosk takes it. That panel did more than any speech in the issue.
- Page 22: "From being asked to choose while frightened." Best line in the issue. Lock is a good villain because he offers real things.
- Page 27: SIMULATED RECOVERY: 11%. They build the thing and it FAILS. I believed the book in that moment.
- Page 28: the sleeve fix, 94%. It rhymes with the bracket on page 1 — Alex's whole talent is joining things that aren't supposed to join. I noticed the rhyme and liked it.
- Page 33: "No." One word, after Lock offers to erase the debt tonight. Earned.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 23–26. Lina, then Suri, then Jalen, then Ren — four new names in four pages, each with one wise line and one machine part. It felt like collecting party members in a game. I skimmed Suri's and Jalen's scenes and only really registered "biofilter lady" and "timing guy."
- Page 31: the shrink-it montage. Fine, but it's the second montage in five pages.

### I was confused
- Page 22: "The collaborators remain employed." What collaborators? We haven't met them yet. It made sense two pages later, but on first read I stopped and went back.
- Page 23: Lina says "You're late." How does she know Alex? Who told her he was coming? B11 was folded in a bag. Never explained.
- The whole estate layer. Adrian has 312 patents, 19 disputed, and somehow four workers in Halyard each hold one piece of a purifier. Did they work with Adrian? Alex is 17 and Adrian's been gone since Alex was small, so the math only works if they're much older than they read. I never understood how Adrian's estate connects to their work, and the book treats it as obvious. Confused and stayed detached — it never resolved.
- Page 30: the patrol can't enter "without triggering a public access record." Why does that stop an armed patrol? Hand-waved.
- Page 35: "OWNERSHIP CLAIM ALTERATION: EXPOSED." Who tried to alter it? Nobody is named. The Seal just catches someone, somehow.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Mara Morrow (page 19, "Mara's still shipping") — Ada knows her; I don't. She appears on page 32.
- Caldera House (page 20), then Vector and Orbital (page 22) — three Houses, never defined. Corporation? Government? Guild? I settled on "company-towns with logos."
- "Charter review" (page 21) — never explained.
- The Maker's Seal (page 24) — a medallion that later warms up and verifies oaths. What it actually *is*, technically, is never said. It's a magic object wearing a hard hat.
- Suri is named on page 23 ("Suri's living biofilter tanks") before she's introduced on page 24.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 20: "Morrow asks customers. The House asks Alpha." / "Same machine?" / "Different wager." I get the gist — two economies — but "different wager" is B11 being cute.
- Page 26: "The House standard rejects the Caldera cycle." / "So the parts are wrong?" / "The edges are wrong." Needed the second read to get that it's literally about connector edges — which then pays off on page 28.
- Page 29: "I counted the record and missed the people." Got it first time, but it's the thesis said out loud, and "What would you do?" — the robot asking the kid for a moral lesson — wobbles toward after-school special. I let it pass because the wristband economics earned it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lock: instantly distinct. The stylus, the calm, the offers.
- Lina (dry — "That explains the smell") and Ren (possessive of his work, files edges by hand): distinct.
- Suri and Jalen: one wise line each. I know them by their machines, not their voices.
- The names are a real problem. Lina VEY, Ada VEYRA, Mera VALE — three different women with near-identical surnames. And MERA Vale versus MARA Morrow — I mixed those two up at least twice; on page 19 I thought "Mara's still shipping" might be the Oasis woman. Whoever named this cast never said the names out loud.

### I felt something
- Page 20: the hand staying on the component.
- Page 22: "protection from being asked to choose while frightened" — I felt how tempting the offer is. Good villainy.
- Page 29: the unregistered workers at the window.
- Pages 33–34: the naming. A little speechy, but when each of the four says their contribution out loud, it landed.

### I predicted
- Alex would refuse Lock's deal — yes, but I didn't predict the naming-into-the-recorder move, which is better than what I expected.
- The Seal would go to Alex — yes, from page 24.
- Lock would be the book's final boss — WRONG. He vanishes after page 35. Issue 3 has no Lock at all.
- Mera would turn out to be one of the Covenant three — yes.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Who gets to own the things that keep people alive — and whether the people who did the work get to keep their names on it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 24. Third new face in two pages, each dispensing one aphorism; if I were browsing I'd have shelved it there. The 11% failure on page 27 is what would have made me regret it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 36: a summons to appear at a node. It's a door, not a cliffhanger — nobody I care about is in danger, and "SCOPE: EVIDENCE / ATTRIBUTION / OBLIGATION" reads like a meeting invite. I'd turn to issue 3 anyway, but on momentum (the estate unresolved, the debt untouched, Tomas missing), not because the summons grabbed me.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
- Page 39: "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" Then "Did you know where I was?" / "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." Fastest, hardest scene in the issue. No machines in it. I noticed that.
- Page 43: "First people stopped selling to him." / "Then people stopped standing near him." How a record kills a person without touching him. Best pair of lines in the issue.
- Page 50: the refusal has a price — debt unchanged, clearance expired, workshop sealed. I braced for a cheap win and didn't get one.
- Page 51: "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." The refrain, now in a suit. Still works.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 41–44. Bands, custodians, two-of-three rules, record cards, a denial field — the book stops walking and starts lecturing. I skimmed the record cards.
- Page 47: three artifacts on a table each running a VERIFY check. The book's theme rendered as a control panel. I read it like a menu.
- Page 53: the room fills with arguments. I skimmed the noise and waited for the decision, which is "keep talking."
- By this issue everyone talks in one-liners — Tomas, Leona, Mera, B11 all sound like the same quotable voice. That uniformity is exactly the thing that sets off a class's "this was generated" radar, and mine pinged.

### I was confused
- Page 40: "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." Read it three times. I think she chose a short visible horror over a long invisible one, but I'm still not sure which days were which.
- Page 41: "During the Water Wars, three people held the Covenant." First and only explanation of the Water Wars — one line, page 41 of 54. The war that built this whole world gets one sentence. Confused and detached; the book never came back to it.
- Page 42: "Two can expose an obligation. Two cannot restore the old permission." Okay after a re-read, but the band rules — what two can do, what three can do, why Adrian's band works without Adrian — never went stable in my head.
- Pages 44–45: Mera's override was denied — by Alpha? By the other custodians? And is Adrian's flooding station the same event as the door closing on page 45? I think so, but the geography of Adrian's death(?) is fog.
- Page 48: "Don't give me that much power." / "I didn't offer it to you." / "You offered it to us." When did he offer it to anyone? I get what Ada means — taking it decides for everyone — but the wording tripped me hard.
- Page 52: is Nadi a villain or a dupe? "Someone changed the route after the record was signed." *Someone.* The book's final antagonist is a faceless "Orbital industrial branch" interest, and Lock is nowhere in this issue. The bad side never gets its face back.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The Water Wars (page 41) — one line, never again.
- The Covenant (the word arrives page 36, the rules page 41) — I pieced it together, but the piecing was work.
- Leona Veyra (page 38) — Ada's mother, dropped in with sixteen pages left, carrying a fifteen-year backstory.
- Adrian's band inside B11's torso (page 41) — he was carrying it the whole time. "I did not know when it would matter." B11 keeps turning out to have pockets nobody mentioned.
- What the Seal and the bands actually ARE as objects — they warm, they pulse, they verify. Technology? Relics? Never said.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 40: "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." (above)
- Page 42: "Two can expose an obligation. Two cannot restore the old permission."
- Page 48: the whole "Don't give me that much power" exchange.
- Page 53: "Don't ask the number to do the seeing." Got it, liked it — but it's another aphorism in an issue full of them.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Leona: immediately real. Her scene with Ada is the best writing in the issue.
- Nadi and Olani: functions, not people. Tablet and ledger.
- Mera finally became a person on page 44 ("I tried after the door was closing") — thirty-six pages after she first appeared.
- Still stumbling over Vey / Veyra / Vale, and now there are two Veyras.

### I felt something
- Pages 39–40: Ada and Leona. "You chose safety instead of me." That one stayed with me after I closed the book.
- Page 46: "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Alex gets to hold both. The book has been consistent about this since page 4 and I felt the consistency pay off.
- Pages 49–50: the refusal and its bill. Respect more than emotion.
- Page 54: Grandmother sets the cloth down instead of refolding it. Small, and I caught it, and it worked.

### I predicted
- He'd refuse the authority — saw it coming from page 47. The book's whole shape points at it, so it landed as correct rather than surprising.
- Mera was the third custodian — yes.
- Tomas Phantum would be explained — NO. Dropped completely. Page 18 asked the question and the book forgot it.
- Adrian might be alive — the recording "cuts before any evacuation or death is shown," which is sequel bait I can smell. Fine, but I smell it.
- Page 54's ledger: 418,607 down to 402,110 is 16,497 credits paid. At 40 a day that's over a year of full wages with nothing spent on food or medicine. I checked. The math wobbles.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether anyone should hold emergency power forever — and a kid deciding that publishing the truth beats taking the throne.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 42. Band rules, light colors, two-of-three mechanics — this is where the book most feels like the homework it is. I'd have finished anyway because it's almost over, but page 42 is where I'd have started bargaining with myself about skim-reading the rest.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 54: no — and I mean it as a near-compliment. The ledger still says 402,110, the workshop's sealed, Grandmother finally sets the cloth down, "Neither moves for the other." It ends like a story that knows it's done. The threads it leaves — Adrian maybe alive, Tomas Phantum never explained — feel like bait, not hunger. I wouldn't buy the next book, because this one finished.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 42; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 47. Three objects on a table running VERIFY checks and a node lighting up "HUMAN DESIGNATION REQUIRED." It's the book's argument drawn as a settings menu, and it's the exact moment a skeptical class decides the book is a lesson wearing a costume. Runner-up: page 41, where the Water Wars get one line. First audible groans: page 20, "Different wager."

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a company town inherits his dad's massive debt, finds out the water company is lying about how much water the town gets — the pipe says 18%, the paper says 62% — and spends the book proving the records lie and the pipes don't. There's a robot with a scarf. It's about who owns water and who gets credit for the work. It's smarter than it looks, and it knows it."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: Dust, debt math, and a number that changes everything — the one I'd reread.
- Issue 02: Collects a team and a thesis; alive when they're building, stiff when they're talking.
- Issue 03: The book stops to explain itself, then ends honestly in a meeting — I respect it more than I enjoyed it.
