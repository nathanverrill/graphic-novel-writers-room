# Cold Read — Prosperity

Read in one sitting, Issue 01 through Issue 03, pages 1–54, in order. I read it as the assigned-homework reader: 16, has read plenty of graphic novels, opened it at 10pm wanting it to be bad, and ready to call it AI the first time it sounds like a worksheet.

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, all of it.** This is where the book got me. It's his birthday, he's seventeen, and the gate scanner hands him his dad's debt: 418,607 credits, 40 earned today, 28.7 years to pay it off. The guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum." and the workers behind him go quiet. I got it straight away and it made me angry for him. Best page in the issue.
- **Page 2, panel 3.** "WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED" plus the casino chip. Okay, the dad's gone and he was a gambler. I wanted to know more.
- **Page 6.** The robot. "I was stored. Sleep is more comfortable." and "It is a fact with a poor record." I liked B11 right away. This page is why I didn't stop.
- **Page 10, panels 3–4.** He steps over the line and loses the whole day's pay to help Tomas. "That is today's pay." / "I know." It's a choice that costs something and I could see the cost. It works.
- **Page 11.** TJ, the dog built from magnet parts, whose head comes off to scout. Silly, and I liked it. "Enough to complain." made me smile.
- **Pages 14–15.** PHYSICAL FLOW 18%, REPORTED FLOW 62%. This is the first point where I knew what the story was: somebody is lying to Keel about its water and stealing the rest. Finally, a problem.
- **Page 16, panel 4.** He hits SEND and every public screen in Keel changes. Big, clear, good.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3.** Shop math. I followed it, but it's a lot of numbers in a row (30, 42, 52, five a shift) right after the numbers on page 2.
- **Page 4, panels 1–3.** This is page 3 again. Same ledger, same five credits a shift, same "tell me before". I skimmed it.
- **Page 5.** The workshop page. Equations, plans, a chip that "matches the loop" and fits nothing. My eyes slid off it.
- **Page 13, panel 4 to page 14, panel 1.** The pipe labels and survey-versus-bolts lines. I skimmed until the percentages turned up.
- **Page 17.** Four places in four panels (Site 6, the chamber, Oasis, Site 6 again). I just looked at the pictures and moved on.

### I was confused
- **Page 3 → Page 4 → Page 9, the token.** On page 3 he can afford the medicine or the token, not both. Inez fronts the medicine and he says he'll take the token tomorrow. On page 9 he has an "unused travel token" in his pocket. When did he buy it? If he bought it, why can't he make a five-credit payment on page 4? I went back and still don't know.
- **Page 4, panel 1.** "Site 6 took the shift." / "The shift took your wages." I don't understand how a shift takes your wages. Also, the Site 6 reassignment doesn't happen until page 8. So did page 4 happen after page 8? The order of events lost me.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** We jump from the pharmacy to Grandmother's apartment, and Grandmother says "Your father was working. That doesn't mean he didn't hurt us." Out of nowhere. Nobody said anything to her. Who is she answering?
- **Page 5, panel 3.** She basically says it again: "He was working when he left." I didn't know what "working" means here. Working on what? It sounds important and I didn't get it.
- **Page 5, panel 4.** "This isn't a route." / "No." Why would he think a key was a route?
- **Page 6, panel 4 / Page 7.** "Would you like the route, or the question first?" What question? Then the dad's recording just plays. I never found out what the question was.
- **Page 8.** Who is Mera? Good guy, bad guy, cop, AI? She clicks TRACK, says "Don't make me choose blind," and then Alex's shift gets moved to Site 6. Did she move it? Why? I spent the rest of the issue unsure whether she was hunting him.
- **Page 9, panel 3–4.** The drone says "Companion clearance denied," Ada says "He's a tool," the drone says "So is the gate," and then the gate opens. Why did it open? Did the line work? Did someone hack it? No idea.
- **Page 12, panel 4.** "Someone has to make this look like an accident." He kicks a cart into a drone in front of everyone. How is that an accident? Which part is meant to look like an accident?
- **Page 13, panel 4.** "The survey says straight. The bolts say left." Left where? I couldn't picture it.
- **Page 16, panel 3.** "If we leave, it stays paper-clean." I think it means "if we sneak out, nobody will ever know." I had to work that out.
- **Page 17, panel 3.** Mera opens a valve: "Give them a route that exists." So she's helping them? Then the water on page 18 is chasing them. Is that her helping or her trying to drown them? I honestly couldn't tell.
- **The big one: what does Alex want?** He wants to go to Halyard. Why? To find his dad? To get work? To get away from the debt? Nobody says. "We know he has a token and wants Halyard, but what's he trying to do?" By page 18 he's running from a flood and I still don't know what he was after before the robot showed up.

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 32.** Mara Morrow on a video call says "Parts cost thirty-two. Labor costs twenty. Margin is eight," which are the numbers we already saw on page 20, then a ledger with PAID, PAID, 8 CR. Nobody is in danger and nobody wants anything. It's a slide from a lesson about small business versus corporations, drawn as a comic. Someone in my class would hold it up and say "this was written by a computer to teach us capitalism," and everyone would laugh and stop reading. Page 20 sets it up and page 32 seals it. Runner-up: Issue 03, page 42 ("Two can expose an obligation. Two cannot restore the old permission.").

The other thing that would lose them is the voice. Across the whole book, people talk in the same clipped "X. Not Y." rhythm: "You know a city. Not a route." "Tied, then. Not square." "Convening isn't owning." "Need is not a balance." "Thin still carries." Every character sounds like the same fortune cookie. To readers already looking for AI, that pattern is the tell.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dad's huge debt, finds a cool robot and a magnet dog, and finds out his town's water is being stolen. Then it turns into a lot of meetings about patents and who gets to be in charge. The robot's the best part."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A great birthday-debt hook and a lovable robot, then lost in a muddled timeline, and finally a clear crime: 62% on paper, 18% in the pipe.
- **Issue 02:** Too many names, a villain literally named Lock, and an economics lesson where the debt from page 2 gets turned down in one word.
- **Issue 03:** Two real scenes (Ada's mother, the dad's last recording) buried in Covenant rules, plus the Water Wars explained on page 41, way too late to matter.
