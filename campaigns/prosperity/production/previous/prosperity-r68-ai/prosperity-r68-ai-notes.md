# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a pass.** The brief establishes a coherent 54-page book, but `world.md` and `story.md` contradict the Director’s settled canon on the Charter Houses and Alex’s personal lender. The story map also leaves several fixed outcomes under-supported: the debt reduction, the three safeguard handoff, Tomas’s return, and the distinction between Alex’s institutional debt and his personal repayment. These are repairable in development without reopening the book’s central premise or ending.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. The Charter House names contradict the approved brief

- **Location:** `brief.md`, Canon — “Caldera House,” “Vector House,” “Orbital House”; `world.md`, “The Charter Houses” — “Veyr House,” “Kestrel House,” “Orison House”; `story.md`, Issues 01–03 and page plot.
- **Observation:** The Director’s brief canonizes Caldera, Vector and Orbital, while `world.md` marks Veyr, Kestrel and Orison as fixed. The story map and script use Caldera, Vector and Orbital.
- **Problem:** Production cannot know which three names are canon. This risks wrong House names in scripts, layouts, signage, prompts and lettering. The brief outranks `world.md`.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director: amend `world.md` so every House reference uses **Caldera House**, **Vector House** and **Orbital House**. Remove Veyr, Kestrel and Orison from canon and from any derived facts. Do not introduce a fourth naming system.
- **Role:** Director.

#### 2. The personal lender changes from Inez Quispe to Ada Veyra

- **Location:** `facts.md`, fixed lines under Currency and money; `brief.md`, People and story; `world.md`, Food and healthcare; `story.md`, Pages 3–4, Page 9 and Page 54.
- **Observation:** Canon fixes Inez Quispe as Alex’s personal lender and requires Alex to face Inez, agree repayment terms and make visible payments. The story instead has Ada front the medicine and travel token, records the missed installment, and receives the final payment.
- **Problem:** This contradicts fixed project truth and changes the intended contrast between institutional debt and a named personal creditor. It also makes the Page 54 ledger ambiguous.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter/Writer: restore Inez as the person who fronts Grandmother’s medicine and/or token, receives Alex’s missed-payment admission, agrees fixed installments, and receives later visible payments. Ada may still provide route help and challenge Alex’s choices, but she must not become the personal lender unless the Director explicitly reopens fixed canon.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer.

### Major

#### 3. The brief, story and facts do not consistently distinguish the three debt tracks

- **Location:** `brief.md`, People and story; `story.md`, Pages 2–4, 9 and 54; `facts.md`, Alex’s debt and Currency sections.
- **Observation:** The book has at least three obligations: Adrian’s transferred household liability, the 30-credit medicine advance, and Alex’s wages/payment history. The story map merges the personal advance with Ada, then ends with a personal ledger whose balance is not clearly labeled.
- **Problem:** The central theme depends on obligations becoming visible, but the accounting structure is currently invisible or contradictory. Readers may not know whether 402,110 is the institutional debt, the personal loan, or both.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter: specify one continuity path for each obligation. The institutional balance must move from 418,607 to 402,110 through shown paid work and payments; the personal medicine debt must remain separately named and visibly repaid under Inez’s terms. The final scene should show which balance is being paid.
- **Role:** Plotter.

#### 4. The 402,110-credit ending is not causally supported by the story map

- **Location:** `brief.md`, fixed Alex debt values; `story.md`, Page 54 and debt arithmetic proposal; `facts.md`, Alex’s initial and ending debt.
- **Observation:** The ending fixes a reduction of 16,497 credits, but the story map provides no payment schedule, wage total, contract, time span or other lawful source sufficient to produce it. Page 54 is fourteen months later, while the map says only that Alex performs paid work and makes visible payments.
- **Problem:** The fixed number reads as arbitrary and risks contradicting the established 40-credit daily earning figure. The ending’s material proof of change is therefore unearned.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter: add a compact, checkable payment ledger to the beat plan showing the elapsed period, paid repair work and deductions that support 402,110. Do not use debt cancellation, estate transfer or purifier ownership as an unexplained source of money.
- **Role:** Plotter.

#### 5. The three-safeguard convergence is under-planted in the development structure

- **Location:** `brief.md`, Safeguards; `story.md`, Pages 24, 41–50; `facts.md`, Covenant and Maker’s Seal entries.
- **Observation:** The Measure is recovered in Issue 01, the Seal appears in Issue 02, and Adrian’s Covenant band is introduced only in Issue 03. The story map does not specify where the band is visibly planted before its activation, nor how all three objects reach the legacy node together.
- **Problem:** The convergence risks feeling like a plot-required collection rather than a causal result of prior choices. It also risks conflating the safeguards’ distinct functions.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter: add explicit setup beats to the map. Plant Adrian’s band before Page 41; track the Seal’s custody after Lina gives it to Alex; state the physical route by which the Measure, Seal and bands reach the legacy node. Preserve the functions: Measure = evidence, Seal = attribution, Covenant = obligation.
- **Role:** Plotter.

#### 6. Tomas’s survival is a late reveal without an Issue 02 continuity bridge

- **Location:** `story.md`, Page 18, Issue 02 Pages 19–36, Issue 03 Pages 51–53; `facts.md`, Tomas entries.
- **Observation:** Issue 01 ends with Tomas’s signal lost, but the Issue 02 beat map contains no acknowledgment of his unresolved status. The story resumes with the group focused on Halyard and the purifier, then reveals Tomas alive in Issue 03.
- **Problem:** The issue question is not carried through the middle of the book. Tomas’s return can feel like a withheld answer rather than a consequence of the Site 6 action.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter: add one Issue 02 beat in which Alex or Ada checks the unresolved Site 6 status, and plant a nonconclusive trace of Tomas’s continued activity—an incomplete maintenance log, a recovered gauge, or a delayed signal—without confirming survival before Page 51.
- **Role:** Plotter.

#### 7. The story map’s central subjects change without a visible causal bridge

- **Location:** `story.md`, Issue 01 Pages 14–18; Issue 02 Pages 19–35; Issue 03 Pages 37–53.
- **Observation:** Issue 01 centers on diverted water, Issue 02 on ownership and the purifier, and Issue 03 on constitutional authority. The map states these as sequential topics but does not consistently show how one creates the next.
- **Problem:** The book risks reading as three adjacent lessons rather than one escalating problem. The causal chain should be water evidence → who can make/use a remedy → who gets to authorize shared allocation.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter: make each issue’s turn generate the next issue’s problem. The 62/18 evidence should expose the ownership stakes in Halyard; the purifier/estate conflict should force the safeguards and public review; the public review should test the same physical-evidence ethic established in Issue 01.
- **Role:** Plotter.

#### 8. Mera’s role and authority are not aligned across the four files

- **Location:** `brief.md`, World and authority; `characters.md`, Mera Vale; `story.md`, Pages 8, 17, 36 and 47–50; `facts.md`, Alpha entries.
- **Observation:** Mera is described variously as a senior Oasis authority, institutional custodian, former Covenant participant, field administrator and person able to open a conduit valve. Her exact scope relative to Alpha, the Houses and the legacy node is not settled in one operational description.
- **Problem:** Her decisions—TRACK, opening the ascent valve, SUMMON and offering executive authority—may appear arbitrary or contradictory.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director/Plotter: define Mera’s actionable scope in the brief or character file: what she can observe, what she can manually operate, what Alpha can deny, and why she can offer designation but not unilaterally take it. Keep her override failure intact.
- **Role:** Director, Plotter.

- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter: assign the chip one clear function—emotional evidence of Adrian’s gambling and damage—and keep the book and key as separate mechanisms. Remove any implication that the equation operates the workshop.
- **Role:** Plotter.

#### 11. The mother rule is preserved but needs an explicit negative continuity check

- **Smallest repair direction:** Character Designer/Writer: add a production check that no dialogue, photograph, record or visual caption identifies the mother’s community, name, whereabouts, appearance, later life or reason beyond leaving after Adrian lost everything.
- **Role:** Character Designer, Writer.
