## Verdict

**Needs a pass.** The approved 16-page scope and ending are now clear: four-page prologue, twelve-page Chapter 1, ending with Mera choosing **TRACK**. The development files largely agree, but the production-facing script and layouts still contain rejected House names, contradictory visual details, and lettering/layout failures that can make essential information unreadable or visibly break continuity.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Rejected Charter House names remain in the page-facing material

- **Location:** `script.md`, Page 5, Panel 1; `draft-final.md`, Chapter 1 Page 1; corresponding Page 5 layout description.
- **Problem:** The approved canon names are **Helion, Aqualis and Astra**. The page-facing material still names the Houses **Ivers, Calder and Steyn**, which the brief explicitly rejects. Page 5 is the first world-establishing image, so these names become visible project truth.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Replace every occurrence of **Ivers, Calder and Steyn** in the current 16-page page-facing material with **Helion, Aqualis and Astra**, preserving the three institutional functions and accent colors. Check that no rejected name remains in signage, insignia, prompts or lettering.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent, Character Designer.

#### 2. Page 7’s climactic dialogue is not letterable as laid out

- **Location:** Page 7, Panel 6, layout thumbnail and lettering report.
- **Problem:** The four closing exchanges total approximately 51 words in a narrow panel. The thumbnails show balloon overflow and multiple ADA/ALEX balloons overlapping. This is the page’s emotional and thematic argument—Alex wants escape; Ada wants Keel worth staying in—but the current layout makes reading order and speaker ownership unclear.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Letterer and Layout Agent should preserve all four lines but redistribute the balloons along the established two-eyeline composition, with a clear alternating path and no overlap. If the panel cannot hold the text, enlarge Panel 6 within the existing page architecture or use a deliberate balloon-free beat between exchanges; do not cut the argument without Writer approval.
- **Role:** Layout Agent, Letterer.

#### 3. Page 16’s closing lettering collides and corrupts the ending

- **Location:** Page 16, Panel 5, layout thumbnail.
- **Problem:** The retrospective caption and `END OF CHAPTER ONE` overlap. The thumbnail visibly corrupts the caption text (`S+=========+`) and the chapter marker runs beyond the panel border. This damages the required final beat: Mera’s TRACK decision followed by the older-Alex framing line.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Rebuild the caption and chapter marker as separate lettering zones with sufficient separation, keeping the caption readable before the eye reaches the chapter marker. The monitor text in Panel 3 must also remain fully legible, including both `[ REPORT TO ALPHA ]` and `[ TRACK ]`.
- **Role:** Layout Agent, Letterer.

### Major

#### 4. Prologue daughter design contradicts the established character facts

- **Location:** `facts.md`, Alex’s daughter entry; `draft.md`/`draft-edited.md`/`draft-final.md`, Prologue Page 1, Panel 2.
- **Problem:** The facts file establishes the daughter’s orange jacket with H2O patches, teal T-shirt, dark cargo trousers, orange-and-black sneakers and smartwatch. The page-facing draft instead gives her a mustard cardigan, pale blue school shirt, navy skirt, white socks and red sneakers. The contradiction affects a recurring visual identifier in the opening timeline.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Character Designer should choose the approved daughter design for this packet and propagate it word-for-word through the prologue script, layouts and page prompts. If the facts entry is stale, the Director must explicitly supersede it; do not leave both designs active.
- **Role:** Character Designer, Director.

#### 5. Bi11bot’s dormant state is visually ambiguous before the watch clasp

- **Location:** `script.md`, Page 11, Panel 4; `draft.md`/`draft-edited.md`/`draft-final.md`, Chapter 1 Page 7, Panel 4.
- **Problem:** The settled rule is that Bi11bot remains dormant until Alex closes Adrian’s watch clasp. The current wording introduces a “faint amber pulse beneath the cloth” and then a “faint amber flicker” in the watch before activation. Although the eyes remain unlit, these cues can read as Bi11bot already responding or partially awake, weakening the sole-cause activation lock.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Remove the pre-activation pulse/flicker from the machine and watch. Show only ordinary reflected workshop light and completely dull, unlit optics until the clasp closes on Page 12, Panel 3.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent.

#### 6. The page-facing documents do not consistently identify the current chapter/page numbering

- **Location:** `story.md` Page plot versus `script.md` and `brief.md`.
- **Problem:** The brief and facts establish Pages 1–4 as the prologue and Pages 5–16 as Chapter 1. The story page plot labels the same Chapter 1 beats as Pages 5–16 in some places but headings them as “Page 1,” “Page 2,” etc. elsewhere. This is an avoidable production ambiguity when matching script, layouts and page prompts.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter and Writer should retain the chapter-internal labels only as beat names, not page numbers. Normalize all production-facing references to the book-wide labels **Page 1–Page 16**.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer.

#### 7. The current Page 5 questions overpromise answers that this packet does not deliver

- **Location:** Page 5, captions; `story.md` setup/payoff table.
- **Problem:** The captions ask “Where does all our water go?” and “Who really owns what we create?” while the current packet ends before the water discrepancy, House ownership system, collaborators or Maker’s Seal are dramatized. Planting questions is allowed, but the three questions arrive together on a dense splash and two are not yet anchored to a concrete action in this packet.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Keep the questions if they are intentional long-range plants, but ensure the image gives each one a concrete visual anchor: visible unequal water infrastructure, a House-controlled contribution/ownership sign, and the hidden-workshop mystery. If the page cannot carry all three clearly, Director should decide which are primary rather than adding later exposition to this packet.
- **Role:** Director, Plotter, Layout Agent.

#### 8. The final caption on Page 16 creates an unnecessary timeline ambiguity

- **Location:** Page 16, Panel 5; `story.md` Reader vs character knowledge.
- **Problem:** The caption is in older Alex’s voice, but the script does not otherwise identify that voice on the page. Because the prologue has already established a future Alex, the reader can follow it, but the caption risks being read as a present-tense narrator or as a line from Mera’s perspective.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Letterer should give the caption a consistent caption treatment matching the prologue’s future framing, and Writer should verify that the wording is clearly retrospective without adding explanatory narration.
- **Role:** Writer, Letterer.

### Minor

#### 9. Page 6’s debt display is too dense for immediate reading

- **Location:** Page 6, Panel 2, script and layout.
- **Problem:** The checkpoint must communicate seven essential data lines, plus guard dialogue, Alex’s reply, SFX and a caption. The layout reserves a display block, but the thumbnail does not demonstrate that the debt, restriction and clearance time can all be read at production size.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Layout Agent should prioritize **418,607 CREDITS**, **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED** and **PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS** at the largest type, with the remaining fields subordinate but legible. Letterer should confirm the full display in a print-size proof.
- **Role:** Layout Agent, Letterer.

#### 10. Page 8 repeats exposition without enough visual change

- **Location:** Page 8, Panels 1–5.
- **Problem:** The sequence relies on multiple dialogue panels about Grandmother, rumor and Alex’s lack of memory. The visual progression is mostly close and medium views of Alex and Ada, producing a talking-head slowdown immediately after the stronger water/ration page.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Layout Agent should make the visual transitions do more work: let the photograph, the changing basin light and Ada’s turned body carry the emotional progression. Writer should preserve the dialogue but remove any redundant facial reaction or allow one silent panel to carry the pause.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent.

#### 11. Page 9’s “Adrian?” / “Did your father come home yet?” exchange needs a clearer memory-state transition

- **Location:** Page 9, Panels 1–4.
- **Problem:** Grandmother first mistakes Alex for Adrian, then asks whether Adrian has come home, then becomes suddenly precise. The intended state change is present, but the current layout gives the reader little visual distinction between confusion and lucidity.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Character Designer and Layout Agent should make the shift legible through gaze, posture and hand movement—not caricature or helplessness. Preserve Grandmother’s dignity and her established cloth-folding action.
- **Role:** Character Designer, Layout Agent.

#### 12. Page 15 introduces “three artifacts” and “Legacy Protocol” in a dense dialogue block

- **Location:** Page 15, Panel 1; Page 15, Panel 3.
- **Problem:** The immediate mission is clear, but the first panel asks the reader to absorb three artifacts, three locations and a system name at once. The following panels add the Measure, the sealed mine and Alex’s travel restriction. This risks the same “pile of markers” problem identified by the cold-read material.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Layout Agent should make the Keel marker visually dominant and subordinate the two distant markers. Writer should keep the terminology but ensure the immediate objective—**The Measure beneath the sealed mine**—is the first and strongest readable fact.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent.
