## Page 5 (right) — The three cities make inequality and connection immediately visible

**Panel 1.** Full-page establishing image, 2043, viewed from a distant elevated angle so all three cities read in one composition.

At the bottom of the vast mountain basin lies **KEEL**, built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Dense stacked housing, repaired industrial equipment, rusted mining structures, crowded pedestrian lanes and aging factories fill the basin. Dust hangs in the air. Workers pass through small digital checkpoints. Water moves through narrow channels and old pipes. A line of people with jugs waits at a metered public tap. The palette is rust, charcoal and oxidized orange. A large sealed mine entrance sits on the pit rim beneath faded hazard striping. An aging pump gantry has one small work-light.

Midway up lies **HALYARD**, visibly more prosperous and active: workshops, fabrication towers, irrigated greenhouse terraces, elevated trains and advanced industrial facilities. Freight moves both up and down. Three fabrication complexes carry restrained insignia for **House Ivers**, **House Calder** and **House Steyn**.

Above both, partly beyond the cloud layer, lies **OASIS**: pearl-white architecture, designed gardens, broad reservoirs, open fountains, civic spaces and automated transit. A spacecraft rises toward orbit from a distant launch structure carrying the **House Steyn** insignia.

One infrastructure spine connects all three cities: a massive water conduit, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes running vertically through the mountain. The conduit is enormous at Oasis’s reservoirs and narrows dramatically toward Keel. Identical white-and-amber control nodes mark the spine at each tier. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including a permission gate across the rail line between Keel and Halyard.

CAPTION: Year 2043.

CAPTION: Oasis · Halyard · Keel

CAPTION: What if everyone you've trusted has a secret?

CAPTION: Who really owns what we create?

CAPTION: Where does all our water go?

PAGE CHECK: The full-page image establishes geography, class difference, the Houses, the infrastructure spine and the book’s three questions. CHANGED: none.
