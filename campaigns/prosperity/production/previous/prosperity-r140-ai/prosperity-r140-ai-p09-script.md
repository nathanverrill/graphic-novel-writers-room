## Page 9 (right) — Grandmother’s ordinary clue opens Adrian’s hidden workshop

**Panel 1.** Wide interior of a modest Keel apartment at dusk. Handmade quilts, repaired furniture, old books, faded family photographs, plants in recycled containers and two water jugs with ration marks. Grandmother sits folding and refolding a small cloth. A tall bookshelf dominates one wall, with a book-width gap. Alex stands in the doorway with his tool bag.

GRANDMOTHER: Adrian?

**Panel 2.** Inset close-up. Alex stands in the doorway, eyes closed for a moment.

ALEX: Not today, Grandma. It's Alex.

**Panel 3.** Medium. Alex kneels beside Grandmother’s chair. She keeps folding the cloth.

GRANDMOTHER: Of course. Alex. I knew that. Did your father come home yet?

ALEX: Not yet.

**Panel 4.** Tight close-up on Grandmother. For one beat her eyes clear and become precise.

GRANDMOTHER: He's closer than you think.

**Panel 5.** Close on Grandmother’s frail hand pointing toward the top shelf. One worn, hand-bound book sits alone there, its spine marked with a faint balanced water-drop symbol.

GRANDMOTHER: Adrian, get down the book you put up there. The one you want Alex to have when he turns eighteen.

ALEX: ...I'm eighteen today, Grandma.

**Panel 6.** Tall narrow panel. Alex reaches toward the book from behind. Warm room light frames him.

GRANDMOTHER: Careful. He said it was worth protecting.
