# Issue 00

### Pulled me forward
The robot dog TJ breaking into pieces to sabotage the water pump was genuinely cool. It was the only action in the issue and it made me want to see what else the dog could do.

### I slowed down / skimmed
I slowed down on page 8 and 9. The dialogue between Alex and the granddaughter is incredibly vague. They keep talking in circles ("Someday's crowded") instead of just saying what's happening.

### I was confused
Why did Alex just let them steal the water? If he knows who keeps showing up for it, why is he being so cryptic with his granddaughter? 

### Things I was expected to already know
- Wardens (Page 3)
- Phantum's lake (Page 2)

### Lines I read twice
- "Someday's crowded."
- "The record closes around the theft."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I could easily tell Alex, the granddaughter, Nera, and Voss apart. 

### I felt something
I felt bad for TJ when he lost his leg in the pump. 

### I predicted
I predicted the drone component TJ brought back was going to be a clue to Alex's secret past.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old man and his granddaughter trying to figure out who is stealing their lake water.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 9. The cryptic one-liners got annoying fast.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Not really. They are just standing there looking at the lake. It doesn't end on a hook, just a vague statement.

VERDICT Issue 00: put down: page 9; next issue: no

# Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
The debt transfer on page 12 hooked me immediately. Inheriting a dead parent's 418,000 credit debt and 28 years of forced labor is a terrifying concept. That made me care about Alex right away.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 16. The whole "hidden room behind a bookshelf" thing felt really cliché, and I skimmed through them opening it.

### I was confused
Why did the apartment door lock just because the workshop woke up? And what exactly is the "infrastructure spine"? 

### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel, Halyard, Oasis (Page 20)
- Foreman-7 (Page 12)
- The system (Page 20)

### Lines I read twice
- "I'm generous with containers."
- "The part that costs."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandmother, and Bi11bot were all distinct. Adrian is just a hologram/memory, but easy to spot.

### I felt something
I felt a heavy sense of dread for Alex when the terminal told him he had 28.7 years of debt to pay off. 

### I predicted
I predicted that Adrian didn't actually die, he just faked his death to escape his own debt.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teenager trying to escape a lifetime of inherited debt by following clues left by his missing dad.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. The debt concept was strong enough to keep me reading the whole issue.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Mera is tracking them now, and I want to see if they get caught sneaking into the mine.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

# Issue 03

### Pulled me forward
Alex refusing to sell out to Director Lock on page 40. It was a good standoff.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 41 to 43. The whole montage of finding four different inventors and building a water filter felt like a group project in a science class. I completely skimmed the parts about membranes and living root filters.

### I was confused
What is a "living root filter"? Why did Lock just let them build this in a rented room if he has the power to freeze the estate? What exactly is the "Maker's Seal"?

### Things I was expected to already know
- Caldera House, Vector, Orbital (Page 40)
- Intellectual Estate (Page 39)

### Lines I read twice
- "Assign the estate to House administration."
- "Not that one. It lies under heat."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I could not tell Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren apart. They were introduced way too fast, all on one page, and just blurred together into "the science team."

### I felt something
I felt bored during the building montage.

### I predicted
I predicted the House was going to steal their invention at the end anyway.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Teaming up with screwed-over inventors to build a machine and bypass a corporate monopoly.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 41. Introducing four new characters at once just to talk about tech jargon lost me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. They made 38 credits and licensed a purifier. It feels like the story just resolved itself into a small business venture.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 41; next issue: no

# Issue 06

### Pulled me forward
Seeing Alex's debt balance actually go down on page 63. After the setup in Issue 01, it was satisfying to see the number drop.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 60 to 62. The climax of the book is literally a town hall meeting where people argue about "denominators" and "residential branches." I skimmed all the bureaucratic arguing.

### I was confused
The transition on page 64 is really jarring. It says "A worker places the damaged drone component from 2099 beside the terminal's evidence tray." Wait, are we back in 2099? Or is the worker in 2043 placing a component from the future? It took me a minute to realize the book just jumped 50 years forward again.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Alpha (Page 61 - it finally gets a display screen, but I still barely know what it is).

### Lines I read twice
- "The revised denominator improves delivery." (This sounds like math homework).
- "You counted the pipe after you stopped counting the homes."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Mara Soto and the Caldera Representative were just talking heads in a boardroom. I didn't care who they were.

### I felt something
I felt disappointed that the big finale was just paperwork and public ledgers being opened.

### I predicted
I predicted he'd blow up the system, but he just made it open-source.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Fixing a corrupt system by making the data public and forcing people to argue it out instead of having a dictator.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 62. People arguing about water allocation proposals is incredibly boring.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No, the story is over. The connection back to the old man in Issue 00 makes sense now, but I wouldn't read a sequel.

VERDICT Issue 06: put down: page 62; next issue: no

# Whole Book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 41. The montage of inventors talking about membranes, living root filters, and fabrication cases. It feels exactly like an educational video they make us watch in science class.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid inherits his dead dad's massive debt and goes on a quest to fix the city's water supply, but the big final battle is just a town hall meeting about plumbing.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 00: Cool robot dog, but the old man talks in annoying riddles.
- Issue 01: Inheriting a 400k debt is a terrifying concept that hooked me instantly.
- Issue 02: Sneaking into a mine to prove rich people are stealing water.
- Issue 03: A boring group project to build a water filter.
- Issue 04: Mom drama and dry political lectures.
- Issue 05: Finally finding out what happened to dad in a cool flooded bunker.
- Issue 06: The grand finale is a math argument about water pipes.
