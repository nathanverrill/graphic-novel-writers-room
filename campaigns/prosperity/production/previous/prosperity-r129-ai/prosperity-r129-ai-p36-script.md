## Page 36 (left) — Lock makes concentrated ownership materially tempting.

**Panel 1.** Lock stops before Alex.

LOCK: Alex Phantum. Halyard has been waiting a long time for an heir.

ALEX: I didn't know I was one.

LOCK: Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value.

**Panel 2.** Lock gestures toward the registry.

LOCK: Your father's work could clear every debt attached to your name.

LOCK: It could also secure you a position. Somewhere in Halyard that suits you.

**Panel 3.** Ada steps between them.

ADA: In exchange for what?

LOCK: A simple assignment of rights.

LOCK: You sign. The estate transfers to administration. Your name is clear by tonight.

**Panel 4.** Lock remains calm.

LOCK: Your father was a brilliant inventor. But an invention isn't a business.

ALEX: And the Houses can make it one?

LOCK: We have the capital, factories, engineers, and customers. We can take discoveries from a workshop to the entire city.

ALEX: And if someone wants to build something different?

LOCK: They're welcome to try. But scale is what makes innovation useful.

LOCK: Take the day. Halyard isn't going anywhere.
