# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a pass.** The 64-page script now exists and broadly follows the approved four-issue structure, with the central causal spine, major safeguards and issue hooks present. However, one execution artifact directly contradicts the script, several visual/canon locks are inconsistent, and the ending still asserts cooperative ownership, debt reduction and public representation without enough page-level setup or payoff. The book is not ready for execution until those continuity and production risks are corrected.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Page 46 layout and thumbnail depict a different scene from the approved script

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Page 46; `layouts.md`, Page 46; `thumbnails.md`, Page 46.
- **Observation:** The script Page 46 is the barrier cliffhanger: Alex is separated from Ada, Lina is taken behind a service shutter, and a summons to Oasis appears. The layout and thumbnail instead show Leona, Mera and Ada in Leona’s apartment discussing Alpha association risk and the Covenant.
- **Problem:** This is not a staging variation; it is an entirely different scene, with different characters, location, dialogue and dramatic function. It also appears to duplicate material from the intended Issue 03 Ada/Leona sequence, displacing the approved Issue 02 hook.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Layout Agent:** discard the current Page 46 layout and thumbnail and rebuild the page from the script’s four-panel barrier action. **Letterer:** do not letter the existing apartment balloons. **Writer:** no script change is required unless the Director sends the page back after layout review.
- **Role:** layout, letterer.

#### 2. Issue 00 misrenders TJ’s fixed 2099 visual identifier

- **Location:** `characters.md`, TJ visual lock; `facts.md`, B11 and TJ; `script.md`, Issue 00 Pages 1–3 and Page 7.
- **Observation:** TJ’s fixed distinction is plain scratched ceramic camera lenses in 2043 and a small adjustable brass ring around his right eye in 2099. Issue 00 is set in 2099, but Pages 1–3 describe TJ with “two scratched optical lenses,” and Page 7 does not establish the brass ring.
- **Problem:** The prologue fails to deliver the required visual crossover identifier and risks giving 2099 TJ the 2043 appearance. The brass ring is specifically required to distinguish the later TJ from his earlier form, while its transfer remains unexplained.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** specify TJ’s adjustable brass ring around the right eye in the 2099 prologue’s first clear close-up. **Character Designer:** ensure the 2099 TJ sheet carries the ring and the 2043 sheet does not. Preserve the unexplained continuity of how it moved.
- **Role:** writer, character_designer.

### Major

#### 3. The five-maker ownership is not legible at the point where the Maker’s Seal activates

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Pages 43–44; `facts.md`, Honest business; `brief.md`, Canon.
- **Observation:** Alex publicly names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren on Page 43. The Page 44 registry then reports `NAMED CONTRIBUTORS: 5`, but the fifth contributor is not named in the dialogue or visually identified as Alex. The script also says the design is “unpatented and nonexclusive,” but does not establish the five makers as joint owners of Morrow & Sons until the abrupt shop image on Page 63.
- **Problem:** The Seal’s central dramatic question—whether Alex will claim Adrian’s work or accept shared responsibility—becomes numerically opaque. The reader can reasonably ask who the fifth contributor is and whether “nonexclusive” means public access, House ownership, or cooperative ownership.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** add Alex’s name to the public contribution record or have Alex explicitly identify himself as the fifth maker while accepting responsibility for the release. Show the five-person ownership relationship through a concrete shared action, not a governance explanation. **Letterer:** keep the five names readable without turning the panel into a screen-heavy list.
- **Role:** writer, letterer.

#### 4. The ending’s debt reduction and Morrow & Sons income are asserted rather than causally shown

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 03 Pages 60–64, especially Page 63; `brief.md`, Canon; `facts.md`, Economics and debt.
- **Observation:** Page 63 jumps to an active Morrow & Sons shop, a payment toward Alex’s debt and a balance of 402,110 credits. The script does not show when the shop reopened, how Alex’s wages and equal profit share work, how much time has passed, or how the required public repayment to Tomas relates to the final ledger.
- **Problem:** The 16,497-credit reduction reads as an unexplained status update rather than the consequence of visible work and payments. The approved ending requires debt to remain active and the reduction to be supported by visible labor and payments; the current page provides only a ledger and a shop sign.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Plotter:** reserve a concise visual bridge before Page 63 or use Page 63’s panels to show accumulated work, Tomas’s paid balance and the five-maker wage/profit ledger. **Writer:** establish that Alex’s payment is one of several visible payments and that the final balance is not produced by the protocol or purifier itself. Do not add a financial exposition page.
- **Role:** plotter, writer.

#### 5. The first public review does not clearly establish the representatives’ mandates

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 03 Pages 60–62; `world.md`, Distributed verification protocol; `facts.md`.
- **Observation:** Page 60 names three temporary representatives from each affected place as part of Alex’s proposal. Page 61 then shows three tables and “three temporary representatives” at each, but only Tomas is individually identified. The script does not show that the representatives were selected through open local meetings or that their communities can recall them.
- **Problem:** The ending risks reading as a generic hearing rather than the first operation of the approved protocol. The reader may not understand why Tomas can challenge the House proposal or whose agreement is required.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** add one visual mandate marker per table—such as locally issued strips, a public selection record or a representative receiving instructions from affected residents—without introducing a lecture. Make Tomas’s standing visible as a Keel representative rather than merely an interested miner.
- **Role:** writer.

#### 6. The first public review catches a category error, but the physical evidence does not fully prove the units are not community supply

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 03 Page 62; `facts.md`, Water allocation evidence.
- **Observation:** The proposal counts `420 UNITS` as community supply. The next panels show Caldera-marked purifier crates behind a House barrier and gauges showing they are not connected to Keel’s line.
- **Problem:** The visual proves the units are not currently connected, but not necessarily that they are House-owned or that they cannot be deployed under the proposal. The reader may understand “not delivered water,” but the exact attempted manipulation remains underdefined.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** put the ownership and delivery distinction into one physical record or label: for example, House-owned inventory counted as delivered community supply while the crates remain sealed in a House yard. **Layout Agent:** give the inventory record and physical crates a clear before/after reading path.
- **Role:** writer, layout.

#### 7. The 2099 prologue links the theft to Alpha infrastructure in a way the brief does not yet support

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 00 Pages 9–10; `brief.md`, Canon.
- **Observation:** Page 9 reveals a white-and-amber control node embedded in the stolen drone component, and Page 10 leaves the fragment glowing between Alex and his granddaughter.
- **Problem:** The brief says Issue 00 links to the principal story only through water and TJ and contains no explanation of Alpha or the safeguards. The node creates a third link—Alpha/control infrastructure—and may imply that the 2099 theft is directly connected to the 2043 authority plot.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Director:** decide whether the node is intended as a generic water-management component or a deliberate Alpha continuity link. If the Issue 00 link must remain only water and TJ, **Writer:** remove the Alpha-specific visual identity and use an unmarked mechanical drone component. If retained, label it a proposal rather than allowing it to function as established history.
- **Role:** director, writer.

#### 8. The Covenant’s physical continuity is still difficult to follow despite the corrected activation sequence

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 17; Issue 03 Pages 52, 56–60; `brief.md`, Canon.
- **Observation:** Page 17 shows Adrian’s band in the workshop. Page 52 says Leona’s band links to Adrian’s “dark band carried by B11.” Page 56 then presents the Measure, Seal and linked bands together, while Page 60 registers the revised protocol without clearly showing what happens to the linked bands or Mera’s dark band.
- **Problem:** The reader can follow the emotional action but may not understand that the original three-custodian model is incomplete, that Mera’s band remains dark, and that the revised protocol qualifies affected communities rather than restoring founder authority.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Writer:** use one unambiguous physical image at the registration: Leona’s and Adrian’s bands remain linked, Mera’s band remains dark and separate, and the public process begins with community representatives rather than founders. **Layout Agent:** make the three physical states readable without adding interface text.
- **Role:** writer, layout.

#### 9. The script introduces the Measure’s inscription as explanatory text without establishing whether it is approved lettering

- **Location:** `world.md`, Key props; `script.md`, Issue 01 Pages 27–28; `facts.md`.
- **Observation:** The world file gives the Measure the inscription `WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE`, but the script never specifies it on the page. The brief prohibits speech or captions stating the lesson.
- **Problem:** If the inscription is added in production, it risks becoming a thematic thesis printed on a prop rather than discovered through action. If omitted, the prop description and script are inconsistent.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Director:** decide whether the inscription is visible canon or production-only prop texture. If visible, **Writer/Letterer:** keep it partial or incidental and do not frame it as a readable lesson. If the room wants the Measure learned only through use, remove the readable inscription from the production description.
- **Role:** director, writer, letterer.

#### 10. The Halyard maker sequence remains overcompressed for four named collaborators and a new product

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Pages 37–44; `story.md`, maker risk.
- **Observation:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren each receive one contribution scene, then all four, Alex and Ada occupy the build and public-release sequence. The page architecture introduces four people, their House positions, four technical components, the incompatible standards, the purifier, the 91% limitation, Lock’s threat and the Seal in eight pages.
- **Problem:** This matches both cold reads’ main pacing complaint: the makers blur into a “science team,” and the reader has little room to understand why their exposure matters. The later cooperative ownership and Page 46 danger therefore carry less emotional weight.
- **Smallest repair direction:** **Plotter:** combine or remove redundant technical handoffs and reserve one consequential choice per maker rather than one explanatory introduction per maker. **Writer:** let each maker’s action alter the build or risk—especially Jalen’s decision to enter the room and Ren’s choice to make the connector—without adding pages.
- **Role:** plotter, writer.
