### Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
Page 10, panel 4. Alex losing his whole day's pay just for stepping over a line to help someone. That made the debt feel real. 
Page 11, panel 4. The little robot dog TJ detaching its head to crawl into a gap. That was actually cool.
Page 18, full page. The water rushing in and Tomas's light going dark. It felt like actual stakes.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 8. Mera at the Oasis control room. It was just a bunch of UI text and system warnings. I skimmed the bold text.
Page 14 and 15. Connecting the "Measure" to the different pipes. It felt like reading a manual for plumbing I don't understand.

### I was confused
Page 12, panel 3. Why does Tomas have a brass key to Adrian's secret door? Did Adrian just hand these out?
Page 16, panel 4. They press "SEND" and suddenly public screens everywhere update. How does Alex's random wristband hack into public billboards?

### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel (Page 1)
- Departure token (Page 3)
- Old works (Page 3)
- Oasis (Page 8)
- Legacy node (Page 7)

### Lines I read twice
Page 3, panel 3: "I know a direction. / You know a direction. Not a route."
Page 5, panel 2: "He built and bet with the same hands."
Page 12, panel 3: "He ask you to keep him out? / He didn't ask enough."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex and Ada are easy to tell apart. Tomas is the guy in the mine. I couldn't really picture Mera—she's just a lady looking at screens in a different place. 

### I felt something
Page 4, panel 2. When Inez says "Good. I need the money." Usually the shopkeeper in these stories is secretly nice and forgives the debt, but she just flat out needs to get paid. I liked that.

### I predicted
Tomas is dead under the water. The rich people in Oasis are stealing the water from Keel.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teen inherits his dad's massive debt and a secret robot, and has to survive a flooded mine to prove the government is stealing water.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 8. The cutaway to Mera looking at computer screens and saying "Track, then" completely killed the momentum of finding the secret robot.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. They are trapped in a pipe with water rising, and I want to know if Tomas actually died. It's a real cliffhanger.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 8; next issue: yes

### Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
Page 22, panel 4. Alex pushing the contract back at the rich guy (Lock) and refusing to sell out the creators. 
Page 36, panel 4. Alex jamming his wrist guard into the closing security door to stop it. 

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 23 through 29. I completely glazed over this whole section. It's just a bunch of people I don't know yelling about membranes, biofilters, nodes, and casings. I skimmed until they put the thing in a box.

### I was confused
Page 20, panel 2. What is a Charter kiosk? What is Caldera House? 
Page 34, panel 3. What exactly does the bronze Maker's Seal do? It just projects a mismatch and then they publish it? I didn't get the mechanics of this at all.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Charter containment review (Page 19)
- Caldera House (Page 20)
- Intellectual Estate (Page 21)
- Maker's Seal (Page 24)

### Lines I read twice
Page 28, panel 1: "The node sends. / The membrane receives. / The casing chokes."
Page 30, panel 4: "I counted the record and missed the people."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I could tell Lock apart because he's the corporate suit. I could not tell Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren apart at all. They were introduced in two pages and just blurred into one collective "science team" doing fake plumbing. 

### I felt something
Page 35, panel 4. When Ada catches Alex's wrist before the barrier closes. It sucked that they got separated right after escaping.

### I predicted
The House was going to steal their purifier design anyway because they have more money. 

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex teams up with underground inventors to open-source a water filter so an evil corporation can't own it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 24. Four new characters are thrown at me at once, doing maintenance on a biofilter. It felt like I accidentally skipped an issue.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Alex and Ada are separated by a glass wall, and Alex is being forced to go to Oasis. I need to see what Oasis actually is.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: yes

### Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
Page 39, panel 4. Ada confronting her mom. It was tense and didn't feel like a generic reunion.
Page 45, panel 4. Seeing the recording of Adrian locking himself in to drown so the lower works wouldn't flood. 
Page 51, panel 4. Tomas showing up alive on the TV feed. That was a great payoff.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 47 to 50. The whole climax of the book is people standing around a table looking at a "designation lever" and linking bracelets. I skimmed the system text about allocations and flow shifts.

### I was confused
Page 40, panel 3. How do the Covenant bands work? Why do they get warm or stay cold? 
Page 49, panel 4. Alex doesn't pull the lever, and that somehow fixes things? I didn't really understand what the "executive designation" meant.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Covenant band (Page 39)
- Civic Administration (Page 38)
- Orbital Industrial Branch (Page 37)

### Lines I read twice
Page 40, panel 1: "I saw days I could prevent. I didn't see the years I was making."
Page 46, panel 3: "I may have mistaken that for an instruction."
Page 52, panel 4: "Don't stamp it while the short line is still speaking."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona was easy to spot because she's Ada's mom. Nadi and Olani were just random talking heads in a meeting room, I didn't care about them at all.

### I felt something
Page 54, panel 2. Seeing Alex's ledger at the end. He still owes 402,110 credits. I actually liked that it wasn't magically erased. It felt realistic.

### I predicted
I thought Alex was going to use the lever to wipe his debt and flood Oasis. 

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex and his friends force the city council to look at real physical evidence instead of trusting their rigged computer systems.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 48. The climax of the story is a debate over a lever in a boardroom. It felt like a civics lesson, not a comic book.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. It feels like a solid ending. Ada leaves, Alex is still working off his debt, but things are slightly better. I wouldn't buy the next book because I don't want to read more about water allocation meetings.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 48; next issue: no

***

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 24. Dropping four new characters at once (Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren) and immediately having them talk about biofilter cultures and power nodes. Everyone I know would close the book right there.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid with a massive inherited debt finds his dead dad's robot and tries to prove the rich part of the city is stealing water, but the ending is basically just a city council meeting.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
Issue 01: Cool robot dog and a good flood cliffhanger, but bogged down by UI text.
Issue 02: A confusing detour to build a water filter with people I couldn't tell apart.
Issue 03: Tomas being alive was awesome, but the final battle was literally a debate over paperwork.
