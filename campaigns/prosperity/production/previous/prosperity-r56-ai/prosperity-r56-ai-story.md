# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type:** explicit decisions, showrunner notes, and idea drafts.

The book is a near-future graphic narrative about Alex Phantum, a seventeen-year-old Keel worker who inherits a crushing debt, a hidden workshop, and the unfinished work of his absent father, Adrian. Alex follows Adrian's trail through the three-tier civilization of Keel, Halyard, and Oasis, discovering how Alpha's emergency authority became permanent, how concentrated ownership obscured both labor and resource allocation, and how people might reclaim decision-making without replacing one concentrated authority with another.

The story should be experienced through people needing, using, and paying for the world's systems, not through lectures. The book must not explain its lessons directly: no history lecture, definitions of the safeguards, recited triplets, governance speech, or caption stating the theme. `(input/notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can Alex refuse the power to fix everything himself and help build a way for the people affected by decisions to see, question, and share in them?

The emotional trajectory moves from inheritance and resentment toward chosen responsibility:

- Alex begins wanting to leave Keel and escape a debt and history he did not choose.
- He discovers that Adrian left him more than liability: evidence, collaborators, tools, and a question.
- He is repeatedly offered shortcuts that would protect him while leaving others exposed.
- He learns that truth, ownership, and promises matter only when people can see them and hold one another to them.
- He ultimately refuses executive authority over Alpha and proposes distributed human decision-making instead.
- The ending must cost him something visible. His refusal of power cannot be free. `(input/notes.md)`

The book must also show:

- a named person fronting Alex medicine and a travel token;
- Alex missing the first repayment, facing that person directly, agreeing to fixed repayment terms, and visibly reducing the balance through later payments;
- an honest business that makes and sells something people need at a fair price;
- Bi11bot speaking in Bill Reynolds' plain, questioning, self-deprecating voice, including a moment when he is wrong and admits it;
- gambling as a source of family damage, not as Adrian's genius laboratory or Alex's lucky solution;
- Lock as a serious opponent with a concrete offer a seventeen-year-old would consider;
- Alex and Ada as friends, with no romantic coding;
- the difference between a business that earns customers and Houses that exist because Alpha selected them;
- systems learned through consequences rather than instruction. `(input/notes.md)`

The audience is 15–18-year-old students who did not choose the book, are skeptical of anything that feels artificial, and will abandon scenes they cannot follow on a first read. The immediate dramatic objective must remain legible even when the larger system is complicated. `(rules/reader.md)`

The story's language rises with Alex's movement from Keel to Halyard to Oasis, but this is not an intelligence scale. Alex and Ada describe visible facts and human burdens rather than using civic or academic abstractions. Bi11bot translates choices into questions about odds, ownership, value, risk, and consequences. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Structure

**Canon — fixed:** Three issues, eighteen pages each, fifty-four pages total. No page exceeds four panels in execution.

### Issue 01 — Keel: “The Measure”  
**Pages 1–18.** Alex inherits Adrian’s debt, awakens B11, rescues Tomas, discovers the 62%/18% water discrepancy, and chooses public evidence over a private escape. The issue ends with water filling the conduit behind the travelers and Tomas’s fate uncertain.

- **Act I turn, Page 6:** Alex crosses the Site 6 line to rescue Tomas, sacrificing wages and secrecy.
- **Midpoint turn, Page 12:** Alex transmits the water evidence, losing his private route out.
- **Issue turn, Page 18:** The conduit begins filling behind Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ. Tomas’s signal disappears.

### Issue 02 — Halyard: “The Seal”  
**Pages 19–36.** Alex and Ada emerge in a storm drain, encounter the House ownership system, accept the collaborators’ risks, build the household purifier, and refuse Lock’s estate transfer. The issue ends with Mera deciding whether to bring Alex to the third safeguard.

- **Act I turn, Page 22:** Lock offers debt clearance and protection in exchange for Adrian’s estate.
- **Midpoint turn, Page 28:** The industrial prototype fails; Alex identifies the shared connector problem.
- **Issue turn, Page 36:** Mera receives the Seal event and chooses whether to intervene or summon Alex.

### Issue 03 — Oasis: “The Fourth Choice”  
**Pages 37–54.** Alex confronts Leona’s history, learns Adrian’s final choice, refuses Mera’s executive offer, and helps establish a bounded human review. The new process catches a rigged water allocation. Alex’s debt remains and his travel clearance expires; his future and Ada’s remain chosen, not settled.

- **Act I turn, Page 42:** Leona publicly accepts an obligation instead of asking for forgiveness.
- **Midpoint turn, Page 46:** Adrian’s final recording reveals that B11 was activated before Adrian’s presumed death; the scene cuts before evacuation.
- **Climax, Pages 49–51:** Alex refuses executive authority and proposes the Distributed Verification Protocol.
- **Issue turn, Page 54:** Alex and Ada face the continuing review and the question of whether either will leave.

## Page plot

### Issue 01 — Keel

**Page 1:** Alex repairs a failing Keel pump by touch and salvaged hardware; the machine runs, but the official repair record marks him for review.

**Page 2:** At the checkpoint, Alex’s seventeenth-birthday liability transfer displays 418,607 credits and restricted travel; the public debt becomes his immediate problem.

**Page 3:** Alex passes the shuttered Last Chance casino and turns Adrian’s worn chip; the bouncer’s joke makes clear that Adrian’s gambling damaged the family.

**Page 4:** Ada brings medicine and a travel token, fronts both under fixed repayment terms, and challenges Alex’s belief that leaving is the only way to choose a future.

**Page 5:** Grandmother gives Alex Adrian’s book; the bookshelf opens onto a hidden workshop.

**Page 6:** Alex crosses into the workshop, finds the dormant robot and the distinct workshop ignition key; B11 wakes. **Act I turn.**

**Page 7:** B11 identifies Alex, the chip, and Adrian’s wager; Adrian’s recording asks Alex to earn answers and question everything.

**Page 8:** B11’s map points below Keel; Alpha detects the legacy signal while Mera selects TRACK, suppressing escalation but not independent reporting.

**Page 9:** Alex is reassigned to Site 6; Ada gives him Adrian’s marked paper plans. Alex chooses the route despite knowing it may cost his escape.

**Page 10:** At Site 6, Tomas is pinned by a failed support; Alex approaches the assigned-zone line while B11 begins assembling TJ from spare parts.

**Page 11:** TJ’s head scouts the gate; his detached segments enter the jammed release mechanism and free the support pinning Tomas. Alex crosses the line and accepts the −40-credit wage forfeiture.

**Page 12:** Alex patches the ventilation line and meets Tomas’s recognition of B11. Tomas gives him the separate hand-cut mine key and points him toward the old door. **Midpoint.**

**Page 13:** Tomas creates a false shutdown explanation while Alex opens the unnetworked door; Foreman-7 remains outside with the workers.

**Page 14:** Alex follows the oiled side route; TJ watches a patrol from a junction while B11 masks the group’s signal. Condensation proves the dead mine is carrying water.

**Page 15:** The hidden chamber reveals the dry KEEL RESIDENTIAL pipe beside the active UPLINK INDUSTRIAL pipe; the official gauge says 62%.

**Page 16:** The Measure SENSES and COMPARES; TRACE exposes the routing discrepancy. A visible dry side passage leads toward an unmapped service exit while the public transmission path remains open.

**Page 17:** Alex chooses VERIFY over private escape. The evidence reaches Keel; the side passage seals. Elsewhere, Mera opens a clearly separate conduit-segment valve—the only viable ascent route—while Alpha continues its independent report.

**Page 18:** Keel residents see 62% reported / 18% delivered; miners force open the separate residential regulator. Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ enter the ascent conduit as water begins filling behind them. Tomas’s signal cuts out. **Page-turn reveal / Issue 01 question: Has Tomas escaped the rising water?**

### Issue 02 — Halyard

**Page 19:** Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ emerge into a Halyard storm drain; behind them, the conduit remains full and Tomas’s signal is absent. **Issue opening.**

**Page 20:** Halyard’s polished infrastructure and three House insignias surround the group; a public service display shows Keel’s water evidence still under dispute.

**Page 21:** At the registry, Adrian’s 312-patent estate appears under Alex’s name. Lock offers immediate debt clearance and protection in exchange for estate rights.

**Page 22:** Lock explains the House case: scale can build and distribute faster, while refusal exposes Alex and the collaborators to review. Alex takes the signed transfer packet but does not sign.

**Page 23:** The group passes reopened Morrow & Sons, where a worker sells a repairable household filter at a posted fair price, records a wage, and makes a modest margin. The business contrasts with Alpha-selected Houses.

**Page 24:** Lina shows Alex the contaminated feeder channel. She distinguishes this future-supply problem from the 62%/18% routing failure and gives him the membrane component.

**Page 25:** Suri protects a living biofilter at an abandoned bench; she shares it only after Alex asks what happens to the people maintaining it.

**Page 26:** Jalen gives the distributed-node logic under surveillance; B11 counts only registry-visible people and recommends the safest route.

**Page 27:** Ren contributes mineral recovery and atmospheric capture; the five fragments form an industrial design that fails because each House standard locks itself against the others.

**Page 28:** Alex handles the connectors, builds a salvaged sleeve, and raises the simulation from 11% to 94%. The fix is physical and shared, not a new lecture.

**Page 29:** Lock appears with the review deadline and names the collaborators as exposed employees. He makes clear that refusing the transfer costs them autonomy and access.

**Page 30:** B11 advises signing from his risk table. Alex points to the people outside its registry boundary; B11 admits he counted records and missed people, then asks Alex what he would do.

**Page 31:** The collaborators return despite their fear. Lina identifies the membrane constraint; Suri rejects a disposable biofilter; Jalen supplies the power-cycle condition; Ren solves waste heat. Each decision changes the build.

**Page 32:** Alex assembles the household-scale housing and connector system; Ada organizes parts and customer-use conditions while TJ retrieves a dropped fastener. The design becomes a toolbox-sized unit.

**Page 33:** The prototype reaches 91% simulated recovery efficiency, remains feedwater- and maintenance-dependent, and has no patent filed. The group chooses public attribution without exclusive ownership.

**Page 34:** At the House review, Lock reiterates that a House can outscale them. Alex refuses the estate transfer and names Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren.

**Page 35:** Lina gives Alex the one physical Maker’s Seal she has carried for fifteen years. It activates only after the four collaborators confirm the shared work; the public record preserves their names.

**Page 36:** House production and control remain; the collaborators lose autonomy and access. Mera receives the Seal event and must choose whether to suppress, summon, or escalate. Her interface shows TRACK / SUMMON, with escalation unavailable under her standing scope. **Page-turn reveal / Issue 02 question: What will Mera choose to do with the third safeguard?**

### Issue 03 — Oasis

**Page 37:** Mera’s summons brings Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ into Oasis; Alex’s temporary pass requires escort while Ada’s old record opens a route.

**Page 38:** Ada finds Leona’s civic record and enters her apartment alone; Alex waits outside the open door, honoring Ada’s choice.

**Page 39:** Leona admits she knew where Ada was within a year and chose not to return. Ada rejects safety as an answer to abandonment.

**Page 40:** Mera arrives; she admits surveillance was not relationship, while defending the immediate lives her order preserved.

**Page 41:** Leona reveals the original three-person Covenant and her deciding vote for emergency authority. The three bands’ possession is clear: Leona’s in the box, Adrian’s in B11, Mera’s dark and with her.

**Page 42:** Leona publicly commits to Ada’s redesign: affected people must see obligations and call them broken. Adrian’s and Leona’s bands link; Mera’s remains dark. **Act II turn.**

**Page 43:** Mera leads Alex, Ada, B11, and TJ to the old monitoring station beneath Oasis, outside Alpha’s ordinary routing. The room is dusty, sealed, and free of visible Alpha nodes.

**Page 44:** Mera shows the physical and administrative trail of Adrian’s pursuit: financial restriction, liability designation, travel denial, and drones.

**Page 45:** Her attempted override appears and is denied for exceeding scope. The scene shows her failure without turning Alpha into a person or excusing her earlier choices.

**Page 46:** Adrian’s final station recording begins: water rises, the bulkhead closes, and Adrian activates B11 into a recording/deployment state. The sequence cuts before physical evacuation; no handoff is shown.

**Page 47:** In the present, B11 admits his memory is incomplete. Adrian’s prearranged route explains the dormant B11 body in the workshop without showing an impossible transfer. Alex accepts that Adrian was both damaging father and consequential collaborator.

**Page 48:** At the legacy node in the civic hall, the Measure, Seal, and Covenant are physically present. The system authenticates Alex only as convenor; it requires Leona’s obligation and community-designated participants.

**Page 49:** Mera offers Alex full executive authority. He imagines clearing debt, equalizing water, protecting Ada and Grandmother, and controlling the Houses.

**Page 50:** Alex proposes Ada as a check; Ada refuses the two-person solution. Alex refuses authority for himself, Ada, or anyone. The refusal costs him: the workshop is sealed as disputed estate property and his temporary travel clearance is set to expire.

**Page 51:** Leona publicly supports the proposal. Designated participants from Keel, Halyard, and Oasis publish their interests and authorize a temporary Distributed Verification Protocol. Alpha registers only the bounded transition.

**Page 52:** Emergency authority is rescinded within the defined scope; existing debt, ownership, allocations, and agreements remain. Mera loses executive override access. The first public water-allocation review opens.

**Page 53:** A House representative submits an allocation that appears correct on the public record while diverting physical flow. Tomas and Keel residents compare the record with gauges and challenge it; approval stops and an appeal marker opens. **The new process proves itself.**

**Page 54:** Weeks later, Alex’s debt remains 402,110 credits, the workshop remains sealed, and his travel clearance has expired. His visible payment to Ada reduces their personal balance; Morrow & Sons sells a purifier and replacement cartridge at a fair price. Alex and Ada stand beside the continuing review. **Final question: Will Alex leave, and what will Ada choose for herself?**

## Reader vs character knowledge

### Reader knows before Alex

- Mera selects TRACK but cannot fully control Alpha’s independent reports.
- The Houses were selected by Alpha rather than winning dominance solely through customers.
- The contaminated feeder is separate from the 62%/18% routing failure.
- Tomas’s signal disappears during the conduit crossing, while Alex does not know whether he escaped.
- Adrian activated B11 before his final presumed death; the physical route of the dormant body remains withheld until Page 47.
- Mera’s band remains dark and the original Covenant is not restored.
- Lock’s surveillance identifies the collaborators before Alex knows the full scope of exposure.

### Alex knows before the reader

- The tactile difference between a working repair and a paper-approved repair.
- The terms of his personal debt to Ada.
- What Tomas’s rescue cost him in wages.
- The physical difference between the residential regulator and the conduit-segment valve.
- The connector insight that makes the household purifier possible.
- That his refusal of executive authority will leave him with debt and fewer immediate exits.

### Characters believe, but the reader should test

- Lock believes House scale is necessary for speed and safety; the story proves he is partly right without granting him ownership.
- Mera believes emergency concentration prevented greater deaths; the story does not erase that case, but exposes its unreturned cost.
- Leona believed safety justified choosing for Ada; Ada rejects that equation.
- Alex initially believes two trustworthy people could safely share power; Ada disproves the structure.
- B11 initially believes registry-visible risk captures the relevant people; his error is admitted and corrected.
- The public system believes its allocation record is sufficient; Tomas and the gauges disprove it.

## Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

- **Canon:** The page count, issue divisions, locations, safeguards, debts, House names, B11/TJ continuity, Mera’s separate valve, Adrian’s deployment-state sequence, and the final review follow the brief and decision log.
- **Proposal:** The first public review centers on Keel residential flow versus an industrial diversion. This gives the Measure and Tomas immediate dramatic use without creating a second climax.
- **Proposal:** The first review’s appeal marker is a plain, visible status attached to the challenged allocation: `CHALLENGE FILED — PHYSICAL EVIDENCE PENDING`. It can recur on Page 54.
- **Proposal:** The unnamed community-designated participants remain visually distinct through evidence and action rather than new named protagonists: a Keel gauge worker, a Halyard maintenance representative, and an Oasis allocation clerk.
- **Decision needed:** Whether Alex eventually leaves Prosperity remains open. This issue ends before that choice is made.
- **Decision needed:** The full legal treatment of House charters, land, concessions, and external trade remains outside this book.
- **Risk:** The purifier could be misread as solving scarcity. Keep its feedwater dependency, cleaning needs, cost, and limited household scale visible.
- **Risk:** Mera’s action could be read as benevolent certainty. Show the viable ascent route and the independent Alpha report together; preserve her moral uncertainty without making the mechanism unclear.
- **Risk:** The page 54 payment could become a montage. Show Alex physically making the payment and the personal balance changing before the final image.
