# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, pages only, night-before-homework conditions. I did not choose this book. I
opened it wanting it to be bad. Here is what actually happened.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
- Page 1: the three-tier city reads instantly — poor at the bottom, shiny at the top, pipe
  getting thinner as it comes down. I've seen this image before in every dystopia ever, but
  it's clear and the kid with the wrong bracket fixing the pump anyway gives me a person,
  not just a world.
- Page 2, panel 2: the checkpoint screen is the hook. Inheriting your father's debt on your
  seventeenth birthday, 418,607 credits, 40 earned today, 28.7 years to clear. The math
  makes it real. "Happy birthday, Phantum." Okay. I'm in.
- Page 3, panel 1: "You look expensive." / "It came with a twenty-eight-year warranty." Alex
  and Ada's banter is the first time the book sounds like people.
- Page 6: bookshelf swings open. Fine, you got me. Secret room.
- Page 8, panel 3: the robot's eyes igniting. "...Well. That took longer than expected."
- Page 16, panel 4: "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." Genuinely creepy. Best
  two-line exchange in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 11: the three-tools lecture. Measure, Maker's Seal, Covenant bands, "tools for three
  different kinds of damage," "that depends who gets hurt." This is the first page where the
  book stops showing me things and starts explaining them, and the explanations are vague.
  My eyes slid off it.
- Page 13: map talk, ink ticks, "look for what doesn't belong." Setup porridge.
- Pages 11–13 as a run: after the strong opening, this is where the book becomes homework.

### I was confused
- Page 12, panel 3: "The first node is under the pit." First node of *what*? Nobody has
  mentioned nodes before. "And the second?" "Higher. Unconfirmed." I have no idea what
  system we're counting nodes in. (Confused and detached, not intrigued.)
- Page 12, panel 2: who is Mera Vale? A woman appears in a control room, chooses TRACK over
  REPORT TO ALPHA, and I'm supposed to feel a threat. I don't know her, Alpha, or what a
  legacy protocol is.
- Page 12, panel 1: why is Alex pressing the amber node at all? What does he think will
  happen? He just does it because the plot needs the pulse detected.
- Page 17, panel 3: Ada is suddenly crawling through a separate storm culvert into the old
  works. When did she enter the mine? Page 16 showed Alex going through the bulkhead with
  the robot; Ada wasn't there. I flipped back twice.
- Page 17, panel 4: a dog-robot assembles out of Bi11bot's chest with zero warning. TJ.
  Okay? Surprise, but I rolled with it.
- Page 18, panel 4: "Mera's authorization reaches a conduit valve. CONDUIT SEGMENT —
  RELEASE AUTHORIZED." So the control-room woman is drowning them? That's what the page
  says. Filed it, because it matters later that this is never dealt with.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The casino chip (page 2, panel 3). Alex carries it, turns it, it's etched with an equation
  that matches the chalkboard (page 7) — and nobody ever says where he got it. It's treated
  as loaded from its first panel. Never explained.
- Alpha (page 7 "pre-Alpha pipes," page 12 "REPORT TO ALPHA"). Used like I know it. I don't.
  AI? Government? Never defined in the whole issue.
- The Measure, the Maker's Seal, the Covenant bands (page 11). Capital-letter Things,
  explained only in riddles.
- Mera Vale (page 12). No introduction at all.
- "Legacy protocol" (page 12). No idea.
- Foreman-7 (page 14) — a human man with a number for a name. Why -7? Never said.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then don't spend them proving he was right about people leaving" (page 3). Parsed it
  eventually — if Alex leaves he confirms the runaway-dad story — but it took work.
- "I think everybody's been very sure of a story nobody can show you" (page 3).
- "That depends who gets hurt" (page 11). Answer-shaped fog.
- "They make it harder to hide who saw it, who made it, and who owes a response" (page 11).
  Got it on reread. It's a mouthful.
- "A versatile word" (page 17) — read twice but in the good way. Liked it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Clear: Alex (wry, tired, "I know"), Ada (teasing, watchful), Bi11bot (dry robot — most
  distinct voice in the issue), Grandmother.
- Blurry: Tomas and Foreman-7 are both big work-site men; the wrist-guard reveal separates
  them, barely. Mera is a silhouette with a tracking ring.

### I felt something
- Page 2: the birthday debt transfer genuinely got me. Angry on his behalf.
- Page 5: "Some days she knows me. Other days she calls me Adrian."
- Page 10: "Especially me" landing as Alex unfolds the photo and the faces match. The folded
  photo paying off is the best-constructed beat in the issue.
- Page 14: a full day's wage docked the second he steps over the line to save Tomas, and all
  he says is "I know."

### I predicted
- The gambling story is a lie and dad was working (page 4 all but announces it).
- The sheet-covered figure is a robot and it wakes up (page 7).
- The official water numbers are fake (the moment the gauge said 62% next to a dry pipe).
- Adrian is dead. "Whereabouts unrecorded" for fifteen years means dead.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his father's crushing debt in a city stacked like a pyramid scheme and
discovers his father left him tools to prove the system is lying.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 11. The opening ten pages earned my attention; the tool museum spent it. That's where
my phone came out.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but not because of the flood — I don't believe for a second they'll drown in issue 1
of 3, so the water is noise. The thing I actually want is the city reacting to the 62/18
broadcast. That's the real cliffhanger and it's the quieter one.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 11; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
- Page 19: crawling out of a storm drain into the bright city is a good gross entrance, and
  TJ's leg clicking a beat late is the one bit of robot personality that isn't a speech.
- Page 20, panel 2: the kiosk. "She made it." / "The House owns it." Halyard's whole deal in
  one transaction — clean and nasty.
- Page 21, panel 1: 312 patents. That genuinely recontextualized the debt for me. I sat up.
- Pages 28–29: the scrap sleeve working. I wanted it to work. "KLIK" and 91% got a real
  reaction.
- Page 36, panel 4: the woman with Ada's hair in the black glass who isn't there. Little
  chill. Best hook in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 23–26: the four collaborator visits are the same scene four times. Aphorism,
  fragment, "find the next one." By Suri I was skimming; by Jalen I was reading every third
  balloon. I re-engaged at page 28 when the prototype started sparking.
- Pages 31–33: the build montage and the "nobody gets to call it a miracle" insistence.
  Nobody *was* calling it a miracle. The book keeps arguing against positions nobody in the
  scene took, and I started skimming the dialogue because every line is a motto.

### I was confused
- Page 35, panel 1: Lina pulls the Maker's Seal out of her uniform and says Adrian gave it
  to her before he vanished. But page 11 showed the Maker's Seal sitting in the workshop
  with Bi11bot. I flipped back to check. Same name, same description, two places. "Wait,
  what?" — confused and annoyed, not intrigued.
- Page 21, panels 3–4: Lock never speaks. He offers a tablet, Alex talks at him, scene ends.
  Is he mute? It reads like his dialogue got deleted.
- Page 23, panel 2: Alex says "So the 62 percent comes back through a different pipe" —
  that's not what Lina said; she said it's a separate, physical problem. His line is a non
  sequitur and her correction has to mop it up.
- The travel restriction. Page 2 said RESTRICTED in big letters. Then Alex walks around
  Halyard, scans his ID at a public registry (page 21), and nothing happens. A drone told
  him to remain for protective detention one issue ago. Is he a fugitive or not? The book
  stops caring, so I stopped knowing.
- The flood. Page 18 said Mera's authorization released the water. Issue 2 never mentions
  it. Nobody is chasing them. Did anyone try to kill them or not?
- Also: Alex leaves his dementia grandmother alone in Keel for what reads like days or
  weeks. Page 13 made a point of "medicine first," then he just goes. I noticed.

### Things I was expected to already know
- What a "House" is (pages 20–21). Aqualis, Helion, Astra — companies? Families?
  Governments? They run everything and I couldn't tell you what they are.
- Why a 312-patent estate can coexist with a 418,607-credit debt (page 21). The obvious
  question — why didn't the patents cover it — is never answered.
- "Charter House" (page 30) and what its review can actually do.
- Who Lock is and where his authority comes from. He's just... polished and there.

### Lines I read twice
- "A clean owner also makes them disappear cleanly" (page 22).
- "Then don't confuse relief with ownership" (page 24).
- "Careful gets you buried under a collapse same as reckless" (page 26). Read it three
  times. Still only half sure.
- "Those look identical from here" (page 30). Took me a second to get that the punishment
  notices and the review notice are the same machine.
- "You found the part that wasn't waiting for you" (page 29). Good once decoded — everything
  else was left by his father; the sleeve is his — but I had to decode it.
- "Small enough that no House can pretend the people inside it are optional" (page 31).

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lina: yes. "I corrected him. Frequently." has an edge.
- Ren: mostly — scorched sleeve, dry delivery.
- Suri and Jalen: no. Both soft-spoken wise fragment-keepers; their lines are
  interchangeable. The four collaborators as a group blur into "the mentors," because all
  four talk in the same balanced one-liner register.
- Lock: a tablet with a haircut. He never does anything, so there's nothing to tell.

### I felt something
- Page 24, panel 3: "They removed the team under 'efficiency.' I stayed because something
  had to keep breathing." That one got through.
- Page 29: the sleeve seating and the sparks stopping. Earned.
- Page 36: the reflection. Creepy in the right way.

### I predicted
- The withheld third name is Ada's mother. Page 36 hands it to you — withheld name plus a
  reflection with Ada's hair. No points for guessing.
- The Houses would retaliate the second the review was filed (page 30).
- The fix would be Alex's unapproved scrap part. Telegraphed from page 1 — his whole
  character is "repairs that aren't in the manual," so of course the machine needs a repair
  that isn't in the manual. Satisfying, but I saw it coming from another city.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Same fight as issue 1, one level up: who gets credit for the work — the debt, the patents,
and the erased collaborators are all the same theft wearing different clothes.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 25, in the middle of the fragment parade, at "They don't watch intentions. They watch
repetitions." Third wise stranger in a row and I was done.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes — and this hook is better than issue 1's flood because it's about a person. Ada's mother
is a question I want answered about someone I know. The flood was a loud noise; this is a
face.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 25; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
- Page 37: "It knew my name." Good payoff on the reflection.
- Pages 39–40: the mother-daughter door scene. I read every word. "I didn't come for tea"
  echoing Ada's "Tea's free" from page 3 — I caught it and it landed.
- Page 48: Adrian's death. The near-monochrome station flooding while he records instead of
  escaping is the best sequence in the issue. I felt it and I was furious at him at the same
  time.
- Page 50, panel 4: "Don't give me that much power." The one thesis line that sounds like a
  person saying it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 42–44: the Covenant explanation is a lot of people sitting in pale rooms being
  precise at each other. I half-skimmed until "I made the deciding choice."
- Pages 51–53: the book ends with three pages of meetings. I skimmed the screens. The
  climax of the book is administrative text updating.

### I was confused
- The bands. Page 11 showed three dark Covenant bands in the workshop. Page 42, Leona's band
  has been in her box for fifteen years. Page 44, Mera has her own pearl-grey band — and
  Bi11bot's chest suddenly holds Adrian's *and* Leona's. How many bands are there, and where
  have they been? And what does linking them actually *do*? They lift and glow and I don't
  know what changed.
- Page 48 vs. page 8: Bi11bot's eyes ignite beside Adrian in the flooding station — but in
  the workshop he wakes up saying "That took longer than expected," like a first activation.
  And how did a robot sealed in a flooded room under Oasis end up under a dust sheet behind
  grandma's bookshelf in Keel? Nobody carried him out. The bulkhead held. This hole is huge
  and the book never looks at it.
- Where is Adrian's body? Page 46 says the station's dust is undisturbed for fifteen years.
  He drowned in that room. So either his body is in there while everyone has feelings around
  it, or it vanished. Nobody mentions it. I kept waiting for someone to say one word about
  it. Nothing.
- The flood from issue 1. Page 18 said Mera's authorization released the conduit water on
  them. Page 41 tells me Mera spent years secretly protecting Ada. Those two things cannot
  both be true, and the book never reconciles them. This is the thread that bothered me most
  across the whole read.
- Page 51, panel 4: "The fourth choice is yours." What were the first three? I counted
  tools, choices, bands — nothing cleanly makes four.
- Why does Alpha just... agree? Page 51: Alex says "designate a process" and the screen says
  EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED. Fifteen years of emergency rule by the system that
  (apparently) killed his father, ended by request, no resistance, no scene. Alpha never
  once says no to anyone in this issue. The all-powerful thing has no face, no voice, and no
  fight in it, so the climax has no opponent.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The water wars (page 42). First mention, one memory panel, never detailed. Who fought?
  Over what, exactly, beyond "water"? The event the whole world is built on gets one panel.
- What Alpha actually *is*. Fifty-four pages and I cannot tell you. An AI, a government, a
  person, a policy? It tracks, restricts, authorizes, executes — and is never once defined.
- "Convenor," "handoff," "safeguards" (page 49). The vocabulary arrives all at once, late,
  in a speech.
- Mara Doss, Frank Reed, Elias Renn (page 52). Three brand-new people walk in on page 52 of
  54 and run the resolution. Who are these people?

### Lines I read twice
- "I thought she had disappeared. I stopped asking whether that meant dead or unwilling"
  (page 38). Read twice — in the good way.
- "It was the part of the protection I could still perform" (page 41).
- "The Covenant failed when fear made promises smaller than survival" (page 42). Sounds
  carved on a monument. I had to unpack it.
- "I want the decision to know who it can hurt" (page 43).
- "He carried it for fifteen years" / "Carrying is not honoring" (page 44). Who is "he"?
  Adrian? Bi11bot? And the second line is pure fortune cookie.
- "Then the system will be wrong through you" (page 49).
- "I don't trust any structure that needs that much trust in one person" (page 50). Got it,
  agree with it, noticed I was being taught it.
- "Just one that can't pretend it's clean" (page 53).

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Ada finally becomes her own person — the door scene is hers and it's the best her
  character ever gets.
- Leona and Mera are distinguishable as roles (mother vs. functionary) but speak in the
  exact same measured cadence. Cover the names on pages 41–43 and you can't always tell
  who's talking.
- The three representatives are interchangeable except Mara's one good question.
- Bi11bot remains the most distinct voice in the book, which says something, because he's
  the robot.

### I felt something
- Page 40, panel 3: "You chose safety for me." / "Yes." / "You chose it instead of me."
  That's the emotional peak of the whole book for me.
- Page 45, panel 2: "I'd like permission to keep trying." / "You can keep trying."
  Restrained and honest. No hug, no fix. Good.
- Page 48: grief and anger at once — he spent his last minutes on a message instead of a
  door. The book half-knows it was a selfish choice (page 49: "He also made the choice to
  leave you the burden") and half-excuses it, and I side with the half that doesn't.
- Page 54: grandmother's hands finally empty, "Mine now."

### I predicted
- Leona was the deciding vote. "I made the deciding choice" was visibly coming from the
  moment the two-of-three rule was stated.
- Alex would refuse the power. The book has never once let him take the easy win, so the
  refusal was right and completely unsurprising.
- Mera's band would stay dark.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether anyone should hold unchecked power — Alex's answer is "not even me," and then the
book turns to me and asks the question out loud.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 52. Three strangers arrive to chair the ending and I felt the book hand itself over to
a meeting. I finished because there were two pages left, not because I was pulled.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The ending is honest — debt remains, scarcity remains, nothing is magically fixed — and
I respect that more than a clean victory. But the FINAL HUMAN QUESTION caption ("Can people
accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee?") is the moment the book admits
it's a lesson. That's a discussion prompt, not an ending. The story is closed, there's no
person I'm following forward, and the last page told me to write an essay about it. I
wouldn't buy the next book because this one told me it was finished and then assigned me
homework.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 52; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 11. It's the first page where the book stops showing and starts lecturing, and it comes
right after the best stretch in the book (pages 6–10), so it wastes the exact moment it had
us. Thirty kids, thirty phones. The ones who survived page 11 would be picked off by the
fragment parade (pages 23–26) and the meeting ending (pages 51–53), but page 11 is where
the class as a class checks out.

And one thing the class would say out loud, because they're openly suspicious of anything
that smells machine-made: this book's dialogue sounds generated. "Thinking about it isn't
doing it." "Carrying is not honoring." "Scale is not a promise." "Quiet isn't the same as
safe." Every character — miner, scientist, director, grandmother, robot — speaks in the
same polished, balanced, quote-card antithesis, and the names (Alex Phantum, Mera Vale,
Bi11bot with two 1s) have that same too-smooth ring. Nobody in this book talks like a
person. A classroom of seventeen-year-olds would clock that in the first ten pages and
never fully trust the book again.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a poor mining city inherits his dad's giant debt on his seventeenth birthday,
finds his dad's hidden robot, and proves the rich cities are lying about water — and then
instead of taking over the system, he makes it hold public meetings. It's less fun than
that sounds, because everyone talks in proverbs and the big showdown is a hearing. The mom
scene and the dad's death are genuinely good, though."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: A killer first two pages — the birthday debt inheritance — that stalls in a
  tool museum and ends on a flood I never believed.
- Issue 02: A fetch-quest tour of four wise mentors who all talk in the same quotable
  one-liners, saved by a scrap-metal fix and one creepy reflection.
- Issue 03: The mother at the door and the father's death are the real thing, but the
  climax is a meeting the all-powerful system doesn't resist, and the last page hands me
  an essay question.
