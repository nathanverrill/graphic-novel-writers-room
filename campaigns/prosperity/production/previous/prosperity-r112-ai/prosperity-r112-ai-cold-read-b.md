# Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
TJ the magnetic robot dog breaking himself apart to jam the water thieves' pump (Pages 4-5) was actually pretty cool. I also liked the reveal of the hidden workshop behind the bookcase on Page 16.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 12. The giant text box about "PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER" and debt balances felt like reading a math syllabus. I skimmed the exact numbers. I also skimmed Bi11bot explaining the infrastructure spine on Page 20. 

### I was confused
Page 11 threw me completely. Alex is an old man with a granddaughter on page 10, and suddenly on page 11 he's 17? It took me a few pages to realize the entire first 10 pages were a flash-forward and the rest of the book is a flashback. 

### Things I was expected to already know
Wardens, Phantum (I didn't know if this was a title or his last name until page 12), Alpha, Site 6.

### Lines I read twice
- "Sometimes the work sends an account." (Page 7) - Sounds deep but I have no idea what it actually means.
- "The balance disagrees." (Page 13)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex and the granddaughter are clear. Voss and Nera are just generic thieves, I already forgot them. Ada just kind of appears on page 13 without much introduction, but I could tell her apart from Grandmother.

### I felt something
Page 9, when the granddaughter tries to fix TJ and Alex looks at the lake and says "So will the lake." It made the environmental damage feel personal.

### I predicted
That Alex's dad didn't actually gamble the money away, but was secretly building a rebellion or a machine to save the town.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teenager inherits his dead dad's massive debt and finds out his dad was secretly a rebel engineer.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 12. The massive text dump of debt and the robot saying "Happy birthday, Phantum" felt super cliché.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 22 ends with a tracking ring closing on their signal. It's a solid cliffhanger. I'd read the next one to see if they get caught.

`VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 12; next issue: yes`

# Issue 02

### Pulled me forward
The mine collapse on Page 24 and Tomas using the secret old door to help them escape on Page 27. The broadcast to the whole city on Page 34 was a cool moment of fighting back.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 32. The readout of "ROLLING PERIOD: 30 DAYS, REPORTED: 62%, DELIVERED: 18%". It felt like I was being assigned a word problem. 

### I was confused
Page 35. The neighbors pool 500 credits, and suddenly Alex's travel restriction is lifted inside the mine? It took me a minute to connect that paying his overdue bill unlocked the gate for him. 

### Things I was expected to already know
Halyard, travel tokens, "wage forfeited", industrial diversion.

### Lines I read twice
- "Make it real." (Page 23)
- "Adrian hid the answer where the question hurt." (Page 31) - Again with the riddle speak.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Tomas is distinct. Foreman-7 is just a robot or a guy in a mask, I don't really care about him. 

### I felt something
Page 35, when the community pools their money, even the clinic envelope, to pay Alex's debt so he can escape. 

### I predicted
They are going to take the train to the rich city (Halyard) to shove the proof in the faces of the people stealing the water.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Sneaking through an abandoned mine to find proof that the rich city is stealing water from the poor city.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 32. The percentages and flow gauges are boring. 

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 37. They're just sitting on a train heading to Halyard. It's not a cliffhanger at all, but I want to see what Halyard looks like, so I'd keep going.

`VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 32; next issue: yes`

# Issue 03

### Pulled me forward
The montage of them building the water purifier out of scrap parts (Pages 42-43) moved fast and was fun to look at.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 39 with the "Intellectual Estate" registry and the creator bonuses. 

### I was confused
Page 41. Who are Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren? They literally just spawn into the story in one panel, and suddenly they are Alex's best friends helping him build a machine. I have no reason to care about any of them.

### Things I was expected to already know
Caldera House, Vector House, Orbital House, Maker's Seal.

### Lines I read twice
- "Customers should be able to fix it." (Page 43) - Felt a bit like a preachy right-to-repair ad.
- "And who carries the work after." (Page 44)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren completely blur together. They each get exactly one line and one job (chemistry, plants, routing, metal) and have zero personality beyond that.

### I felt something
Page 44, seeing the first unit bought in Keel and the money splitting up fairly to pay off Alex's debt and fund the creators. 

### I predicted
The corporate Houses are going to steal the purifier design or make it illegal in the next issue.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Using open-source engineering to bypass a corporate monopoly on clean water.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 41. Introducing four random characters at once and expecting me to care about their "restricted contributions" lost me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 44 ends on a totally happy note. The machine works, people are buying it, the debt is going down. There is zero hook for the next issue. I'd probably stop reading here if I wasn't forced to.

`VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 41; next issue: no`

# Issue 04

### Pulled me forward
Ada confronting her mom in the archive (Pages 46-48). It was the first time two characters actually felt like they were having a real, emotional argument instead of just talking about pipes.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 47. The flashback projection about the shortage, the agreement, and who voted yes or no. 

### I was confused
What exactly is a Covenant band? Is it a voting device? A magic ring? A USB drive? When Leona links hers with Adrian's on page 48, I didn't understand what that mechanically accomplished.

### Things I was expected to already know
Covenant bands, "temporary emergency authority", Direct Water Equalization.

### Lines I read twice
- "Don’t apologize where it costs nothing." (Page 48) - Actually a really good line.
- "Two of three. Again." (Page 50)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona and Mera. They are both older women in power in Oasis who talk in formal sentences. I had to double-check who was the mom and who was the villain.

### I felt something
Page 46, when Ada calls out her mom for secretly watching her grow up but never actually helping her. 

### I predicted
Alex is going to refuse the ultimate executive power because he doesn't want to be a dictator, even a good one.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Arguing with politicians about whether it's better to have a dictator who fixes things instantly or a messy democracy.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 47. The lore dump about the three bands and the old voting history was dry.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 50. Alex turns down the god-mode button and Mera gets mad. I need the next issue to see what Mera does to retaliate.

`VERDICT Issue 04: put down: page 47; next issue: yes`

# Issue 06

Tomas slamming the physical gauge down on the table to prove the corporate suit is lying about the water (Page 62). 

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 60-61. A lot of dialogue about "human review structures", "allocations", and "revised denominators". It felt like reading a city council meeting transcript.

So Alex just... asks the computer to change its government style, and the screen just says `HUMAN REVIEW STRUCTURE ACCEPTED`? Why didn't Mera or Leona just type that in years ago? It felt way too easy.

### Things I was expected to already know
"Revised denominator", "evidence challenge filed".

### Lines I read twice
- "This makes an obligation visible. It doesn’t make anyone obey." (Page 59)
- "You counted the pipe after you stopped counting the homes." (Page 62)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
The Caldera Representative is just a generic corporate suit. 

### I felt something
Page 63, when Tomas gets his 40 credits paid back on the receipt. It was a nice callback to the beginning.

### I predicted
I predicted he'd destroy the AI, but he just demoted it to an accountant.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Winning a revolution by filing a paperwork dispute and forcing the government to show their receipts.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 61. The screens saying "HUMAN REVIEW STRUCTURE ACCEPTED" felt like a really boring, anti-climactic way to win a revolution. It felt like a terms of service update.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 64. It wraps up nicely, showing the older Alex from the prologue. The story is completely over. I wouldn't buy a sequel because there's no conflict left.

# Whole Book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 32. The readout of "Rolling Period: 30 Days, Reported to Keel: 62%, Physically Delivered: 18%". It looks exactly like a math word problem and everyone would immediately zone out.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A teen inherits his dad's massive debt and goes on a quest to prove the rich cities are stealing their water, ending up inventing a water filter and changing the government through a town hall meeting.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Cool robot dog, but the time jump and the massive debt text boxes were confusing.
- **Issue 02:** Sneaking through an abandoned mine to do math.
- **Issue 03:** A sudden montage of open-source engineering with four characters I don't know or care about.
- **Issue 04:** Politicians arguing about who gets to be the dictator.
- **Issue 05:** A sad hologram of a dad drowning.
- **Issue 06:** Winning a revolution by filing a paperwork dispute.
