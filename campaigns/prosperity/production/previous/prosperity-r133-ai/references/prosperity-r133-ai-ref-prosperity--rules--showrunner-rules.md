# The showrunner's standing rules

These bind the book. A name, a fact or a prohibition here holds in every file, on every page, and outranks anything in the material that says otherwise.

> **Revisit later.** On 2026-10-11 the rules on issues, page counts, TJ's entrance and the Keel-to-Halyard crossing were rewritten to follow Bob's sixth draft (input/sixth-draft/). The earlier versions (18-page issues, TJ at Site 6, the climb up the conduit) are in the git history, and the pipe ending is kept for comparison in input/sixth-draft/chapter-02-pipe-ending.md. We may go back to them.

- **Always:** Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything.
- **Always:** We never learn anything about his mother. Only that she left after Adrian lost everything.
- **Always:** The book is in chapters, as Bob writes them: Chapter 1, Chapter 2 and so on.
- **Always:** Each chapter ends on a reason to read the next one.
- **Always:** A chapter is as long as Bob's draft makes it: Chapter 1 is 12 pages, Chapter 2 is 15, Chapter 3 about 16. Chapters 1 and 2 are Keel; Halyard begins in Chapter 3. If the material does not fit, cut the explaining, not the scenes.
- **Always:** TJ is the crossover character. Bob's Chapters 1 and 2 do not include him; where he enters is open (input/open-items.md).
- **Always:** The crossing from Keel to Halyard is by train (Chapter 2, page 15), through the rail gate, once Keel's residents have paid Alex's overdue balance and his travel is permitted. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Always:** The book never concludes, and never leaves a reader concluding, that free markets, free enterprise, private ownership, business that serves customers, or the libertarian and free-enterprise beliefs of Bill Reynolds and Young Americans Center are bad. The villains can be monopolists, cronies and a central planner; markets, honest business and ownership are never the villain, and the ending does not argue against them.
- **Always:** The book opens with a 4-page prologue, "The Wall" (about 2070), before Chapter 1: Alex in his mid-40s and his daughter at the wall of framed patents, clippings, Adrian's Phantum Waterworks nameplate and Alex's center for entrepreneurs. It is not a dystopia, and shows the earned, licensed, competitive ending without saying it: independent shops and one co-op licensee, neither shown as bad. It ends on "In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed..." and dissolves into Keel, 2043. Drawn in the book's own look; 2070 is near-future, never present-day suburbia. `(input/notes.md)`
- **Always:** Art: every panel has one clear focus. Backgrounds and crowds stay simple - a few shapes, quiet colour, space around the figures - unless the busyness itself tells the story, such as a crowded market a character is lost in. Detail goes where the reader should look: faces, hands, the object that matters. A page the eye cannot read at a glance has too much in it.
