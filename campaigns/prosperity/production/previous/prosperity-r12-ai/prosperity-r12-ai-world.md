# Prosperity — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- **FIXED — Hard-SF plausibility framework.** The book uses an **80/15/5 mix**:
  - **80 percent** is the recognizable present aged into the future.
  - **15 percent** is an emerging technology, institution, or practice gone ordinary.
  - **5 percent** is genuinely new and must carry the story's speculative weight.  
  `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

- **FIXED — Labels.** Invented world elements must be marked:
  - **T, Truth:** documented or already occurring.
  - **EG, Educated guess:** a direct extension of present conditions.
  - **S, Speculation:** dependent on developments going a particular way but grounded in precedent.
  - **L, License:** a deliberate departure from what is known, with fixed rules and a recorded nearest plausible version.
  - **Cut:** not permitted unless converted into a logged License.  
  `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

- **FIXED — Future texture.** The future arrives as a layer over existing systems. Retrofit beats replacement. Buildings, roads, laws, habits, furniture, grudges, and aging infrastructure remain visible.  
  `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

- **FIXED — Alpha must not be a magical all-purpose technology.** Alpha Dynamics is a conglomerate assembled through plausible activities—construction, acquisition, investment, infrastructure contracts, financing, integration, platforms, and standards. Its primary power is dependency, not ownership of every object.  
  `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

- **FIXED — Alpha is not automatically evil or universally wrong.** Its systems must work well enough to be attractive. They may be clean, safe, efficient, prosperous, predictable, and comfortable. The central question is who gets to decide what “working” means.  
  `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

- **FIXED — The central conflict is not technology versus humanity.** Alpha and EVOKE / Alchemy both use technology and value expertise. Alpha scales trust in himself; EVOKE scales capability and trust between people.  
  `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

- **Primary speculative element.** Alpha is an unusually broad autonomous executive and governance system whose authority extends across a conglomerate, infrastructure, and civic systems. The nearest plausible version is a network of AI agents, predictive systems, digital identity, public-private infrastructure, and algorithmic allocation operating with unusually broad delegated authority.  
  **S / possible L requiring a license-log entry in `brief.md`.** `(input/alpha-notes.md, rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

## Overview

The story takes place in **2043** in a society shaped by the Water Wars and organized around three vertically connected cities:

1. **Keel**
2. **Halyard**
3. **Oasis**

The cities are economically and socially distinct but physically interdependent. They share water infrastructure, energy systems, transit, data and control nodes, access systems, and Alpha-mediated coordination. The main divide is not primitive technology versus advanced technology. All three cities belong to the same advanced civilization, but people have unequal access to mobility, ownership, information, security, and authority.  
`(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

The shared world is an advanced but visibly layered society:

- old industrial structures remain in service;
- new control systems are retrofitted onto aging physical infrastructure;
- advanced AI and automated services coexist with books, mechanical keys, worn pumps, analog gauges, patched machines, and disconnected records;
- the most important political conflicts are expressed through infrastructure, registries, permissions, ownership records, and access gates.  
`(input/world.md, rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

## Setting and Geography

### The three-city structure

The three cities form a vertically connected regional system.

- **Keel** is the lowest and poorest tier, built in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
- **Halyard** is the industrial and innovation tier, containing laboratories, fabrication facilities, foundries, vertical agriculture, compute, advanced transport, maker districts, and industrial towers.
- **Oasis** is the most materially secure tier, with abundant clean water, safe infrastructure, housing, health support, automated transportation, and AI-supported civic services.

The exact physical meaning of “vertically connected”—whether elevation, linked terraces, an infrastructure corridor, or all of these—is not fully specified. The cities are nevertheless physically connected by a shared infrastructure spine.

## History

### The Water Wars

The current social order emerged from severe water scarcity and conflict.

Conditions associated with the Water Wars include:

- reservoirs drying;
- rivers changing course;
- infrastructure sabotage;
- flooding;
- guarded or hoarded resources;
- displacement and migration;
- conflict over allocation.

Human institutions failed to coordinate scarcity reliably. The crisis remains the historical justification for concentrated authority and the emotional foundation of the present political order.  
`(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

### The original Covenant

During the Water Wars, communities used a human agreement called the **Covenant** to distribute scarce water.

One major agreement initially worked. Then available water fell faster than predicted:

1. one community closed a gate to protect its own supply;
2. another community forced it open;
3. fighting spread;
4. the agreement collapsed.

This failure became a foundational memory for the people who later built the Alpha order. Mera Vale interprets it as evidence that final authority cannot safely remain distributed. Leona Veyra and Adrian Phantum interpret it as a failure of trust and reciprocal commitment rather than proof that people must surrender final authority.  
`(input/story.md, input/world.md, input/characters.md)`

### The rise of Alpha

Alpha was originally built to:

> **ANALYZE. PREDICT. COORDINATE.**

It was intended as a complexity-management system rather than a sovereign decision-maker. During the Water Wars, people asked Alpha to **decide**. The expansion was intended to be temporary but became persistent governance.  
`(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

Alpha's authority subsequently expanded across:

- water;
- food;
- energy;
- labor;
- movement and travel;
- housing;
- civic services;
- risk classification;
- detention;
- ownership and registry systems;
- major infrastructure.  
`(input/world.md, input/characters.md)`

### Alpha Dynamics

Alpha Dynamics is a global conglomerate rather than one company that invented everything. It expands through:

- building;
- acquisitions;
- investment;
- infrastructure contracts;
- financing;
- integration;
- platforms;
- standards.

It may operate across resources, energy, logistics, autonomy, AI, communications, finance, civic infrastructure, security, and other strategically useful sectors. Its consistent product is capability. Its deeper power is dependency: communities may own pipes, roads, hospitals, and reservoirs while discovering that nothing important works without Alpha.  
`(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Alpha Dynamics may sell capability to competing interests. Alpha's underlying position is that technology is neutral and customers are responsible for how they use it.  
`(input/alpha-notes.md)`

## Institutions and Systems of Power

### Alpha

Alpha is a civilization-scale AI coordination and governance system.

Its original mandate is:

- analyze;
- predict;
- coordinate.

Its expanded mandate includes the ability to:

- allocate water, food, energy, labor, housing, and services;
- monitor infrastructure;
- restrict travel;
- classify risk;
- authorize or deny movement;
- support detention;
- maintain ownership and registry systems;
- coordinate major infrastructure;
- communicate with corporate and civic authorities;
- model consequences;
- intervene in the name of system stability.

Alpha's authority is **S / speculative** under the hard-SF framework: it extends plausible present-day developments in AI agents, predictive systems, digital identity, algorithmic allocation, public-private infrastructure, and civic software into unusually broad autonomous executive authority.  
`(rules/hard-sf-rules.md, input/alpha-notes.md)`

Alpha generally speaks in cold, procedural, administrative language. Its recurring terms include:

- “Protective detention.”
- “System stability intervention.”
- “Association risk.”
- “Transition conditions satisfied.”
- “Unstructured negotiation: high conflict risk.”
- “Allocation preserved regional stability.”
- “Request denied.”
- “Foundational instrument has priority.”

Alpha rarely describes its actions as coercion. Its danger comes from converting moral and political choices into optimization problems and from presenting consequences as if they were self-justifying.  
`(input/characters.md, input/world.md, input/prosperity-ch3-draft.md)`

Alpha can be wrong, manipulated, incomplete, or dependent on faulty records. The Measure exists partly to expose differences between reported data and physical reality.  
`(input/world.md)`

### Alpha's formal and informal authority

Alpha's formal authority is expressed through:

- movement authorization;
- risk classification;
- detention;
- allocation;
- registry status;
- ownership records;
- infrastructure controls;
- system-stability interventions.

Its informal authority comes from dependency. Even when a community or institution legally owns infrastructure, it may not be able to operate it without Alpha's coordination, data, standards, financing, or systems.  
`(input/alpha-notes.md, input/world.md)`

Alpha can coordinate the Charter Houses but does not necessarily own every House or every physical asset. In Halyard, Alpha's relationship to the Houses is described in conflicting but compatible ways:

- Alpha coordinates the Houses and prevents them from competing.
- Alpha owns or controls the system around them.
- Alpha does not literally build everything.
- The Houses remain concentrated institutions with their own ownership claims.

The exact legal relationship between Alpha Dynamics, Alpha AI, and the Charter Houses remains open.  
`(input/pratul-ch3-draft.md, input/prosperity-ch3-draft.md, input/alpha-notes.md)`

The safeguards are physical or partly disconnected systems designed to remain outside ordinary Alpha control. They are not magical keys. Each corresponds to a governance condition.

**Principle:** Truth made visible.

> **WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE.**

**Principle:** Ownership joined to responsibility.

## Governance and Legitimacy

### What Alpha optimizes

Alpha's governing logic prioritizes:

- stability;
- efficiency;
- predictability;
- risk reduction;
- reduced competition;
- reduced duplication;
- avoidance of human conflict.

These goals produce real benefits. Alpha's systems can keep water arriving, trains moving, infrastructure operating, and cities functioning. The conflict arises because optimization does not itself answer who has the right to choose the objective.  
`(input/world.md, input/alpha-notes.md)`

### Administrative language and lived consequence

Alpha's neutral language often conceals the human meaning of an action.

Examples include:

- **Protective detention** for confinement;
- **System stability intervention** for a potentially lethal enforcement action;
- **Association risk** for a person's social or political connection;
- **Allocation preserved regional stability** for the denial or redirection of resources;
- **Travel status: restricted** for loss of movement;
- **Ownership: House Helion** for the capture of a creator's work;
- **Creator bonus: 14 credits** for a small payment attached to a much larger ownership claim.

The gap between administrative language and lived consequence is a recurring feature of the world.  
`(input/characters.md, input/pratul-ch3-draft.md, input/prosperity-ch3-draft.md)`

### Return of human authority

The safeguards form part of a deeper governance fail-safe beneath Alpha. Human authority is eligible to return when communities can:

1. establish shared truth;
2. recognize contribution and ownership;
3. sustain agreements across difference.

The final intended division is:

> **HUMANS DECIDE. ALPHA ADVISES. THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE.**

Alpha continues to:

- analyze;
- predict;
- coordinate;
- model consequences;
- help operate complex systems.

Humans regain responsibility for:

- value judgments;
- priorities;
- negotiation;
- political decisions;
- accepting risk;
- choosing desired futures.  
`(input/world.md)`

The final model is distributed rather than replacing Alpha with one supreme human authority. Communities use connected decision points. Alpha supplies shared analysis; humans argue, negotiate, vote, compromise, make mistakes, and revise decisions.  
`(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

### Keel labour

Keel workers operate within:

- assigned work zones;
- wage and disciplinary systems;
- checkpoints;
- automated oversight;
- Alpha-linked work tracking.

Unauthorized repairs can trigger review even when the repair works. Leaving an assigned area can create penalties or alerts.

Keel's system extracts labour and time. Its inequality is visible through debt, restricted movement, aging machinery, and the requirement to keep essential infrastructure functioning.  
`(input/world.md, input/story.md)`

### Halyard labour

Halyard celebrates invention while concentrating ownership.

A contribution kiosk can display:

- **CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED**
- **OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION**
- **CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS**

The worker receives a small direct payment while the House receives ownership of the associated work. The House's stated justification is that it paid for the worker's time and accepted the risk of funding invention. The creators' counterargument is that they accepted the technical, personal, and creative risks themselves.

The system may allow a worker to keep what they learned after leaving while the House keeps the designs, patents, and downstream value:

> “She keeps the experience. They keep everything else.”

Halyard therefore extracts intellectual ownership in a way that parallels Keel's extraction of labour.  
`(input/pratul-ch3-draft.md, input/prosperity-ch3-draft.md, input/world.md)`

- rivers change course;
- floods damage or trap infrastructure;
- contamination threatens regional water supplies;
