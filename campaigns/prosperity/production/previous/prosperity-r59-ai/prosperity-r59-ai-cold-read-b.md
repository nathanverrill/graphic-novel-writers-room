# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read in one sitting, night-before-homework conditions. I did not want to open it. Pages 1–2
beat that out of me fast, and I resent them a little for it.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
Page 2. The debt screen. The whole world in one screen — inherited debt, missing father,
travel restricted, 28.7 years — and then "Happy birthday, Phantum." That's the moment the book
won me. Page 1's "Then don't approve it" / "Just notice it works" had already told me who Alex
is in two lines. The bookshelf workshop (pages 4–6) and B11 waking up — "Well. That took longer
than expected" — kept it moving. Page 13: "The survey says straight. The bolts say left." Page
15: the 62%/18% reveal. Page 16: "Then they see it." I read this issue straight through and
never once thought about my phone.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 3 is all setup and the banter is a little pleased with itself ("You look expensive." /
"It came with a twenty-eight-year warranty."), but it's short. Page 8, the control room — I
didn't know who I was looking at or why I was there. That's it. This issue moves.

### I was confused
- **Page 9, Ada's line: "You crossed a line for Tomas before you knew his name."** I flipped
  back a page convinced I'd skipped something. Tomas doesn't exist yet. The line-crossing for
  Tomas happens on page 10, after this conversation, and page 12 confirms it was their first
  meeting ("You stopped for a stranger"). This line is sitting in the wrong scene and it broke
  the book's spine for a full minute.
- **Page 10: TJ.** B11 unfolds a disassembled robot dog out of the tool bag and starts
  assembling it. Where did the dog come from? I re-read the workshop scenes (pages 5–6): no
  dog. Nobody ever says where TJ came from, ever. The dog is fun once he exists, but he
  materializes like a magic trick.
- **Page 17:** Ada appears inside a sealed chamber via "the miners' opened service route."
  What route? When? The side passage slammed shut on page 16. She just shows up.
- **Page 18:** Whose water is flooding the pipe they're climbing? The miners opened the
  *residential* regulator, which page 17 said was separate from the ascent conduit. So why is
  the conduit filling? I couldn't stage the climax in my head.
- **Page 18: "TOMAS PHANTUM-LINK — SIGNAL LOST."** Phantum?? Is Tomas related to Alex? Nothing
  before or after says so. Also, why is Alex's wristband linked to Tomas at all? This is a
  bombshell the book drops in a screen line and never picks up.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Alpha** — page 5 ("pre-Alpha pipes"), page 8 ("REPORT TO ALPHA"), page 12 ("never wired
  into Alpha"). The book says the name constantly and explains it on page 41. I guessed "the AI
  that runs everything" early and ran on the guess.
- **The looping equation** on the casino chip (page 2, matches the chalkboard page 5). Never
  explained.
- **Mera** — page 8. No name in dialogue, no motive, picks TRACK over REPORT. Confused but
  intrigued; I trusted it would pay off. (It does, but not until page 40.)
- **The Measure** — page 14, capitalized like I should know it. Its job is shown on page 15,
  so it gets away with it.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 9: "You crossed a line for Tomas before you knew his name." Three times. Still broken.
- Page 18: "TOMAS PHANTUM-LINK — SIGNAL LOST." Twice. Never resolved.
- Page 3: "It came with a twenty-eight-year warranty." Got it on the second beat; fine.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, B11, Grandmother, Tomas — all distinct. B11 has the best voice in the book
("Protective?" / "A versatile word."). Mera is a pair of hands and a glass of water; I couldn't
have told you one thing about her. Foreman-7, the guard, the bouncer — background, fine.

### I felt something
Page 2, the debt screen. Page 10: Alex steps over the yellow line and the screen docks him
40 credits in real time — that cost landing *while* he does the right thing got me. Page 16:
"Then they see it."

### I predicted
Dad left the robot and the route on purpose (yes). Mera is watching for someone, not hunting
(mostly yes). Alex sends the evidence (obvious — but the cost, sealing his own exit, I did not
see coming, and it's the best beat in the issue). Tomas survives; "signal lost" didn't scare me
for a second.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who inherits his father's debt finds his father's hidden machine and proves the official
water numbers are lies.

### Where I would have put it down
Honestly, nowhere in this issue — pages 1–2 had me and page 10 kept me. The only stumble is
page 9's Tomas line, and "did I miss a page?" is a bad feeling, but page 10's rescue pulled me
straight back.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, I'd open Issue 02 immediately — for the four people in the flooding pipe. The Tomas half
of the cliffhanger is noise: he was never in the pipe, he was back at Site 6, and I never
believed he was dead. Half real cliffhanger, half loud noise.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
Lock. Every Lock scene (pages 21–22, 29, 34). "Protected from what?" / "From being asked to
choose while frightened" is the best villain line in the book — polite, reasonable, and
completely rotten. Page 27: the first assembly fails at 11%, good. Page 30: "You counted the
record." Page 36: the Houses get production *and* the collaborators stay under review — no easy
win, and I respected it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 23–26, the four recruitment stops. By Suri's page I'd seen the pattern — one page, one
component, one wise line — and I started skimming. Page 31's four-panel chain ("Household scale
means less membrane area. / Less area means more cleaning. / More cleaning means local
maintenance. / Local maintenance means people need the parts.") — I read it like a worksheet.
Page 20's kiosk economics: I got the point in panel 1 and the page kept making it. This is the
issue where the book stops hiding that it's a lesson, and my class can smell that from a mile
away. "This was built to teach me" is a put-down smell.

### I was confused
- **Page 22:** Alex names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren before meeting any of them. The estate
  record on page 21 didn't list them. Where did he get the names?
- **Pages 27–28:** four pages of "these systems cannot share," then one filed sleeve takes it
  from 11% to 94%. Too easy. I noticed the convenience.
- **Page 35: the Seal.** It warms, it hums, "CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION REGISTERED." Registered
  *with whom, to what end?* Lina carried it for fifteen years waiting for someone who
  "understood what it was for" — and Alex qualifies by... naming people? It's a chosen-one
  artifact and I don't know what it does.
- **Page 36:** "BOUNDED SUMMONS ISSUED." Summons to where, under whose authority?

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The Houses** (Caldera, Vector, Orbital) — pages 19–20, logos and kiosks, never explained.
  I inferred "companies that own everything."
- **The Charter Compact** — page 21, never explained, never comes back.
- **Morrow & Sons** — page 20, "reopened." Reopened? Whose shop is this? It just exists as the
  good-business example, then returns on page 54 like I know it.
- **"Selected for scale"** — page 20. Selected by whom? Never answered.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 20: "The Houses were selected for scale." Selected by *who*? Read twice, no answer.
- Page 30: "For the registry-visible group." Jargon; had to parse it.
- Page 21: "Rights under dispute are not a wallet." Read twice because it's good.
- Page 22: "That's what he's selling." Took a second, then landed hard.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lock — vivid. Alex, Ada, B11 — still themselves. **Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren — no.** One page
each is not enough. By page 31's chain dialogue I was flipping back to remember who owned which
component. Suri and Jalen blur worst. Ren is "a welder" and that's all I kept. Their surnames
appear exactly once, on a tablet (page 26).

### I felt something
Page 29: "You said protection." / "I said protection was available." Cold and good. Page 30:
B11's "I counted the record and missed the people. That was my error." Page 36's non-victory.

### I predicted
Alex refuses Lock — never in doubt, so the signing pressure had no suspense for me; Lock's
dialogue carried it. The prototype works. The collaborators get named and punished anyway. And
by page 35 I'd clocked the object pattern — one capitalized Artifact per issue — so the Seal
felt like a piece being placed on a board.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Who gets to own what people make — and whether naming the makers matters more than who builds
it fastest.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 24, mid-montage, when I realized each new person gets exactly one page, one part and one
aphorism. That's where the issue stopped being a story and started being a list.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
"BOUNDED SUMMONS ISSUED" is paperwork as a cliffhanger. Issue 01 ended with water rising
around people I liked; this ends with a form being filed. I'd read Issue 03 because I'm
two-thirds in — momentum, not the hook.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
Pages 38–40, Ada and Leona — the best scene in the book, no contest. No quips, no screens,
just a wound. Page 44: "First, people stopped selling to him. Then people stopped standing near
him." Pages 45–46: Adrian's last recording, and Alex's verdict — "He was still a gambler." /
"And he still built something worth finding." Page 52: Tomas and the two gauges — "Paper says
sixty-two. Pipe says eighteen." Page 54: "Every day." / "Good."

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 47–51. The legacy node, the consequence panel, the public table, the protocol
registration — I read these the way I read terms and conditions. Page 51's full-width system
panel is a policy slide. I skimmed, and I only stopped skimming at page 52 because Tomas
showed up with a gauge.

### I was confused
- **Page 37: "VEYRA, A."** Ada is a Veyra. Lina is Lina VEY (page 26). Related? I waited the
  whole issue. Never said. Either it's a dangling thread or it reads like an error.
- **Page 41:** Adrian's Covenant band is suddenly inside B11's chest compartment. When did that
  get there? It's TJ all over again — an object that materializes when the plot needs it.
- **Page 41:** the Covenant, the Water War, the three custodians, "Alpha recorded 'later' as
  permission to continue." This is the explanation of the *entire world* and it arrives on page
  41 of 54. Oh — THAT'S what Alpha is. Why did I have to wait forty pages? The actual problem
  of the story is presented too late for me to be attached to it.
- **Page 45:** the scene cuts before Adrian's evacuation or death. Grandmother said "He's
  closer than you think" (page 4). The book never answers. Is he alive?
- **Page 52:** Tomas is alive, limping, no explanation of how he got out or what "signal lost"
  meant. Issue 01's cliffhanger is a retroactive fake-out.
- **Page 54:** the debt math. 418,607 down to 402,110 is 16,497 credits in "weeks," at 40
  credits a day. Somebody didn't do the math, and my class will, because the numbers are so
  specific they invite it.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The Covenant and the Water War** — page 41, explained there, in about four lines, forty
  pages after Alpha started running the plot.
- **What Mera actually is** — a custodian? An Oasis officer? Both? It assembles by page 44,
  but she's been on-page since page 8.
- **"CONVENOR / AUTHENTICATOR"** — page 47. "Convenor" is a word I had to sound out.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 41: "Alpha recorded 'later' as permission to continue." Twice — the good kind. It's the
  key to the whole book.
- Page 40: "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." Good kind
  of twice.
- Page 48: "Don't give me that much power." Good kind.
- Page 47: "HUMAN ROLE REQUIRED: CONVENOR / AUTHENTICATOR." Bad kind.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Ada becomes a deeper person here — but Issue 01 Ada (dry, quippy creditor) and Issue 03 Ada
(abandoned daughter) barely know each other; the new one is interesting, the join shows. Leona —
vivid in one scene. Mera — finally a person, thirty pages late. The four collaborators come
back at the table and I *still* couldn't tell Suri from Jalen without flipping.

### I felt something
Page 39: "You chose safety instead of me." Page 40: "Then don't call it care." Page 44: Mera's
confession. Page 46: the two "yes" answers. Page 49: "Then the refusal has a price." Page 54:
"Good." This issue has the most feeling in the book and also the most homework.

### I predicted
Alex refuses executive authority — never in doubt, so the temptation scene (page 48) had no
teeth; Ada's line saved it. Tomas alive — called it. The debt wouldn't clear — right, and I
respect the book for it. Adrian's fate — I predicted the book would answer. It doesn't.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Emergency power that nobody ever switched off, and a kid who gets offered the switch and
chooses a slower, public fix instead.

### Where I would have put it down
Pages 50–51, the committee-forming. I'm 50 pages in so I'd finish, but if a classmate asked
"where are you?" I'd say "the part where they register the protocol," and they'd laugh.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
The last page is quiet and I liked it — two people at an overlook, nothing personally resolved,
"Every day." / "Good." Do I need a next book? No — and not because it failed. It closed. The
loose threads (Adrian's fate, Mera's dark band, "Tomas Phantum-link," Vey/Veyra) feel like
threads, not hooks. If a next book about Adrian existed I'd notice it. I wouldn't pre-order it.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 51; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 51.** A full-width panel that reads "EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED WITHIN DEFINED
SCOPE / ALPHA ROLE: OPERATION / MONITORING / MAINTENANCE / ACCOUNTING / ALERTS" is not a comic
page; it's a slide from a civics presentation. Runner-up: page 31's four-panel logic chain.
Both are pages where the book stops pretending it isn't a lesson, and a room full of
16-year-olds who didn't choose this book will smell that instantly and call it AI-made
homework. The tragedy is that page 52 — "Paper says sixty-two. Pipe says eighteen." — proves
the book knows how to make the same argument with a human being and a gauge.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a mining town inherits his dad's giant debt on his birthday, finds his dad's hidden
robot behind a bookshelf, and proves the city is lying about how much water people actually
get. Then he gets offered control of the whole system and turns it down." Friend: "Does the
water get fixed?" Me: "There's a process now." Friend: "...huh."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A debt screen, a robot behind a bookshelf, and a number that doesn't match the
  pipe — I barely breathed.
- **Issue 02:** A polite man with a tablet threatens everyone while a team assembles one page
  at a time.
- **Issue 03:** The two best scenes in the book (a mother, a father's last room) — and then a
  committee forms.
