# Cold Read: Prosperity

I read it the way the reader file describes: 16 or 17, it's homework, it's the night before, and I opened it hoping it would be bad. I read all three issues straight through in one sitting. I only know what the pages showed and said. Character names come from balloon labels, which a printed page doesn't show. Where a name is never actually said or shown on the page, I've marked it.

---

## Issue 01: Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1.** The splash is fine. "Then don't approve it. / Just notice it works." gives me a kid who fixes things and doesn't take orders. That's a quick read on who he is. A bit smug, but okay.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** This is where the book got me. On his seventeenth birthday a screen hands him 418,607 credits of his dad's debt. He earns 40 a day, the projection says 28.7 years, his dad's whereabouts are "unrecorded", and then the guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum." I understood it straight away, and it's unfair in a way I actually felt. Best page in the issue.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The bouncer scene told me about the dad in four lines without a flashback. I liked it.
- **Pages 4–6.** Grandma with dementia, the book on the shelf, the shelf opens, a secret workshop, a robot under a sheet. I've seen the bookshelf door a hundred times, but it still works on me. B11's first line, "Well. That took longer than expected.", made me want to know him.
- **Page 7.** "I'm not everyone. I was in the room." Good. The chip/river exchange is good too.
- **Page 10, panel 3.** Alex steps over the line and the screen says WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR. I'd just watched him earn exactly that, so I knew what it cost. It landed.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** "Someone has to make this look like an accident." Tomas stays behind. I liked him right away.
- **Page 13.** "The survey says straight. The bolts say left." Clear and smart. "Protective? / A versatile word." made me smile.
- **Page 15, panel 3.** REPORTED 62%, DELIVERED 18%. Simple and clear: someone is stealing Keel's water. After a lot of fog, finally a concrete secret.
- **Page 16.** The choice is clear: leave unseen, or send the proof and lose the exit. He sends it. I understood the cost.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3.** The Ada banter goes on a beat too long. "We can skip the educational portion" is a writer being clever, not a person talking.
- **Page 8.** I skimmed the whole page. It's a woman I don't know, in a room I don't know, pressing a button labelled with a word I don't know.
- **Page 14, panel 3.** "Adrian hid the truth where everyone trusted the lie" sounds like a movie poster. I rolled my eyes.

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 3 / page 5, panel 3.** The "looping equation" on the chip matches the chalkboard. I don't know what it means, and the book never tells me.
- **Page 3.** Who is Ada? A friend, a dealer, a girlfriend? She never says her name and nobody else says it. I worked out she's a friend who lends him things.
- **Page 3 vs. page 9.** On page 3 she says "I didn't ask for today." On page 9 he says "I missed the first installment." When was it due? Then "the token is Ada's debt." So she borrowed money for him? I wasn't sure who owed what to whom.
- **Page 8.** "LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED / REPORT TO ALPHA / TRACK / INDEPENDENT STANDING REPORT: SIGNAL RECORDED." I don't know what a single line of that screen means. This is confused and detached, not confused and intrigued.
- **Page 9, panel 4.** "You crossed a line for Tomas before you knew his name." Alex hasn't met Tomas yet. That happens on the next page. I flipped back to check whether I'd missed a page. That's the worst confusion in the issue. It made me think the book didn't know its own order.
- **Page 9, panel 2.** Why is his shift reassigned to Site 6? Is someone setting him up? Did Mera do it? It's never followed up.
- **Page 10, panel 4 / page 11, panel 1.** TJ comes out of a tool bag in pieces. I didn't know there was a second robot. The head stays outside the gate while the legs and body crawl separately. I couldn't picture it. "That dog yours?" was the first moment I understood it was a dog.
- **Pages 14–16.** The brass gadget is never named on these pages. I just called it "the thing from under the gauge."
- **Page 16 → page 17.** He says sending the proof means going back through Tomas's door. Then he doesn't. A route opens because the control-room woman flips a valve, and Ada is suddenly in the mine. How did she get there? "You took the impossible way. / You took the long way." I read that twice and still don't know which way either of them took.
- **Page 18, panel 1.** The miners open the regulator and water floods the conduit behind our people. Did the miners nearly drown them? Is that what killed Tomas? Was it on purpose?
- **Page 18, panel 3.** "TOMAS PHANTUM-LINK." Is Tomas related to Alex? Phantum is Alex's last name. I spent the gap between issues thinking Tomas might be an uncle.

### Lines I read twice
- p2: "It changed something. / For him? / For everybody else." I got it on the second read.
- p8: "INDEPENDENT STANDING REPORT: SIGNAL RECORDED". Gave up.
- p9: "You crossed a line for Tomas before you knew his name." Read it three times. I think it's out of order.
- p14: "Adrian hid the truth where everyone trusted the lie."
- p15: "Trace shows where. Not why."
- p15: "One route leaves unseen. One sends the evidence to Keel."
- p17: "You took the impossible way. / You took the long way." Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, B11, Grandma and Tomas were all clear and distinct. Ada was clear by attitude, but as a no-name person. The supervisor, the guard and the bouncer were fine as walk-ons. The control-room woman is just a back and a hand. Foreman-7: human or robot, I couldn't tell.

### I felt something
- p2, the debt screen on his birthday.
- p10, losing the day's pay to save someone.
- p12, Tomas staying behind.
- p18, "SIGNAL LOST." A small drop in my stomach. I'd known him for three pages, but he was the best of them.

### I predicted
- When the shelf moved, I knew there'd be a workshop.
- I guessed Dad is alive somewhere (Grandma's "He's closer than you think"). See issue 3 for how that went.
- On p16 I knew he'd send the proof.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his gambler dad's debt finds out the town's water is being stolen and proves it.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 8.** The control-room page is where I went to my phone. The page 9 Tomas mix-up nearly kept me there. Page 10 (the accident) pulled me back.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mostly yes. The cliffhanger is real-ish: I want to know if Tomas is dead, and I like him. But it's muddy. I don't know who flooded the tunnel or why, and "Phantum-link" made me think I'd missed something. I'd read the next issue tonight, but because of Tomas, not because of the water plot.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 8; next issue: yes

## Issue 02: Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19.** "Seeing isn't the same as changing." Honest, and I liked that the reveal didn't magically fix anything.
- **Page 20, panel 1.** CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS. A girl makes a thing and a company owns it. Clear and quick.
- **Page 21.** 312 patents. Okay, the dad was a genius. Lock offering to clear the debt tonight is a real temptation, because I remember that number from page 2. "Protected from being asked to choose while frightened" is creepy in a good way. Lock is the most interesting new person.
- **Page 29.** "You said protection. / I said protection was available." Good villain line.
- **Page 32.** The water comes out cloudy. "Not a city. / Not yet. A household." I believed that more than the 94%.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 20, panels 3–4.** The Morrow & Sons screen that says COST / WORKER WAGE / SHOP MARGIN, then B11: "One business earns customers. The Houses were selected for scale." This is where I felt the book teaching me something. It read like an economics worksheet with a robot on it. Openly: this is where I started suspecting it was written to be assigned.
- **Pages 23–26.** Four people in a row each hand over a part and say one wise thing about maintenance. It plays like a video game fetch quest. By Ren I was skimming the dialogue and just looking at the objects.
- **Page 31, panel 2.** "Household scale means less membrane area. / Less area means more cleaning. / More cleaning means local maintenance. / Local maintenance means people need the parts." I skimmed straight down it. That's a slide, not four people talking.
- **Page 33.** I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 19 onward.** Where is Tomas? He maybe died at the end of issue 1, and this whole issue never mentions him. Nobody asks, nobody grieves. I kept waiting. It made the cliffhanger feel like a trick after the fact.
- **Page 21.** "Charter Compact." Never explained. Is it the Houses? The government?
- **Page 22, panel 3.** "Including Lina. Suri. Jalen. Ren." Who? Alex has never met these people. How does he know their names? Did he read them off the registry? I had no idea and lost the thread for two pages.
- **Page 23, panel 2.** "This is not the Keel routing failure... This is what's in a different feeder." So there are two water problems now? Stolen water and dirty water? I needed one problem and got a second without warning.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** "Five people. Two machines. The patrol will classify six active bodies." 5+2 is 7. I did the math twice. I think TJ doesn't count? The joke dies while you count.
- **Page 30, panel 4.** B11 asks "What would you do?" and the next page doesn't answer. It cuts to engineering.
- **Page 33.** 94% on p28, then 91% here. Did it get worse? Is that bad?
- **Page 35.** Lina's medallion "warms" and HUMs. Is it magic? Tech? What does "CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION REGISTERED" actually do for anyone?
- **Page 36, panel 1.** HOUSE PRODUCTION AUTHORIZED / CONTRIBUTOR ACCESS REVIEW PENDING. So did they win or lose? The Houses get to make it anyway, and his friends still get punished? I genuinely couldn't tell if this issue ended in victory.
- **Page 36, panels 2–4.** "Covenant band," "TRACK / SUMMON," "ESCALATION: UNAVAILABLE UNDER STANDING SCOPE," "BOUNDED SUMMONS ISSUED." Same problem as page 8, but worse because it's the ending.
- **Who's in the room.** The kid didn't sign on p22. What exactly is the deadline Lock keeps mentioning (p26)? It never seemed to arrive.

### Lines I read twice
- p20: "The Houses were selected for scale." Selected by who?
- p20: "In Halyard, it often gets mistaken for proof."
- p21: "Rights under dispute are not a wallet."
- p23: "This is what's in a different feeder."
- p25: "The patrol will classify six active bodies." Did the math, still lost.
- p27: "Not when nothing is allowed to share."
- p28: "The parts aren't wrong. The edges are." Fine on the second read.
- p36: "ESCALATION: UNAVAILABLE UNDER STANDING SCOPE." Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, B11 and Lock were clear. TJ is clear as "the dog". Suri stands out a bit (the living filter, "Don't make it disposable", and she blocks his hand). Jalen is the nervous one with three minutes. Lina and Ren blurred together: both hand over a part and say a thing about systems. If you covered the names on p31 I couldn't tell you who said which line, and I don't think it would matter. The three House reps on p34 are furniture. They all say versions of the same idea, which is why they blur.

### I felt something
- p21, a little: the debt could vanish tonight and he doesn't take it.
- p30, a little: B11 admits he was wrong ("I counted the record and missed the people"). It's a nice moment for the robot, but it's also a stated moral, so it half-landed.
- p34, not much. He says their full names like it should be a big moment, but I'd known them for one page each.

### I predicted
- From p21 I knew he'd refuse to sign.
- I knew the first prototype would fail and the kid would fix it with junk. He did, in one page, by filing a sleeve. Too easy: 11% to 94% in a panel.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid refuses to sell his dad's inventions to big companies and builds a water purifier with his dad's old coworkers instead, so nobody owns it.

(That's a different book from issue 1. Issue 1 was "someone is stealing the water." Issue 2 is "who owns ideas." The stolen water mostly disappears for 18 pages.)

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 24.** By the second "here is a piece Adrian left me" I knew the pattern of the next two pages, and nobody had mentioned Tomas.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The last page is the control-room woman pressing SUMMON. I don't know her name, I've never heard her speak, and I don't know what a "bounded summons" is. Nobody I care about is in danger. The person I did care about (Tomas) has been forgotten. It's a loud button with nobody standing behind it. I'd only open issue 3 because it's assigned.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: no

## Issue 03: Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 37, panel 2.** VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND. "It knew my name." Ada is *from* the rich city? That's the first surprise in the book I didn't see coming. It woke me up.
- **Page 38.** "I stopped asking whether that meant dead or unwilling." Good line, and sad.
- **Page 38, panel 3.** "I can come in. / I know." That's the most real the two of them feel.
- **Page 39.** This is the best scene in the book. "Within a year. / And you didn't come." "You chose safety instead of me." Short and clear, and it hurt a little.
- **Page 44.** First people stopped selling to him, then people stopped standing near him. The system slowly erasing a person is the scariest idea in the whole book, and I wanted more of it.
- **Page 45.** The flooding flashback: "Alex. What you do with this is up to you." I felt that.
- **Page 46.** "He was still a gambler. / Yes. / And he still built something worth finding. / Also yes." I liked B11 a lot here.
- **Page 52.** "Paper says sixty-two. / Pipe says eighteen." A great callback to issue 1, and the clearest use of the "system" stuff in the book.
- **Page 54, last panel.** "You still thinking about leaving? / Every day. / Good." Quiet, and kind of good. I didn't hate the ending.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 41, panels 3–4.** Finally an explanation (the Water War, three custodians, two votes), and it's dumped in two panels of dialogue on page 41 of 54. I had to slow down to catch up on stuff the book had been using since page 8.
- **Pages 47–51.** I skimmed most of this. CONVENOR / AUTHENTICATOR, DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL, and the full-width screen on p51 with five lines of system text. That's a page of a terms-of-service agreement. I read none of p51 properly.
- **Page 50.** Three nameless people I've never met represent three cities. I didn't care who they were.

### I was confused
- **Page 40, panel 3.** The control-room woman walks in and tells Ada "I promised your mother I would keep you safe." Her mother is standing right there. Is Leona not her mother? Is there another mother? I read it three times. I think it means she promised Leona, but it reads like a plot twist that never comes.
- **Who is the control-room woman?** Nobody ever says her name, in the whole book. She was a hand on a button for two issues, and now she's apparently been secretly watching Ada for years. Why? What's her job? I only get "I gave it authority to move faster than I could stop" (p44). That's interesting but too late.
- **Page 40, panel 1.** "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." What were the terrible days? I never learned.
- **Page 41.** Adrian's band has been inside B11 the whole time? Nobody mentioned it once in 40 pages.
- **Alpha.** On page 41 I finally get that Alpha is some AI or system that kept emergency powers forever. That idea is the whole book, and I learned it at page 41.
- **Page 45, panel 4.** Is Dad dead or not? I get that the book is leaving it open on purpose, but then what did Grandma's "He's closer than you think" (p4) mean? Was it just this room? It felt dropped.
- **Page 46, panel 2.** "Adrian had a prearranged route for my dormant body." So B11 walked himself from Oasis to the Keel workshop while asleep? How?
- **Page 48.** She offers him total power and he almost takes it. Why does the control-room woman want him to? Is it a test? A trap?
- **Page 49.** He refuses, and the screen says WORKSHOP: DISPUTED, TRAVEL CLEARANCE: EXPIRING. So refusing to be king costs him his dad's workshop? I couldn't follow why those things are connected.
- **Page 52.** Tomas is alive! And... that's it? No reunion with Alex, no "how did you survive the flood." He just shows up at a gauge. Two issues of wondering, and it gets a panel caption.
- **Page 54, panel 3.** The debt went from 418,607 to 402,110. That's 16,000 credits paid off. He earns 40 a day, and he's paying Ada too. Where did that money come from? I noticed because the book trained me to watch that number on page 2.
- **Page 54, last panel.** "Ada touches the rivet on her strap." What rivet? I'd never noticed it.

### Lines I read twice
- p40: "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't."
- p40: "I promised your mother I would keep you safe." I read this one three times.
- p40: "Watching is not knowing."
- p41: "Alpha recorded 'later' as permission to continue." I got it on the second read, and liked it.
- p47: "I convene it. I don't own it."
- p50: "The proposal is public. The obligation is public. The people affected can challenge it."
- p51: the whole screen. Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Ada was clearest here, finally a full person. Leona was clear. Alex, B11 and Tomas were clear. The control-room woman and Leona: two older Oasis women, both with bands, both with guilt about Ada, both "chose" something. In the p40 three-shot I briefly mixed up who was who. The Keel gauge worker, Halyard rep, Oasis clerk and House rep were all interchangeable. Grandma never appears again, and I noticed.

### I felt something
- p39, Ada and her mom. The real one.
- p45, Dad's recording.
- p54, "Every day. / Good." A small, honest feeling.
- p52, Tomas alive: relief, then annoyance that it was thrown away.

### I predicted
- From p48 I knew he'd refuse the power.
- I knew the House would try to fake the numbers in the first review, and that someone would catch it with a real gauge.
- I did not predict Ada being from Oasis.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid gets the chance to fix his broken world by taking total control, and instead turns it over to a slow public process where anyone can check the numbers.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 51.** It's a full-width panel of system text about rescinded authority and unchanged records, and no person does anything.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the last issue. Would I buy a next book? No. The ending is honest: the debt is still huge, the workshop is sealed, the fight is "slow but visible." I respect it, and I liked the last three lines. But that's an ending you nod at, not one that makes you want more. I'd want a book about Ada and her mom, or about how Alpha erased Adrian. This book spent its time on the parts list and the review board instead.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 51; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 51.** Or, if a teacher made us stop earlier, **page 8**, because that's where half of us would have decided the book was going to be screens full of words we're not meant to understand. Page 51 is the same thing at the climax. Five lines of system text are the payoff of the whole story, and nobody's face is in the panel. Anyone already sure an AI wrote this book would point at page 51 (and the "X means Y means Z" chain on page 31) as proof.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dad's gambling debt in a mining town, finds a robot and a dog in a secret room, proves the rich cities are stealing the water, and then, uh, sets up a committee. The first issue and the scene with Ada's mom are good. The rest is kind of a lesson."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A strong hook (the debt, the robot, the stolen water) that got muddy at the end, and made me care about Tomas just before it lost him.
- **Issue 02:** A parts-collecting trip with four people I couldn't tell apart, teaching me about ownership, while nobody remembered Tomas.
- **Issue 03:** One great mother-daughter scene and a quiet, honest last page, buried under the review-board screens.
