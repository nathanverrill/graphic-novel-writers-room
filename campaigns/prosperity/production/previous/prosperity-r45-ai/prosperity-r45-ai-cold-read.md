# Cold Read: Prosperity (Issues 01–03, pages 1–54)

I read it the way I was told to: homework, the night before, wanting it to be bad. I read all three issues in order, in one sitting, and only once. Where something worked on me I've said so. Where it didn't, I've said that too.

---

## Issue 01: Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 3.** The debt screen got me. "TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY", then 418,607 credits, then "EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS", then "PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS". Then the guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum." Getting your dad's debt as a birthday present is a real hook. I could do the math myself (40 a day against 400,000) and that made me angry for him without anyone telling me to be. I didn't go back to my phone here.
- **Page 2, panels 1–2.** The pump scene is good. He fixed it the wrong way, it works, the official gauge says it's broken. In two panels I knew who this kid is.
- **Page 3, panel 1.** "It came with a twenty-eight-year warranty." Funny. Sounds like a person.
- **Page 4, panel 4 to page 5, panel 4.** Grandma. "Before he went to work." is creepy in a good way. The bookshelf click is a cliché, but I liked it anyway.
- **Page 7, panel 4.** "Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't." / "...I thought about it." Best exchange in the issue. The robot is funny without trying too hard.
- **Page 16, panel 3.** "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." That woke me up.
- **Page 17, panel 2.** "The bolts say left." I liked that he figured something out from looking at real stuff, not from a screen.
- **Page 18, panels 1–3.** REPORTED 62% / DELIVERED 18%. I understood instantly that they're stealing Keel's water. The choice between a secret way out for him and showing everybody was clear, and I could feel it cost him something. Best page in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 10, panel 3 to page 11.** The history lesson. A hologram shows "Water crises, drought, floods, sabotage…" and then the robot explains three magic objects in a row. I skimmed page 11. It felt like the part of a game where it tells you the controls. "The Seal makes ownership answer to contribution": I didn't care yet, because I hadn't seen anybody's ownership get taken.
- **Page 12.** I skimmed the casino stuff. "The Last Chance" shows up as a place I'm supposed to recognize.
- **Page 13, panel 4.** I had to stop and do the math on the credits, and then I still didn't get the point (see confused).

### I was confused
- **Page 4, panels 3–4.** They're in the apartment, and then they're "walking home." Wasn't he already home? And how does Ada know anything about Adrian ("He wouldn't just leave")? Alex was two. Is she older? Did her family know him? Nothing tells me.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." Who was right? Adrian? About what? Adrian is the one who left.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** "Grandma said he wasn't gambling." She never says that anywhere I read. Then: "She's half right. Both were true." Half right and both true at once? I gave up on that line.
- **Page 13, panel 2.** Ada annotates Adrian's map "from memory." Whose memory? How does she know his plans?
- **Page 13, panel 4.** What is the travel token for? Why does it matter that nobody scans it? He doesn't buy it and it never comes up again. And then Grandma's medicine is in his tool bag. Why is it in his bag if he bought it for her?
- **Page 14, panel 4.** "Your plan needed you forgettable." What plan? Nobody told me a plan. I know he's going to Site 6. I don't know what he's supposed to do there or how he gets out after. This is the "we know he has a map and the mine is sealed, but what's he trying to do?" problem, exactly.
- **Page 15, panel 4.** Another key? He already has a key from the workshop (page 6). Are there two?
- **Page 16, panel 2.** "Today? Another small act of resistance." Another? Has Tomas been resisting? It sounds like a slogan, not a guy talking.
- **Page 18, panel 3.** Where did the Measure come from? On page 11 it was a hologram. Now he's clamping a real one to a pipe. Did he bring it? (Page 19 says it's in the robot's chest. I didn't know that when I read 18.)
- **Page 18, panel 4. This is the big one.** Ada is suddenly in the mine. She was never with him. She wasn't at Site 6, she didn't go through Tomas's door, and now she's running up a pipe with him. And who is TJ? A robot dog "newly assembled", assembled by whom, out of what, when? I flipped back looking for it and it isn't there. A new character shows up in the last panel with no introduction.
- Where are they going? The water fills the pipe behind them. Is that the miners turning the wheel? Are the miners drowning them by accident? Is Tomas okay? I don't know if the big moment helped Keel or not.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." (p3) Gave up.
- "Everything people need to survive." / "And nearly everything they need to choose their own future." (p10) Read twice, still not sure what "it" is.
- "None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." (p11) Riddle.
- "She's half right. Both were true." (p12) Gave up.
- "Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it." (p13) I got it on the second read: a trap? Or a hint?
- "Sealed isn't the same as gone." (p9) Fine. It just sounds like a movie trailer.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Grandma and Bi11bot are all clear and distinct. Bi11bot has the best voice in the book.
- Ada: I can tell her apart, but she mostly talks in wise sentences ("That's not the same as nobody trying"). She feels more like a conscience than a friend.
- Tomas: great for two pages, then gone.
- Foreman-7, the supervisor, the guard: blur together.
- The glass-of-water woman is recognizable by her glass and cuff, which is nice. But she has no name for me.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the birthday debt. Real anger.
- Page 7, "...I thought about it." A small smile.
- Page 18, pressing VERIFY. I actually wanted him to send it, and I felt the loss when he did.
- Page 8: "Especially me." A little bit.

### I predicted
- That Adrian is alive somewhere in Oasis ("He's closer than you think"). I believed this for two whole issues.
- That the glass woman is the villain, or at least the one pulling strings.
- That Site 6 was a trap set by the glass woman (she moved his tile). That's half confirmed, I think, but never really paid off.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his missing dad's huge debt, finds his dad's secret robot and lab, and learns the system running the cities is stealing Keel's water.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 11.** Three objects, three definitions, nobody doing anything. If I were flipping through it in a shop, this is the page it goes back on the shelf.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Kind of. The water reveal on page 18 earned it. But the actual last panel is a trick. It's loud: water rushes in, "Keep moving!". The people in it are Ada, who wasn't there, and a dog I've never met. The question I actually cared about (did the miners get their water, is Tomas okay, is Grandma okay with Alex gone) isn't the one being asked. I'd read issue 2 tomorrow, not tonight.

## Issue 02: Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19.** Quick, funny arrival. "Still disagreeable." / "Halyard may consider that a distinction." The robot is still carrying the book.
- **Page 20, panel 3.** "She made it. The House owns it?" / "She keeps the experience. They keep everything else." I got the problem of this city in one panel. That's a real thing people complain about, and it landed.
- **Page 21, panel 2.** 312 PATENTS. "You said your father left you nothing." / "He left me debt." Good flip.
- **Page 27, panel 1.** "Adrian never understood manufacturing tolerances." / "Or microbial balance." / "Or network security." / "He was, however, excellent at finding people who did." That's the first time Adrian felt like a real person with flaws. I liked him here.
- **Page 29, panel 4.** "Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Funny.
- **Page 36, panel 4.** The silhouette with Ada's hair in the black name field. Creepy, and it made me want to know.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 22–23.** Two pages of saying the same thing. "They were never his alone." "He built them with others." "The people who built them with him." "These people co-authored the patents." I got it the first time. I skimmed page 23.
- **Page 30.** Citywide contribution map, 71.3%, "stole its purpose." Skimmed.
- **Page 31.** Lock watches screens. Nothing happens to a person.
- **Page 33.** The full-page montage caption. "Some stayed with the Houses. Some became partners. Some walked out and began again." This reads like the end of a social studies chapter. Honestly, this is the kind of paragraph that makes people in my class say "AI wrote this." I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 20, caption.** "From the train, it looked like that might even be true." What train? They came out of a storm drain one page ago. And whose voice is this caption? Nobody narrated issue 1.
- **Page 21, panels 3–5. I have no idea what is happening here.** Alex says "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." Then Lock arrives and Alex says "I didn't know I was one." One what? Then "In exchange for what?" In exchange for what Lock said, except Lock says nothing. I'm reading half a conversation. I went back twice looking for Lock's balloons.
- **Page 22, panel 1.** Where are we? Suddenly at a "worktable." Whose room? Did they go with Lock? Escape him?
- **Page 22, panel 3. TOMAS REED.** Is this the Tomas from the mine? The one who oiled the door for fifteen years? I honestly thought it was, and that he'd somehow ended up in Halyard. Then on page 27 he's a fabrication guy who doesn't seem to know Alex. Two Tomases with nothing to tell them apart broke my brain for a while.
- **Page 23, panel 3.** "Names dim or vanish…" / "That doesn't sound like my father." What doesn't? The names vanishing? He didn't erase them. The line doesn't answer anything.
- **Page 23, panel 4.** "Then we start with the one who still wants to be found." Which one? How do they know? The next page is Lina, so I guess her, but nothing showed me why.
- **Page 24, panel 1.** Lina: "Adrian Phantum's son." Bi11bot: "I corrected him. Frequently." Alex: "You knew him?" Bi11bot: "Nearly continuously." I don't know who is talking to whom. Did Alex ask Lina and the robot answered? Did the robot "correct" Alex, or Adrian?
- **Page 24, panel 2.** Ada: "You think he left this to you? No. He left it to all of us." Who is she saying this to, and why now? Nobody said he left it to Alex.
- **Page 25, panel 1.** Bi11bot: "Adrian made each of us keep one piece." Us? The robot kept a piece? Or is that Lina's line? It reads like the robot is one of the collaborators.
- **Page 25, panel 2.** "I've been waiting twenty years." Alex is 17. Adrian's been gone about 15. Twenty years of what?
- **Page 25, panel 4.** TJ finds a "hidden physical archive." What's in it? It's never mentioned again.
- **Page 26.** Alpha tells Lock "18.4 percent." Reconstruction of what? Then "ownership classifications may become contestable." Lock says nothing. I don't know what Lock wants or what he's going to do.
- **Page 27, panel 2.** One page ago they were finding the collaborators. Now they're all at a table and Alex has invented a distributed water network. And on page 28 "We did it." Did what? I never saw them build anything, struggle, fail, or get chased. It just happened.
- **Page 28.** The worst page in this issue for following along. "And them?" Who? A contract appears. I never heard the offer. Tomas Reed: "We accepted the risk." What risk? "And if I don't sign?" Lock answers… nothing. Panel 3 shows the debt going to zero, so I worked out that Lock is offering to clear his debt. But I had to piece that together from a picture. Nobody said it.
- **Page 29.** He refuses. Then on **page 32** he makes the same choice again with a big button. Didn't he already decide?
- **Page 30, panel 1.** "Someone hid it here?" / "No. Someone connected it to the registry." Isn't that the same thing? What's the difference I'm supposed to care about?
- **Page 30, panels 3–4.** "It remembers everyone." / "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." So it only remembers the forgotten people? That sounds deep but I don't know what it means.
- **Page 31, panel 2.** "Request denied." What request? Did Lock ask for something? We never see him ask.
- **Page 31, caption.** "Lock realizes the system he trusted is no longer entirely under the Houses' control." The book just tells me what a character is thinking, flat out, like a note. It doesn't feel like a caption. It feels like the summary on the back.
- **Page 34.** "So did Mera." Who is Mera? First time I've seen the name. I had to guess it was the glass-of-water lady, and I'm only guessing because of the glass on page 35.
- **Page 35, panel 1.** "Not by possession." Then on page 38 the Seal just opens it. So what was the rule?
- **Page 35, panel 3.** How is Mera talking to them through a display? Has she been watching the whole time? Does Alex know who she is? He just says "Good."
- **Lina Vey vs. Ada Veyra.** When Lina showed up I thought, "Vey… Veyra… that's Ada's mom." I spent pages 24–35 waiting for that reveal. Then the mom turned out to be someone else entirely.

I'd read the next one, mostly to find out if the dad is alive.

## Issue 03: Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 38, panel 2.** "That's my mother." Ada's thumb stopping on the rivet. I noticed the rivet habit was paying off and I liked that.
- **Pages 39 and 41, Ada and Leona.** "You let me believe you were dead." / "I let you believe what kept you unmarked." And: "You protected me from knowing you." That last line is the best line in issue 3. It sounds like a real hurt person.
- **Page 43, panel 4.** "The offer is real." / "That's the problem." Great line the first time.
- **Page 44, panels 3–4.** "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" Ada finally gets to be smart through an action instead of a slogan. The room going quiet worked.
- **Page 45.** Elias releasing the Oasis water data: "You don't know what we'll do with it." / "No. That is why it counts." I understood that, and I bought it.
- **Pages 50–51.** Adrian's death. The gates closing, PUMP ACCESS: DENIED, VENTILATION ACCESS: DENIED, Mera's override REJECTED. This is the only scene in issue 3 where I wasn't reading a meeting. I felt it, even though I'd stopped expecting anything to hurt.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37 caption.** "It had not learned how to belong to one another." Skimmed. Also, that sentence is broken. A city can't belong "to one another."
- **Page 40.** The Water Wars flashback is fine but it's narrated one fact per panel. I already knew it from page 10.
- **Pages 46–49.** Meeting, signing, displays, displays, displays. Page 49 is four panels of slogans on screens: "WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC", "CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED"… I skimmed the whole page.
- **Pages 53–54.** "Humans decide. Alpha advises." "The Charter holds both accountable." "The future remained uncertain." "The decision did not belong to one person." This is where I'd hear someone in my class say "this is literally ChatGPT." It sounds like a lesson summary. I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 38, panel 3.** "You thought she was dead." Did we know that? Never mentioned. "I thought she left after she died." Is that a joke? A typo? Gave up.
- **Page 38, panel 1.** The record just opens. Page 35 said it couldn't be opened "by possession." What changed?
- **Page 41, panel 2.** "Mera called for emergency suspension. I agreed." Suspension of what? The Covenant? People voting? I think it means "let Alpha decide," but I'm guessing.
- **Page 41, panel 3.** "Mera suppressed your association risk." I sort of get it: Mera hid Ada from Alpha. So Mera protected Ada? Is Mera good? Bad? Both? By now I didn't know which side she was on.
- **Page 43.** Why does Mera want the Measure and the Seal? The Seal is plugged into the Halyard registry. Did they bring it?
- **Page 44.** Three brand-new people (Mara Doss, Frank Reed, Elias Renn) show up on page 44 of 54 and decide everything. Who picked them? Who called the meeting? "Provisional meeting room" whose?
- **MARA and MERA.** Two characters with almost the same name, in the same scenes. I mixed them up on page 46 and had to go back.
- **Frank REED.** Related to Tomas Reed? Related to Tomas from Keel? Now there are three people I'm trying to connect by surname.
- **Page 46.** "Keel can release emergency water for thirty days." Keel? Keel is the city with no water. The whole of issue 1 was Keel getting 18%. Keel is giving water to who?
- **Page 47, panel 4 / page 48.** Their actions "appear as marks" on the components and the clocks align. I understand that something unlocked. I don't understand how, or what actually changed in anyone's life except screens.
- **Page 49, panel 3.** "Without being granted property or citizenship." Is that good or bad? It felt like a footnote.
- **The Charter.** "The restored local compact was called the Charter." But in issue 2 the bad guys were "Charter Houses." So is the Charter good or bad? Same word for both.
- **Page 50, panel 1.** "B11 WITNESS RECORD VERIFIED / ADRIAN PHANTUM — TRANSFER COMPLETE." Transfer of what? To whom? It's on the screen as if I'd know.
- **Page 52.** Why is Alex Alpha's "authorized human successor"? Because he's Adrian's son? Because he used the three tools? It just announces it.
- **Page 53, panel 4.** "The empty center chair." What does it mean? Whose chair?
- **Page 54, panel 3.** "Leona's restriction" suspended. What restriction? I didn't know she had one, beyond giving up her "authority."
- **The ending.** Does Alex still owe 418,607 credits? He turned down the debt clearance twice (p28, p32) and declined the "cancel unlawful liability" on p52. Is he still travel-restricted? Where is he walking to? **Grandma never comes back.** The person who started the whole thing, who knew about the bookshelf, who said "He's closer than you think," is gone after issue 1. So is Tomas from Keel. So is Lock, who just disappears.
- **Page 51.** "Forty million people." First time I hear the scale. I pictured three small towns this whole time.

### Lines I read twice
- "It had not learned how to belong to one another." (p37)
- "I thought she left after she died." (p38) Gave up.
- "My silence for your protection." This one is said twice (p39, p41). The second time felt copy-pasted.
- "Trust begins when no one holds all the power." (p42) Not hard. It's a poster.
- "We could fix it and still make one person final." (p53) Read twice. "Make one person final"?
- "The Charter holds both accountable." (p53) Both who? Humans and Alpha?
- "What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?" (p50) I got it on the second read, but it's riddle voice at the moment his dad is dying.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Ada is strongest in this issue. She finally has her own story.
- Leona: distinct, cold, sad. Worked.
- Mera: finally has a face. Her glass trembling on page 51 is a good touch. I still wasn't sure if I was supposed to hate her.
- Mara / Mera: kept mixing them up.
- Frank and Elias: one fear each. Elias is the one I remember because of the data release.
- Alex: honestly, he fades. In issue 1 he was a mouthy kid who fixed pumps. In issue 3 he says lines like "Humans decide. Alpha advises." He stops sounding seventeen.
- Bi11bot gets one line in the whole climax ("It recorded what you chose."). I missed him.
- TJ: still nothing.

### I felt something
- Page 41: "You protected me from knowing you." Yes.
- Page 50–51: Adrian dying, Mera's override rejected. Yes. But Alex's whole reaction to finding out his dad was killed is "It killed him." That's it. Two words, and then a policy discussion. I felt more than Alex seemed to.
- Page 54: Ada taking Leona's hand "not yet forgiving her." A small yes.
- The meetings: no.

### I predicted
- That Adrian was alive. **Wrong.** This was the biggest surprise in the book and it actually landed on me. "He's closer than you think" turned out to mean his body is under Oasis, which is dark and good. But Grandma isn't there to hear it.
- That Alex would turn down Alpha's offer. Right. By this point I knew every big button would end with him picking the humble one. There were three buttons (p18, p32, p53) and he picks the same way every time.
- That Mera would turn out to be not fully evil. Right.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Three cities are run by an AI that decides for them, and a kid and his friend collect his dead father's tools to make people decide together again, even though it's messier.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 44.** Three strangers walk in to hold a meeting ten pages from the end. I'd already been through the mom reveal, and it seemed like the story was turning into a committee.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the end, so there's nothing to buy. But as a last page: it's two captions that tell me what the theme was. "The future remained uncertain." I didn't feel uncertain. I felt like I'd been told the moral. The open hand around the chip is a nice image, and it would have done the job without the captions. And I closed the book still wanting to know if his debt is gone and if Grandma is okay. Those were the first two things the book made me care about, and it never went back to them.

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 21.** Lock shows up, Alex answers lines nobody said ("I didn't know I was one." / "In exchange for what?"), and the villain never speaks. Page 11 bores people. Page 21 is where they decide they can't follow it and stop trying. Close second: **Issue 02, page 28**, the same problem again: a contract, an offer and a deal, all with no words from the person offering.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a mining town gets his missing dad's debt on his 17th birthday, finds a secret lab and a sarcastic robot, and finds out the AI running everything is stealing his town's water. The first issue's actually good. Then it turns into meetings."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** The best one: a birthday debt, a funny robot and a real water heist, until Ada and a robot dog teleport into the last panel.
- **Issue 02:** A city I understood in one panel and a plot I couldn't follow, run by a villain who never says a word and a guy named Tomas I thought was the other Tomas.
- **Issue 03:** One great mother-daughter fight and one genuinely awful death, buried under a committee meeting and a stack of slogans on screens.
