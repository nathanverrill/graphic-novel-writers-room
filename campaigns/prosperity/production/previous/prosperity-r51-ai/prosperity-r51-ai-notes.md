## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. Page 46 layout is for the wrong scene

- **Location:** `layouts.md`, Page 46; `thumbnails.md`, Page 46; `script.md`, Issue 03 Page 46.
- **Observation:** The script’s Page 46 is Mera leading Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot into Adrian’s unconnected monitoring station beneath Oasis. The approved action is the empty station, sealed bulkhead, dead terminals, and Bi11bot recognizing the projector. The layout instead depicts Leona’s apartment, Mera’s association-risk confession, and Ada confronting Leona.
- **Problem:** This is not a staging variation; it replaces the scripted page with a different scene and duplicates material from Pages 41–42. The page turn, character knowledge, location continuity, and Adrian-death sequence all break.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Layout Agent: discard the current Page 46 layout and thumbnail. Rebuild the page from the script: one Oasis subterranean monitoring station, no visible Alpha nodes, Mera, Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot only, with the sealed bulkhead and old projector as the dominant visual anchors. Letterer: remove the apartment dialogue and letter the Page 46 script instead.
- **Role:** Layout, Letterer.

#### 2. TJ does not perform his fixed Site 6 action

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Pages 14–18, especially Page 17 Panel 4; `facts.md`, TJ; `characters.md`, TJ.
- **Observation:** The fixed sequence requires TJ to be introduced early at Site 6; his head watches the biometric gate; he separates his legs and body segments, enters a jammed mechanism, and releases the support pinning Tomas, giving Alex the opening to rescue Tomas and patch the ventilation line. The script keeps Bi11bot in the tool bag through the rescue, introduces TJ only after Alex and Tomas have already reached the old-works junction, and never shows TJ releasing the support.
- **Problem:** This contradicts fixed character/action canon and removes TJ’s first consequential contribution. It also weakens causality: Alex rescues Tomas before the robot dog has done the action that canonically makes the rescue possible.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: move Bi11bot’s magnetic assembly of TJ into the Site 6 rescue sequence before Alex reaches Tomas. Show TJ’s head watching the biometric gate, then his detached body segments entering the jammed hauling-frame mechanism and releasing the support. Alex must use that opening to free Tomas and patch the ventilation line. Preserve the page count by compressing the current separate-route/convergence material rather than adding pages.
- **Role:** Writer, Plotter.

### Major

#### 3. The Page 11 safeguard scene still gives the Seal an unsupported activation behavior

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 11, Panel 3; `facts.md`, The Maker’s Seal; `story.md`, Issue 01 safeguard proposal.
- **Observation:** Alex places the Measure on a pipe, touches the Maker’s Seal to the repair mark, and the Seal “records the worker’s name.” Bi11bot then says the objects make it harder to hide who saw, made, and owes a response.
- **Problem:** The scene risks making the Seal a generic touch-activated attribution scanner. Canon says the Seal supplies named contribution records and activates in response to multiple real contributors; it wakes only after Alex names Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren and refuses sole ownership. The current test also names a worker the reader has never met and does not show how that name becomes a meaningful contribution record.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: keep the injured worker and the administrative record, but make the Seal visibly inert in this scene. Let Alex identify the uncredited repair by name in dialogue or on the record while Bi11bot states that the Seal cannot establish a full contribution chain from possession or a single touch. The Measure may expose the physical discrepancy; the Seal should plant a limit, not perform its later payoff early.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 4. The causal distinction between the workers’ response and Mera’s conduit release is still too easy to misread

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 17 Panel 1; Page 18 Panels 1–4.
- **Observation:** Keel workers “force the regulator open” while Alex’s group reaches the conduit. Page 18 then shows Mera’s authorization reaching a conduit valve, followed by water filling behind the travelers. The script does not clearly identify which regulator the workers are forcing or which valve Mera controls.
- **Problem:** A reader can still conclude that the workers’ action caused the conduit flood, making Mera’s intervention decorative rather than causal. The fixed story requires the Measure’s public transmission to cause the Keel pipe to fill, with Mera separately authorizing or triggering the conduit release.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: distinguish the two physical systems in the panel descriptions and dialogue. Show Keel workers opening the residential distribution regulator at Site 6 while Mera’s remote authorization opens a separate conduit segment behind Alex. Use a clear visual match—different valve housings, locations, or labels—so the reader can track both actions without exposition.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 5. The Measure’s “private exit” is not visually established before Alex sacrifices it

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 18 Panel 3; Page 18 Panel 4.
- **Observation:** The Measure offers “a private unmapped exit” versus public broadcast, but the page does not show the exit’s physical route. The group then enters the conduit anyway, so the reader cannot tell what exactly was lost.
- **Problem:** Alex’s defining choice becomes abstract. The reader sees public transmission and a dangerous route, but not the concrete alternative he gave up.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer/Layout: show the private TRACE route as a visible dry tunnel or side passage leading toward an unmonitored exit, then show it sealing or becoming inaccessible after VERIFY. The conduit must remain the only route after the choice, making the cost legible before the water rises.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 6. Issue 02 opens with a location transition but not enough spatial orientation

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 02 Page 19; Page 20.
- **Observation:** Page 19 is a full-page splash of the group emerging from a Halyard storm drain. Page 20 immediately moves through a service corridor toward a public district and a contribution kiosk.
- **Problem:** The sequence establishes Halyard visually but does not establish how the group moves from the storm drain into a public-access area while under scrutiny. Because the group has restricted status and has just crossed through an illegal legacy route, the immediate change from soaked escapees to kiosk observers feels convenient.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Layout Agent/Writer: make Page 19 show one concrete exit condition—an unsecured maintenance hatch, a concealed service ladder, or a storm-drain access corridor opening onto the public edge of Halyard. On Page 20, retain one checkpoint or obstruction between the drain and kiosk so Ada’s route-reading and the group’s risk remain active.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout.

#### 7. Mera’s dual role remains under-planted

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 01 Page 12; Issue 03 Page 41; `facts.md`, Mera and Leona.
- **Observation:** Mera chooses TRACK on Alex’s legacy pulse, then later says she suppressed Ada’s association risk. The script does not show what Mera’s tracking order reports, what she withholds, or how Alpha continues to track beyond her authority.
- **Problem:** The reader can reasonably read Mera as either secretly protecting the protagonists or simply hunting them until the plot needs her confession. Her moral ambiguity becomes information ambiguity.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer: add one concrete distinction at Page 12 or Page 18: Mera’s TRACK action observes the safeguards and suppresses escalation, but the interface shows that Alpha retains an independent reporting channel. At Page 41, make her admission specifically about suppressing Ada’s association flag, not controlling all surveillance. Do not make Mera benevolent; make her limits and competing obligations visible.
- **Role:** Writer.

#### 8. The first public review is too compressed to prove the new protocol works

- **Location:** `script.md`, Issue 03 Pages 52–53; `brief.md`, Canon; `facts.md`, Civic administration and governance.
- **Observation:** Page 52 presents three representatives and resident testimony; Page 53 quickly supplies a temporary order, appeal status, water movement, Aqualis units, debt, ownership, and travel review.
- **Problem:** The ending contains the right ingredients but not enough visible process. The reader cannot tell which human disagreement produced the temporary order or how Alpha is executing rather than deciding.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter/Writer: give the review one legible dispute and one observable resolution. For example, show Keel’s representative demanding immediate flow, Halyard’s representative naming purification capacity as the constraint, and Oasis’s representative setting a reserve threshold; then show all three agreeing to the temporary, appealable order. Keep the debt and ownership displays as aftermath, not competing beats.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer.
