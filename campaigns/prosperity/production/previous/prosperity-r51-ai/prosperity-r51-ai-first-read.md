# First Read: Prosperity, Issue 01, pages 1–3 (Auditions A and B)

I read this as the assigned reader: 16, it's homework, it's the night before, and I want it to be bad. I read A first and B second. That means B was not a fully cold read. I already knew about the debt screen and the chip, so B's reveals hit me a little softer than they would have otherwise. I tried to give B the same patience anyway.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **Issue 01, page 1, the splash.** I got it before I'd read a word: rich people at the top, a giant pipe, and the pipe is a skinny rusted straw by the time it reaches the kid at the bottom. That's the whole world in one picture. I didn't need it explained.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** "That's not the approved repair." / "Then don't approve it." This is where I first liked him. He's cocky and good at his job, and he doesn't care.
- **Page 2, panel 3, the scanner screen.** This is the hook, and it's the only thing in the three pages that actually got me. The guard says "Happy birthday" and then the screen shows 418,607 credits, 40 credits earned today, 28.7 years. Getting your dad's debt on your 17th birthday is horrible, and I understood it instantly. I did the maths in my head, which means I was paying attention.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** His fist closes on "something unseen" in his pocket. It was a small tease, but it worked, and I turned the page to see what it was.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3, panel 2.** "They hit you with the balance?" / "Four hundred eighteen thousand. Plus interest." I just saw the screen one page ago. I skimmed this.
- **Page 3, panel 3.** I skimmed Ada's line about the mother because it felt like it was written for me and not for him (see below).

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The gate locks and he says "Keep it locked. I might run." Is he locked inside the pump hall now? Can he not go home? I think "travel restricted" means he can't leave the city, but the picture shows a locked gate at his work exit, so for a second I thought he was being held there. I also read the joke twice before I got it as sarcasm.
- **Page 3.** Who is Ada to him? She's 20 and she brings him tea. Is she a sister, a girlfriend, a neighbour? No idea. I wasn't intrigued by not knowing, just a bit detached.
- **Page 3, panel 2.** "My inheritance." Does he mean the chip or the debt? I liked not knowing at first. Then nothing came of it.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Keel | p1 (art only) | The name first shows up in dialogue on p3 ("getting out of Keel"). I worked out it's the bottom city. |
| Halyard | p1 (art only) | Never named. To me it's "the middle city." |
| Oasis | p1 (art only) | Never named. To me it's "the white towers." |
| Alpha / the white-and-amber node and scanner | p1, p2 | Never named. It's just "the scanner thing." |
| Adrian Phantum | p2 screen, "GUARANTOR" | I guessed he's the dad. Nobody says "dad," "father" or "my father." "Before I end up like him" on p3 is what told me. |
| Credits | p2 | Obvious enough. |
| The casino chip with an equation on it | p3 | No explanation. I'm guessing gambling. |
| "The official story" | p3 p4 | What story? That he ran. But ran from what, and why would anyone lie about it? |

### Lines I read twice
- "Keep it locked. I might run." (p2 p4)
- "Your mother left when he lost everything. But Adrian didn't just walk away." (p3 p3) I read it twice because I didn't believe a person would say it. She's telling him what happened to his own mother. He already knows that. It's the "as you know" thing, and I rolled my eyes.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex is clear: dry, tired, wants out.
- Ada is a delivery system. She shows up, explains his family to him, and asks the cliffhanger question. I couldn't tell you one thing about her as a person, except that she has teal eyes and rubs a rivet.
- The supervisor and the guard are fine as background.

### I felt something
- At the screen on page 2: a real little drop in my stomach. The "Happy birthday" right before it made it worse, in a good way.
- After that, nothing. By page 3 I was getting information, not feeling anything.

### I predicted
- Dad gambled everything away (the chip), or he didn't, and the chip is a clue. The equation means he was some kind of genius. Dad isn't really a deadbeat, and Alex is going to find out what really happened. I'm fairly sure I'm right, and that's a little bit of a problem.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck at the bottom of a rich/poor city inherits his missing dad's debt and starts to wonder whether the dad really ran out on them.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 3, panel 3, at the mother line. I wouldn't throw the book across the room, but that's where it started to feel like a book that was explaining itself to me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
"What if the official story is wrong?" is a question, but it's a generic one. I don't know the official story well enough to care whether it's wrong. The thing that actually has me is the 28.7 years, and the last page doesn't push on that. I'd keep reading tomorrow if I had to. I wouldn't turn the page tonight because I wanted to.

## Version B (read second, so not fully cold)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, the splash** works the same way A's did. The pipe narrowing at the bottom does it again. The captions "OASIS. HALYARD. KEEL." told me the names, and because they run top to bottom I could match them to the picture. That helped.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** The other workers' small balances flash up and disappear, and then Alex's number swells up and dwarfs all of them. Even second time round that hit harder than A, because now I can see he's worse off than everyone around him.
- **Page 2, panel 3.** "No one laughs. A worker glances at Alex's balance, then looks away." This is the moment I felt something in B. It's embarrassing and lonely. Nobody is mean to him. They just look away.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** Ada looks at the sealed mine entrance before saying "What if they're wrong?" That gave the question somewhere to point. All of a sudden there's a *place* where the answer might be, and my brain went straight there.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1.** There is a lot going on in the picture (rails, lines, greenhouses, gardens, a repair diagram on a little screen). I'd have looked at the big shape and skipped the details. I never noticed the repair diagram not matching his bracket until the supervisor said so.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** "Just notice it works." It's one line too many. "Then don't approve it" was the punchline, and it already landed.
- **Page 3, panels 2–3.** The banter. It's quick and I read it fast, but halfway through I stopped really following and just let it bounce.

### I was confused
- **Time of day.** Page 2, panel 4 is "late morning" at the rim, where he says "One day, I'm leaving." Page 3 is "late afternoon at the same overlook." Has he been sitting there all day? I'd have read it as one continuous scene and not noticed the light changed, and then wondered why he says the same kind of thing twice.
- **Page 3, panel 3.** He goes from "*One day*, I'm leaving" on page 2 to Ada saying "you're planning to leave *before tomorrow*." When did "one day" turn into tonight? How does she know? Is he actually running tonight? This is the biggest thing B tells me, and I only half caught it inside a run of quips.
- **"Where you were all night?" / "Working." / "before dawn."** I couldn't tell if this matters (is he hiding something?) or if it's just banter. It was confusing and it didn't make me curious. I let it go.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** "Then don't spend them proving he was right about people leaving." Who is "he"? The dad? Why would the dad be right about people leaving? Who left? B never mentions a mother, so I had nothing to hook this onto. I gave up on it.
- **"Paying for what he did."** What did he do? A at least told me he "lost everything." B gives me nothing.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Oasis / Halyard / Keel | p1 caption | Yes, roughly. The caption plus the picture tells me which city is which. |
| Alpha / white-and-amber nodes | p1 | Never named. It's "the scanner thing." |
| "Debt Transfer Day" | p2 screen | Sort of. It sounds like a thing everyone knows about. I don't know if everyone gets one or just him. |
| Adrian Phantum | p2 screen | Assumed dad. Never said. |
| The casino chip with the equation | p2 p3 | Not explained. |
| The sealed mine | p2 p4 (art), p3 p4 | It's pointed at, not explained. This one felt deliberate and I was fine with it. |
| "People leaving" / who left | p3 p4 | Never. |
| "The story" | p3 p4 | Implied: that he left on purpose. |

### Lines I read twice
- "You look expensive." (p3 p1) Got it the second time: the debt.
- "It's the part where you pretend it isn't." (p3 p3)
- "Then don't spend them proving he was right about people leaving." (p3 p4) I gave up on this one.
- "I think everybody's been very sure of a story nobody can show you." (p3 p4) I got it on the second read, but it's a sentence you have to untangle.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex is clear: dry and deflecting.
- Ada is more of a person than in A. She needles him, she knows his habits, and she watches him instead of the view. But she and Alex both talk in the same clever-comeback rhythm, so on page 3 they started to sound like one voice doing both sides. "Tea's free." / "Nothing's free." / "You say that like you invented debt." / "I only inherited it." It's a bit too polished. Honestly, this is the kind of back-and-forth my friends would call AI-sounding.
- I still don't know what she is to him. She feels like an old friend, maybe more. At least in B that felt like something to find out instead of a gap.
- The workers in the queue are background, but they did a job.

### I felt something
- Page 2, panel 3: the worker looking away. That was the strongest moment for me in either version.
- Page 3, panel 4, "The chip stops turning": I noticed the beat when he got hit by what she said. Small, but it landed.

### I predicted
- Same as A: dad didn't just run, and the chip is a clue. Plus a new one: something happened in the sealed mine and the dad is tied to it. And Alex is going to try to leave tonight, but Ada's question will send him into the mine instead.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who inherits his dad's debt on his 17th birthday is about to run, until his friend makes him wonder whether his dad's disappearance has something to do with the sealed mine.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 3, panel 3, the "where were you all night / before dawn / before tomorrow" exchange. It's the stretch where I was reading lines and not following a scene.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
More than with A, a bit. "What if they're wrong?" is the same question, but the look at the sealed mine gives it a direction, and "before tomorrow" gives it a clock. I'm not desperate, because I still don't care about the dad himself. But I'd turn the page to see if Alex runs tonight. Part of the last page lost me, though ("proving he was right about people leaving"), so I came out of it slightly fuzzy instead of sharp.

---

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
B, narrowly, with complaints. The deciding moment was **B, page 2, panel 3**: the worker who glances at Alex's balance and looks away. Nothing on A's page 2 made me feel the debt in front of other people like that. The sealed-mine glance at the end of B also gave me somewhere to look. Against that, A is faster, cleaner and easier to follow on one read, and I never got lost in it. B's page 3 lost me twice. A lost me with one fake line about his mother. If I'm being honest, the hook in both is the same scanner screen. A delivers it clean and then goes flat. B delivers it with a witness and then gets too clever.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A great debt screen, followed by a friend who reads out the backstory.
- **B:** A kid embarrassed in a queue and a sealed door worth wondering about, buried under some dialogue that's too pleased with itself.
