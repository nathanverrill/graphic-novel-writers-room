# Analysis — Prosperity, script pages 1–64

I read the pages only. Every claim below names a page. Where I go past what a profile supports, I mark it **inference**.

**What the surface invites vs. what the story supports.** For the first 40 pages the book looks like an anti-corporate debt dystopia: a debt-transfer day (p12), a company water grab (p32), wage docking (p24), name-stripping (p39). The story underneath does not hold that view. The real villain is an emergency authority and an automated system, Alpha, that outlived its emergency (p47, p52, p61). The Houses are shown as cronies who profit from it. The fix is honest measurement, recorded ownership and a small business that sells a product it stands behind (p43–44, p63).

The weak point: the book never shows the link between the Houses and the emergency authority. A reader has to infer that Caldera's diversion runs through Alpha (p32 "INDUSTRIAL DIVERSION," p61 "RESCINDED").

**Who stops reading because of the surface.** A conservative Denver parent or teacher who flips to p12 and p30–32 and puts the book down as "corporations steal water from poor towns." So does a student who reads only Issue 01, because Issue 01 ends (p22) before any good business appears. The good business arrives at p41.

**Does the book conclude, or leave a reader concluding, that free enterprise is bad?** No. The pay-off pages are pro-enterprise: p42–44 and p63. Cronies and the planner lose, not markets: p59–62.

## Young Americans Center

**What they would like**
- p23–24: Tomas lends 40 credits "separate from the household account," on Alex's word: "Pay me from Halyard work." / "I will." It is still on the ledger at p63 ("TOMAS NOTE: 40 CREDITS… REPAYMENT IN PROGRESS"). This is exactly a teenager using credit as a tool.
- p40, p50, p58: Alex turns down debt clearance three times, from a crony and from a planner. That is the opposite of debt cancelled by the powerful, which they object to.
- p42: willing to fail ("11% SIMULATED EFFICIENCY… Good. Now we know where it breaks.").
- p41 Lina: "They want my name on an unsafe claim. I'm not signing it." This is integrity under pressure.
- p44 and p63: a business that serves customers and makes money. It is licensed, has a warranty reserve and a posted price. Inventors are named, so intellectual property is protected. This directly answers their v5 complaint about "altruism over capitalism."
- p19, p48: owning mistakes in public.

**What would bother them**
- p12: inherited debt gets credit wrong, and they are a real bank. A teacher would have to say "this isn't how debt works." That is the kind of error they named.
- p35: neighbours pay his overdue installment. It reads as a gift that solves the plot ("They bought the gate"), and the book never shows it repaid.
- p63: "PURIFIER ALLOCATION" and "OTHER POSTED REPAYMENTS" are vague. A banker would also ask how 12 credits a unit takes 104,000 credits off the balance.
- p1–10: ten pages of 2099 before the story starts. Students who are "quick to put a book down" meet a robot dog and a heist they cannot place.
- Alpha is still never explained (p28, p53, p61). That is a v5 concern unchanged.
- The prose is still riddle-like: "Someday's crowded" (p9), "The part that costs" (p14), "Badly, eventually" (p16), "He marked what survived them" (p28).
- A partisan cue a progressive teacher might flag in reverse: Alex turns down "DIRECT WATER EQUALIZATION" (p50). **Inference:** most will read it as refusing a dictator, not refusing fairness.

**Likely verdict: partly.** This is far closer to something they can present than v5. The business half (p41–44, p63) is what they asked for. The opening pages, the unexplained debt mechanics and Alpha still leave a teacher doing a lot of explaining.

## Bill Reynolds

**What he would like**
- p19: "I borrowed too much. I gambled some of it." This is nearly his own beer-hall line.
- p23 and p63: a handshake loan, paid back in instalments, with the lender saying "Took you long enough." His Paul-the-banker story in miniature.
- p58: the windfall refused. "A windfall isn't a pathway" is acted out, not said.
- p29: the mentor is wrong ("I read that wrong.") and the boy says "Keep reading." p25: "Yes. What would you do?" He asks more than he tells.
- p39, p44: ownership made concrete: a registry, names, a recorded claim. This is de Soto on the page.
- p47, p51–52: emergency government that keeps control and turns people into victims. This is his view of government shown as story, not speech.
- p64: "I'm showing up." It matches "It cannot replace presence."
- p62: Caldera is a crony gaming a public count, which is his kind of villain.

**What would bother him**
- p35: the neighbours' 500 credits is charity that moves the plot, and it is never written as a loan.
- The big lender is faceless. Alex never "goes to the bankers" over the 418,607. He pays a screen.
- Business owners are the default villains through p40. The Houses have no honest member until a Caldera rep revises his proposal (p63, panel 1).
- TJ (p1–10) may be named for a Founder, but the page never says so, and TJ disappears after p10.

**Does Bi11bot sound like him? No.**
- By the profile's voice notes (plain, slow, roundabout, Western, a question first, then a story from his own life, then one plain line, jokes at his own expense), Bi11bot has the method but not the voice.
- He asks (p25, p43) and admits error (p29).
- He never tells a single story, has no Western cadence, and his jokes are dry quips, not self-mockery: "Adrian's jaw. His poorer timing." (p18), "B11 if the room is on fire." (p18), "Abandoned mines do not sweat." (p27).
- He has about a dozen lines and nearly vanishes after p44. He places a band at p48 and says one line at p55.

**Likely verdict: yes.** His beliefs carry the plot: refusing the windfall, repaying the lender, ownership, the planner as villain, a mentor who is wrong. He will be disappointed that the robot carrying his name is a quiet machine instead of him.

## A comic writer

**The hook**
- The real hook is p12: "Happy birthday, Phantum." Then 418,607 credits and "28.7 YEARS." It is strong, clear and personal.
- The p1–10 prologue is a fun visual heist with a robot dog that comes apart, but it delays that hook by ten pages.
- The prologue's questions (who sent the thieves, the maker's mark, "who owns the water") are never answered.
- p64 places "the damaged drone component from 2099" in a 2043 scene. That is either a continuity error or a time paradox nobody set up.

**Page turns.** Reveals land well on left pages after a turn: the debt display (p12), the hidden workshop (p16), Bi11bot rising (p18), the temptation chamber (p50), the debt erasure offer (p58). The structure is professional.

**Splash pages.** There is one full page (p10) and one full-width panel (p64). Arriving in Halyard (p38) and Oasis (p45) are single panels. A pro would make those splashes, because they sell the world the partner says is missing.

**Dialogue**
- It is tight and clipped, but everyone speaks in the same aphoristic register: Alex, Ada, Tomas, Lock and Bi11bot all trade two-to-five-word riddles.
- Three names nearly collide: Mera, Mara and Nera.
- Ada's mother scene (p46–48) is the best-written sequence: "Safe for whom?" "Don't apologize where it costs nothing."

**What they would cut**
- Prologue down to 4 pages.
- The civic hall, p60–62, down to one page.
- The unexplained equation motif (p16, p17, p30, p53).
- Adrian's 312-patent estate (p39–40) is set up and never paid off. The collaborators appear in a montage (p41) with no scene of how Alex finds them.

**Would they take the job?** Yes, as a rewrite. The spine is there: debt clock, three cities, three safeguards, the father's fate behind a bulkhead (p55). They would ask for room to fix the opening and the ending.

## A game designer

**There is a game here, and it is unusually clean.**
- **Loop:** repair, measure, expose, earn, repay. The debt balance is a visible score (p12, p35, p63).
- **Choices that cost something:**
  - save Tomas or keep the wage (p24, "−40 CR");
  - Alpha's straight route or the father's map (p28–29);
  - sell the estate or freeze it (p40);
  - take executive power or don't (p50, p58).
- **Systems a player would want to push on:**
  - the water allocation and its "denominator" (p32, p62);
  - license splits (p44, 12/18/8);
  - warranty reserve against failure;
  - surveillance records that make people stop helping you (p51);
  - three cities linked by one shared route for water, rail and data (p20).

**Would they want to build it?** Yes. It is a management sim with a moral economy, close to Frostpunk crossed with a trading game. The missing piece is any failure state after the purifier works. Nothing on p63 can go wrong.

## A streaming showrunner

**The engine.** A vertical class world (Keel at the bottom, Halyard, Oasis at the top) and a debt clock on the hero's wrist. Each episode can climb one level. That is a sellable engine.

**Characters an audience would follow for a season**
- Alex.
- Ada, who has the stronger emotional arc (p13, p45–48).
- Tomas.
- Mera, the most interesting antagonist: she tried to stop it and couldn't (p52).

Lock is a suit. Bi11bot is too thin to be the breakout character a robot should be.

**Season-one cliffhanger.** Adrian's hand under the bulkhead, "The image cuts before showing what happens beyond it" (p55), plus the old man in 2099 who still won't say. It is good.

**What is missing from the first issue**
- An antagonist with a face before p22 (Mera gets one panel).
- A clear want for Alex. "The part that costs" (p14) and "I want the answer" (p37) are moods, not goals.
- A reason the prologue's dog matters.

**Verdict from issue one alone:** interested, not buying. They would ask for the bible.

## Does it smell like school

**Pages where the explaining is the point**
1. **p51.** An archive montage that exists to show how the record system ruins people, capped by a stated moral: "People stopped helping when the record became more dangerous than the man."
2. **p60.** Procedure: baseline, production record, "PUBLIC REVIEW OPEN." Representatives tabling documents.
3. **p61.** A whole page of system displays changing state ("RESCINDED," the list of functions, "OPEN TO HUMAN REVIEW"). No character wants anything. It exists to explain the new order.
4. **p62.** The denominator argument. It is borderline: Tomas's line "You counted the pipe after you stopped counting the homes" is good drama, but it is still a civics hearing.

**Count: 4, including one whole page that exists to explain (p61).**

Single moral lines that will not lose a reader: p50 "If we hold all the keys, everyone waits for our hands," p59 "No single hand," p41 Ren's product line.

**Pages that teach without smelling of it, because people need the thing and pay for it**
- **p23–24:** the 40-credit loan and the wage docked for doing the right thing.
- **p32:** 62% against 18%, discovered by hand.
- **p35:** the installment paid and the gate opens.
- **p42:** the 11% failure.
- **p43–44:** names, a warranty reserve, a licence and a split of 38 credits.
- **p63:** a customer agrees to a posted price, and Tomas's note.

The money lessons land cleanly. The government lessons smell.

## What would change the verdicts

1. **The p20–21 scene: show what Alpha is and what Alex is trying to find**, through action, on the page, in Issue 01. *Satisfies YAC and the students; answers two of their v5 concerns at once.*
2. **p35: make the neighbours' 500 credits a written loan** on the communal ledger, with that loan visibly repaid on p63. *Satisfies Bill (no charity solving the plot) and YAC (credit shown correctly).*
3. **p12 and a later scene: give the 418,607 debt a mechanism and a face.** A lender or a contract Alex can sit across from and renegotiate, as in Bill's Paul story. *Satisfies YAC (a real bank) and Bill (facing the lender).*
4. **One Bi11bot scene, around p29 or p43, where his question leads into a short failure story of his own and a plain line.** Not a speech: an exchange Alex pushes back on. *Satisfies Bill on voice.*
5. **Fold the p60–62 civic hall into one page and give the freed pages to a real cost.** For example, the purifier fails in a Keel home and the warranty reserve pays. *Satisfies the students (school smell drops to 3 or fewer pages) and YAC (resilience and the warranty in action).*
