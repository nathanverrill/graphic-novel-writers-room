# notes.md

## Verdict

**Needs a rethink.** The development files do not agree on the book’s page architecture, prologue, House identities or later-character canon, and the current script is not an edit of the approved four-page prologue and Chapters 1–3. The strongest settled spine—Alex’s debt, the Measure, the water discrepancy, the community payment, the train, the estate and the Maker’s Seal—is usable, but the script currently builds a different prologue and substantially different Chapters 4–6 around it. Do not proceed to execution until the Director and Plotter reconcile the story files and the Writer returns the script to the approved page plot.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. The script’s page architecture contradicts the fixed 64-page structure

- **Location:** `brief.md` Canon and Format; `facts.md` Project structure; `story.md` Structure; `script.md` throughout.
- **Observation:** Canon fixes a four-page prologue on Pages 1–4, Chapter 1 on Pages 5–16, Chapter 2 on Pages 17–31 and Chapter 3 on approximately Pages 32–47. The script instead labels Pages 1–10 “Chapter 00,” Pages 11–22 Chapter 1, Pages 23–37 Chapter 2 and Pages 38–44 Chapter 3, then begins different Chapters 4–6 on Pages 45–64.
- **Problem:** This is not a compression or local numbering error: it changes which scenes occupy the fixed page budget and moves every approved chapter turn.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director and Plotter, restore the approved page allocation and chapter turns before further line or layout work. Writer, fit the draft’s existing scenes into that allocation rather than treating the current 64-page script as the settled structure.
- **Roles:** Director, Plotter, Writer

#### 2. The prologue is a different story and violates the fixed 2070 frame

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 1–10; `brief.md` Canon; `facts.md` The 2070 frame.
- **Observation:** The approved prologue is four pages, set around 2070, titled “The Wall,” and shows Older Alex’s house, daughter, patent wall, paid-in-full statement and pump-bracket sketch. The script opens in “Lake Keel, 2099,” spends ten pages on TJ stopping a water theft, introduces Nera and Voss, and omits the approved wall, daughter setup, paid-in-full statement and bracket conversation.
- **Problem:** The prologue’s date, length, dramatic transaction, premise and page-turn into 2043 all contradict fixed material. This also makes the later Page 64 callback impossible to interpret cleanly.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, return Pages 1–4 to the approved “The Wall” scenes in the draft’s order. Remove the 2099 lake-theft sequence, Nera, Voss and the prologue TJ plot; do not solve its mysteries elsewhere.
- **Roles:** Writer, Plotter, Director

#### 3. The script uses prohibited Charter House names

- **Location:** `script.md`, Page 39 Panel 1 and Page 40 Panel 2; `script.md`, Pages 1–3 of Chapter 3.
- **Observation:** The script and layouts use “Caldera House,” “Vector” and “Orbital.” Canon fixes the only House names as House Calder, House Ivers and House Steyn; the decision log explicitly rejects the conflicting names.
- **Problem:** This is a direct project-truth contradiction and will propagate into lettering, signage, uniforms and page prompts.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director, Plotter and Writer, replace every occurrence with House Calder, House Ivers and House Steyn, preserving each House’s established specialization and color. Layout Agent and Letterer must audit all visible House text afterward.
- **Roles:** Director, Plotter, Writer, Layout Agent, Letterer

#### 4. Chapters 4–6 introduce an unapproved Ada-mother storyline and new canon

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 45–48; `characters.md` Ada and Alex’s mother; `brief.md` People and Relationships; `facts.md` Ada Veyra and Grandmother.
- **Observation:** The script introduces Leona Veyra as Ada’s mother, gives her a history with Adrian and Mera, depicts her Covenant band and has her disclose that she voted for emergency authority. The approved character material does not establish Ada’s mother, and the current brief does not authorize this biography or history.
- **Problem:** This is a substantial new subplot, not a visual elaboration of the deferred Covenant material. It also risks confusing Ada’s established family role with Alex’s protected mother rule and makes Leona’s history part of the book’s objective truth without Director approval.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director, decide whether Leona and this parentage/history are canon. Until approved, Writer must remove these facts and return Chapter 4 to the settled Mera/Covenant meeting trajectory. Do not use the archive scene to disclose additional family history.
- **Roles:** Director, Writer, Character Designer, Plotter

#### 5. The script changes the Covenant’s custodial chain and operating setup without approval

- **Location:** `world.md` Covenant entry; `facts.md` The Covenant; `script.md`, Pages 46–56.
- **Observation:** Approved material says the Covenant is associated with Adrian, Leona Veyra and Mera in the world file but leaves the settled Chapters 1–3 material without a completed Covenant. The script makes Leona an active custodian, has Adrian’s band appear from Bi11bot, links the bands in a private station and then moves directly to a civic-hall authority interface.
- **Problem:** The script turns an open later-chapter procedure into a specific mechanism and assigns a major role to an unapproved character. The brief’s proposals explicitly leave the Covenant’s public procedure, appeal structure and enforcement limits open.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Director, settle the custodians, linking conditions and authority-return procedure. Plotter and Writer, keep any later mechanism within that decision and do not treat “designated executive authority” or “emergency authority rescinded” as canon until approved.
- **Roles:** Director, Plotter, Writer

### Major

#### 6. The current script does not follow the approved draft’s page plot in Chapters 1–3

- **Location:** `story.md` Page plot and `draft.md`; `script.md`, Pages 11–44.
- **Observation:** The approved plot has Chapter 1 Pages 5–16, Chapter 2 Pages 17–31 and Chapter 3 Pages 32–47. The current script moves the workshop, Legacy Protocol and Mera beat to Pages 16–22, shifts the mine and train sequence to Pages 23–37, and compresses the estate/purifier sequence into Pages 38–44.
- **Problem:** The room’s instruction is to edit the showrunner’s draft and preserve its scenes and order. The current script changes chapter boundaries, removes or relocates beats and causes later setup/payoff failures.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter, create a page-by-page reconciliation against the approved `story.md` plot. Writer, restore the draft’s scene order and place each beat in the fixed page ranges; do not solve the mismatch by changing canon.
- **Roles:** Plotter, Writer

#### 7. The Page 20–23 route is not yet represented consistently in the script

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 20–23; `layouts.md`, production decision “Page 20–23 bridge.”
- **Observation:** The script locks the apartment door on Page 20, reaches the first marker on Page 21 and starts at Site 6 on Page 23. The layouts supply an unwired workshop hatch and maintenance path, but the script’s corresponding panels do not consistently show the full route and its tracking state.
- **Problem:** The reader must understand how Alex and Ada leave the apartment without treating the route as a loophole that cancels the travel restriction.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, show the manual hatch, maintenance route and arrival at Site 6 as the physical continuation, while keeping Alpha tracking active and the Halyard travel restriction intact.
- **Roles:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 8. The 500-credit community payment is under-motivated and financially ambiguous

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 34–35; `brief.md` Money, Water and Debt; `facts.md` Governance, travel and enforcement.
- **Observation:** Keel receives the water evidence and immediately produces exactly 500 credits. The script does not clearly show whether the amount is a travel installment, a collective payment to Alex, a payment to the administration or a combination of these.
- **Problem:** The payment can read as a windfall or unexplained bailout. The canon requires that it make the overdue condition current without cancelling the inherited debt.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, show the community explicitly raising the overdue travel condition, identify the recipient and show the debt moving only from 418,607 to 418,107 credits. Preserve care funding and keep Tomas’s 40-credit note separate.
- **Roles:** Writer, Letterer

#### 9. Alpha and Bi11bot are not cleanly distinguished for the reader

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 20–29; `facts.md` Alpha; `characters.md` Bi11bot.
- **Observation:** The script calls the projection on the mine route “Alpha’s projection,” while Bi11bot supplies route projections elsewhere. Alpha is not clearly named as the distributed administrative network at its first intervention.
- **Problem:** Cold-read readers may treat Alpha as Bi11bot or as a single visible machine. This undermines the central institutional conflict.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, identify Alpha at the locked door as the distributed network controlling movement, records and infrastructure. Use distinct visual grammar for Alpha displays/control nodes versus Bi11bot’s warm shoulder projector.
- **Roles:** Writer, Layout Agent, Character Designer

#### 10. The collaborator search is too abrupt and loses the ownership causality

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 39–41; `story.md` Pages 36–39.
- **Observation:** Lock says “Then find the people who made it,” and the next page presents Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren in four isolated panels. Their records, locations and relationship to Adrian are not visibly what leads Alex to them.
- **Problem:** The collaborators feel delivered by the outline rather than found through Alex’s investigation, and the ownership turn becomes a technical montage.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, use the registry/archive material to identify each contributor and route Alex to the next location before the character introductions. Give each collaborator one consequential choice tied to the House pressure already established.
- **Roles:** Writer, Plotter

#### 11. The Maker’s Seal appears without sufficient setup

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 41–44; `facts.md` Maker’s Seal.
- **Observation:** Lina hands Alex the Seal on Page 44, but the script does not establish that she carries it, why she has it or why it waits for Alex’s attribution choice.
- **Problem:** The second safeguard feels like a reward object rather than a bounded artifact requiring deliberate human action.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, plant the worn bronze Seal in Lina’s left breast pocket when Alex first meets her and make its transfer conditional on naming the contributors and refusing sole ownership. Do not let it clear debt or prove more than provenance and rightful ownership.
- **Roles:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 12. Adrian’s Covenant band is not planted before it is retrieved

- **Location:** `script.md`, Page 48; `facts.md` Continuity ledger.
- **Observation:** Bi11bot retrieves Adrian’s band from his chest compartment without a prior scene establishing that he carries it.
- **Problem:** The band appears from nowhere and weakens the physical custody chain.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, show the band in the fitted compartment during the first Covenant discussion or an earlier inventory beat. Layout Agent, preserve its location until retrieval.
- **Roles:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 13. The prologue’s TJ material conflicts with the established robot rules

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 1–10; `brief.md` Visual direction; `facts.md` Bi11bot and 2070 frame.
- **Observation:** The script makes TJ a central 2099 action character with a detachable body and a lost leg. The approved material excludes TJ from the 2043 story and only permits him in the 2070 prologue if used there; the fixed prologue itself is the four-page Wall frame.
- **Problem:** Even if TJ remains absent from 2043, this version changes the approved prologue’s future-world evidence and creates unresolved ownership/lake questions that the book does not pay off.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, remove the 2099 TJ sequence rather than attempting to explain it later. If the Director wants TJ in the 2070 frame, that choice requires a new approved prologue design and payoff plan.
- **Roles:** Director, Writer, Plotter

#### 14. The final debt balance is stated without an auditable transaction path

- **Location:** `script.md`, Page 63 Panel 2; `brief.md` Money, Water and Debt.
- **Observation:** The balance falls from 418,107 to 314,159 while the ledger lists categories but no amounts or dates.
- **Problem:** The fixed number reads as arbitrary—especially because it resembles pi—and the reader cannot verify that the reduction came from shown work rather than cancellation or an unshown windfall.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, retain the fixed balance only if the page shows a compact, readable set of actual posted payments with amounts sufficient to support the reduction. Letterer, avoid an unreadable six-line ledger.
- **Roles:** Writer, Letterer

#### 15. The ending repeats the same refusal beat too many times

- **Location:** `script.md`, Lock’s offer; Mera’s offer; Pages 57–59.
- **Observation:** Alex refuses debt relief and concentrated control from Lock, then again from Mera, then again from the civic interface.
- **Problem:** The repeated choice is predictable and makes the climax feel like a third version of an already settled decision. Cold-readers specifically identified Pages 57–58 as a pacing sag.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Plotter and Writer, make the final choice materially different: the climax should test whether Alex can convene and accept public limits, not whether he will once more say no to personal power. Preserve the earlier refusals as setup, but give the final scene a new consequence.
- **Roles:** Plotter, Writer

#### 16. The public-review climax is not yet legible as action

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 60–63; `story.md` Pages 58–63.
- **Observation:** The ending depends on a denominator revision, baseline, physical gauge, evidence challenge, proposal revision and repayment ledger, but the script does not define the two compared denominators in plain visual terms before the dispute.
- **Problem:** Readers can understand “62% reported / 18% delivered,” but may not understand what the House removes from its later denominator or what decision changes because of the challenge.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer and Layout Agent, show two side-by-side diagrams: the original baseline includes the Keel residential branch; the House revision excludes it. Then show the same physical gauge applied to both. Keep the dialogue concrete and limit administrative terminology.
- **Roles:** Writer, Layout Agent

### Minor

#### 17. The old equation is overused before its dramatic function is clear

- **Location:** `script.md`, Pages 16–19, 30–31 and 53–54.
- **Observation:** The looping equation appears on the watch, chalkboard, brass plate, pipe housing, Covenant station and projections before the book clearly establishes that it models human decision-making and the limits of prediction.
- **Problem:** Repetition becomes prop inventory rather than escalating clue; the reader cannot tell which appearances are authentication, research or thematic callback.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, reserve the equation’s early appearances for one clear match and one consequential later use. Let Bi11bot explain its relevance through the decision-making problem, not a lecture.
- **Role:** Writer

#### 18. The narrative voice is too uniform across the cast

- **Location:** Throughout the script; especially Pages 13, 16, 25, 40–44 and 48–63.
- **Observation:** Many characters speak in clipped aphorisms and polished reversals: “Someday’s crowded,” “Badly, eventually,” “No single hand,” “The part that costs.”
- **Problem:** Cold reads report that the cast begins to sound like one voice, weakening class, occupation and character distinctions.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, differentiate speech by function: Alex concrete and mechanical, Ada obligation-focused and direct, Tomas compressed work speech, Bi11bot precise, Lock commercially fluent, Mera administrative. Replace only lines that obscure immediate action.
- **Role:** Writer

#### 19. The character introductions overload pages with new faces

- **Location:** `script.md`, Chapter 3 Pages 41–44; later Pages 46 and 60.
- **Observation:** Four collaborators are introduced in four adjacent panels, then Leona and Mara Soto are added later without sufficient visual or narrative preparation.
- **Problem:** Readers cannot retain names, specialties and stakes, and Mara/Mera/Nera are easily confused.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Character Designer, give each collaborator a strong recurring silhouette and prop. Writer, attach each name to a decision rather than a résumé. Director, decide whether Mara Soto’s name is worth retaining given the Nera/Mera collision.
- **Roles:** Character Designer, Writer, Director

#### 20. The script/layout object conflict on Page 64 must be removed from all artifacts

- **Location:** `script.md`, Page 64 Panel 2; `layouts.md`, Page 64 correction; `thumbnails.md`, Page 64 description.
- **Observation:** Layout and thumbnail direction correctly specify a 2043 verified Measure record, but the script still names “the damaged drone component from 2099.”
- **Problem:** This is a cross-artifact continuity hazard even after the page architecture is repaired.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Writer, delete the 2099 component reference and describe only the verified Measure record. Layout Agent and Letterer, audit page prompts and visible labels for the same correction.
- **Roles:** Writer, Layout Agent, Letterer
