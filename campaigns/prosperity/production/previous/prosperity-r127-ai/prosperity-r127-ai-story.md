# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material status:** explicit decisions, binding rules, revised draft material, and showrunner notes.

The book is a near-future graphic-novel adventure about Alex Phantum, a seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic who inherits his missing father's debt, a watch engraved with a strange equation, and a trail leading into the hidden history of Prosperity.

The intended experience is a science-fiction mystery that high-school readers can follow on a first read:

- what makes life hard in Keel;
- why water matters;
- what Alex inherited from Adrian;
- what Alex hopes to find;
- where his first quest begins;
- why someone in Oasis is watching.

Mystery should concern the answers, never the mission. Adventure, danger, humor, robots, financial choices and character should carry the learning.

The central dramatic question develops from:

> Who gets to decide how people live, and what happens when those decisions cannot be checked?

The recurring answers are not delivered as slogans. Alex trusts physical evidence over official labels. Ada notices obligations and who carries the burden. Bi11bot translates choices into stakes, odds, ownership and consequences. Adrian's legacy is questioning accepted truths, learning from failure, and taking responsibility for what people create.

The book must not conclude that honest business, private ownership, free enterprise or markets are bad. The antagonistic forces are monopoly, cronyism, concentrated power and an unaccountable central planner. The desired trajectory is toward independent ownership, competition, businesses that serve people and make money, and debt paid back rather than erased.

## Premise

In 2043, Prosperity is a three-tier Andean high-desert colony:

- Keel, built around exhausted mining works, is poor, rationed and heavily restricted.
- Halyard is a technical and manufacturing city dominated by three Charter Houses.
- Oasis is the wealthy administrative city where Alpha's governing systems are managed.

After the Water Wars, Alpha was built to coordinate emergency response and was given temporary authority over essential resources. That authority became effectively permanent. Its decisions keep the three cities visibly unequal.

On his seventeenth birthday, Alex's inherited liability transfers to him: **418,607 credits**, with travel restricted and projected clearance in **28.7 years**. He cannot leave Keel, but his missing father Adrian left a trail of artifacts that begins beneath the sealed mine.

Alex discovers Adrian's workshop, wakes the worn industrial robot Bi11bot and learns of three safeguards:

1. **The Measure** independently checks real conditions and exposes where official information is wrong.
2. **The Maker's Seal** establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it.
3. **The Covenant** makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible.

Together, deliberate human action with all three can return governing authority from Alpha to people.

The first quest takes Alex into the old mine, where he proves that Keel is receiving **18%** of the water officially reported as **62%**. He chooses to publish the evidence even though Alpha can identify him. Keel responds by protecting him and helping him travel to Halyard.

In Halyard, Alex discovers that Adrian's intellectual estate contains **312 patents**, including **47 restricted** and **19 disputed**, and that the work was built with collaborators trapped inside the Charter Houses. Director Cassian Lock offers to clear Alex's debt if he signs over the estate. Alex refuses to surrender ownership to one dominant buyer. He and the collaborators instead build a small, affordable purification unit that independent businesses can make and license. The Maker's Seal activates when Alex names the contributors and refuses to claim sole ownership.

The 64-page book contains the four-page prologue and Chapters 1–6. The prologue and Chapters 1–3 are established draft material. Chapters 4–6 below preserve the deferred trajectory while supplying the page budget required by the brief.

## Structure

### Prologue — Pages 1–4: The earned future

A 2070 domestic frame shows older Alex's lived-in Oasis home, his paid debt, independent purifier businesses and the ordinary consequences of choices made decades earlier. His daughter finds an unpatented pump-bracket sketch. Alex begins the story of the debt and rationed water that came before.

**Turn:** Page 4 dissolves from the future sketch to seventeen-year-old Alex repairing a Keel pump.

### Act I / Chapter 1 — Pages 5–16: Inheritance and activation

Alex receives Adrian's debt, argues with Ada about leaving Keel, opens the hidden workshop and wakes Bi11bot. Adrian's message directs him to question everything. The first artifact, the Measure, lies beneath Keel. Alex activates the Legacy Protocol, and Mera chooses **TRACK**.

**Turn:** Page 16 — someone in Oasis is following Alex.

### Act II-A / Chapter 2 — Pages 17–31: Evidence has a cost

Alex learns Alpha's emergency history and the limits of the three safeguards. He enters the old mine, rescues Tomas at a visible wage cost, finds the Measure and verifies the 62%/18% water discrepancy. He publishes the evidence rather than escaping privately. Keel responds with manual action, money and protection, allowing Alex and Ada to cross to Halyard.

**Turn:** Page 31 — a follower reports that Alex has the Measure.

### Act II-B / Chapter 3 — Pages 32–47: Ownership has a cost

Alex discovers Adrian's contested intellectual estate. Lock offers debt relief and institutional security in exchange for control. Alex finds Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren, combines their work and helps create a 91% simulated compact purifier. He rejects Lock's transfer and names the contributors. The Maker's Seal activates.

**Turn:** Page 47 — Mera reveals that Alex must come to her for the Covenant.

### Act III-A / Chapter 4 — Pages 48–53: The third custodian

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot follow the Covenant trail toward Oasis. Mera meets them under controlled conditions and reveals that Adrian came to her because he needed a custodian outside both Alpha and the Houses. She does not surrender the Covenant as property; she requires Alex to make a public, answerable commitment.

**Turn:** Page 53 — Mera presents Adrian's band, but the Covenant cannot link until Alex chooses what authority he is willing to return.

### Act III-B / Chapter 5 — Pages 54–59: Authority is not a prize

The three bands link only after Alex, Ada, Mera and the collaborators state concrete obligations and limits. The Covenant opens the dormant authority-return mechanism but does not shut Alpha down. Alpha continues non-discretionary infrastructure functions. Its governing recommendations become subject to public records, appeal and designated human review.

Lock counters with a denominator revision that makes House delivery appear compliant. Keel workers, using the Measure's verified baseline, expose the omission.

**Turn:** Page 59 — Alex must face the public review instead of taking private control of Alpha.

### Act III-C / Chapter 6 — Pages 60–64: Accountable continuation

Alex convenes a public review with Keel, Halyard and Oasis participants. He refuses to become ruler or sole interpreter of the safeguards. The community establishes a provisional, transparent process for reviewing allocations, ownership disputes and agreements. Alex's debt is reduced through shown payments, licensing income and earned work, not cancellation. The ending leaves scarcity, House resistance and Adrian's disappearance unresolved while showing a credible future path.

**Final turn:** Page 64 — the 2043 verified Measure record sits beside a public water terminal as people begin making the next decision. The question is no longer whether authority can be returned, but whether people will keep answering for it.

## Reader vs character knowledge

### Reader knows before Alex

- The prologue establishes that Alex eventually participates in independent ownership, earns enough to pay his debt and lives in a functioning Oasis.
- Mera chooses **TRACK** immediately after Alex activates the Legacy Protocol.
- Mera is the person watching Alex before Alex knows her name.
- The reader sees the 62% official record and the nearly dry Keel residential pipe before the broader public understands the discrepancy.
- The reader sees that Lock's institutions are not simply malicious; they offer real scale, employment and commercial capacity.
- The reader sees the Maker's Seal in Lina's pocket before Alex knows what she carries.
- The reader sees Mera's band and understands that the Covenant has a human custody chain before Alex reaches Oasis.

### Alex knows before the reader

- The physical feel of machinery and the difference between a working repair and an approved repair.
- What Grandmother's care costs in daily life.
- The emotional weight of Adrian's watch and photograph.
- Which details in Ada's map do not match Adrian's official map.
- How much it costs him to cross an assigned work boundary.
- What the compact purifier's physical connectors feel like when they are out of true.

### Characters know that Alex does not

- Grandmother knows Adrian preserved the workshop and waited for Alex to be ready.
- Tomas knows Adrian asked him to guard the old door.
- Bi11bot knows Adrian's mistakes but not Adrian's final fate.
- Adrian's collaborators know their contributions were obscured by House systems.
- Lock knows House surveillance has identified the collaborators and can convene review.
- Mera knows why Adrian entrusted her with a Covenant band, but her full institutional position remains open.
- Alpha knows Alex's movement and records but cannot legitimately decide the human obligations at stake.

### Objective truth left unresolved

- Adrian's final fate and current whereabouts.
- The full terms and lenders behind Adrian's debt.
- Mera's formal office and ultimate affiliation.
- The Covenant's later enforcement limits beyond the provisional process established here.
- Whether Alex ultimately remains in Prosperity or leaves after the larger system changes.
- The final legal treatment of House charters, land and external trade.
