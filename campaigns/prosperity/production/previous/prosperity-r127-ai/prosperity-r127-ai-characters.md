# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is seventeen years old, lean, wiry and approximately average height, with a compact mechanic's build shaped by lifting tools and working around machinery. He has mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended features: a strong brow, wide-set dark brown eyes, a straight nose, a firm mouth and a jaw that recalls Adrian without making Alex a younger copy of him; his warm medium-brown skin is usually marked by dust, grease and small work scratches. His thick dark brown hair is cut short at the sides and left uneven on top, with a stubborn lock that falls toward his forehead. He wears worn charcoal mining trousers, a rust-orange work shirt, a patched dark utility jacket, heavy scuffed work boots, fingerless work gloves and a weathered tool bag. His silhouette is narrow, forward-leaning and practical, with one shoulder often weighted by the tool bag. He wears Adrian's old steel watch on his left wrist: a worn steel wristwatch, slightly too large for him, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that stays dark; Adrian's looping equation is engraved on the steel caseback. Alex keeps the watch half hidden under his sleeve or covers its face with his thumb. His signature detail is the habit of placing his palm against machinery to feel vibration before he trusts a gauge. In the 2070 prologue, Alex is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, lightly stubbled, and still wears a worn work jacket over a T-shirt with scuffed boots and Adrian's old steel watch on his left wrist.

**Voice** — Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete lines shaped by machinery and physical evidence. He is skeptical, mildly abrasive and sarcastic when defensive; he does not naturally use civic or academic abstractions. He trusts what a pump, pipe, gate or person actually does more than an official label.

- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic, pump worker and inherited claimant to Adrian's intellectual estate.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel, escape the debt restriction and learn what happened to Adrian.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must learn that freedom is not merely departure; it requires accepting responsibility for costs, collaborators and people who cannot simply leave.
- **Leverage / capability:** Exceptional mechanical intuition, physical courage, practical repair skill and the ability to recognize when official records disagree with working machinery.
- **Vulnerability:** He is seventeen, debt-restricted, financially responsible for Grandmother's care and emotionally exposed by his lack of direct memories of Adrian.
- **Mistaken belief:** He initially believes a private escape is the clearest form of freedom and that inherited responsibility can be outrun.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly abandon Grandmother or leave a person in immediate danger to protect his own route out.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He publishes the Measure's verified evidence even though keeping it private would preserve his safest route to Halyard.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada continually connects his choices to the people who carry their consequences; Adrian's collaborators force him to separate finding work from owning it.
- **Pressure sequence:** He repairs an unauthorized pump, activates the Legacy Protocol, rescues Tomas despite losing 40 credits, publishes the 62% / 18% discrepancy, refuses Lock's transfer and names the contributors behind the purifier design.

**Arc in this book** — Alex begins as a capable, defensive teenager who wants to escape Keel, Adrian's reputation and a 418,607-credit liability he did not create. He first chooses investigation over hearsay, then chooses public evidence over a private escape, and finally chooses contributor-owned licensing over guaranteed personal relief through one dominant buyer. He does not become a ruler or solve debt and scarcity. His proof of change is that he accepts visible risk while preserving independent ownership and shared authorship.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is twenty years old, compact, strong and approximately average height, with a steady, rooted posture shaped by life and work in Keel. She has mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla ancestry, a broad expressive face, warm olive-brown skin, a straight nose, high cheekbones, freckles across her cheeks and nose, and light teal-blue eyes. Her long copper hair is wind-tangled and usually gathered loosely at the nape, with escaped strands lifting in the basin wind. She wears a dark high-collared work top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, durable charcoal trousers, worn boots and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck; a ration card or folded hand-drawn map is often tucked into the strap. Her silhouette is grounded and triangular, with her feet planted and shoulders square while Alex leans toward exits. Her signature detail is a thumb resting on one rivet of her shoulder strap when she is thinking or refusing to answer quickly.

**Voice** — Ada speaks in short, direct Keel language, but she notices obligations, avoidance, fear and who carries the burden rather than machinery first. She is warm without being soft, and she lets silence expose what another person is avoiding.

- “Not everything has a price, Alex.”
- “You don't get to make that choice for them.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Keel resident, longtime friend, practical investigator and community organizer.
- **Immediate want:** Make Keel worth staying in and keep Alex's choices connected to the people affected by them.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must learn that leaving temporarily to gather necessary evidence is not the same as abandoning Keel.
- **Leverage / capability:** Local infrastructure knowledge, hand-drawn mapping, social trust, practical planning and an instinct for hidden obligations.
- **Vulnerability:** Keel's scarcity is personal; every trip, ration and risk can deprive someone else.
- **Mistaken belief:** She initially treats staying as the most reliable proof of commitment and suspects departure is a form of surrender.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex decide for other people without asking who bears the cost.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She saves part of her own water ration for Alex, then later chooses to travel with him because Keel needs information more than it needs another person standing still.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's wish to leave forces her to distinguish escape from purposeful movement; Grandmother's care makes her account for practical consequences.
- **Pressure sequence:** She challenges the story about Adrian, marks the hidden pipe on her map, stays after learning the danger of the Legacy Protocol, organizes Keel's travel support and joins Alex in Halyard as an investigator rather than a follower.

**Arc in this book** — Ada begins as the person insisting that Keel must become worth staying in while Alex dreams of leaving. She becomes an active investigator who accepts movement as a tool rather than a betrayal of home. Her proof of change is that she travels to Halyard for evidence Keel needs while continuing to judge every choice by who carries its cost. She and Alex remain close friends, never romantic partners.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a rugged, weathered industrial robot with an oversized round head of segmented faded industrial-blue panels; two large round warm-amber optical eyes in heavy metal rings; side-mounted round ear assemblies; broad rounded shoulders; angular chest armor marked with a white `Bi11`; exposed pistons, articulated metal fingers, compact legs and broad feet; chipped mustard-yellow paint and stripe; exposed steel, dark joints, rivets, scratches and rust. His right eye ring clicks or rotates when focusing. He wears an old scarf. He can fold up to fit in a backpack. His silhouette is broad-shouldered and compact, never threatening. He is expressive through his two warm optical eyes and practical gestures, never through a human face. He is never visually menacing.

**Voice** — Bi11bot is dry, economical, patient, precise and warm underneath. He uses engineering, probability and financial language only when the moment requires it, teaching through a concrete example, a question and a consequence rather than a lecture.

- “Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes.”
- “Who carries the downside?”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Adrian's industrial robot, guide, archive and physical carrier of the safeguards' trail.
- **Immediate want:** Guide Alex to evidence without deciding for him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must shift from waiting to execute Adrian's instructions toward trusting Alex's judgment when no instruction covers the choice.
- **Leverage / capability:** Mechanical knowledge, stored maps and recordings, projections, probability analysis, financial literacy and the ability to fold into a tool bag or carry the Measure.
- **Vulnerability:** He is old, fallible, dependent on damaged records and unable to replace human responsibility.
- **Mistaken belief:** He does not fully mistake facts, but he begins with an inherited assumption that Adrian's prepared route is sufficient for Alex.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not choose the central moral decision for Alex.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He refuses to optimize Alex's choice between private escape and public evidence, saying, “Which is why I'm not choosing.”
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex repeatedly tests Bi11bot's claims against physical evidence; Ada prevents him from treating Alex as the only person whose judgment matters.
- **Pressure sequence:** He awakens, explains Alpha and the safeguards, assists the mine entry without becoming a solution, carries the Measure and helps translate ownership risk without claiming authority.

**Arc in this book** — Bi11bot begins as a dormant machine waiting for the correct person to activate Adrian's plan. He becomes an active companion who offers evidence, costs and questions while respecting Alex's agency. His proof of change is restraint: he can calculate the likely outcomes but leaves the consequential choices to people.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man in his late thirties in the surviving recordings and photographs, lean, long-limbed and slightly stooped from years over workbenches. He has pale olive skin, dark ash-brown hair receding slightly at the temples, grey-green eyes, a narrow nose, a strong jaw and a tired half-smile that makes him visibly related to Alex without making them duplicates. He wears a faded work shirt with sleeves rolled to the elbows, a patched brown utility vest, dark trousers and scuffed workshop boots. His silhouette is angular and forward-curving, often bent over diagrams or a physical prototype. His signature detail is a looping equation written on paper, metal, glass or the caseback of Alex's watch.

**Voice** — Adrian speaks plainly and personally, with affection that does not excuse him. He acknowledges uncertainty and failure instead of presenting himself as a flawless mentor.

- “If you're seeing this... then you found the workshop.”
- “Question everything. Especially me.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Inventor, researcher, collaborator and absent father.
- **Immediate want:** Build safeguards that let people check conditions, recognize contribution and make accountable agreements.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to protect human choice but repeatedly gambles with money, trust and the safety of the people around him.
- **Leverage / capability:** Brilliant systems thinking, invention, physical prototyping, mathematical modeling and relationships with collaborators.
- **Vulnerability:** Debt, overreach, unfinished work, disappearance and the harm his absence causes Alex and Grandmother.
- **Mistaken belief:** He believes a sufficiently careful trail can prepare Alex for responsibility without Adrian being present to answer for his own choices.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly claim sole authorship over work made with collaborators.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He divides the safeguards and purification work among people and physical locations so no single institution or inheritor can simply take control.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's future judgment pressures Adrian retrospectively; Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren force him to treat collaboration as ownership rather than assistance.
- **Pressure sequence:** He borrows for research, leaves debts and harm, builds Bi11bot and the safeguards, hides the Measure near a false record and leaves Alex a method instead of a finished answer.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian's present-tense arc is unresolved because his disappearance remains unexplained. Retrospectively, he changes from a gambling rumor and absent creditor into a brilliant, loving and seriously fallible collaborator. His proof of meaningful legacy is not that Alex obeys him; it is that Alex questions him and improves on his ownership model.

- **Vulnerability:** Not established.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is an older European-descended woman with a small, frail frame, thin silver hair usually pinned loosely behind her head, pale weathered skin, soft grey eyes and a lined face that can become suddenly sharp when her memory clears. She wears a repaired rust-brown cardigan over a faded cream blouse, a long charcoal skirt and soft house shoes. Her silhouette is seated, folded inward around her hands, with a small cloth constantly being folded and refolded. Her modest Keel apartment contains handmade quilts, repaired furniture, old books, faded family photographs, potted plants in recycled containers and a tall bookshelf concealing Adrian's workshop. Her signature detail is the small cloth held between her hands even during a moment of clarity.

**Voice** — Her memory is unstable, but her clearest lines are deliberate and quietly authoritative. She sometimes calls Alex “Adrian” and asks whether Adrian has come home; she does not sentimentalize the danger she has preserved.

- “He said you'd know when you were ready.”
- “I believed you. I just didn't know it yet.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Alex's grandmother, caregiver and guardian of Adrian's hidden workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex safe, supplied with medicine and connected to the truth she believes Adrian was working toward.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must release Alex into danger even though protecting him has defined her remaining role.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knowledge of Adrian's workshop, physical custody of the book and access mechanism, emotional authority over Alex.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory loss, frailty, dependence on medicine and the practical limits of living in Keel.
- **Mistaken belief:** She is not mistaken about Adrian's work, but she cannot make Alex accept her account by insisting on it.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Adrian's workshop until Alex is ready.
- **Decision only she would make:** She quietly preserves the workshop for years and gives Alex access without demanding that he forgive Adrian.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's need for medicine and care makes his departure a real cost rather than an abstract adventure.
- **Pressure sequence:** She gives Alex the book, permits the workshop to open, confirms Adrian's message and arranges for Nell to care for her while Alex travels.

**Arc in this book** — Grandmother begins as the quiet guardian of Adrian's materials and Alex's home. She becomes the person who gives Alex permission to leave and trusts him to return. Her proof of change is not recovery; it is deliberate release.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Keel miner with a broad, weathered build, medium-brown skin, deep-set dark eyes, a broken-nose ridge and short iron-grey hair under a battered work helmet. He wears a patched charcoal mining jacket, dust-stained trousers, heavy boots and a worn miner's wrist guard bearing a carved balanced water-drop mark. After the Site 6 accident, his lower leg is bruised and wrapped. His silhouette is sturdy and slightly stooped from years underground. His signature detail is the carved water-drop on his wrist guard and the careful way he oils an old lock by hand.

**Voice** — Tomas speaks in compressed, dry Keel language shaped by physical work. He treats courage as a practical behavior, not a speech.

- “You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you.”
- “Somebody has to take the blame for the button.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Older miner, local worker and keeper of the hidden old-mine entrance.
- **Immediate want:** Protect the old route and survive the consequences of helping Alex.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must decide whether preserving Adrian's private instruction still matters more than acting publicly for Keel.
- **Leverage / capability:** Mine knowledge, worker trust, local memory and access to the unwired door.
- **Vulnerability:** Injury, wage dependence, monitoring and the risk of being blamed for the emergency shutdown.
- **Mistaken belief:** He initially believes he can preserve the obligation quietly without needing to know what Alex will do with it.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not open the old route for someone who has not shown they will stop for another worker.
- **Decision only he would make:** He creates a credible accident and takes responsibility for the shutdown so Alex can reach the door.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's rescue proves that Adrian's test of character still operates in the present.
- **Pressure sequence:** He watches Alex, trusts him after the rescue, gives him the hand-cut key, protects him from Foreman-7 and helps workers adjust the diversion safely.

**Arc in this book** — Tomas begins as a guarded worker preserving one private promise. Alex's choice to rescue him converts that promise into active trust. Tomas then helps turn the Measure's private evidence into collective, careful action.

She has olive skin, dark brown hair cut into a precise jaw-length bob, dark hazel eyes, a long composed face and an upright posture that rarely relaxes. She wears a pearl-grey structured jacket over a graphite high-collared blouse, narrow dark trousers, polished flat shoes and a small amber authorization band at her wrist; no House insignia identifies her affiliation. **Causality**

- **Leverage / capability:** Access to Oasis monitoring systems, Legacy Protocol alerts and field agents.

- **Mistaken belief:** Not established.
- **Boundary at the start:** She chooses tracking rather than immediate seizure or report.
- **Decision only she would make:** She selects **TRACK** when the Legacy Protocol activates and later permits Alex to reach the Maker's Seal without intervention.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's independent choices force her to move from distant observation toward direct contact.
- **Pressure sequence:** She tracks the first activation, receives the field report from the train, observes the Maker's Seal activation and declares that Alex will need the Covenant from her directly.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock** — Director Cassian Lock is a composed adult man in his late forties to early fifties, tall and lean, with silver at his temples, pale olive skin, close-trimmed dark-and-silver hair, light grey eyes and a calm, narrow face. He wears a charcoal suit with a platinum-grey shirt, polished dark shoes and no House crest or insignia. His silhouette is long, upright and unhurried, with his hands usually loosely clasped in front of him. He is accompanied by aides and, when appropriate, security drones at a respectful distance. His signature detail is that he looks at the work, registry or projected record before he looks at the person who may own it.

**Voice** — Lock speaks in complete, professional Halyard language with commercial fluency and administrative euphemism. He makes coercive choices sound like ordinary accommodation and never needs to raise his voice.

- “A simple assignment of rights.”
- “We have the capital, factories, engineers and customers.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Halyard director and representative of Charter House administration.
- **Immediate want:** Bring Adrian's estate and Alex's purifier design into concentrated House ownership.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes scale makes useful technology real, but his solution to risk removes the independent ownership and competition that could challenge House dominance.
- **Leverage / capability:** Debt clearance, administrative access, capital, factories, engineers, customers, surveillance reports and Charter House review procedures.
- **Vulnerability:** He cannot simply seize work without exposing the ownership question; Alex's refusal makes the House system appear less inevitable.
- **Mistaken belief:** He assumes a materially superior institutional offer is enough to define the rational choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** He offers transfer and review rather than immediate physical violence.
- **Decision only he would make:** He uses the collaborators' exposure as pressure while continuing to frame the offer as protection.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex forces Lock to confront an ownership model that is commercially credible without House control.
- **Pressure sequence:** He offers to clear Alex's debt, warns of review, acknowledges the independent design's value and answers with accelerated House production when Alex refuses.

**Arc in this book** — Lock's position remains steady. He begins as a courteous administrator offering a clean exit and ends as a credible institutional counterforce whose Houses can scale faster, but cannot erase the legitimacy of independent ownership.

### DR. LINA VEY

- “I corrected him. Frequently.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Membrane chemist, House Calder worker and Adrian's collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Make the purification work safe and useful for Keel.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must risk institutional stability to preserve contribution and ownership without pretending the Houses have no legitimate capacity.
- **Leverage / capability:** Membrane chemistry, House Calder facility access, technical authority and custody of the Maker's Seal.

- **Boundary at the start:** She will not hand over her piece or the Seal to someone who has not demonstrated understanding.
- **Decision only she would make:** She keeps the Maker's Seal until Alex names the collaborators and refuses sole ownership.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's willingness to identify what he did not invent changes her assessment of him.
- **Pressure sequence:** She gives Alex the membrane shard, reveals the contaminated Keel flow, joins the compact prototype and presents the Seal after the ownership choice.

**Arc in this book** — Lina begins as a guarded senior collaborator who expects inherited claimants to overlook the people behind the patent. She becomes a participant in a contributor-owned licensing venture while accepting continued House employment and institutional risk.

### SURI ANIK

She wears a worn pale research coat over a dark green shirt, practical trousers, soft laboratory shoes and a narrow wrist sensor stained with biofilter medium. Her silhouette is narrow and inward-focused, often surrounded by sample jars and living root systems. **Voice** — Suri speaks in quiet, technical sentences with stubborn personal conviction. She describes living systems as things that can fail visibly and require continued care.

**Causality**

- **Public role:** House research worker and biofilter specialist.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the living biofilter design functioning long enough to prove it works.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must accept collaborators and commercial risk after surviving by working alone.
- **Leverage / capability:** Biofilter research, experimental persistence and knowledge of failure conditions.

- **Boundary at the start:** She will not surrender the biofilter component without evidence that it will be kept alive and credited.
- **Decision only she would make:** She continues testing a design everyone else has abandoned because she believes the failure is informative.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's practical willingness to redesign rather than claim her work lets her re-enter collaboration.
- **Pressure sequence:** She gives Alex her shard, joins the compact unit, redesigns the biofilter's power cycle and accepts contributor ownership.

**Arc in this book** — Suri begins isolated at an abandoned line of research. She becomes an active collaborator once Alex treats her as a maker rather than a source of inherited property. Her proof of change is choosing a risky shared build over quiet continuation alone.

### FOREMAN-7

**Visual lock** — Foreman-7 is a middle-aged Keel mine foreman with a broad, compact build, dark brown skin, a shaved head, tired eyes and a square jaw. He wears a patched charcoal-and-oxidized-orange mine supervisor's jacket, reinforced trousers, heavy boots, a white-and-amber control node mounted at the chest and a battered radio clipped to his belt. His silhouette is blocky and supervisory, with his body habitually positioned between workers and restricted doors. His signature detail is the cold scanner light reflecting across his chest node when he is forced to enforce a rule he does not control.

**Voice** — Foreman-7 uses direct work language and official wording when pressure requires him to document a violation. He is not a sadist and does not perform villainy.

- “Worker Phantum. Bay 6.”
- “Obstructing Alpha's officer is an offence.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Site 6 foreman responsible for worker assignments, safety and Alpha compliance.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the mine operating and avoid being blamed for a system breach.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must choose between enforcing the assignment boundary and recognizing that workers are in danger.
