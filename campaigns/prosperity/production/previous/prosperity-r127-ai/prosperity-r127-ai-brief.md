# Prosperity — Brief

## Logline

When seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic Alex Phantum inherits his missing father’s 418,607-credit debt and discovers that Prosperity’s governing system is hiding water and erasing ownership, he must follow Adrian’s trail of safeguards and choose between personal relief through concentrated power or a riskier future built on evidence, shared responsibility and independent ownership.

## Theme

Freedom is not escape from obligation; it is the ability to make costs, ownership and authority visible and answerable.

## Tone & comps

- **The Dispossessed** — material scarcity, labor and ownership made concrete; take the political seriousness without turning the book into a lecture.
- **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — damaged environments, ecological consequence and humane conflict.
- **Arrival** — restrained speculative wonder, emotional revelation and mysteries that change the meaning of earlier evidence.

A tactile YA science-fiction adventure and mystery first; an infrastructure and ownership thriller second. Humor is dry and work-shaped. The book teaches through choices, tools, consequences and behavior, never through lectures.

## Audience & rating

Older YA, approximately ages 15–18, including readers who need immediate clarity about systems, stakes and objectives.

**PG-13:** peril, poverty, coercive institutions, grief, displacement, implied death, mild language and sustained ethical conflict. No graphic gore.

## Format

- **64 pages total**, no more and no fewer.
- Four-page prologue, set in **2070**.
- Chapters 1–6 occupy the remaining sixty pages and remain chapters, not issues.
- The current development material settles the prologue and Chapters 1–3; Chapters 4–6 must preserve the canon below.
- Default page rhythm: 4–6 panels, with full pages or sparse pages only when the reveal, scale or emotional pause earns them.
- Pages must be legible at first read. One clear dramatic action per panel; lettering is designed with the image, not added afterward.
- Every chapter ends on a forward-driving question or decision.

## Visual direction

Inked graphic-novel realism with heavy contour, textured hatching, painterly digital color, expressive acting and tactile architectural detail. The future is mostly recognizable infrastructure aged, repaired and institutionalized, with emerging technology ordinary and genuinely speculative mechanisms rare.

Prosperity is one mixed Andean high-desert basin, not three ethnic enclaves. Indigenous Andean, mestizo, European-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed and international populations live across all three cities. Class, access, occupation, health, clothing, grooming, speech, environment and institutional power create the principal visual distinctions.

- **Keel:** compressed, wind-scoured and dignified; rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust, patched machinery, narrow channels, old pipes and crowded housing.
- **Halyard:** productive and commercially polished; bronze, gold and engineered green; laboratories, fabrication towers, maker districts, House facilities and elevated rail.
- **Oasis:** spacious, quiet and controlled; pearl-white architecture, reservoirs, geometric gardens, formal transit and clean institutional interiors.

Alpha has no face, body or human-like consciousness. Show it through white-and-amber control nodes, cold displays, gates, records, drones, restrictions and administrative language.

Alex remains visibly shaped by Keel as he moves upward. Bi11bot is rugged, foldable, scarfed and expressive through two warm optical eyes: patient, dry, useful and fallible, never menacing or a substitute for human judgment. TJ appears only in the 2070 prologue, if used there; he does not appear in the 2043 story.

## Canon

Everything marked **FIXED** in `characters.md`, `world.md` and `story.md` is canon. The following decisions settle the material’s open points and control contradictions.

### World and institutions

- Prosperity is a fictional Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony in **2043**. No real country, external government or official basin name is used.
- Keel is the lowest city, built around exhausted lithium workings and active extraction. Halyard is the middle technical-industrial city. Oasis is the wealthy upper administrative city, partly above the cloud layer.
- One infrastructure spine carries water, energy, transit, communications and Alpha-connected control nodes through all three cities. Shared infrastructure does not mean equal access.
- The Charter Houses are **House Calder**—water and chemistry, jade-teal; **House Ivers**—energy and compute, amber-orange; and **House Steyn**—mining and launch, steel-violet. These names supersede conflicting draft names.
- The Houses are legitimate, capable private institutions that scale technology, provide work and operate major systems. The conflict is concentrated ownership, protected dominance and suppressed competition—not honest business, private property, profit or markets.
- Alpha began as emergency coordination after the Water Wars. Its temporary decision-making authority became permanent. It remains a distributed administrative and predictive network, not a person or company.
- Alpha can analyze, predict, route, allocate, authenticate, monitor, restrict and publish records. It cannot legitimately decide human responsibility, ownership, consent or collective values.
- The three safeguards are physical and bounded:
  - **The Measure** verifies real conditions against official records and traces discrepancies.
  - **The Maker’s Seal** verifies contribution and rightful ownership.
  - **The Covenant** enables legitimate agreements and shared accountability.
- Possession alone does nothing. The safeguards require deliberate human action and do not magically repair infrastructure, transfer property or compel promises.
- The completed authority-return mechanism is not a shutdown switch. Useful infrastructure and non-discretionary Alpha functions remain necessary; only unaccountable governing authority can be withdrawn.

### Money, water and debt

- Money is always measured in **credits**.
- Alex receives a household liability transfer of **418,607 credits**, with projected clearance of **28.7 years** and restricted travel.
- The **62% reported / 18% delivered** comparison uses one consistent rolling 30-day denominator: reported allocation to Keel versus physically delivered Keel residential flow.
- The contaminated Keel Homes pipe is a separate consequence. More water through old lithium workings can still be cloudy and unsafe.
- Finding an artifact never pays Alex’s debt. Every reduction must be shown as a payment, wage, repair, royalty or other earned transaction.
- Keel residents satisfy the overdue travel condition without cancelling Alex’s inherited debt. The community payment is visibly raised to make the balance current and open the rail crossing.
- The compact purifier is a **91% simulated field-test design**, not finished potable-water infrastructure or a solution to all scarcity.
- Its contributor-owned venture licenses the design to independent businesses for upfront fees and royalties, with a maintenance and warranty reserve. Honest businesses may build, sell, service and profit from it.
- The future frame shows paid ownership, competing independent companies and a debt paid in full; it does not present a giveaway, collective ownership without owners or abolition of markets.

### People and relationships

- Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything. **Nothing else about her is learned in this book.**
- Alex and Ada remain close friends, never romantic partners. Ada has independent agency and continually tests the human cost of Alex’s choices.
- Adrian was brilliant, loving and seriously fallible. He gambled, borrowed for research, failed, worked with collaborators and left real harm behind. His disappearance remains unresolved.
- Mera Vale is the Oasis-based watcher introduced by the choice to **TRACK**. Her formal role, institutional affiliation and complete motive remain deliberately open until the later chapters require a decision.
- Alex does not become ruler of Alpha or Prosperity. He becomes a proposer and convenor who must accept public scrutiny and consequences.
- Alex’s debt, House resistance, ownership disputes, scarcity and disagreement survive the ending.

## What we take from the material

**Keep and build:** the four-page 2070 prologue; Alex’s pump repair and debt transfer; Ada’s ration-saving friendship and disagreement over leaving Keel; Grandmother and the hidden workshop; Adrian’s watch, recording and mixed legacy; Bi11bot’s awakening and financial questions; the Water Wars and Alpha’s permanence; Site 6; Tomas’s rescue and preserved door; the Measure and the 62%/18% discrepancy; public evidence and Keel’s collective response; the train crossing; Halyard’s registry and House system; Lock’s materially credible offer; Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren; the compact purifier; the Maker’s Seal; Mera’s tracking; and the unresolved path toward the Covenant.

**Use the causal spine:** Alex activates the Legacy Protocol; enters the mine; rescues Tomas at a wage cost; follows physical evidence over Alpha’s map; publishes the Measure’s findings instead of escaping privately; accepts community help without treating it as debt cancellation; refuses Lock’s transfer; recognizes the collaborators’ ownership; builds a commercially viable independent alternative; and continues toward the Covenant.

**Set aside:** explanatory lectures, riddle-like captions, any implication that the Measure moves water, any implication that the purifier ends scarcity, any romance between Alex and Ada, all conflicting House names, any future information about Alex’s mother, and any scene or prop that turns Alpha into a human-like villain.
