# Analysis: *Prosperity*, pages 1–64

Read cold, pages only. Page parity is from the script: odd pages are on the right, even pages on the left, so a reveal lands best on an even page, just after the reader turns.

---

## The political read

| Reading | Signal | Pages that carry it |
|---|---|---|
| **Progressive** | **Strong on the surface** | p12: debt is passed to a 17-year-old by a "PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER". p14: residents queue at a dry ration channel while Halyard's lights glow above. p28: "REPORTED 62% / DELIVERED 18%", and Ada asks "Who gets the rest?" Alex: "Someone with a full pipe." p34: "OWNERSHIP: CALDERA HOUSE / CREATOR: UNLISTED / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS". The words "affected people" and "community supply" (pp51–52, 61–63). |
| **Socialist** | **Some** | p29: workers "close ranks" against the foreman. That reads as a walkout. p63: the ledger has "EQUAL PROFIT SHARES". p62, Tomas: "Owned units aren't delivered water." On its own that line can read as being against owning things. |
| **Capitalist in a bad way** | **Some, and it is ambiguous** | The Houses (Caldera, Vector, Orbital), "Director" Lock, clerks who drag Lina through a shutter (p46), and "AREA CONSOLIDATION — EFFICIENCY REVIEW" (p38). The pages never say whether a House is a company, a guild or a chartered arm of the state. A 16-year-old will default to "megacorp". |
| **Libertarian** | **Strong in the story** | Alex refuses the debt-clearing assignment (p35–36). He refuses the executive lever ("No executive designation," p59). Central authority is withdrawn: "EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED" (p60). Property is left alone: "EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS" (p60). Verification is spread out instead of trusted to one authority (pp28, 60–62). Alpha, a faceless central system, is the real antagonist (pp20, 57). |
| **Conservative** | **Some** | Alex keeps his word to Tomas (pp15, 23, 63), cares for his grandmother, and pays down the debt himself instead of escaping it (p63: 418,607 falls to 402,110). Leona taking on a public obligation (p52). |
| **Capitalist in a good way** | **Strong at the end, weak before it** | A customer buys repairable equipment. Ren on p40: "build it so a customer can repair it." The honest warning label on p42 ("NOT A POTABLE-OUTPUT GUARANTEE"). The small shop on p63 with a real ledger: customer payment, materials, maintenance, wages. The informal loan, with the terms written down (p15) and paid in full (p63). |

**Surface versus story.** Issues 01 and 02 open like a dystopia about debt and the company town: a corporate-looking system takes the water and takes the inventor's credit. That invites the progressive reading. The story under it argues for something else. The villains are a central planner (Alpha) and privileged charter-holders. The hero refuses every handout and every crown. Property records stay intact. The ending is a customer-serving shop and a loan repaid.

**Who stops reading because of the surface.** A conservative-leaning teacher or parent flipping through Issue 01 sees debt bondage, a foreman in a harness and workers closing ranks on p29, and may shelve the book before Issue 03. A student who reads only Issues 00–01 for the assignment comes away with "the system is rigged." The rescue of that reading comes from p35 on.

**Does the book conclude, or leave a reader concluding, that free enterprise is bad?** **No.** Pp35–36 and 59 refuse the bailout and the central control. P60 leaves ownership records unchanged. P63 makes an honest business the resolution. The residual risks are pp34, 44 and 62, covered below.

## Young Americans Center

**What they would like.**
- A teenager borrows to buy medicine and writes the terms on a receipt (p15: "Make it real").
- He misses a payment, shows up anyway ("You brought your face. / Lost the money."), proposes a revised schedule, and later pays in full (p23, p63: "MEDICINE ADVANCE: PAID / WEEKLY TERMS: COMPLETE"). That is the lesson of their bank, made into drama.
- He refuses to have the big debt wiped by a powerful man (pp35–36) or by taking power (p59), and the balance at the end is lower because he earned it down (p63).
- A business that serves customers: a repairable product (p40), an honest warning label (p42), a ledger a teacher could put on a whiteboard (p63).
- Integrity under pressure: he gives up 40 credits to save Tomas (p24).
- "Then we give it a smaller failure" (p41) is a good line about being willing to fail.

**What would bother them.**
- The opening 28 pages look like an argument that the system is rigged and effort does not matter: 28.7 years of debt, travel restricted, wages forfeited. A teacher who previews Issue 01 sees that first.
- The Houses are undefined, so students will treat them as "corporations" (p34).
- The debt transfer is presented with no sign that it is unreal (p12).
- The partisan-sounding civic language and the walkout image (pp29, 61).
- The book is hard to follow for their actual users. Alpha, the Covenant band, the Convenor, the Maker's Seal and "Morrow & Sons" are never explained. Lina is taken on p46 and never rescued on the page, yet she is on the shop sign on p63.
- The time order of pp22–24 is confusing: he is reassigned "immediately," it is "Friday evening," and then Tomas is pinned at Site 6.

**Likely verdict.** Partly satisfied. The values are right and the loan subplot is close to ideal. But the first half could be pulled from a classroom on a skim, and students will hand in confused summaries.

## Bill Reynolds

**What he would like.**
- His beer-hall story is here in miniature: "Lost the money," a revised note, payments made, paid off (pp23, 63).
- The windfall is refused twice: Lock's "DEBT: CLEARED" (p35) and the executive lever (p58–59).
- Earned success: the balance falls only by work (p63).
- Ownership made concrete. P34 ("CREATOR: UNLISTED") is de Soto's dead capital: work nobody has recorded. P44 puts five names on the public record ("NAMED CONTRIBUTORS..."). He would recognise that.
- A faceless central system is the villain, and the human decision is pushed back to many people (p57: "Alpha can transfer the old authority. It cannot choose who should hold it.").
- The mentor is wrong and asks instead of telling (p25: "I predicted we could leave him... / You were wrong. / Yes. What would you do?"). The word "capitalism" never appears.

**What would bother him.**
- P44: Alex alone presses "PUBLIC RELEASE" and makes a design owned by five people unpatented and nonexclusive. This comes one page after "No one gets to turn five into one." Nothing on the page shows the other four agreeing. The owners giving away their property could be fine; the hero giving away theirs is the opposite of his point about ownership.
- P62: "Owned units aren't delivered water" can be heard as being against owning things, although its target is false reporting.
- Pp56–61 slide into a lecture on how institutions are built.
- The dog may be named for a Founder (TJ for Thomas Jefferson is my inference), but the page never says so, so no reader gets the joke.
- B11 vanishes for most of Issues 02–03. In that respect he does not replace people, which Reynolds would approve of. But it leaves the mentor thin.

**Does B11 sound like him?** No, by the profile's own voice notes. Reynolds is plain, slow, roundabout and Western: a question, then a story from his own life, then one plain line, with jokes at his own expense. B11 is clipped, dry and epigrammatic, closer to a deadpan butler robot:
- "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." (p18)
- "A fact with a poor record." (p18)
- "It is a route. The destination objected." (p30)

B11 does have some of him:
- He asks questions ("Do you want answers or an exit?" p19; "What would you do?" p25).
- He makes jokes at his own expense ("I am carrying less than I appear to be," p31; "I may have mistaken that for an instruction," p54).
- He is wrong and admits it (p25).

But he never tells a story of his own failure, never says anything Western, and never takes the long way round. Reynolds would see his questions on the page, but not his voice.

**Likely verdict.** Partly satisfied, close to yes. The plot carries his core beliefs, but his avatar does not sound like him and p44 cuts against his view of property.

## A comic writer

**Hook.** The cold open is strong and visual. A robot dog comes apart in mid-stride and its head glides across a dark lake to sabotage a water-theft drone (pp1–7). It is mostly silent and it is easy to draw. "Let's see what they came to take" (p1) is a good first line.

**Page turns.** The turns are well disciplined. The big reveals land on left-hand pages:
- p12: the debt balance
- p18: B11 unfolds
- p20: the Alpha trace
- p28: the 62%/18% gap
- p34: the 312 patents
- p58: the lever

Someone in the room knows the form.

**Splashes.** There are none. All 64 pages are four-panel grids. Three entrances are wasted as single panels: the Halyard reveal (p33 P1), Oasis above the clouds (p48 P1), and the hidden workshop (p17 P2). A pro would call the rhythm monotonous.

**Dialogue.** It is tight and quotable. "You brought your face" (p23). "I'm about to become paperwork" (p26). "Don't hold it like a crown" (p44). But everyone speaks in the same aphoristic register: thieves, Tomas, Ada, Leona, B11, Mera. Some lines are riddles a first-time reader cannot decode:
- "I traded days I could prevent for years I couldn't see" (p50)
- "He always sees the part that costs" (p6)
- "Don't make two people the answer for everybody" (p58). Who are the two?

**What they would cut or fix.**
- Ten pages of frame (pp1–10) that never come back.
- The whole-page procedure on pp56 and 61.
- Undefined proper nouns: Alpha, Covenant band, Convenor, Morrow & Sons.
- Lina's capture (p46) is never resolved.
- The granddaughter calls her grandfather "Alex" (p4).
- The p22–24 time order.

**Would they take the job?** Yes, as a rewrite. The bones and the page-turn craft are professional. The Issue 03 civics and the dropped threads are the work.

## A game designer

**There is a game here, and it is the strongest professional read.**

- **Loop:** shift, wage, weekly payment, debt counter. The UI is already written: p12 "PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS," p24 "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR."
- **Choices that cost:**
  - leaving your zone to save Tomas (p24)
  - answers before the exit, "rarely the cheaper order" (p19)
  - Lock's offer to clear the debt (p35)
  - the executive lever (p58)
  - whether to release the design publicly (p44)

  Each has a visible price.
- **Systems a player would push on:**
  - **Investigation:** the Measure's reported-versus-delivered check (p28, p62) works like *Papers, Please*.
  - **Recruiting:** four contributors, each with a condition (pp37–40).
  - **Crafting:** incompatible standards, plus Suri's culture that dies if it overheats (p41).
  - **Traversal:** TJ's modular body as a puzzle tool (pp3–7, 25, 31).
  - **Reputation:** your lender's trust, a quantified Tomas.
  - **Late game:** a shop run on the p63 ledger.
- YAC already runs simulated towns (Young AmeriTowne), and Reynolds funds learning through games, so the fit is obvious.

**Would they build it?** Yes. The repayment and verification systems are the hook. The governance chapter would become a meta-layer, not a level.

## A streaming showrunner

**Engine.** Several mysteries run at once:
- an inherited debt with a clock on it
- a city whose water is being skimmed
- a vanished gambler-inventor father whose death is uncertain ("PRESUMED DEAD / LEGAL STATUS: MISSING," p55; his hand disappears behind a bulkhead on p54)
- a 2099 frame where Alex is "the old man with everything" and thieves ask "Water paid for it? / Or water made him rich" (p3)

That last question carries a season: did our hero earn it, or did he become what he fought?

**Characters an audience would follow.**
- Alex, Ada and Tomas, who is the best supporting character: lender, miner, keeper of Adrian's door, and the one who stands his ground (pp15, 26, 29, 61).
- TJ is a merchandising dream.
- B11 is underused after Issue 01.

**Season-one cliffhanger.** Several are available:
- Lina taken (p46)
- Adrian possibly alive
- the 2099 component "they didn't want found" (p9)
- Ada not answering (p64)

The 2099 thread is the strongest, and it is never picked up again.

**What is missing.**
- A villain with a face. Alpha has none (p57). Lock disappears after p43. Foreman-7 is a functionary.
- Personal stakes for Alex beyond his ledger. The emotional climax (Ada and Leona, pp49–52) belongs to Ada and plays out away from him.
- A reason Issue 03 is not a city council meeting.

**From the first issue alone:** they would option it, with a note to dramatise the antagonist.

## Does it smell like school

Pages where a scene exists so the reader learns how a structure works, or where the lesson is spoken outright:

1. **p52.** A clerk records Leona as "subject to challenge and recall," and the bands link only after she sits among the affected people. The scene shows an accountability procedure working.
2. **p56.** The whole page lays out three objects, and Mera names their meanings: "Evidence. Attribution. Obligation." Ada: "None of them decides." The page exists to state a framework.
3. **p60.** Alex dictates a constitution: "Three temporary representatives from each affected place. Public records. Recall if they stop answering." The displays then spell out the result.
4. **p61.** The whole page is a demonstration of representative tables with paper strips and instruction strips. It has a good closing line ("Before you stamp it, show the water"), but it is a demonstration.

**Count: 4.** Two of them (pp56, 61) are whole pages that exist to explain.

Borderline pages, not counted:
- p51: Leona states the terms of her oath
- p43: "No one gets to turn five into one," a single moral line, which is acceptable
- p62: "Owned units aren't delivered water"

All four counted pages are in the last twelve pages, so the night-before reader meets them when patience is lowest. These pages teach civics, not money or enterprise.

**The skeptical, AI-wary teen.** The uniform aphoristic voice and the capitalised terminal readouts will feel generated to some of them. So will the trio of named talismans (Measure, Seal, Covenant) and the triad on p56.

**Pages that teach without smelling of it,** because someone needs the thing and pays for it:
- p15: the medicine loan, with the terms on a receipt
- p23: the missed payment and the renegotiation
- p24: the cost of a wage forfeited for a choice
- p28: an audit that compares the reading with the actual pipe
- p40: building something a customer can repair
- p42: an honest product label
- p63: a ledger and a loan paid off

These are the book at its best. Nobody explains anything; people need medicine, water and money.

## What would change the verdicts

1. **p44: the five makers decide the release together, and someone could dissent.** It is their property. *Satisfies Reynolds (ownership is real only if the owners decide) and YAC (integrity).*
2. **pp56–61: compress the civic build into one page and give the freed pages to dramatising the fallout,** such as Lina's capture (p46) or a confrontation with Lock. *Satisfies the teen reader, the comic writer and the school-smell verdict.*
3. **pp33–35: one panel or scene showing a House is a charter-holder protected by the administration, set beside an independent small maker who sells honestly and gets shut out.** *Satisfies YAC and Reynolds: the villain becomes cronyism, not business.*
4. **One scene in Issue 01 or 02 where B11's own earlier wrong call, from his years with Adrian, cost something, he owns it, and the scene ends on his question.** It is an action, not a speech. *Satisfies Reynolds on Bi11bot's voice and character.*
5. **Return to the 2099 frame at the end, tying old Alex's wealth to the shop and the ledger, and answering the thieves' "Or water made him rich" on p3.** *Satisfies the showrunner (it closes the season loop), Reynolds (success that was earned) and the teen who was lost by the opening.*

VERDICT Young Americans Center: partly; Bill Reynolds: partly; smells like school: yes; school pages: 4
