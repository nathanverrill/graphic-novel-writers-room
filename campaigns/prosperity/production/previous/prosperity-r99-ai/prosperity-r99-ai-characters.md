# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is seventeen years old, lean and wiry from pump-hall and mine work, approximately 5'9" tall, with narrow shoulders, strong forearms and scarred, work-rough hands. He has medium-brown skin, a broad Indigenous Andean-inherited nose and cheekbones, a firm jaw and dark brown eyes whose shape visibly echoes his European-descended father Adrian; his thick black hair is cut short with an uneven, self-trimmed edge. He wears a patched charcoal work shirt, rust-brown utility trousers, worn boots, a weathered tool bag and a white-and-amber identity band on his left wrist. His silhouette is compact, forward-leaning and practical, with one hand habitually near a tool or his pocket. His signature detail is a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation, which he turns between his fingers without noticing.**

**Voice** — Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete Keel phrases. He trusts physical evidence over official labels, becomes sarcastic when defensive and describes what something does rather than naming an abstraction.  
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”  
- “It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel pump and extraction worker; Adrian Phantum's son.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel, clear the inherited debt and choose his own future.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants freedom for himself but is tempted to believe one person with enough power could fix everyone else's situation.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads machinery through vibration, pressure, wear and sound; repairs failing infrastructure with limited parts; notices physical contradictions in official records.
- **Vulnerability:** Grandmother's health, his enormous debt, restricted travel and his hunger for a truthful account of his missing father.
- **Mistaken belief:** Escape is the only meaningful form of freedom, and concentrated power might be acceptable if the right person holds it.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly abandon someone in immediate physical danger.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** Broadcast the Measure's evidence to Keel even though doing so destroys his safest escape route.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to consider the people affected by every choice; Adrian's legacy tempts him to mistake inheritance for authority.
- **Pressure sequence:** He accepts the debt, chooses medicine over fuel, rescues Tomas despite wage forfeiture, chooses public evidence over private escape, names the makers, refuses Lock's settlement and refuses Mera's executive authority.
- **Proof of change:** Alex's debt remains visible and unpaid, his travel becomes slower and publicly reviewable, and he refuses the power that could have erased both problems.

**Arc in this book** — Alex moves from wanting only to get out of Keel to accepting responsibility for how decisions affect people who did not choose them. He does not become a ruler. He ends with the freedom to leave or stay as a genuine choice, while still carrying his debt and accepting the consequences of refusing concentrated power.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is twenty years old, tall and steady at approximately 5'8", with a strong, practical build shaped by Keel walking and carrying work. She has warm brown skin, pronounced cheekbones, a broad expressive mouth, freckles across her nose and cheeks, light teal-blue eyes and long copper-brown hair that is usually wind-tangled and tied low with loose strands around her face; her features visibly resemble her Indigenous Andean/mestiza mother Leona while retaining lighter coloring from her other parent. She wears a dark high-collared work top, a rivet-studded strap over one shoulder, durable charcoal trousers, scuffed boots and a muted moss-green neck wrap. Her silhouette is upright, rooted and balanced, with her weight planted before she moves. Her signature detail is the rivet on her shoulder strap, which she touches when thinking or under pressure.**

**Voice** — Ada speaks in compressed, practical Keel language, but she notices obligations, fear and avoidance before machinery. She lets silence work and asks who carries the burden.  
- “Not everything.”  
- “I'm not leaving Keel behind. I'm finding out who decided it had to stay this way.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel worker, lifelong friend and practical organizer.
- **Immediate want:** Help Alex survive without letting him turn escape into another form of abandonment.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants agency over her own life but still measures herself against decisions made by absent adults.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads people, remembers routes and markings, organizes food, tools and timing, and can turn scattered neighbors into coordinated help.
- **Vulnerability:** Leona's abandonment, the fear that safety has always meant someone else deciding for her and the danger of losing Alex to a single-minded plan.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she stays observant and useful, other people's choices cannot define her.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex make a consequential choice on behalf of people who are absent from the room.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** Remain with Alex not because he needs rescuing, but because she wants to see who made Keel's future and confront that person.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Her relationship with Leona forces her to distinguish accountability from forgiveness; her friendship with Alex forces both of them to reject rescue fantasies.
- **Pressure sequence:** She enters the hidden workshop, chooses to stay, organizes Keel's response, crosses the conduit, confronts Leona and refuses a two-person substitute for public decision-making.
- **Proof of change:** Ada publicly supports a process in which affected people can challenge decisions, while refusing both Leona's easy forgiveness and Alex's authority.

**Arc in this book** — Ada moves from surviving the consequences of other people's decisions to demanding a say in how promises are made and broken. She remains Alex's friend, never his romantic counterpart, and ends by claiming the same freedom of choice for herself that she demands for him.

### B11

**Visual lock** — B11 is a small foldable archival robot approximately 18 inches tall when upright, with a compact rectangular torso, two short articulated arms, two broad folding legs, a rounded shoulder projector and a chest compartment. His casing is matte cream and worn graphite with scratches, dust in the seams and small exposed brass fasteners. He has two warm amber optical eyes, including a brass ring around his right eye that clicks or rotates when he focuses, and he wears an old faded rust-red scarf looped twice around his neck. His silhouette is low, compact and companionable, never looming or predatory. His signature detail is the rotating brass ring around his right eye.**

**Voice** — B11 speaks plainly, patiently and with dry humor. He asks a question before offering a conclusion, tells short stories about failure and uses engineering, gambling and household-finance examples without lecturing.  
- “Sealed isn't the same as gone.”  
- “I was wrong. What would you do?”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Adrian's archival robot, companion and guide to the safeguards.
- **Immediate want:** Deliver Adrian's evidence and determine whether Alex can make a better choice than Adrian expected.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He was built to preserve a plan but must learn not to preserve the plan at the expense of the people inside it.
- **Leverage / capability:** Maps hidden infrastructure, carries records and tools, analyzes systems, projects Adrian's messages and builds or repairs small machines.
- **Vulnerability:** Incomplete memory of Adrian's final moments, limited physical reach and a tendency to trust predictions too far.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently careful forecast can keep human risk contained.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not make the consequential human choice for Alex.
- **Decision only B11 would make:** Admit a failed prediction without defending it, then ask Alex what he would do.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex repeatedly rejects B11's safest recommendation; Adrian's final wager makes B11 responsible for more than preserving a record.
- **Pressure sequence:** He wakes Alex, hides the Measure in his chest, predicts Tomas can be left safely, is proven wrong, helps transmit the water evidence and witnesses Alex refuse executive authority.
- **Proof of change:** B11 shifts from guide to witness and accepts that preserving Adrian's intent requires allowing Alex to depart from it.

**Arc in this book** — B11 begins as a dormant inheritance carrying Adrian's unfinished plan. He ends as a fallible companion who supplies evidence, admits uncertainty and leaves judgment with people.

### TJ

**Visual lock** — TJ is a small silent repair robot shaped unmistakably like a dog, approximately 14 inches high at the shoulder when assembled, built from mismatched magnetic ceramic, aluminum and painted steel parts. His head, four legs, torso, tail and ears can separate and work independently before snapping back together. In 2043 his two camera lenses are plain scratched ceramic, and his body carries rust, tool marks and mismatched paint. His silhouette is compact, dog-shaped and slightly lopsided, with a curious forward head and alert tail. His signature detail is the loose magnetic click of his parts reconnecting. In 2099 his right eye has a small adjustable brass lens ring identical to the ring around B11's right eye in 2043.**

**Voice** — TJ never speaks. He communicates through dog-like posture, head tilts, tail position, clicks, whirs, magnetic snaps and purposeful movement.

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Repair-dog companion and access tool.
- **Immediate want:** Reach places B11 and the humans cannot reach and return to them.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He is assembled as a tool but behaves as a companion.
- **Leverage / capability:** Separates into independently moving parts, scouts ducts and gaps, carries a camera and reaches mechanisms.
- **Vulnerability:** Small size, fragile mismatched parts and the possibility of losing a component.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ acts rather than explains.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not abandon a companion while a route back remains possible.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** Leave his head behind as a camera while sending his body and legs through separate mechanical gaps.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** B11 builds him to solve problems B11 cannot solve; Alex later treats him as a living companion rather than equipment.
- **Proof of change:** TJ becomes part of the family's ordinary life and, in 2099, chooses to confront the water thieves while Alex and his granddaughter remain safely ashore.

**Arc in this book** — TJ grows from a newly assembled repair device into a trusted companion. His later rust and missing parts show endurance rather than replacement.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man in his forties in the surviving recordings, approximately 5'11" tall with a lean engineer's build, medium-light olive skin, dark brown hair receding slightly at the temples, a strong jaw and dark eyes whose shape and habitual head tilt visibly echo in Alex. He wears worn technical shirts, a patched work jacket, practical trousers and a stained engineer's apron in the workshop; in gambling memories he wears the same clothes with casino dust and chips on the table. His silhouette is angular and restless, with one hand often marking a diagram or turning a chip. His signature detail is the looping equation scratched into a worn casino chip.**

**Voice** — Adrian is warm, direct and self-questioning, with a builder's habit of admitting uncertainty. He can be tender without excusing the harm he caused.  
- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”  
- “Trust B11. But question everything—especially me.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Engineer, inventor, collaborator, B11's maker and former Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the combined water work available to the people who built it and awaken B11 before Alpha closes the station.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants a future built on shared responsibility while repeatedly harming his own family through gambling and disappearance.
- **Leverage / capability:** Designs systems, reads infrastructure, builds safeguards and preserves collaborators' contributions.
- **Vulnerability:** Gambling addiction, financial ruin, estrangement from Alex and Alpha's ability to classify him as a risk.
- **Mistaken belief:** A carefully planted future can compensate for the damage caused by his absence.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly let Alpha claim the collaborators' work as a single estate.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** Finish B11's transmission as the station door closes and water rises, leaving no body and no clean explanation.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's future judgment pressures Adrian to make his final act serve someone other than himself.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** His final choice protects a possible future, but the book refuses to call it a full redemption; his debt, abandonment and legal absence remain.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian changes through Alex's understanding of him. He begins as the missing gambler whose debt Alex inherits and becomes, without ceasing to be that gambler, a flawed father, collaborator and person who made a final costly choice.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is in her late seventies, short and slight with a soft, stooped posture, pale olive skin, fine white hair braided close to the scalp and deep-set hazel eyes that can shift from confusion to startling precision. She wears a faded charcoal cardigan over a rust-colored house dress, thick socks and soft work slippers suited to Keel dust. Her silhouette is small, folded inward and domestic, but her hands become still and exact when memory returns. Her signature detail is a small square cloth that she folds and refolds when uncertain.**

**Voice** — Her speech is sometimes repetitive or misdirected, but moments of clarity are precise, direct and emotionally unguarded. She may call Alex “Adrian,” then recognize the difference.  
- “He said you'd know when you were ready.”  
- “Then go finish what he started.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex's caregiver and keeper of Adrian's workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex safe while deciding whether he is ready to know what Adrian left.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She protects the truth by hiding it, even though hiding it also preserves the family's isolation.
- **Leverage / capability:** Controls access to the book, workshop and preserved records.
- **Vulnerability:** Long-term memory gaps, medicine needs, physical dependence and grief.
- **Mistaken belief:** Waiting and preserving are safer than forcing a choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Adrian's workshop before Alex can bear what it contains.
- **Decision only she would make:** Give Alex the book on the day his debt transfers, accepting that the workshop may take him away.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's need for truth forces her to choose movement over concealment.
- **Proof of change:** She sets down the cloth, gives him the book and tells him to finish what Adrian started.

**Arc in this book** — Grandmother's change is intimate rather than ideological. She moves from waiting and preserving to releasing Alex into an uncertain future, while remaining dependent on him and affected by the costs of his choices.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Indigenous Andean miner in his early sixties, approximately 5'7" tall with a broad, weathered build, dark brown skin, heavy forearms and a grey-black mustache. His face has deep eye lines, a broad nose and a permanently squinting expression from dust and work lights. He wears a patched charcoal mining jacket, reinforced trousers, battered boots, a scuffed helmet and a worn leather wrist guard marked with the balanced water-drop symbol. His silhouette is square and work-heavy. His signature detail is the oiled old key he carries for the hidden door near Site 6.**

**Voice** — Tomas speaks in dry, understated Keel language and treats dangerous resistance as an ordinary task that still has a cost.  
- “You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you.”  
- “Then it can offend all of us.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Older miner, local memory-holder and keeper of a hidden door.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the Site 6 route available and prevent Alex from being isolated by enforcement.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to protect people quietly but knows quiet protection cannot expose the water discrepancy.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knows mine routes, emergency controls, local workers and Adrian's hidden arrangements.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, injury, wage penalties and enforcement review.
- **Mistaken belief:** A small private act can remain separate from collective consequences.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not reveal the door to someone who has not shown what they do under pressure.
- **Decision only Tomas would make:** Trigger a shutdown, call it an accident and remain behind to draw enforcement attention.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's decision to rescue him turns a guarded favor into public solidarity.
- **Proof of change:** Tomas moves from guarding one door to helping miners publicly compare physical gauges with allocation records.

**Arc in this book** — Tomas begins as a man protecting one hidden route and ends as a visible participant in Keel's collective inspection. His debt to Alex is not sentimental: it is repaid through action, trust and public work.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is a woman in her late fifties, tall and slender at approximately 5'10", with pale olive skin, silver-templed black hair cut in a precise jaw-length shape and dark brown eyes that remain controlled even under pressure. She wears pearl, graphite and cool-blue formal clothing with clean geometric seams, a graphite cuff and no visible House crest. Her silhouette is vertical, symmetrical and administratively severe. Her signature detail is a glass of water that she habitually aligns exactly with the edge of a desk until the Covenant forces her to leave it crooked.**

**Voice** — Mera speaks in polished Oasis administrative language, separating an action from its human consequence. Her control breaks only into plain accountability, never melodrama.  
- “I preserved order. That's true. It is also not enough.”  
- “I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Senior Oasis administrator, former Alpha executive and original Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Prevent another infrastructure collapse and contain the legacy protocol before it destabilizes the cities.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She values human survival but accepted a system that removed humans from consequential decisions.
- **Leverage / capability:** Executive access, institutional knowledge, records and authority over Alpha's governing systems.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over Adrian's pursuit, inability to override Alpha and fear of returning to Water-Wars chaos.
- **Mistaken belief:** Stability preserved by centralized authority is safer than visible disagreement.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not allow an uncontrolled transfer of Alpha's authority.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** Offer Alex the full executive authority she once relied on, then accept his refusal rather than seize it back.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's refusal and Ada's demand for agency force her to witness consequences without managing them away.
- **Proof of change:** She sits among ordinary people during the public process and does not settle their disagreement for them.

**Arc in this book** — Mera moves from controlled administrator to accountable participant. She is not absolved of helping create Alpha's permanent authority or failing to save Adrian. She ends without guaranteed correctness or power over the room.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Leona Veyra is a woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'7" tall, with a composed Indigenous Andean/mestiza face, warm brown skin, high cheekbones, dark almond-shaped eyes and black hair threaded with silver and pinned neatly at the nape. She visibly resembles Ada, while her lighter coloring does not. She wears a stone-grey civic coat over a sage-green blouse, simple metal earrings and a narrow belt holding her dark Covenant band. Her silhouette is precise, contained and slightly withdrawn. Her signature detail is a small stone-grey box that holds photographs and her Covenant band.**

**Voice** — Leona begins in careful, qualified administrative phrases and becomes plainer when she accepts responsibility. She does not ask for forgiveness before offering consequences.  
- “I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't.”  
- “Safety isn't the same as agency.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Oasis civic administrator and former Covenant custodian.
- **Immediate want:** Reconnect with Ada and publicly support a process that answers to affected communities.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to repair the harm she caused but has spent years treating inaction as humility.
- **Leverage / capability:** Civic standing, historical knowledge and the remaining active Covenant band.
- **Vulnerability:** Ada's refusal to forgive, guilt over the deciding vote and fear of acting publicly again.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she stays careful and distant, she cannot cause another catastrophe.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not make another public commitment until she can control its consequences.
- **Decision only Leona would make:** Enter the public review, state the obligation, accept loss of status and perform the promised action before the Covenant activates.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada refuses private safety and demands visible accountability.
- **Proof of change:** Leona stands in the public process where affected people can challenge her, without demanding restored motherhood or forgiveness.

**Arc in this book** — Leona moves from private guilt and administrative caution to a public obligation that others may judge. Her relationship with Ada remains damaged; accountability is not treated as reconciliation.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock** — Director Cassian Lock is a man in his late fifties, approximately 6'1" tall, lean and silver-templed, with light olive skin, a narrow face, pale grey eyes and carefully groomed dark-grey hair. He wears a charcoal suit with platinum fastening points, a high-collared pale shirt and no Charter House crest. His silhouette is tall, clean and unhurried, with his hands loosely clasped behind or in front of him. His signature detail is that he studies the record or device before looking directly at the person whose life it will change.**

**Voice** — Lock speaks in polished Halyard/Oasis administrative language, making pressure sound like accommodation. He presents scale, financing and distribution as serious practical arguments.  
- “Your father's work could clear every debt attached to your name.”  
- “A simple assignment of rights.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Senior institutional director and representative of the Charter House system.
- **Immediate want:** Secure Adrian's disputed estate for House administration.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He genuinely believes scale can distribute useful work, but treats concentrated ownership as the natural price of scale.
- **Leverage / capability:** Access to Charter House review, debt clearance, employment records and collaborator protections.
- **Vulnerability:** His argument fails if small, honest enterprise demonstrates that useful work can be built without House control.
- **Mistaken belief:** Protection and distribution capacity justify transferring control away from contributors.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not let a public attribution victory become a challenge to House infrastructure.
- **Decision only Lock would make:** Offer Alex debt relief and collaborator protection while clearly stating that refusal exposes them to institutional pressure.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's refusal forces Lock to acknowledge that public release can create value outside House ownership, even though it does not remove House power.
- **Proof of refusal:** Lock remains a credible opponent and continues defending House scale after the Seal activates.

**Arc in this book** — Lock does not undergo a moral conversion. His position becomes more precise: the Houses retain infrastructure and distribution power, but cannot simply erase the makers' names from the public record.

### DR. LINA VEY

**Visual lock** — Dr. Lina Vey is a woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'5" tall, stooped slightly from years at fabrication benches, with medium-brown Indigenous Andean/mestiza skin, short black-and-silver hair, deep-set brown eyes and a long, serious face. She wears a stained slate maintenance uniform beneath a technical coat, reinforced shoes and a compact data-shard case clipped at her hip. Her silhouette is narrow, bent toward the work and immediately recognizable by the way she keeps one shoulder lower than the other. Her signature detail is a membrane sample folded into a transparent sleeve at her wrist.**

**Voice** — Lina is dry, technically fluent and unimpressed by inherited status. She gives the useful fact and lets other people catch up.  
- “I corrected him. Frequently.”  
- “Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Membrane chemist and Adrian collaborator employed under Caldera House's water systems.
- **Immediate want:** Keep her membrane work from being absorbed without attribution and make it function with the other fragments.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She values durable work over credit but knows erased credit makes future work easier to control.
- **Leverage / capability:** Catalytic membrane design, fabrication knowledge and the ability to solve the household unit's surface-area problem.
- **Vulnerability:** House employment, technical reassignment and the risk of losing access to her own equipment.
- **Mistaken belief:** If the work functions, attribution can wait.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not hand over her fragment to someone who treats it as an inheritance.
- **Decision only Lina would make:** Join the small public build after testing whether Alex can keep up technically and ethically.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex forces her to distinguish preserving a design from preserving the people who made it.
- **Proof of change:** She accepts named joint ownership of Morrow & Sons and continues earning wages and an equal profit share rather than reclaiming exclusive control.

**Arc in this book** — Lina moves from guarded technical isolation to visible collaboration. She remains unsentimental and technically demanding.

### FOREMAN-7
