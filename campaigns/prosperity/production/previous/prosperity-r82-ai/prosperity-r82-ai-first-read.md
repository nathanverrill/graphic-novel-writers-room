# First Read: Prosperity, Issue 01, pages 1–3 (Audition A and Audition B)

I read this as the assigned reader: 16, doing it the night before, wanting it to be bad. **I read A first.** So B was my second pass through the same events. I already knew about the birthday and the debt going in, which probably took some of the punch out of B, page 2.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **A, page 1, panel 3:** "The manual doesn't have to drink the water." This is the first line in either version where I thought *okay, this kid has a mouth on him.* It's short, it's a comeback, and it tells me water is a problem here without anyone explaining it. It got me to page 2.
- **A, page 1, panel 4:** the supervisor forcing a green checkmark over the red warning. I liked that without words. The adult is covering for him, and "don't push your luck *today*" made me ask why today.
- **A, page 2, panel 3:** "Happy birthday, Phantum." This is where it got me. The big screen of numbers, then the guard looking embarrassed and saying happy birthday. Now I know what "today" meant on page 1: on his 17th birthday he got handed 418,000 in debt. That's a mean, clear idea and I got it on the first read.
- **A, page 2, panel 4:** he just says "Yeah." Good. He doesn't make a speech.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **A, page 2, panel 2:** I skimmed the display. Seven lines of capital letters. I read DEBT BALANCE and PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS and skipped the rest until the guard spoke. "PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER" is a phrase my eyes slid right off.
- **A, page 3:** I sped through it. It's a quiet counter scene and nothing in panels 1–3 made me slow down.

### I was confused
- **A, page 3, the money.** Inez says thirty credits, in installments. Alex says "I'll have it after next shift," so he didn't pay yet? Then Ada says "You spent the travel token money." So he *did* pay? With what? I couldn't follow whether money changed hands. Confused and detached, not intrigued.
- **A, page 3, panel 4:** "She needs the medicine." **Who is she?** Not Inez, obviously. Somebody at home? I have no idea. The last line of the section hangs on a person I've never met or heard of.
- **"Travel token."** What is it, and why does it matter that he spent the money for it? Is he trying to leave? I'm guessing. The book doesn't say.
- **The casino chip with an equation (page 2, panel 4).** I saw it and had no idea why a kid in a pump hall has a casino chip, or what the equation is. Confused but intrigued, barely. It's a close-up so I know I'm meant to care, but I don't know why yet.

### Lines I read twice
- "PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE." I read it twice, then gave up and let the guard explain it.
- "If you don't, you come tell me before I have to ask." Fine on the second read. On the first I thought she was threatening him, then decided she was being fair. I'm still not sure which.
- "You spent the travel token money." I reread it trying to match it to the previous panel and couldn't.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: clear. Quiet, good with his hands, a little cocky with bosses.
- Supervisor: clear for one page. Gruff, but covering for him.
- Guard: clear, one line, and it's the best line on the page.
- Inez: I could tell her apart by the job and the ledger. Personality: strict but fair, maybe.
- **Ada: I couldn't place her at all.** She's in the background for three panels and then has the most loaded line. She comes off as a prop who gets to say the plot.

### I felt something
Yes, once: **A, page 2, panel 3.** The guard's pity plus "Happy birthday." I felt a little sick for him, for real. Nothing on page 3 matched it.

### I predicted
- He's going to try to find his dad (Adrian, "whereabouts unrecorded").
- The supervisor's cover-up will come back to bite one of them.
- The casino chip is Dad's and the equation is a clue.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid gets stuck with his missing father's debt on his 17th birthday and is going to have to choose between paying for someone's medicine and getting out.

### Where I would have put it down
Not in these three pages. Page 2 bought me a few more. If page 4 is another quiet counter-style scene, that's where I'd go back to my phone. **A, page 3** is the weakest page, and I was already drifting by panel 3.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
This isn't an issue ending, but taken as "where do I stop tonight," page 3 ends on "She needs the medicine" about a person I've never seen. I'd turn the page, but out of leftover momentum from the birthday, not because page 3 asked me anything. There's no hook in it, just a shrug.

## Version B

(Second read of the same events. Not cold.)

### Pulled me forward
- **B, page 1, panel 1 and panel 4:** the gauge reads red while the water is visibly flowing. Then it goes to amber, but the little warning icon stays. I liked that. The machine says broken, the kid's fix works, and the machine still won't fully let it go. I understood that without any dialogue.
- **B, page 2, panel 2:** the workers behind him go quiet without looking at him. Better than the numbers. It made it feel public and humiliating.
- **B, page 2, panel 4:** the debt display stays hanging over the checkpoint after he walks through, "like a sign nobody can take down." Strongest image in either version. He leaves, the number doesn't.
- **B, page 2, panel 3:** "Twenty-eight point seven." I liked him saying the number to himself. It's what I'd do.
- **B, page 3, panel 3:** "Your grandmother needs this tonight." Finally I know who the medicine is for. That alone made page 3 easier to care about than A's.
- **B, page 3, panel 3:** "I know where." / "You know a direction." This is the moment I sat up. Now I know he's planning to leave and doesn't have a real plan. That's a goal. I didn't get one in A.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **B, page 1:** his dialogue goes "It's holding." / "Then don't approve it." / "Just notice it works." Three lines of the same attitude. By the third I was skimming. I'd already got that he's stubborn.
- **B, page 2, panels 1–2:** the display is split over two panels and it's *more* text than A: "TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY," then a red "REVIEW REQUIRED: HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY" added on. I skimmed all of it. "DEBT TRANSFER DAY" spells out what the guard's line was going to make me figure out myself.
- **B, page 2, panel 1:** "Happy birthday" comes before I've even seen the 28.7 years. It landed softer for me than in A. (That may be because I'd read A.)
- **B, page 3, panel 2:** I skimmed the money talk. "I'll front both. You return the medicine money first. The token after." I stopped trying to do the math.

### I was confused
- **B, page 3, the money, again.** He has 52 credits available. The medicine is 30. So why does Inez need to "front" anything? Is the token more than 22? I don't know what a token costs, so I couldn't follow it. Confused and detached for two panels.
- **"Site 6."** "If Site 6 pays you." Is that where he works? Is it the pump hall? Does it sometimes not pay? His jaw tightens, so it matters, but I don't know why.
- **"REVIEW REQUIRED" shows up twice,** on the pump (p1) and on his debt (p2). Is that the same review? Is the system coming after him because of the repair? I couldn't tell if it was a connection or just the same computer phrase.
- **"This one has a date." (p3, last line)** A date when? A date printed on the token? A deadline? A departure? I read it three times. Confused, mostly intrigued, but it's a riddle and it's the last thing on the page.
- **Casino chip:** same as A, but at least B tells me it's Adrian's. Still don't know what the equation is.

### Lines I read twice
- "Just notice it works." Read it twice to get the tone. Is he asking or ordering?
- "REVIEW REQUIRED: HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY." Didn't understand it either time.
- "I'll front both. You return the medicine money first. The token after." I read it twice and still couldn't do the math.
- "Knowing is not paying." Fine on the second read, and kind of good.
- "You already owe the whole mountain." Twice. Mountain = the debt? Or the place?
- **"This one has a date."** Three times. Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: clear, but a bit more of a mouth than in A. Three comebacks on page 1 made him sound like he's trying.
- Supervisor: less clear than in A. I couldn't tell if he was helping or just closing a pop-up.
- Guard: lowers his eyes. Fine.
- Inez: clearer than in A. Strict, a little dry ("Knowing is not paying," "Don't make me find you"). I believed her.
- **Ada: much better than A.** She checks the label, deliberately leaves the token, and argues with him. I still don't know who she is to him, but now she reads like his friend who's smarter about this than he is. She's still not named in dialogue.

### I felt something
- **B, page 2, panel 4:** the sign hanging there after he leaves. A quiet, bad feeling.
- **B, page 3, panel 3:** "You know a direction." A small jolt. Someone finally called him out.
- The birthday line itself did less for me here than in A.

### I predicted
- He's leaving Keel with that token to find Adrian, and the "date" is something about when or where.
- The "review required" flags will catch up with him, from the pump, the debt, or both.
- Inez will have to come find him, or something will happen to the grandmother while he's gone.
- Ada either goes with him or tries to stop him.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid handed his missing father's debt on his 17th birthday is about to spend money he doesn't have on a ticket out to go find him, and he's leaving a sick grandmother behind to do it.

### Where I would have put it down
**B, page 1, panel 4,** on "Just notice it works." It felt like the third version of the same comeback, and the art description is very heavy for a scene where a guy fixes a pipe. If I'd opened B first in a shop, I'm not sure I'd have reached the birthday. Page 3 dragged in the money panel but picked up after it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
More than A. Page 3 ends with him taking the token anyway, against advice, and that's an actual decision. I want to know where he's going. But the very last line, "This one has a date," is a riddle, so the hook ends on me puzzling rather than worrying. Taken as an issue ending, I'd read the next one this week, not right now. It's a half-real cliffhanger: there's a person I'm starting to know (Alex, a bit Ada), a decision, and a fuzzy line on top.

---

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
**B,** decided at **B, page 3, panel 3: "I know where." / "You know a direction."** It's the first time in either version that I knew what the kid *wants*. In A he wants nothing. He pays for medicine for an unknown "she" and the page stops.

But **A has the better first two pages.** "The manual doesn't have to drink the water" is a stronger opening line than anything on B's page 1, and A's birthday hits harder because the guard's line comes right after the numbers and explains them, rather than a screen that announces "DEBT TRANSFER DAY." If I'd opened B in a shop, page 1 might have lost me before page 3 had the chance to win me.

Both versions lost me on the pharmacy money, and neither tells me who Ada is.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A sharp, sad birthday, then a counter scene that just kind of stops.
- **B:** A slower, wordier start that finally gives the kid somewhere to go, then ends on a line I couldn't decode.
