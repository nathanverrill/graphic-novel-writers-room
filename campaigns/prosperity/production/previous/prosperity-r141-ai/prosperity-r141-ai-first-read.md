# First Read: Prosperity, opening pages 1–3 (Audition A and Audition B)

Who I read as: a 16-year-old who got this as homework, opened it the night before, and expected it to be bad. I know comics. I'm watching for anything that feels AI-made.

**Order:** I read A first, then B. So B wasn't a cold read. The robot dog falling apart in mid-air surprised me once, in A. By B I knew it was coming. Keep that in mind on everything I say about B's page 1.

---

## VERSION A

### Pulled me forward
- **A, p1, panels 3–4.** The dog walks off the dock into nothing, then falls apart in mid-air. *KLAK.* For half a second I thought I was watching a robot dog kill itself. That got me. It's the best moment in A, and it has no words.
- **A, p2, panel 3.** The old man just says "Watch." He's calm and not explaining anything. I wanted to know what he knew.
- **A, p3, panel 4.** The head clamps onto the bottom of the barge and the amber eye lights up. *clk.* It's quiet and creepy and cool, and it was a good image to stop on.
- "He stole it first." Wait, the old guy stole a lake? That's the first time I thought the story might be more than "bad guys steal water."

### I slowed down / skimmed
- I didn't skim anything. There's barely anything to skim. Three pages went by in about forty seconds.
- **A, p3, panels 1–3.** The thieves' talk slowed me down. It wasn't boring. I just had to stop and work it out (see "Lines I read twice").

### I was confused
- **A, p2, panel 1.** "They're taking it." Taking what? The dog? The barge? I worked out it meant the water from the hoses on p1. That was confused but intrigued, not lost.
- **A, p2, panels 1–2.** Who is this girl to him? She calls him "Alex," so I assumed she's not family. Maybe she works for him, or she's a neighbour. Nothing on the page tells me she's his granddaughter. I didn't know that until B.
- **A, p2, panel 4 vs p1, panel 4.** On p1 the dog splits into head, torso and legs. On p2 only the head and legs go into the water. Where did the torso go? I flipped back to check.
- **A, p3, panel 2.** "Nobody stops us." Is that a brag, or is he nervous that nobody's coming? I couldn't tell how to hear it.
- **A, p3.** Which thief is which? I lost track of who was asking and who was answering.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| "Keel" | A p1 caption, "Keel. 2099." | No. A town? A lake? A company? I guessed it was the lake's name. |
| TJ (the dog's name) | never said | **No.** A never tells me the dog has a name. |
| Alex | A p2 p2, the girl says it | Yes, his name. No, not who he is. |
| The girl | A p2 | No name, and no relationship to Alex. |
| Why the water is worth stealing | throughout | No. I assume the water is rare, but the page never says so. |
| "He stole it first" (stole what, from whom, how) | A p3 p3 | No. It's a hook, but it's left hanging. |
| The amber eye ring | A p1 p2, again p3 p4 | No. It looks important and I don't know why. |

### Lines I read twice
- "Made it fair, you think?" Who's asking? Made *what* fair? I read it twice and I'm still not sure.
- "Nobody gets a lake fair. He stole it first." Second read, I got it: Alex stole the lake, so they're stealing it back. On first read it was a riddle.
- "Nobody stops us." I wasn't sure of the tone.
- "He's got enough." That one was fine once I read the next line.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex and the girl: easy. Old guy, teen girl.
- The dog: easy, but I don't know his name.
- **Thief 1 and Thief 2: couldn't tell them apart at all.** Same gear, no names. To me they're one person talking to himself.

### I felt something
- A small jolt at the dog dropping into the dark. Then relief, sort of, when it turned out the dog was doing this on purpose.
- When Alex says "Watch," I trusted him a bit.
- I felt nothing for the girl. She grabs a wrench, gets stopped, and that's all she does.

### I predicted
- The dog sabotages the barge or tracks it home.
- Alex has done this before.
- Alex really did steal the lake somehow, and the book will make me decide whose side I'm on.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old man and his robot dog are defending a lake that other people say he stole.

### Where I would have put it down
I wouldn't put it down in these three pages. It's too short and too strange to drop. If I wobbled, it would be **A, p3, panel 3**, the thieves' riddle talk. One more page of two anonymous guys trading half-sentences and I'd be on my phone.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
I'd turn the page. I wouldn't *need* to. The eye glowing under the hull is a great image, but nothing's at stake yet. The dog isn't in danger. The barge just has something stuck to it. I want to see what the dog does. I'm curious, not worried. And I couldn't tell you the names of anyone in it except "Alex."

## VERSION B

*(This was my second read. The dog-splitting surprise was already spent.)*

### Pulled me forward
- **B, p1, panel 2.** "Is TJ broken?" / "Not usually." I actually laughed. It gives me the dog's name, and it made me like the old man. That was the moment B got me.
- **B, p2, panel 2.** "That's Phantum's lake." / "It's a lake." / "It's clean." Those three short lines finally told me why anyone would steal this water: clean water is rare. A never told me that.
- **B, p3, panel 1.** "Something's under us." That's simple and it builds tension. I knew what it was and they didn't, which was fun.
- **B, p3, panel 3.** Alex stops her from calling the wardens. Why doesn't he want the cops? That made me suspicious of him, and that pulled me forward more than anything the thieves said.
- **B, p3, panel 4.** The shutters close around TJ's head. Now I'm worried about the dog.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **B, p2, panel 1, and B, p3, panel 1.** I skimmed the thieves' work chatter: "Lower the pull." "We're already late." "Pressure's good." It sounds like technician filler and I read past it.
- B has more talking overall. Page 1 had a back-and-forth in nearly every panel, and I started reading faster to get to the dog.

### I was confused
- **B, p1, panel 3.** The far shore has dry channels and empty basins, but the lake is full. Am I supposed to notice that? If I did, I wouldn't know what it means. Is someone else's land dry? I'd probably miss it entirely.
- **B, p1, panel 4.** "Let's see what they came to take." Didn't they come to take the water? Is it something else? Confused but intrigued, sort of.
- **B, p3, panels 3–4.** "Let TJ show us where it goes." So the plan is tracking? Then TJ's head gets jammed in the shutters. Is that part of the plan, or is he being crushed? I honestly couldn't tell, and I wanted to.
- "We're already late." Late for what? For whom? It's never picked up.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Lake Keel / 2099 | only in the issue header | If that header isn't printed in the comic, I never learn the place or the year in B. Nothing inside the panels says it. |
| TJ | B p1 p2 | Yes, right away. |
| "Grandpa" / Alex | B p1 p1 | I know he's her grandpa. I never hear "Alex" said on these pages. I'd only know him as Grandpa and "Phantum." |
| "Phantum" | B p2 p2 | I guessed it's Alex's last name. That seems right, but it isn't confirmed. |
| Nera and Voss | never spoken | No. I wouldn't know those names. But I could tell them apart anyway (see below). |
| Lake wardens | B p3 p3 | Partly. Some kind of authority exists, and Alex doesn't want them. |
| Why clean water is rare | B p2 p2 | Hinted ("It's clean"). Enough for now. |
| "He stole it first" | B p2 p4 | No, same as A. |

### Lines I read twice
- "Then he stole it first." The "Then" threw me. Then *what*? It reads like a reply to a line that isn't there.
- "Let's see what they came to take." Is it a hint or just a line? I read it twice.
- "Nobody gets water that clean by being polite." That was fine on first read. Clearer than anything the thieves say in A.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Grandpa, the girl and TJ: easy.
- **The thieves: I could tell them apart.** Red hood vs. lamp helmet. One works, one watches and worries. I didn't know their names, but I knew which was which, which I never managed in A.
- The girl still doesn't have a name. She has more lines in B, but most of them ask questions for my benefit.

### I felt something
- I liked Grandpa because of "Not usually."
- I worried about TJ on the last panel. That's the first time in either version I felt anything for the dog beyond "cool robot."
- A small itch of distrust when Alex won't call the wardens. That was a good feeling to have.
- The girl irritated me a little. She's 15 and lives with this dog, and she asks "Is TJ broken?" She also says "That's not a fishing boat." Those feel like lines written so I get told things, not things she'd actually say. That's the closest either version came to feeling generated.

### I predicted
- TJ gets out of the shutters, rides the water back to wherever it's going, and Alex finds out who's behind it.
- Alex has a reason for avoiding the wardens that makes him look worse.
- "He stole it first" will turn out to be at least partly true.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A grandfather who may have done something shady to get his clean lake uses his robot dog to track down the people stealing it.

### Where I would have put it down
Not in these pages. The closest was **B, p2, panel 1**, "Lower the pull" / "We're already late," where it turned into work talk from two people I didn't know. "Something's under us" pulled me back.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. I'd turn the page right now. It's a real cliffhanger: the shutters are closing on the dog I just started liking, and I don't know if it's part of the plan. That's a question about someone I care about, not just a loud noise. The one weak spot: if it turns out TJ was always fine, I'll feel tricked.

---

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
**B.** It was decided on **B, p1, panel 2**, "Is TJ broken?" / "Not usually.", and sealed on **B, p3, panel 4**, when the shutters close on TJ. In B I knew who everyone was, I could tell the thieves apart, I understood why the water mattered, and the last page put someone I liked in danger.

To be fair to A: A's silent drop on p1 hit harder than anything in B, and B only lost that surprise because I read A first. A felt cooler and more confident. It also felt more like a puzzle I'd have to do as homework. B felt clearer but chattier, and the girl's lines sometimes sound like they're explaining things to me. Both versions use "he stole it first," and in both it's the best hook and the least explained line.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** It was a cool, silent trailer where I never learned the dog's name or which thief was talking.
- **B:** It was a clearer, chattier opening that made me like the dog just in time to be scared for it.
