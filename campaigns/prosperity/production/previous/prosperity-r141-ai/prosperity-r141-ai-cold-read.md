# Cold Read — Prosperity

Read in one sitting, start to finish, the night before, as a 16-year-old who got it as homework. I read it once. Where I went back a page, I say so.

One thing up front, because my class would say it first: almost everyone in this book talks the same way. Short, clipped, every line a little comeback ("Someday's crowded." "Badly, eventually." "I'm generous with containers."). After about twenty pages it stopped sounding like people and started sounding like one voice doing all the parts. Readers who already think a book is AI-made will take that as proof.

## Issue 00 — Lake Keel, 2099 (pages 1–10)

### Pulled me forward
- p1 panel 2: TJ jumping off the dock and coming apart in midair. "Is TJ broken?" / "Not usually." Good start. I liked the dog right away.
- p1 panel 4: "They're taking it." / "Watch." Grandpa knows something and isn't scared. That made me curious.
- p2 panel 4: "Nobody gets water that clean by being polite." / "Then he took it first." First hint that Grandpa is maybe not the good guy. That's the best hook in this issue.
- p9: the granddaughter matching the mark on the component to his watch, and him covering it with his thumb. A clear secret I could see.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- p4 to p6: robot dog jams a pump. It's mostly sound effects, and I skimmed it. I couldn't really picture where TJ's head, legs and body were at any point, so I just waited for it to finish.
- p8: lost me, see below.

### I was confused
- p3 panel 4: "Let him show us where it goes." Where *what* goes? The water? The barge? TJ never shows anyone where anything goes. The barge just leaves. So what was the plan?
- p3: why not call the wardens? He says "Not yet" and then never calls them. Nobody is stopped. Did the theft just… happen?
- p8: I have no idea what happens on this page. "The terminal records the reduced withdrawal." "Then this stays?" "Where the next person can find it." "The record closes around the theft." Why are they leaving the evidence on the dock? Who is "the next person"? What does a record closing around a theft look like? I read it twice and still didn't get it.
- p7 panel 4: suddenly there's a "public water terminal" right there. I didn't know there was one.
- Nera and Voss: who sent them, and why does it matter? It never comes back in this issue.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Phantum ("That's Phantum's lake") | p2 | Partly. Alex's last name is Phantum (p12), but I never learned why everyone in 2099 knows the lake as his |
| Wardens | p3 | No |
| The maker's mark | p7 | Kind of. It's probably the water-drop on the watch (p17, p26), but nobody says so |
| Public water terminal / "the record" | p7–8 | Sort of, by the end of the book |
| The old mine / Keel | p1 | Yes, from Issue 01 on |
| TJ | p1 | Never explained. He's never in the 2043 story |

### Lines I read twice
- "The record closes around the theft." (p8)
- "Let him show us where it goes." (p3)
- "Where the next person can find it." (p8)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, the granddaughter and TJ were clear. Nera and Voss were interchangeable apart from the hood and the helmet. The granddaughter never gets a name, which is weird for the person asking all the questions.

### I felt something
A little on p9 when he covers the watch. Grandpa is hiding something from a kid who really wants to know.

### I predicted
That Grandpa took the lake from someone, or that his father did, and the book is about finding out what it cost.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old man who controls a clean lake is hiding how he got it, and his granddaughter wants the truth.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 8. That's where I stopped understanding what anyone was doing.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
p10 is a nice picture. "That isn't an answer." / "No." is the same dodge we've had for three pages. Still, it's a real question about someone I care about a bit, Grandpa. I'd turn the page, but slowly.

VERDICT Issue 00: put down: page 8; next issue: yes

## Issue 01 — The Inheritance (pages 11–22)

### Pulled me forward
- p12: this is where the book actually got me. A 17-year-old gets his missing dad's debt *on his birthday*: 418,607 credits, 28.7 years. "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "Twenty-eight point seven." I get that right away, and I'm angry for him. Best page so far.
- p13: Ada. "I'm your friend, Alex. Not your exit plan." She felt like a real person.
- p16: the bookcase opening into a hidden workshop. Fun.
- p18: Bi11bot waking up. "Adrian's jaw. His poorer timing." and "B11 if the room is on fire" made me laugh. I liked him straight away.
- p19: Adrian's message. "I borrowed too much. I gambled some of it. I hurt you." He admits it, which I didn't expect.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- p15: Grandmother, Pilar, a dose grid, a notebook. Quiet, and I moved through it fast. The "calls him Adrian, then corrects herself" bit worked, though.
- p17: chalkboard, brass plate, map with ticks. A list of props, and I skimmed it.
- p20–21: projections, the infrastructure spine, three locations. My eyes glazed.

### I was confused
- p12: "Foreman-7" is a robot name, but he's "visibly human"? So is he a person or a machine? I never found out why he has a number.
- p12 panel 4: "the Last Chance bar is closed and dust-covered." Why show me this? It's never mentioned again.
- p14: "Adrian with his face folded away." Folded how? Is the photo folded, or is something wrong with his face?
- p16: "The looping equation." Which equation? It's used like I already know. It turns up again on p17, p30 and p53 and is never explained.
- p20: why does the door lock? "The workshop woke. The system noticed." What system? Who's watching?
- p21: "The first is below Site 6." The first *what*? Three markers of something. Nobody says what they are, and they're the whole plot. I didn't learn it until Issue 06.
- p22: Mera. Who is she, and why does she care? The glass-of-water thing meant nothing to me.
- The door is locked at the end of p20. In Issue 02 they're just at Site 6. How did they get out?

| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Foreman-7 (human? robot?) | p12 | No |
| Identity band | p12 | Mostly, from use |
| Last Chance bar | p12 | No |
| The looping equation | p16 | No |
| Balanced water-drop mark | p17 | Only that it's Adrian's sign |
| The "system" | p20 | Later, I think it's Alpha |
| Halyard, Oasis, the infrastructure spine | p20 | Halyard and Oasis yes, in later issues |
| Site 6 | p21 | Yes, it's a mine site |
| Three locations | p21 | Only in Issue 06, when they turn out to be the "safeguards" |
| Mera | p22 | Issue 04 |
| Legacy protocol | p22 | No |

### Lines I read twice
- "The part that costs." (p14). The part of what?
- "Badly, eventually." (p16)
- "To whoever writes the screen." (p20)
- "Adrian's jaw. His poorer timing." I read it twice, but I liked it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandmother and Bi11bot were clear. Pilar was just "the one with the doses." I wasn't sure whether Pilar is Ada's aunt or Alex's family, so I had to go back to p15.

### I felt something
p12, the debt on his birthday. Also p19, a father apologizing from a recording.

### I predicted
That Adrian isn't really a gambler and was set up, and that he's either alive or the bad guy.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his dad's debt finds his dad's secret robot and has to follow a trail to work out what his dad was really doing.

### Where I would have put it down
I almost did on p21, when the three markers came with no explanation. The debt on p12 kept me reading.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
p22 is a tracking ring on the signal and a woman saying "Track." It's a fine little threat, but I don't know Mera, so it's more a noise than a person. I'd keep going because of Alex and the debt, not because of this page.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — The Price of Departure (pages 23–37)

### Pulled me forward
- p24: Alex steps out of his zone to save Tomas and loses 40 credits on the spot. "You know that costs you." / "I noticed." Clear, and I liked him for it.
- p27: "Foreman! Your safety report is on fire." Funny.
- p27 panel 4: "Abandoned mines do not sweat." Good creepy line.
- p32: **62% reported, 18% delivered.** I got this instantly. Keel is being lied to about its water. Best idea in the book, and the most anger I felt.
- p35: the neighbours putting credits on the table to pay off his 500. "They bought the gate." The first moment the book felt warm.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- p28–29: the left tunnel or the straight tunnel. Fine, but it felt like a beat from a video game.
- p30–31: pipes, uplinks, gauges. I read the pictures and skipped the words.
- p36–37: drones, bulkhead, train. An action exit I've seen many times.

### I was confused
- p23: his band says travel is restricted, so why buy a "travel token" from Tomas for 40 credits? Is it the same thing as the 500?
- p25: Bi11bot says "My route assumed we left Tomas where he fell," then asks "What would you do?" Is B11 cold, or is he testing Alex? I couldn't tell, and it made me trust him less than the book seems to want.
- p26: "From anyone who came for the work." What work? Same problem as "the part that costs."
- **p28: "Alpha's projection."** This is the first time anyone says Alpha. Is Alpha the robot? Is B11 Alpha? Is it the system? The next panel says "B11 projects," so I thought they were the same thing. I didn't work out that Alpha is the big computer that runs everything until about p53 or p61.
- p34: Ada is "over speakers" across Keel, but she's in the mine with Alex. How? Did the transmitter do that?
- p34 panel 3: "We're already in it." I didn't get what Tomas meant.
- Why does the Measure "need both" (p31)? I sort of got it, but only sort of.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Alpha | p28 | Only by guessing, around p53 and p61. Never said straight |
| The Measure | p31 | Yes, from what it does |
| Industrial uplink / diversion | p30, p32 | Yes |
| House accounts / "household offense" | p25 | Vaguely |
| The private route | p33 | No, not really |

### Lines I read twice
- "He marked what survived them." (p28)
- "Then he took it first." (p2). I went back to this after p32 to check whether Keel was the thief.
- "We're already in it." (p34)
- "WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE." I didn't read it twice. I rolled my eyes. It's the book saying its point out loud.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Tomas is great. He's clear, he's funny, and he's the first adult who feels like he's on Alex's side. Foreman-7 is just a voice reading rules.

### I felt something
p35, the neighbours paying. Also p24, Alex choosing Tomas over his wage.

### I predicted
That Halyard is where the stolen water goes, and the rich people there are the villains.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Keel is being lied to about its water, and a kid in debt proves it with his dad's tools.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. This was the best issue.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
p37: "You still want out?" / "I want the answer." Then the train climbs. It's not a cliffhanger, but I wanted to see Halyard, and Alex has changed. Yes.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — The Price of Ownership (pages 38–44)

### Pulled me forward
- p38: "More isn't clean." Smart. The win from last issue isn't really a win.
- p39: the kiosk takes a worker's invention, gives him 14 credits and erases his name. I got that straight away and it made me mad.
- p40: Lock offers to clear the debt, and Alex says no. The deal was tempting, so the refusal meant something. The first time, anyway.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- p41 to p44, the whole back half. It turned into a group project. Meet four people, test parts, fail, fix, success, split the money. I skimmed most of it.
- p44 panel 4: a revenue split of 38 credits into 12, 18 and 8. It felt like a worksheet. This is where I felt the "educational program" behind the book.

### I was confused
- p41: four new people on one page, each with one line, and no idea how Alex found them. Lock says "find the people who made it," then we cut and they're all just there. Who are they? Are they Adrian's old team? Random House workers?
- p43: "Who carries the downside?" / "The people who sign it." I didn't know what we were talking about.
- p44: Lina hands Alex "the Maker's Seal." Where did she get it? Was she keeping it for Adrian? It's handed over like I should know it. Later I worked out it's the second of the three things from p21, but nobody tells me here.
- p40: what exactly is Lock's job? "Director" of what?
- Mera tracked them at the end of Issue 01. This issue forgets her completely.
- "WEEKS LATER" twice in two pages. What happened to the debt, Grandmother, and the tracking in those weeks?

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| The Houses (Caldera, Vector, Orbital) | p39–40 | Caldera is a company. Vector and Orbital are never seen again |
| Intellectual estate / patents | p39 | Yes |
| Maker's Seal | p44 | What it does, yes. Where it came from, no |
| Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren | p41 | Barely |

### Lines I read twice
- "It lies under heat." (p42)
- "If the small shop can't service it, it isn't a product." (p41)
- "Who carries the downside?" (p43)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren: I couldn't tell them apart and still can't. Lina is "membrane," Suri is "plant filter," Jalen is "mirror guy," Ren is "fix it." They're jobs, not people. Lock was clear, but a flat suit.

### I felt something
p39, the name disappearing. Nothing after that.

### I predicted
That Lock or Caldera would steal the purifier. The "competing unit" notice says that's coming. (It mostly doesn't.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Who gets credit and money for inventions. It seemed to drop the water mystery to teach me that.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 41. Four strangers, one line each, and I stopped caring.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
p44: "COMPETING INDUSTRIAL UNIT IN DEVELOPMENT." A sign on a screen behind a man. It's a trick, not a cliffhanger. Nobody I care about is in danger. I wouldn't have bothered with the next issue if it weren't homework.

## Issue 04 — The Broken Covenant (pages 45–50)

### Pulled me forward
- p45: Ada's band says "RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED." Wait, Ada has a past here? That woke me up.
- p46: "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" Great. Then "I knew where you were within a year." / "Then you knew enough to come." This hit. It's the best-written conversation in the book.
- p48: "Don't apologize where it costs nothing." Ada is the strongest character, and this line is why.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- p49–50: Mera shows up and offers ultimate power. I skimmed it because I'd just watched Alex turn down Lock's deal.

### I was confused
- **Ada's mother.** On p15 Pilar is Ada's aunt, and that's all I was told. Nobody ever said Ada's mother left, or is alive, or is in Oasis. It came out of nowhere, so I felt it less than I should have. The problem shows up way too late for me to be attached to it.
- p45: "Ada presses the largest rivet on her strap." Is that a habit? A signal? I've never seen her do it before.
- p46: "a sage-gold Covenant band," "the child's object inside the box." What child's object? What's a Covenant band?
- p47: younger Leona, Adrian and Mera, three bands, crowds at a storehouse, a vote for "emergency authority." I got that something bad happened and Leona voted to give someone power. Give *whom* power? Mera? Alpha? I couldn't tell. Was this the water wars? Nobody uses the word.
- p48: Bi11bot has Adrian's band? Since when?
- p49: Mera. So the woman who tracked them in Issue 01 was one of Adrian's friends? Is she the villain? She doesn't act like one. She says "Stability is not a crime" and then shows them around.
- p50: "Mera's unlinked band remains beside the two linked bands." I didn't know what linking bands does.

| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|

| Covenant / Covenant bands | p46 | Partly |

### Lines I read twice
- "If we hold all the keys, everyone waits for our hands." (p50)
- "Come see what happens when nobody can decide." (p49). I thought she was threatening them. She wasn't?

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona was clear and I felt her. Mera was clear to look at but not as a person. I couldn't tell what she wanted.

### I felt something
Yes, p46–48, Ada and her mother. The only real gut-punch in the book, and it lasted three pages.

### I predicted
That Mera is the real villain and will turn on them.

Adrian, Leona and Mera made some kind of pact during a crisis, it went wrong, and the kids have to decide whether to take the power it left behind.

### Where I would have put it down
p47, the archive flashback, where I lost track of what was voted on and why it matters. Ada kept me reading.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
p50: "Then we don't hold them." It's a decision, not a cliffhanger. It's also the second time Alex has turned down a deal that would clear his debt, so I knew how it would go. It's not a reason to read on. The Ada story already ended in this issue.

## Issue 06 — The Fourth Choice (pages 57–64)

### Pulled me forward
- p61: "EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED." and the nodes turning from amber to white. A clean visual for "it's over."
- p62: "You counted the pipe after you stopped counting the homes." Tomas again, the best line in the issue.
- p63: the Tomas 40-credit receipt. "Took you long enough." / "You kept counting." A small, human payoff. I smiled.
- p64 last panel: "I'm showing up" matches "I know who keeps showing up for it" on p10. I noticed the echo and liked it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- p57–58: Alex offered total power *a third time* (Lock on p40, Mera on p50, now here), with the same projected images of Grandmother and clean water. I skimmed it. I knew the answer.
- p60–63: a committee meeting. Baselines, production records, dockets, a "revised denominator," evidence challenges. This is where the homework feeling was strongest. It's a lesson about public review dressed up as an ending.
- p63 panel 2: a six-line ledger. I didn't read it.

### I was confused
- p57: "THREE SAFEGUARDS PRESENT." *This* is where I finally understood that the Measure, the Seal and the Covenant are the three things from p21. I wish I'd known that in Issue 01. I had no idea I was collecting anything.
- **p60: Mara Soto.** Mara. We already have Mera, and Nera from p2. Mara, Mera, Nera. I had to stop and check whether Mara was Mera. I genuinely thought Mera had switched sides for a second.
- p62: "The revised denominator improves delivery." I don't know what that means. I gave up on it.
- p63: the debt drops from 418,107 to 314,159. Why that much? The ledger lists things but no amounts. And 314,159 is pi. My class would notice that and think it's either a joke or a bot.
- **p64 panel 2: "the damaged drone component from 2099."** We're in 2043. How is the 2099 part here? Is this a flash-forward? Is it the same terminal? I read it three times. This is the one place the prologue and the main story touch, and I can't make it make sense.
- p64 panel 3: "the cloth she has folded throughout the book." I don't remember her folding a cloth throughout the book. I saw it once, on p15. If it was meant to mean something, it didn't reach me.
- The prologue: who sent Nera and Voss in 2099? Did Alex end up "owning" the lake or not? Why do the thieves call it Phantum's lake if nobody owns it? I came back to the opening question, and the book never answered it.

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 00, page 8.** It's the first thing we read, the action has just finished, and then comes a page where nobody can say what's happening ("The record closes around the theft"). Half the class would decide the book is nonsense right there and never reach p12, which is the page that would have won them. Runner-up: Issue 03, page 41, four strangers in four panels.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dad's huge debt and finds out the town's water is being stolen. Then it turns into a lot of meetings about who gets credit and who's in charge. The debt part and the water numbers are good. Ada's mom part is good. Skip the middle."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 00:** Cool robot dog, then I didn't know what anyone was doing or why the theft didn't matter.
- **Issue 01:** The birthday debt grabbed me, and then a pile of names and markers nobody explained.
- **Issue 02:** The best issue: 62 versus 18, and the neighbours paying the 500.
- **Issue 03:** A group project with four people I can't tell apart, and the water mystery dropped.
- **Issue 04:** Three great pages of Ada and her mother, which came out of nowhere and ended too fast.
- **Issue 05:** The dad and the rising water finally made me feel something about Adrian.
- **Issue 06:** He said no to power for the third time, a committee voted, and pi was the debt.
