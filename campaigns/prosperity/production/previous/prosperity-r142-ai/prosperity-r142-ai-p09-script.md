## Page 9 (right) — The Old Book

**Panel 1.** Warm, dignified Keel apartment at dusk: repaired furniture, quilts, old books, faded photographs, plants in recycled containers and two ration-marked water jugs. Grandmother sits in her worn chair, folding and refolding a small cloth. Alex enters with his tool bag.

GRANDMOTHER: Adrian?

**Panel 2.** Alex pauses in the doorway, jaw tight but gentle.

ALEX: Not today, Grandma. It's Alex.

**Panel 3.** Alex kneels beside her at eye level. Her hands keep folding the cloth.

GRANDMOTHER: Of course. Alex. I knew that. Did your father come home yet?

ALEX: Not yet.

**Panel 4.** Grandmother’s gaze settles. Her posture firms. The cloth stops moving in her hands.

GRANDMOTHER: He's closer than you think.

**Panel 5.** Her hand points to a worn, hand-bound book on the upper shelf. A faint balanced water-drop mark appears on its spine.

GRANDMOTHER: Adrian, get down the book you put up there. The one you want Alex to have when he turns eighteen.

ALEX: ...I'm eighteen today, Grandma.

**Panel 6.** Alex reaches for the book. Warm apartment light frames him; Grandmother remains seated behind him.

GRANDMOTHER: Careful. He said it was worth protecting.

**PAGE CHECK:** Grandmother’s memory shift is shown through gaze, posture and the stopped cloth-folding, preserving her dignity. The book and water-drop mark create the mechanism for the next page.
