# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read all three issues in one sitting, in order, the night before it was due. I read only the art as described, the captions, the balloons, the screens and the SFX. A few panel descriptions say things no reader could see on the page, like where Ada "has followed" from on p17, what "earlier projected images" looked like on p35, and what "the sequence cuts before" on p45. I ignored those and reacted only to what the panel would show.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1.** "Then don't approve it." / "Just notice it works." In two lines I knew this guy is good with his hands and doesn't care about the rules. The red gauge over a pump that's clearly working is a good picture. I didn't hate him.
- **Page 2, panel 1.** The debt screen is the real hook. 418,607 credits, 28.7 years, transferred to you on your seventeenth birthday because your dad vanished. Then "Happy birthday, Phantum." That got me. It's the first moment I thought *okay, that's messed up, keep going.*
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The bouncer. "Your father always thought the next hand could change everything." / "It changed something." / "For everybody else." Now I know the dad was a gambler who screwed everyone, fast, with no exposition dump.
- **Page 4–5.** The grandma who thinks he's Adrian, the book that's a key, then the bookshelf swinging open. I know that's an old trick and I still liked it. A secret workshop behind a bookshelf is fun.
- **Page 6, panel 4.** "Well. That took longer than expected." The robot is the best thing in the issue. Page 7 kept it going: "You're taller than your father predicted," "I'm not everyone. I was in the room." From here I was reading for B11.
- **Page 10, panel 3.** "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR" / "That is today's pay." / "I know." He steps over the line anyway. Clear, cheap, and I felt it, because page 2 told me 40 is what he makes in a day.
- **Page 13, panel 4.** "Remain for protective detention." / "Protective?" / "A versatile word." Laughed.
- **Page 14–15.** Hands on the two pipes, one cold, one humming. Then "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%." This is the moment I finally knew what the book was about. It's a good reveal. It's also on page 15.
- **Page 16.** The choice is clear: leave quietly and nobody knows, or send it and go back. "Then they see it." I liked him for that.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3.** Ada's "fixed installments, same amount each time, no surprise fees" bit read like the money-management unit in health class. Then she closes on "leaving doesn't make you free if somebody else carries the cost." That's a poster, not a person talking. First time I felt the homework.
- **Page 8.** A woman from behind, a glass of water, two buttons. I skimmed it. I didn't know her and didn't know what the buttons meant.
- **Page 9.** Plans, a token, a receipt, a wristband, a folded map, installments. Lots of objects and not much happening. I skimmed this too.
- **Page 17.** Four different places in four panels: town screens, miners, the Oasis room, Ada showing up. I went fast and lost track.

### I was confused
- **Page 3.** Who is Ada? Friend, girlfriend, sister, loan shark? She just walks up with medicine. Medicine for whom? I guessed the grandma, but nobody says.
- **Page 8.** Who is Mera, and is she good or bad? What is "REPORT TO ALPHA"? What is "INDEPENDENT REPORT: SIGNAL RECORDED," and who sent it? *Confused but kind of intrigued*, but only kind of.
- **Page 9.** "I leave tonight." Why? To go where, do what? He has a map to "somewhere the records forgot." I didn't know what he was trying to do. Running away from debt? Finding his dad? Following the map because it's there?
- **Page 9, panel 4.** "I missed the first installment." On page 3, the same day, she said "same amount next week." How did he already miss one? I read it twice and gave up.
- **Page 9, panel 2.** His shift just happens to get moved to Site 6, exactly where the map goes. Is someone doing that on purpose? Nobody says.
- **Page 10, panel 4 / Page 11, panel 1.** B11 builds a dog out of parts in his bag *during* a rescue. Then the dog's head stays in one place while its legs and body wriggle off somewhere else. I couldn't picture it. I didn't know TJ was a dog until Tomas said "That dog yours?"
- **Page 12, panel 1.** Alex asks "Why me?" before anyone has asked him for anything.
- **Page 13.** Is Foreman-7 a person or a robot? The name says robot.
- **Page 14.** The brass thing he pulls out of the wall. Nobody names it in this issue. Later I found out it's "the Measure."
- **Page 17.** Mera opens a valve and water starts coming into the tunnel they're climbing. Is she helping them escape or trying to drown them? I genuinely couldn't tell. How did Ada get there? How did she know where to go?
- **Page 17 vs page 18.** Page 17 shows Tomas with the miners, "alive and limping." One page later the wristband says "TOMAS — SIGNAL LOST." So is he dead, arrested, or fine? I saw him alive a page ago, so the cliffhanger knocked itself out.

### Lines I read twice
- p2: "It changed something." / "For everybody else." (Got it on the second read. It works.)
- p3: "Leaving doesn't make you free if somebody else carries the cost."
- p4: "He's closer than you think." (Creepy and good. It's never paid off, see Issue 3.)
- p7: "Somewhere the records forgot."
- p9: "I'm making sure you don't mistake a route for a plan." I gave up on this one.
- p9: "If the map lies, look for what doesn't belong."
- p15: "Trace shows where. Not why."
- p17: "The door was closing." / "Then keep it open." Is she mad at him? Is it a metaphor? Both?

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, B11, Grandma, Tomas, the bouncer.
- **Half:** Ada. I know her voice, which is sharp and lectures a bit, but not what she *is* to Alex.
- **Couldn't:** Mera, who is just a back and a hand. The supervisor and the guard are just functions.

### I felt something
- p2, the birthday debt. Real anger on his behalf.
- p4, Grandma calling him Adrian. Small and sad.
- p12, Tomas: "Someone has to make this look like an accident." He's staying behind to take the fall for a kid he just met. I liked Tomas a lot in two pages.
- p16, choosing to send it. A small *yes*.

### I predicted
- From p2: Dad isn't dead, and we're going to find him.
- From p8: Mera is secretly on Alex's side, or she's the dad.
- From p12: Tomas dies.
- From p15: whoever is stealing the water is in the shiny city.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who inherits his missing gambler dad's debt finds the robot his dad left behind and discovers the town's water is being stolen.

### Where I would have put it down
The closest call was **page 9**. After Mera's page I had no idea what Alex wanted, and Ada was talking in riddles. The robot and Tomas got me to page 15, and the water reveal kept me in. In a shop I'd have kept going, barely.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Kind of. The image of climbing a pipe while water rises is fine. But the "SIGNAL LOST" scare is about Tomas, and the panel before showed him alive, so it didn't land. What actually pulls me to Issue 2 is the 62/18 thing (*does anything change?*) and the dad. I'd read it tomorrow, not tonight.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19.** "They saw it." / "Seeing isn't the same as changing." Good opener. It picks up right where Issue 1 left off.
- **Page 21, panel 1.** 312 patents. Wait, the gambler dad was an *inventor*? That's a real twist and I wanted to know more.
- **Page 21, panel 4.** "Protected from what?" / "From being asked to choose while frightened." Lock is creepy-polite, and I liked hating him. Best villain line in the book.
- **Page 22, panel 4.** "You can still walk away." / "That's what he's selling." Good.
- **Page 25.** Jalen: "I'm not coming with you." / "You already are." / "That's not reassuring." The only collaborator with a personality.
- **Page 27–28.** The build fails at 11%. Then Alex feels the edges: "The parts aren't wrong. The edges are." This ties back to page 1, where he fixes things by touch. I liked seeing his one skill matter. "I expected a longer failure." / "You can have one later." Funny.
- **Page 30, panel 4.** "I counted the record and missed the people. That was my error. What would you do?" The robot admitting it was wrong was the most interesting character beat in this issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 20.** This is where I checked out. A kiosk that takes a girl's invention and gives her 14 credits, then a "fair price" purifier shop with its wage and margin on a screen. Then the robot explains, "One business earns customers. The Houses were selected for scale." It's an economics worksheet with pictures. I skimmed the whole page and rolled my eyes.
- **Pages 23–26.** Collect four parts from four people, one page each. It felt like a video game fetch quest. Each person hands over a thing, says one wise sentence and a door closes. By Ren I was skimming.
- **Pages 31–32.** I thought they'd already won on page 28 (94%), so building it all again two pages later dragged.
- **Page 33.** "PATENT STATUS: UNFILED" / "No exclusive owner." Message received. It felt like the moral being handed to me.

### I was confused
- **Page 22, panel 3.** "Including Lina. Suri. Jalen. Ren." Who are these people? I'd never heard of any of them, and Alex rattles off four names like he grew up with them. How does he know them? This is the moment I felt most locked out of the book.
- **Page 22, panel 1.** "Caldera can build the purifier faster." *What* purifier? First mention.
- **Page 21.** "Charter Compact." What's that? Never explained.
- **Page 21–22.** If his dad had 312 patents, why is the family drowning in debt? Nobody says.
- **Page 23.** "This is not the Keel routing failure." So there are two water problems now? I thought the problem was stolen water. Now it's dirty water too, and a purifier fixes the dirty part but not the stolen part? I had to stop and sort this out.
- **Page 25, panel 4.** "Five people. Two machines. The patrol will classify six active bodies." Which five people? Five plus two is seven, so the joke is that TJ doesn't count? I read it three times.
- **Pages 26–27.** Doors close between the group and Ren and the others. Then one page later all of them are in a maker room together. How did they get there?
- **Page 28 vs pages 32–33.** 94%, then cloudy water, then 91%. Is cloudy water good? Was the first one fake? I lost the thread of what "winning" meant.
- **Page 34.** He refuses, and Lock says the Houses will decide anyway. Then page 36 says "HOUSE PRODUCTION AUTHORIZED." So what did refusing get him? Do the collaborators still lose their jobs? Page 36 says "ACCESS REVIEW PENDING," so maybe yes? I couldn't tell if this was a win.
- **Page 35.** Where did the medallion come from? What does it *do*? It warms up and the screen says "CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION REGISTERED." So the magic dad artifact is a… copyright form? That was a letdown.
- **Page 36.** "Covenant band," "ESCALATION: UNAVAILABLE UNDER STANDING SCOPE," "BOUNDED SUMMONS ISSUED." I didn't understand any of this. *Confused and detached.*
- **Where is Tomas?** Issue 1 ended on "SIGNAL LOST," and nobody in Issue 2 mentions him once. Alex doesn't ask. That bugged me more than anything else in this issue. The one character I cared about got dropped.
- **Where did Ada and TJ go?** Ada gets about three lines. TJ fetches a fastener.

### Lines I read twice
- p20: "One business earns customers. The Houses were selected for scale."
- p21: "Rights under dispute are not a wallet."
- p21: "From being asked to choose while frightened." (Read it twice because it was good.)
- p23: "The Measure showed where water went. This is what's in a different feeder."
- p23: "My access is already being counted."
- p25: "Five people. Two machines. The patrol will classify six active bodies."

- p35: "I carried it until someone proved they understood what it was for."
- p36: "ESCALATION: UNAVAILABLE UNDER STANDING SCOPE." Gave up.

Something I noticed by this issue: **everyone talks the same way.** It's always a short two-part fortune cookie, X isn't Y: "Seeing isn't the same as changing." "Time is useful. It isn't a replacement pipe." "Thinking about it isn't doing it." Ada, Lina, Suri, B11 and Lock all do it. That's the moment I started wondering if a person wrote this, and people in my class would say it out loud.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex, B11, Lock, Jalen.
- **Blurred:** Lina, Suri and Ren. I knew them by their part (membrane, living filter, heat), not by personality. If you swapped their lines I wouldn't notice. Suri is a little prickly. That's it.
- **Faded out:** Ada and TJ.
- **Faceless:** the three House representatives.

### I felt something
- p10–p30 bridge: B11 saying "That was my error. What would you do?" A little moment.
- p28: the fix landing. A small *nice*.
- Not much else. I didn't care about the four collaborators losing their jobs because I'd known each of them for one page.

### I predicted
- From p21: he won't sign. (Correct, and obvious.)
- From p29: they'll build it without patents and share it. (Correct.)
- From p36: Mera is going to turn out to be connected to the dad.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex has to decide whether to sell his dad's inventions to a big company to clear his debt, or share them with the people who actually made them.

That's a different book from the one I thought I was reading after Issue 1, which was about stolen water and a missing dad. Neither of those comes up much in this issue.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 20.** The kiosk and the fair-price shop page with the robot explaining it was the shelf moment. Page 22's list of four strangers would have finished it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. A woman I don't know pushes a button called SUMMON on a screen full of words I don't understand. Nobody I care about is in danger. The cliffhanger is a menu. The only reason I'd open Issue 3 is that it's called "Oasis" and I still want to know what happened to the dad. The book has made me wait 36 pages for that.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: no

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 37, panel 2.** "VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND." / "It knew my name." Ada is *from* the rich city? Real surprise. I sat up.
- **Pages 38–40.** The Ada and Leona scene is the best writing in the book. "I stopped asking whether that meant dead or unwilling." "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." "You chose safety instead of me." These are real people saying real, painful things, and for the first time nobody was teaching me anything. I liked that Alex stays in the hallway.
- **Page 40, panels 3–4.** Mera walks in: "I promised Leona I would keep you safe." / "You watched me." / "I did." / "Watching is not knowing." / "Then don't call it care." This went back and changed page 8 for me: she was watching *Ada*. Cool.
- **Page 44.** "First, people stopped selling to him. Then people stopped standing near him." That was chilling, and the only time Alpha felt scary instead of like paperwork.
- **Page 45.** The flashback, with water under the door and the dad sending the robot away. "Alex. What you do with this is up to you." It hit.
- **Page 46.** "He left me the debt. The silence. The machine." / "He left you a choice." Then "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "And he still built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Good, and B11's "Also yes" made me smile.
- **Page 52.** Tomas is ALIVE. Then "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." This was the best callback in the book. It turns the Issue 1 reveal into a weapon, and the guy who saved Alex is the one holding it.
- **Page 53.** "The allocation is compliant." / "The water isn't." Good line.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 41–42.** The bands, which one is which metal, two of three custodians, the deciding vote. I had to slow down to get it, then it turned into a recorded confession at a terminal.
- **Page 47.** "THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED / HUMAN ROLE REQUIRED: CONVENOR / AUTHENTICATOR." Skimmed.
- **Pages 49–51.** I skimmed almost all of these: "DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL," "EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED WITHIN DEFINED SCOPE," "ALPHA ROLE: OPERATION / MONITORING / MAINTENANCE / ACCOUNTING / ALERTS," "EXISTING DEBT, OWNERSHIP, ALLOCATIONS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS." The climax of the book is three nameless people saying one phrase each, then all-caps screens. It's a civics test.

### I was confused
- **Page 41.** "We built the Covenant during the Water War." So *this* is what everything has been about? A Water War, an emergency, three custodians, and Adrian was one of them? The gambler dad from the casino was one of three people who handed the world to an AI? That should be the biggest reveal in the book, and it's one panel on page 41. Everything I needed in Issue 1 shows up here.
- **Page 41.** Why does B11 have Adrian's band in his chest? It was never shown before.
- **Page 42.** The bands "link and glow." What does that do?
- **Pages 45–46.** Is Adrian dead? The flashback cuts away. Grandma said on page 4, "He's closer than you think." That's never explained. Is he alive somewhere? Was she just confused? I wanted that answered more than anything else, and the book acts like it was never asked.
- **Page 47.** Mera's dark band "remains apart" on page 42, then gets put on the node on page 47. Did she agree? When?
- **Page 48.** She offers him total power and he almost takes it. It isn't clear why she has the right to offer it, if her own override failed on page 44.
- **Page 48, panel 4.** "Don't give me that much power." Is Ada saying she doesn't trust herself? Or him? I read it twice.
- **Page 50.** Who are these three people? They decide the fate of the world, and I don't know their names.
- **Pages 52–53.** Where is Tomas? He's in Keel checking a gauge, but the vote is in Oasis. Is he on a screen? Then he's talking in the hall? I couldn't place him.
- **Page 52.** So the water was *still* at 18% weeks later? What happened to the miners opening the valve on page 17?
- **Page 54.** His debt went from 418,607 to 402,110. That's 16,000 credits paid off in "weeks" when he makes 40 a day and just lost his workshop. Math kids will catch it. I did.
- **Page 54.** Where are B11 and TJ? The robot is basically the reason I kept reading, and he's not on the last page.

### Lines I read twice
- p40: "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't." (Worth it once I got it.)
- p41: "Alpha recorded 'later' as permission to continue." (Same, a good line.)
- p42: "I will support a process where affected people can see what is promised and call it broken." Gave up halfway.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Ada (finally a full person), Leona, Mera (finally a person, on page 40), Alex, B11, Tomas.
- **Couldn't:** the Keel gauge worker, the Halyard representative, the Oasis clerk and the House representative. They're job titles. The ending belongs to people I can't name.
- **Gone:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. They built the purifier, then disappear. I don't know whether they lost their jobs.

### I felt something
- p39, "You chose safety instead of me." This was the biggest one.
- p45, the dad in the flooding room, saying his son's name.
- p46, "Also yes."
- p52, Tomas alive. Relief, then a grin at "Pipe says eighteen."
- p54, mixed. I respected that he's still in debt and nothing is magically fixed. I also felt cheated. He did all that, lost the workshop and his travel pass, and paid down about 4%. Some of my class will call this "realistic." Most will call it "so what was the point."

### I predicted
- From p37: Ada's family is powerful. (Sort of right.)
- From p43: the dad is alive in the station. (Wrong. He's gone, maybe dead, the book won't say.)
- From p48: he won't take the power. (Correct, and obvious from the moment it was offered.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid refuses to become the new ruler after finding out his dad helped hand the world to an AI, and instead sets up a slow public process where people can challenge the system.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 51.** Two pages of all-caps screen language at the climax. Pages 38–46 had me, then it turned into a council meeting. Tomas on page 52 pulled me back, but in a shop I'd already have closed it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
For the book overall: no. The last page is quiet, two people looking over a half-dry basin. It ends a book; it doesn't ask for another. I got some of what I wanted (the water, Tomas, Ada's mom). The thing I wanted most, what happened to the dad and what Grandma meant, got left on the table, and that isn't a hook because the book doesn't seem to know it's open. I wouldn't buy a sequel.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 51; next issue: no

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 20.** The contribution kiosk, the "fair price" shop with its wage and margin on the screen, and the robot explaining that the Houses "were selected for scale." Everyone would recognize this as the econ lesson the book was assigned for. After that, nobody believes the story isn't a worksheet. The ones who survive it get lost two pages later on "Lina. Suri. Jalen. Ren." Anyone still reading in Issue 3 gets lost on page 51.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid gets stuck with his dead-beat dad's debt, finds a really funny robot his dad left, and finds out the rich city is stealing his town's water. Then it turns into a thing about patents and voting. The robot's good, and there's a really good part with Ada and her mom."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Strong hook and a robot I liked, but I didn't know what Alpha was or what Alex wanted until page 15, and the cliffhanger about Tomas contradicted itself.
- **Issue 02:** A fetch quest through four strangers' labs that felt like an economics assignment, saved a little by Lock, Jalen and the connector fix, and it forgot about Tomas.
- **Issue 03:** The Ada and Leona scene and "Pipe says eighteen" are the best things in the book, but the world finally gets explained on page 41, the climax is screens, and I never found out what happened to the dad.
