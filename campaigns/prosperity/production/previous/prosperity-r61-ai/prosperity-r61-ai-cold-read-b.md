# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, all 54 pages, three 18-page issues, the night before it was due. I did not choose this book. Page counts check out: Issue 01 is pages 1–18, Issue 02 is 19–36, Issue 03 is 37–54. Everything below is what happened to me while reading, not what I worked out afterwards.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 1: the unapproved-repair exchange. "That isn't the approved repair." / "Then don't approve it." I was expecting a lecture and got a kid with a scarred hand on a pump. Fine, I'm looking.
- Page 2: the birthday debt screen is the best thing in the issue. 418,607 credits, 28.7 years, "Happy birthday, Phantum." I read every line of that screen. That's a real gut-punch and it costs the page nothing.
- Page 13: "The survey says straight. The bolts say left." Concrete, visual, I understood it instantly. This is the book at its best — physical truth versus official record, no speech.
- Page 14–15: the cold pipe and the vibrating pipe, then REPORTED 62% / DELIVERED 18%. I got genuinely angry at that number. That's the hook of the whole book and it works because nobody explains it, they just show two pipes.
- TJ's pieces wriggling across the floor while his head watches the foreman (page 11). Most fun I had in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 8: a woman seen from behind clicks TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. I get that it's meant to be ominous but I don't know who she is, so I watched it happen and felt nothing.
- Pages 15–16: the Measure is sitting in a fitted recess exactly where it's needed, does exactly the three things the plot needs. I muttered "convenient" and kept going.

### I was confused
- Page 9: "I missed the first installment." Ada said "same amount next week" on page 3, and everything since reads like a day or two. When did a week pass?
- Page 9: Ada suddenly knows the secret pipe-mark system ("every third junction has a pipe mark"). How does the medicine lender know the abandoned mine better than the map?
- Page 16: the choice is spelled out as binary — slip out the side passage, or send the evidence and "go back through Tomas's door" (i.e., get caught). He sends it. Then page 17 he's at an ascent entrance and Mera's opened a third route. The stated price of sending just evaporates. I reread pages 16–17 twice to see what I missed. I don't think I missed anything; the consequence got cheated.
- Pages 17–18: who opened what, and why does the "rescue" route fill with water while they're inside it? Miners open a residential regulator, Mera opens a conduit segment, the conduit floods around the heroes. I couldn't tell if the water was helping Keel, attacking them, or just happening. Geography of the climax is mud.
- Page 18: "TOMAS — SIGNAL LOST." I just saw Tomas alive and limping two panels earlier (page 17), at Site 6, with the miners. He was never in the conduit, so "No Tomas is visible" in the cliffhanger panel means nothing, and since when does Alex's wage-band track a coworker's life signal? Read as manufactured stakes. I didn't buy it.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Alpha. Page 5, "pre-Alpha pipes"; page 8, "[REPORT TO ALPHA]." Never explained anywhere in the issue. A company? A government? An era? I guessed "the system that runs everything" and read on, but I was being asked to feel unease about a word.
- The Measure (page 14). Capital-M device, hidden in the wall, three perfect functions. Nobody says who made it or why it's there.
- TJ's name. Page 10's description just starts calling the assembled dog "TJ." Nobody names him on-panel. Minor, but I noticed the name arrive from nowhere.
- Mera (page 8). A name and the back of a head.
- Grandmother's hand-bound book with the water-drop mark (page 4). It works as the shelf key, I think, but it's never opened and never comes back. What's in it?

### Lines I read twice
- "Leaving doesn't make you free if somebody else carries the cost" (page 3). Read it twice. It sounds wise, but I couldn't attach it to anyone specific — and by the end of the issue Alex leaves his sick, confused grandmother alone in Keel and nobody, including the book, says a word about who carries that cost. The line accuses and then the story does the thing.
- "Somewhere the records forgot" (page 7). Cute, vague, read twice, moved on.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, B11, Tomas, Grandmother — all distinct. B11's dry voice ("Thinking about it isn't doing it") is the best voice in the issue. The guard, the bouncer and Foreman-7 blur into one uniform, but they're one-scene men so it doesn't cost much. Mera is a silhouette with a name tag.

### I felt something
- Page 2: the debt transfer. Anger on his behalf.
- Page 10: he steps over the yellow line and loses the 40 credits he earned today — the same 40 from the birthday screen. Felt that.
- Page 15: the 18%. Real anger at a fictional number.

### I predicted
- The dad's secret room (called it at "He's closer than you think," page 4).
- The rich city is taking the water — I called it at page 14's two pipes, before the 18% confirmed it.
- Alex would send the evidence rather than slip away. The book's moral shape is visible from page 1.
- Tomas's "signal lost" is a fake-out. (Page 52 proves me right.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A debt-trapped kid finds physical proof that the city's water records are lies, and the problem is whether proof can do anything.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue. It moves, the hook is clean, and the 62/18 reveal landed. The flooding-conduit muddle on pages 17–18 is where I shrugged, not where I stopped.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 18: climbing toward the light with water rising below. That's a real cliffhanger — people I know, in danger I can see — even though the Tomas "signal lost" beat misfired because I'd just watched him limp around alive. Yes, I'd turn to Issue 02. Mostly I want to know what the 18% proof does to the people upstairs, and whether Adrian is dead.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 21: Lock. "Rights under dispute are not a wallet" — and especially "Protected from what?" / "From being asked to choose while frightened." That's a properly chilling villain line. He's calm, he's reasonable, and he wants everything.
- Page 25: B11 counting bodies. "Five people. Two machines. The patrol will classify six active bodies." / "TJ's a dog." / "The system has not met him." I actually laughed. Best beat in the issue.
- Page 28: the salvaged-sleeve fix — filing an edge so two parts that were never allowed to connect can connect — is a good quiet callback to page 1's unapproved repair. I noticed it and it worked on me.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 23–26: four pages, four collaborators, same scene four times. Arrive, receive part, thematic line, security door closes. By Suri's page (24) I'd spotted the pattern; by Jalen's (25) I was skimming. Ren's page (26) I barely read.
- Pages 31–32: the rebuild montage is four people announcing their sub-problem in turn ("The membrane needs area." / "The filter needs rest." / "The power can cycle." / "The heat can feed the filter."). It reads like a worksheet. Skimmed.
- Page 30: the big theme speech. B11: "I counted the record and missed the people. That was my error. What would you do?" The robot confesses the moral of the book and then hands moral authority to a seventeen-year-old. I didn't slow down because I was lost; I slowed down because I was being lectured, and I can smell a lesson.

### I was confused
- Page 22: Alex rattles off "Lina. Suri. Jalen. Ren." — four names never mentioned before, by a kid who has never met them, before any scene shows him learning them. I flipped back thinking I'd skipped a page. I hadn't.
- Page 27: Lina said "Adrian left each of us a piece" — Adrian's own pieces — and then Adrian's own pieces don't fit together? "Vector standard rejects the Caldera cycle." So were these rebuilt to House standards? If they're the original pieces, why don't they interface? The metaphor (systems built to lock) is clear; the logic isn't.
- Page 28 vs page 33: SIMULATED RECOVERY goes 94% and then later 91%, with nobody remarking on it. The number went down. Why? A math-nerd reader notices.
- Page 31: "The four collaborators return to the room." Suri walked out with the group, Jalen explicitly said he wasn't coming, Ren shoved a module through a closing door and stayed behind it. When did all four get past House security into the maker room?
- Page 35: "Earlier projected images were translucent; this object casts a solid shadow." What earlier projected images? There is no earlier appearance of this medallion in the pages I read. This reads like a callback to a scene that doesn't exist. Gave up on it.
- Page 36: "BOUNDED SUMMONS ISSUED." Summons to where, issued by what authority, and what does "bounded" limit? Also — where did Grandmother go? Alex has been in two cities for days and his demented grandmother is alone in Keel with, I assume, the medicine Ada sells. Nobody mentions her once in the whole issue. The book opened by telling me leaving dumps the cost on someone else.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Morrow & Sons (page 20). A fair Keel business on a screen, nobody we've met, shown like it matters. It matters on page 54; here it's a name.
- The Charter Compact (page 21). The thing Lock wants the estate signed to. Never defined.
- "The Houses were selected for scale" (page 20). Selected how, when, by whom? "That is what the record says" is not an answer, and the book knows it.
- Alpha, again (page 20 — first time anyone says the name aloud: "By Alpha?"). Twenty pages in and the reader still has no working definition.
- The Seal (page 35). A bronze medallion Lina has carried for fifteen years; it warms, a screen says CONTRIBUTION ATTRIBUTION REGISTERED. What it is, what system it registers to, why it matters — all deferred.
- Mera's "dark Covenant band" (page 36, description text). "Covenant" appears with zero context.
- "One deadline" (page 26). What deadline?

### Lines I read twice
- "From being asked to choose while frightened" (page 21). Twice, because it's good.
- "That's what he's selling" (page 22). Twice, because I had to unpack it — walking away is the product. Fine, got there.
- "I counted the record and missed the people" (page 30). Understood on first read; the problem isn't confusion, it's that the book stopped trusting me.
- Page 35's "earlier projected images" line. Read twice, gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lock: completely distinct, best character in the issue. The four collaborators: defined by their components, not their selves. Suri stuck ("someone who notices when it stops breathing" — good line). Jalen stuck only as "the one who wouldn't come." Ren was "welding person." By the page 31 montage I was flipping back to check who brought membrane. And Lina's surname is VEY (page 26), which is going to collide with VEYRA next issue — I can feel it coming.

### I felt something
- Page 21: "choose while frightened" — cold little shiver.
- Page 25: the TJ/bodies exchange — actual laugh.
- Page 30: what I felt was embarrassment, for the book.

### I predicted
- Alex refuses to sign. Obvious from the moment the offer is made.
- The five parts fail on first assembly. Genre rhythm, called it.
- Lock threatens the collaborators. Called it; he does it twice.
- Ada is more than a Keel medicine lender. Her page-9 pipe knowledge made me suspicious she's connected upstairs.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about who owns ideas — the Houses own everything their workers make, and Alex won't hand over his father's estate. (Note: this is a different book from Issue 01's water-theft story. Two issues, two subjects.)

### Where I would have put it down
Page 25. Third identical errand scene in a row, and I'd figured out there was a fourth coming. In a shop, that's where it goes back on the shelf.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 36 is a cursor selecting SUMMON from a menu. As a cliffhanger it's nothing — a bureaucratic click, no question I need answered, nobody I care about standing in it. What carries me into Issue 03 is the leftover business: Adrian's fate, what Mera even is, whether Lock burns the four collaborators. A tired yes, and it's no thanks to the last page itself.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 25; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 37: the gate hesitates for Alex and opens instantly for Ada — "VEYRA, A. — STATUS: UNRESOLVED." Now that's a hook. One screen recontextualized a whole character.
- Pages 39–40: Ada and Leona. "Did you know where I was?" / "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." I sat up. Then "You chose safety instead of me" and "Watching is not knowing." / "Then don't call it care." Best sustained scene in the book.
- Page 44: the three screens crushing Adrian — parts denied, travel restricted, pursuit pattern. "First, people stopped selling to him." / "Then people stopped standing near him." Three UI cards do more worldbuilding than forty pages of the word "Alpha." This is where Alpha finally became scary instead of a name.
- Page 45: Adrian's recording. "Alex. What you do with this is up to you." The cut before his death is right; the scene lands.
- Pages 48–49: the temptation. "Take executive authority… you could correct it tonight." And Ada: "That's still two people deciding for everyone else." / "Don't give me that much power." For one panel, when Alex said "We could share it," I genuinely wasn't sure which way he'd go. Best scene in the issue.
- Pages 52–53: "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." The numbers came back as characters. "The allocation is compliant." / "The water isn't." I grinned. That's the whole book in six words, and nobody speechifies it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 50–51: the procedure pages. Evidence placed, statements read, and then the climax card: DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL. I read it twice because I couldn't believe that was the finale. Page 51's victory screen is a five-item job description for Alpha. I get that the dryness is the point — the emotional climax already happened on page 48 — but my eyes still slid.
- Page 47: "THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED." The Measure, the Seal and the bands are suddenly a matched set of three keys, and I noticed the collect-three-artifacts skeleton only now, at the moment it's cashed. Each piece arrived by convenience (found in a recess, carried in a uniform, sitting in a robot's chest), so the assembly feels dealt, not earned.

### I was confused
- Page 41: Adrian's Covenant band is revealed inside B11's chest compartment, as if I knew it was there. Nobody planted it. (Also that chest now holds a membrane component, a brass band and the Measure. It's a clown car.)
- Page 41: "We built the Covenant during the Water War. Two of three custodians could authorize emergency authority." This is the actual premise of the entire world — what Alpha is, why it has power, why nobody can switch it off — and it arrives on page 41 of 54. I understood the whole book backwards from here. Everything before was me guessing.
- Mera. Three issues in: she has keys, an override panel, a band, a promise to Leona. What is her job? Who does she answer to? Never said. She's a function wearing a name.
- Grandmother. Never mentioned. Not once, in either city, by anyone. Sick, demented, alone, and the story that scolded "somebody else carries the cost" never pays her a thought. I kept waiting.
- Page 54: the debt drops from 418,607 to 402,110 — 16,497 credits — in "weeks," by a kid whose wage is 40 a day. That's over a year of full wages in weeks. Unless purifier money, which is never stated. The math doesn't work.
- Page 54: "Alex makes a payment to Ada… ADA VEYRA — PERSONAL BALANCE REDUCED." If he's repaying her, why does her balance go down? Read it three times. Either she's in debt too (never said) or the screen means his outstanding loan (then say that). Unresolved.
- Adrian's fate: WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED, a flashback that cuts before the death, Grandmother's "He's closer than you think" — and then nothing. Hook or dropped thread, I can't tell, and I felt the lack.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The Water War (page 41). The founding catastrophe, first named at page 41, spoken like common knowledge.
- The Covenant and its two-of-three rule (page 41). The book's actual rulebook, delivered in dialogue near the end.
- The "legacy node" (page 47). A socket that takes the three artifacts, appearing exactly when the plot needs one.
- Alpha. Final verdict on the ledger: name used from page 5, working definition assembled around pages 41–44. Forty pages of being asked to feel a word.

### Lines I read twice
- "I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't" (page 40). Twice; landed.
- "Alpha recorded 'later' as permission to continue" (page 41). Twice; the entire book in one line, thirteen pages from the end.
- "Her personal balance decreases" (page 54). Three times; still unsure.
- "DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL" (page 50). Twice, in disbelief.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Ada becomes the best person in the book this issue. Leona is distinct. And here it is: Lina VEY versus Leona VEYRA versus Ada VEYRA. On page 38 I genuinely thought Lina was about to be Ada's mother or aunt. Three near-identical surnames, no relationship between them, never addressed. The three civic workers (gauge worker, maintenance rep, allocation clerk) are walking job titles, but they're plainly meant to be, so it doesn't hurt.

### I felt something
- Page 39: "You chose safety instead of me." Felt it.
- Page 46: "He left you a choice." / "He was still a gambler." / "And he still built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Felt that too — the book letting Adrian be both things.
- Page 48: "Don't give me that much power." Best thing Ada does all book.
- Page 49: the refusal has a price — workshop sealed, clearance expiring, debt stays. I respected the book here. It didn't pay him off for being good.
- Page 53: "The water isn't." Grinned.
- Page 54: "You still thinking about leaving?" / "Every day." / "Good." Quiet, earned, worked.

### I predicted
- Mera is tied to Ada's past. Right.
- Alex refuses executive power. Right, though the "we could share it" feint briefly got me.
- The debt is not magically fixed. Right — and I was glad to be right; an erased debt would have made me put the book down on the last page out of spite.
- Adrian's death is shown or answered. Wrong — and I'm still a little annoyed about it.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An emergency system never got switched off because the permission had no expiry date, and the answer is that nobody — not even a good kid — should get to hold the controls alone. (Third issue, third subject: water theft, then ownership, then governance.)

### Where I would have put it down
Page 50. The civic-hall procedure is where the book stops being a story and starts being a model of a process. By then I was too close to the end to actually quit, but that's where the reading became duty.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 54 is an overlook, an open appeal marker, and two people not deciding things for each other. It's a good ending for this book — and it closes the book. Nothing on it pulls me forward. The one thread that could (is Adrian alive?) was deliberately left to dangle, but the story just spent three issues teaching me not to expect adventure payoffs, so I don't expect one. If a next book exists, it's about an appeals process. I wish these people well and I would not buy it.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 50; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 51. The climax of the book, the moment everything turns, and the big screen reads: "ALPHA ROLE: OPERATION / MONITORING / MAINTENANCE / ACCOUNTING / ALERTS." The payoff of 51 pages is a job description. Half the class is on their phones by MAINTENANCE. Page 50's "DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL" is the warm-up act that gets them reaching for their pockets.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid inherits half a million credits of his missing dad's debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds out the rich cities upstairs are stealing his town's water and faking the numbers, and instead of blowing anything up he proves it with gauges, builds a home water filter with his dad's old work crew so no company can own it, gets offered emergency control of the whole system and turns it down, and the happy ending is a public complaint process that actually works. There's also a robot dog that comes apart into pieces and it's the best character. It's smarter than it sounds and slower than it should be, and it absolutely is a lesson, but the good bits — the lying gauges, the mum scene, "the water isn't" — are good enough that I finished it without hating it.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: Rust, debt and a number that lies — a kid proves the water's being stolen and I was genuinely in.
- Issue 02: Four strangers hand over four parts in the same scene four times while a polite man threatens everyone with paperwork, and the dog gets the only laugh.
- Issue 03: Mum left, dad's tape plays, the villain turns out to be a permission slip nobody ever cancelled, and the ending is a committee I'm glad exists and never want to read about again.
