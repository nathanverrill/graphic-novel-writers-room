# Prosperity — Audition B

## Page 1 (right) — Alex keeps a failing pump alive by trusting the machine instead of the record.

**Panel 1 — FULL-PAGE SPLASH.** Inside a Keel pump hall before dawn. Alex Phantum, seventeen, lean and wiry in patched charcoal mining trousers, a faded rust-brown work jacket and reinforced boots, braces one scarred palm against a trembling pump housing. His head tilts as he listens through his hand. A salvaged bracket holds the pipe together where the approved part should be. Dust hangs in the work lights. The official gauge shivers in the red while the pump runs steadily beneath Alex’s palm. His battered tool bag lies open at his feet. A white-and-amber control node watches from the wall.

The supervisor stands behind him, arms folded, looking at the bracket, the gauge, and the running pump.

SUPERVISOR: That isn't the approved repair.

ALEX: Then don't approve it.

The pump holds.

SFX: THRUMM—THRUMM—THRUMM

ALEX: Just notice it works.

PAGE CHECK: Alex is introduced through competent, consequential action. The physical machine already disagrees with the official record. Page-turn function: move from what Alex can fix to what the system has decided he owes.

## Page 2 (left) — Alex’s seventeenth birthday turns his father’s absence into a public liability.

**Panel 1 — WIDE.** The Keel exit checkpoint at the end of the shift. Alex, dusty and tired, places his hand against the scanner. A short queue of workers waits behind him. Their balance displays flicker up and vanish. Alex’s display expands above the scanner, much larger than the others.

The screen reads:

**TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY**  
**ALEX PHANTUM — AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN**  
**PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE**  
**GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED**  
**DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS**  
**EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**  
**PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS**  
**TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**

SFX: BEEP

GUARD: Happy birthday, Phantum.

ALEX: I've had better surprises.

---

**Panel 2 — MEDIUM.** The workers pass through the open gate around Alex. His account remains projected above him. One worker glances at the balance, then looks away. Alex stands alone beneath the number, tool bag hanging from one hand.

A red line appears beneath the debt display:

**REVIEW REQUIRED: HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY**

No one speaks.

---

**Panel 3 — MEDIUM CLOSE.** Outside the checkpoint, Alex walks along a Keel lane beneath rusted pipes and stacked housing. His hand turns a worn casino chip in his pocket. A looping equation is etched into its face. Behind him, the checkpoint’s white-and-amber node blinks through the doorway.

ALEX (small): Twenty-eight point seven years.

---

**Panel 4 — WIDE.** Alex passes the shuttered LAST CHANCE casino. Its faded sign flickers over a sealed doorway. An exhausted bouncer leans against the frame, recognizing Alex without needing to ask.

BOUNCER: That your birthday face?

ALEX: It's my debt face.

The bouncer notices the chip in Alex’s hand.

BOUNCER: Your father always thought the next hand could change everything.

ALEX: It changed something.

BOUNCER: For him?

ALEX: For everybody else.

PAGE CHECK: Adrian is established as absent, financially damaging, and connected to the chip without excusing the gambling. Alex’s public humiliation is visible before Ada enters. Page-turn function: leave Alex with a concrete desire—find a way out before the debt becomes his whole life.

## Page 3 (right) — Ada offers Alex a way out, but makes him accept that leaving still creates an obligation.

**Panel 1 — WIDE.** Late afternoon at the rusted overlook above Keel’s exhausted pit. Alex sits on the railing, looking toward Halyard and the pale geometry of Oasis above it. The casino chip rests beside his hand. Ada Veyra approaches through the wind carrying a small paper-wrapped medicine packet and a single travel token. She is twenty, compact and strong from Keel work, with long wind-tangled copper hair, freckles, teal-blue eyes, a dark high-collared shirt, patched brown trousers, practical boots, and a moss-green neck wrap. The metal rivet on her shoulder strap catches the light.

ADA: You look expensive.

ALEX: It came with a twenty-eight-year warranty.

---

**Panel 2 — MEDIUM TWO-SHOT.** Ada sits beside him and places the medicine packet and token on the railing between them. Alex looks at the token first, then the medicine.

ADA: The medicine is for your grandmother.

ALEX: I know what medicine is.

ADA: Good. We can skip the educational portion.

ALEX: I can't pay you today.

ADA: I didn't ask for today.

---

**Panel 3 — CLOSE ON THE OBJECTS AND HANDS.** Ada’s fingers tap the travel token once, then pull back. Alex’s scarred hand hovers over the medicine packet but does not take it.

ADA: Fixed installments. Same amount each time. No surprise fees.

ALEX: You planning to become a bank?

ADA: Banks don't bring medicine up a hill.

ALEX: The token, too?

ADA: If you still want out after you sleep.

---

**Panel 4 — WIDE, QUIET.** Alex takes the medicine. The token remains on the railing between them. Keel’s crowded lanes and dry channels lie below; Halyard’s terraces catch the last gold; Oasis shines above the clouds.

Alex looks at the token, then at Ada.

ALEX: You know I’m leaving.

ADA: You keep saying that.

ALEX: This time I can afford the first step.

ADA: No. This time somebody fronted it.

Alex pockets the medicine. After a beat, he takes the token too.

ALEX: I’ll pay you back.

ADA: I know.

PAGE CHECK: Ada is Alex’s friend and creditor, not a delivery system or romantic counterweight. Alex accepts both immediate obligations under fixed terms; his desire to leave now has a personal cost. The page ends with the token in his pocket and the question of what he will leave behind.
