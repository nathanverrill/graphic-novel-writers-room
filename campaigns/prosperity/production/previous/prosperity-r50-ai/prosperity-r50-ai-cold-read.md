# Cold Read: Prosperity

Read as assigned homework the night before, by a 16-year-old who has read graphic novels, wanted this to be bad, and puts things down fast. I read straight through once, Issue 01 to Issue 03, and only reread a line when I couldn't follow it.

---

## Issue 01: Keel

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 1.** The debt screen. It's my birthday, I'm seventeen, and the government hands me my missing dad's 418,607-credit debt and 28.7 years to pay it. This is the best page in the whole book. I got it right away, it's unfair, and I was angry for him. "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "I've had better surprises." works.
- **Page 4, panel 2.** Grandma's eyes clear: "He's closer than you think." Creepy in a good way.
- **Page 5.** The book into the gap, the CLICK, the bookshelf swinging open. I've seen it a hundred times and it still worked. "Before he went to work." is a good chill.
- **Page 7, panel 4.** "Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't." / "...I thought about it." This is where the robot won me over. He sounds like an actual character.
- **Page 9, panel 4.** The woman from behind choosing TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. I didn't know what Alpha was, but I knew someone important had just seen him and chosen not to tell. Confused but intrigued.
- **Page 18, panels 1–3.** REPORTED 62%, DELIVERED 18%. Finally a number I understand that shows somebody lying. Then he has to choose between his way out and telling everyone, and he picks telling everyone. That was a real choice and I felt it.
- **Page 17, panel 3.** "Protective?" / "A versatile word." made me laugh.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 8.** The hologram dad. "You deserve answers. But they have to be earned." "You'll become a better man than I ever was." I've heard these lines in every movie, so I skimmed. "Question everything. Especially me." is the only line I kept.
- **Page 10, panels 3–4.** The history slideshow. "The crises began with scarcity. They became lethal when people decided who deserved what was left." This is where it started to feel like the homework it is. I skimmed it.
- **Page 11.** I skimmed most of this. Three objects get put on the table and named in one go, and I couldn't hold any of them.
- **Page 13.** The map talk and the money maths. I read it slowly because I had to, not because I wanted to.

### I was confused
- **Pages 1–2, the time of day.** Page 1 is dawn, page 2 panel 1 is end of shift, page 2 panel 3 is morning again. I lost track of what day it was.
- **Page 3.** I didn't know who the girl was. No one says her name until the robot does on page 10. I spent seven pages just calling her "the friend."
- **Page 4, panels 3–4.** Ada is suddenly standing in his apartment, and in the next panel they are "walking toward home." Weren't they at home? And how does she know "He wouldn't just leave"? Did she know his dad? Alex was two.
- **Page 11.** "Adrian built these after the first agreement failed." What first agreement? "They only move when people do." I have no idea what that means. Measure, Maker's Seal, Covenant: three capitalised things at once and I couldn't tell you what any of them does except the first one.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** "Grandma said he wasn't gambling." / "She's half right. Both were true." Half right and both true can't both be right. I gave up on that one.
- **Page 13, panel 2.** "His plans put a pipe under every third junction." Whose plans? Why does Ada know them from memory?
- **Page 13, panel 4.** He slides the medicine toward Grandma, then puts the medicine in his own tool bag? Is he giving it to her or taking it? And with a debt of 418,000, why does the 50-credit token matter? It never comes up again.
- **Page 14.** Who is Tomas? He's just there. TJ assembles out of parts I didn't know were a dog. Bi11bot says "TJ. Reach the release." and Alex answers "Then we need a different plan." That doesn't answer anything that was said. I reread the page and still couldn't tell what the plan was, what the release was, or why a frame collapsing meant they needed one.
- **Page 15, panel 4.** Tomas gives him "the old mechanical key." He already had a key from page 6. Is this a second key? The same one?
- **Page 18, panel 3.** The regulator is in a hidden chamber under a sealed mine, but "outside, Keel workers physically seize the regulator wheel." Which wheel? Where? Then he "loses the route" by choosing to publish, and on the next panel they escape anyway. So what did he actually give up?

### Lines I read twice
- p1: "Then notice it failing." I didn't know what he was answering.
- p3: "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." Was the dad right about people leaving? Wasn't he the one who left?
- p10: "And nearly everything they need to choose their own future."
- p11: "They only move when people do." I gave up.
- p11: "It tells the record to answer reality."
- p12: "She's half right. Both were true." I gave up.
- p14: "Then we need a different plan."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Alex:** clear. Snarky, tired, wants out.
- **Bi11bot:** the clearest voice in the book. Dry, a little smug, kind.
- **Ada:** clear as a person, nameless as a name for too long. Her lines are mostly wise-friend lines, so she started to feel like a fortune cookie.
- **Grandma:** clear and good.
- **Tomas:** a nice guy I met for two pages.
- **TJ:** a dog. Fine.

### I felt something
- p2: anger at the debt.
- p7: the bet about the river.
- p18: pressing VERIFY. Short, but real.

### I predicted
- From p4 on I knew the dad wasn't really a gambler. I was right, and I didn't enjoy being right.
- On p9 I knew the woman at the desk would turn up later as a big player.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his missing dad's debt finds the dad's robot and learns his town's water is being stolen by the rich cities above.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 11.** The three objects and the riddle lines. If it weren't homework I would have closed it there. Pages 14–15 nearly lost me again because I couldn't follow the action.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 18 ends with the water filling up behind them. It's a loud noise, but I like Alex and the robot enough that it's not just a trick. I'd turn the page tonight, mostly because of the choice he made two panels earlier, not because of the water.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 11; next issue: yes

## Issue 02: Halyard

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19.** "Still disagreeable." / "Halyard may consider that a distinction." Bi11bot carries the start.
- **Page 21, panel 2.** 312 PATENTS. "He left me debt." / "Your father left many things badly labeled." Good twist: the dad who left debt also left all this.
- **Page 27, panel 3.** "Well now. There you are." Bi11bot noticing Alex become his own person. Small, but I liked it.
- **Page 29, panel 4.** "Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Funny.
- **Page 36, panel 4.** A woman with Ada's hair in the black glass. That got me, but only on the second look. I almost turned past it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 20.** A caption telling me what the city means: "Every creator seemed to work for someone else." Then "Keel trapped your body. Halyard could trap your ideas." That's a poster in a classroom. Who's saying "your"?
- **Pages 22–23.** These two pages say the same thing twice: the files aren't complete, other people helped, the Houses hid them. I skimmed all of page 23.
- **Page 27.** The montage where four experts agree in four lines. I skimmed it. It was too easy.
- **Page 30.** The 71.3% map. Numbers on numbers.
- **Page 33.** A full page of caption explaining the happy ending. This is where it read like a textbook chapter summary. I skipped it.

### I was confused
- **Page 20, caption.** "From the train, it looked like…" What train? They came out of a storm drain one page ago.
- **Page 21, panels 3–5.** This is the worst page in the book for me. A man shows up ("Director Cassian Lock," and I only know that name because I'm reading a script; on the page nobody says it). He says nothing. Then Alex says "I didn't know I was one." One what? Then "In exchange for what?" In exchange for what Lock never said? Half the conversation is missing. I read it three times. In the words of a real reader: I have no idea what a single one of those balloons is answering.
- **Page 21, panel 2.** "You said your father left you nothing." He never said that to her, that I saw.
- **Page 21, panel 3.** "Charter Houses." New term, never explained, and later the good thing at the end is also called "the Charter." Are those the same thing? They seem to be opposites.
- **Page 22, panel 3.** TOMAS REED. Is that Tomas from the mine? He's working inside a House? He was in Keel oiling a lock. I never found out whether they're the same man.
- **Page 23, panel 3.** "That doesn't sound like my father." What doesn't? Nobody said anything about his father in that panel.
- **Page 24, panel 1.** Lina says "Adrian Phantum's son." and Bi11bot answers "I corrected him. Frequently." Corrected who, the son? The dad? Then Ada: "You think he left this to you? No. He left it to all of us." Who is she talking to? Nobody claimed anything.
- **Page 24, panel 2.** Now the problem is that the river is *contaminated*. In Issue 01 the problem was that the water was being *diverted*. I thought we'd fixed that on p18. Which problem is it?
- **Page 25, panel 1.** Bi11bot says "Adrian made each of us keep one piece." Is Bi11bot one of the keepers? Then Lina says "twenty years." Tomas said fifteen years on p15, and Alex was two. I couldn't work out when anything happened.
- **Page 28.** Same problem as p21. "And them?" "And if I don't sign?" Sign what? There's a contract in the art, but Lock never says a word in this entire issue. The villain is a guy standing there. I had to guess the deal from a picture of a clean apartment.
- **Page 31, panel 3.** "CAPTION / SYSTEM NOTE: Lock realizes the system he trusted is no longer entirely under the Houses' control." If that's printed, it's the book telling me what a face is supposed to show. If it isn't printed, I had no idea what Lock felt. Either way he's a mannequin.
- **Page 34.** "So did Mera." First time I hear the name. I guessed she's the desk woman from p9, but I wasn't sure. Who's the "COLLABORATOR" talking?
- **Page 36, caption.** "The next safeguard did not belong to anyone alone." Safeguard? First time that word appears. Are the Measure, Seal and Covenant the "safeguards"? Nobody said so.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Alex, Ada, Bi11bot, TJ:** still clear.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence

(This isn't the same book I thought I was reading at the end of Issue 01. I thought it was about a debt and a missing dad. In Issue 02 the debt and the dad hardly come up.)

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 23.** After the p21 scene I couldn't follow and a second page repeating the first, I'd have been done. I'd have checked my phone on 21 and closed the book on 23.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
p36: yes, but only because of Ada. It's a real question about someone I somewhat care about. If I'd missed the reflection (and I nearly did), the last page is a black rectangle.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 23; next issue: yes

## Issue 03: Oasis

### Pulled me forward
- **Pages 39 and 41.** Ada and her mom. "You let me believe you were dead." / "I let you believe what kept you unmarked." Then "You protected me from knowing you." That line hit. It's the best Ada moment in the book, and the first time she's a person instead of an advice machine.
- **Page 44, panel 4.** "What are you afraid of losing?" Good question, and the room going quiet after it worked.
- **Pages 50–51.** Finally: what happened to the dad. He didn't leave. A machine locked him in with the floodwater on purpose. That's the answer I'd wanted since page 2. "It killed him." landed.
- **Page 51, panel 3.** "You preserved the part where nobody had to know." Good line.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 40.** Another water-wars flashback with gates and crowds. It's the same picture as p11. I skimmed it.
- **Pages 44–49.** Three people I've never met negotiate. Water for thirty days, purification units, contributor plates, public data. It's a model UN. I skimmed most of 45–47, and by 49 I was reading the displays like slides: WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC, CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED, ESSENTIAL ACCESS IS GUARANTEED. That's a rules page, not a story page.
- **Pages 53–54.** "Humans decide. Alpha advises." "The Charter holds both accountable." These are slogans. I skimmed to the last caption.

### I was confused
- **Page 37 to 38.** We get to Oasis, the rich city I've been looking at since page 1, and it gets one splash and a caption before the plot moves on. How did they get in? Page 2 said travel was RESTRICTED.
- **Page 38, panel 3.** "You thought she was dead." / "I thought she left after she died." I read this three times. You can't leave after you die. I gave up.
- **Page 38, panel 1.** The Seal and Covenant "respond to the completed Halyard provenance review." I just accepted that the machine said a name.
- **Page 41, panel 1.** Why is Mera on the wall of Ada's mom's apartment? Was she listening the whole time?
- **Page 41, panel 3.** "Mera suppressed your association risk." I didn't know what that meant. I think it means Mera hid that Ada was connected to Adrian?
- **Page 43, panel 4.** "The offer is real." / "That's the problem." Why is a real offer a problem? On p52 the book uses the exact same two lines again. The first time I didn't get it; the second time it felt copy-pasted.
- **Page 44.** Who are Mara Doss, Frank Reed and Elias Renn? Where did they come from? Who called this meeting? **Mara** and **Mera** in the same scene: I mixed them up. **Frank Reed**: related to Tomas Reed? Same guy? It's never said.
- **Page 46, panel 2.** "Our own reserve may be the next thing we need." Then why give it away? I got there eventually, but it took a minute.
- **Page 49, caption.** "The restored local compact was called the Charter." So the Charter Houses from p21 were named after the good thing? I'm lost.
- **Page 50, panel 1.** "ADRIAN PHANTUM — TRANSFER COMPLETE." Transfer of what?
- **Page 51, panel 2.** "It killed him." / "Alpha killed him." Isn't that the same sentence? Is Mera correcting him? I read it twice.
- **Page 51, panel 4.** "Forty million people." I thought this was three cities in one basin. Forty million? Since when?
- **Page 52.** Why does Alpha suddenly pick Alex to run everything? "Succession conditions verified" came out of nowhere. Why him?
- **Page 54, panel 3.** His debt gets VOIDED in one screen line, and Alex doesn't react. That was the whole reason I cared on page 2. And where's Grandma? Where's Tomas from the mine? They just disappear.

### Where I would have put it down

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. "The future remained uncertain. The decision did not belong to one person." That's the moral, not an ending. There's nobody in it I'm worried about. The question I cared about (what happened to his dad, is he stuck forever) got answered, and the new thing (a meeting that keeps arguing) isn't something I want to read more of. If there were a book two I wouldn't pick it up unless it was assigned.

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 21** (with page 28 as its twin). A guy walks in, never speaks, and Alex answers questions nobody asked: "I didn't know I was one." "In exchange for what?" Everyone in my class would have flipped back a page to see what they'd missed, found nothing, and decided the book was broken. Being skeptical of AI-made stuff, that's exactly the moment we'd decide it was. A conversation with half the lines missing reads like a machine lost them. The line-for-line repeats ("A versatile word" / "A versatile objection," "The offer is real / That's the problem" twice, "My silence for your protection" twice) and the poster captions ("Keel trapped your body. Halyard could trap your ideas.") would back up the class's suspicion.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dead dad's massive debt and a sarcastic robot, and the first part's actually good. Then it turns into three cities holding a meeting about sharing water, and you find out what happened to his dad way too late to care."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A great birthday-debt hook and a funny robot, buried under three magic objects I couldn't keep straight and a mine sequence I couldn't follow.
- **Issue 02:** A city lecture with a villain who never talks and a scene missing half its dialogue, saved at the last second by Ada's mom in the glass.
- **Issue 03:** One real mother–daughter scene and one real answer about the dad, wrapped in a civics lesson that ends on a slogan.
