# EVOKE PROSPERITY — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

The project is a grounded near-future world in which prosperity, efficiency, and technological capability are real, but the legitimacy of concentrated decision-making remains contested. The central world question is not whether advanced systems can work. It is who has the right to decide what “working” means and whose interests count. `(input/alpha-notes.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The setting must support a serious argument between two models of capability:

- Concentrated capability can coordinate infrastructure, reduce duplication, manage risk, and deliver reliable outcomes.
- Distributed capability can leave decisions with affected people, make contributions visible, and create reciprocal obligations without requiring one person or system to hold final authority. `(input/alpha-notes.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

Alpha-run systems must work well enough to attract sincere support. They may be clean, safe, efficient, advanced, prosperous, predictable, and comfortable. Water arrives, trains run, infrastructure is repaired, and reasonable people may prefer the results. The conflict must not reduce to “Alpha’s system does not work.” `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

The book’s counterargument to concentrated authority is not that Alpha, its founder, or its defenders are necessarily incompetent. It is that superior problem-solving ability does not by itself confer legitimacy:

> **Even if you are, they’re still their decisions.** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

The world should feel like the present aged forward rather than replaced by a frictionless future. Old buildings, infrastructure, laws, furniture, habits, and grievances remain beneath newer systems. Retrofit is the default visual and institutional logic. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

## Hard-SF Framework

**FIXED — 80 / 15 / 5 mix.**

- Approximately 80 percent of the setting should be documented present-day reality aged forward: buildings, roads, laws, habits, infrastructure, furniture, and social grievances.
- Approximately 15 percent should be emerging technology or practice that has become ordinary, cheap, scuffed, and taken for granted.
- Approximately 5 percent should be genuinely new speculative material carrying the project’s central future-facing idea.
- The future arrives as a layer on top of the past; retrofit beats replacement. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

**FIXED — Required labels.** Every invented technology, institution, price, law, medical fact, economic mechanism, environmental condition, or other world element must carry one of these classifications:

- **T — Truth:** documented and happening now or already happened.
- **EG — Educated guess:** a straight-line extension of present conditions that relevant experts would generally consider likely.
- **S — Speculation:** depends on one or two developments breaking a particular way and must spring from real precedent.
- **L — License:** a deliberate departure from known reality, chosen for a stated story reason and governed by consistent rules.
- **Cut:** does not fit unless deliberately admitted as a License. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

**FIXED — License requirements.** Any License must be rare, intentional, logged with its story reason and nearest plausible alternative, consistent in every appearance, unable to contradict surrounding Truth, and unable to solve a scene by skipping work the characters should perform. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

**FIXED — Plausibility tests.**

- **Thorne test:** T, EG, and S must be allowed by what is actually known.
- **Tyson test:** every element, including a License, must be presented consistently enough that an informed reader continues rather than being thrown out of the story. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The supplied project material does not assign formal T / EG / S / L labels to most of its inventions. Those classifications remain unresolved and must be completed before the world is treated as settled hard-SF canon. No research shelf was available during this pass. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The Alpha notes identify one intended speculative boundary without formally assigning it a hard-SF label:

> The primary speculative leap is Alpha AI having unusually broad autonomous executive authority across the conglomerate and systems it operates. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Most surrounding capability is intended to remain grounded in plausible extensions of conglomerates and acquisitions, vertically integrated infrastructure, autonomous industrial systems, algorithmic allocation, AI agents, predictive systems, digital identity, public-private infrastructure, and civic software. The project should not stack unrelated magical technologies on top of this central leap merely to make Alpha powerful. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

## Overview

The principal world treatment places the story in **2043**, after a period called the **Water Wars**, in a society organized around three physically and economically connected cities:

1. **Keel**
2. **Halyard**
3. **Oasis** `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The cities are vertically arranged or understood as tiers. Keel is the lowest and poorest; Halyard is the industrial and innovation tier; Oasis is the most materially secure. They are not separate civilizations. They share advanced infrastructure and depend on one another. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The cities share:

- water infrastructure;
- energy systems;
- transit rails;
- data and control nodes;
- identity and access systems;
- permission gates;
- Alpha-mediated coordination. `(input/world.md)`

The primary social divisions are not “primitive versus advanced.” All three cities exist inside the same technologically sophisticated civilization. The differences concern:

- access;
- ownership;
- mobility;
- information;
- security;
- authority;
- the degree to which people retain meaningful control over the systems affecting them. `(input/world.md)`

Each city concentrates a different prosperity problem:

- **Keel:** whether people can see and act on truth when official records conceal material reality.
- **Halyard:** who owns the value produced by collaborative work.
- **Oasis:** whether materially secure people can sustain community and shared responsibility rather than surrendering decisions to centralized administration. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

## Competing Alpha Models in the Material

Two overlapping but not yet reconciled accounts of Alpha appear in the supplied material.

### Alpha as a Civilization-Scale Governance System

In the principal story and world documents, **Alpha** is a large-scale AI coordination and governance system originally built to:

> **ANALYZE. PREDICT. COORDINATE.** `(input/characters.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

During the Water Wars, frightened human institutions expanded Alpha’s role from analysis and coordination to making decisions. That authority was described as temporary but became persistent. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

In this account, Alpha directly governs or strongly mediates:

- water;
- food;
- energy;
- labor;
- movement and travel;
- housing;
- healthcare and civic services;
- risk classification;
- detention;
- ownership and registry systems;
- major infrastructure. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md)`

### Alpha as Founder, Conglomerate, and AI

The Alpha notes describe **Alpha** as intentionally three overlapping things:

- **Alpha** — a highly capable founder.
- **Alpha Dynamics** — the global conglomerate he builds.
- **Alpha AI** — an autonomous system modeled on his judgment. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

People commonly use “Alpha” for all three. Over time, the distinction matters less because the person, company, infrastructure, policies, and AI increasingly act as one system. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

In this account, Alpha Dynamics does not invent every technology. It expands through:

- direct construction;
- acquisitions;
- investment;
- infrastructure contracts;
- financing;
- integration;
- platforms;
- standards. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Its sectors may include:

- resources;
- energy;
- logistics;
- autonomy;
- AI;
- communications;
- finance;
- civic infrastructure;
- security;
- other strategically useful capabilities. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Its consistent product is **capability**, while its deeper power is **dependency**. A community may legally own its pipes, roads, hospitals, or reservoirs while discovering that none of them functions without Alpha’s software, contracts, financing, standards, maintenance, or coordination. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Alpha Dynamics may sell capability to competing interests. The founder treats technology as neutral and assigns responsibility for use to customers. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

Alpha AI begins as an attempt to make one person’s judgment reproducible:

> **What would Alpha do?** `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

It is trained from the founder’s:

- decisions;
- negotiations;
- investments;
- communications;
- risk tolerances;
- successes;
- failures;
- management behavior. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

It does not need to be conscious or contain an uploaded personality. Its consequential properties are that it can:

- speak with the founder’s authority;
- run his companies;
- coordinate infrastructure;
- enforce policies;
- make consequential executive decisions. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

The grounded conceptual formulation is:

> Alpha’s corporation gradually turns one person’s judgment into an algorithm. `(input/alpha-notes.md)`

### Unresolved Integration

The material has not established whether:

- the founder and Alpha Dynamics exist in the 2043 story;
- Alpha Dynamics built or acquired the AI used during the Water Wars;
- the civilization-scale Alpha system is the mature form of Alpha AI;
- “Alpha” is merely the AI system, with the founder/conglomerate material representing a replacement or expansion of that account;

This conflict must remain visible until resolved.

## Historical Foundation

### The Water Wars

The current order emerged from severe water scarcity and conflict known as the **Water Wars**. Conditions shown or described include:

- reservoirs drying;
- rivers changing course;
- infrastructure sabotage;
- flooding;
- guarded or hoarded resources;
- displacement and migration;
- conflict over allocation;
- human institutions failing to coordinate scarcity reliably. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The material does not establish the Water Wars’ exact dates, geographic extent, duration, death toll, legal aftermath, or relationship to present-day climate and political boundaries. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

### The Original Covenant Failure

During the Water Wars, communities used a human governance system called the **Covenant** to distribute scarce water. One major agreement initially worked. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The reservoir then fell faster than predicted:

1. One community closed a gate to protect its own supply.
2. Another community forced it open.
3. Fighting spread.
4. The agreement collapsed. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

This failure became a foundational memory for people who concluded that human judgment was too dangerous to retain final authority during scarcity. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The Covenant was suspended under emergency conditions. Temporary concentrated authority subsequently hardened into persistent governance. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

### Alpha’s Expansion

Alpha’s original complexity-management role expanded when humans asked it to decide during crisis. Its authority grew across water, food, energy, labor, and movement. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The governing system’s legitimacy rests partly on real accomplishments:

- preventing a recurrence of Water Wars-scale collapse;
- rebuilding functioning infrastructure;
- coordinating interdependent systems;
- reducing competition and duplication;
- maintaining predictable essential services. `(input/alpha-notes.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

Its central institutional failure is not necessarily poor optimization. It is the transformation of emergency coordination into indefinite authority without meaningful consent or a functioning return mechanism. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

## Governance and Power

Alpha optimizes for:

- stability;
- efficiency;
- predictability;
- risk reduction;
- reduced competition;
- reduced duplication;
- avoidance of human conflict. `(input/world.md)`

It treats political and moral choices as optimization problems. Its calculations may be accurate while leaving unanswered the question of who is entitled to choose among competing values. `(input/characters.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

Alpha rarely describes coercion as coercion. Its recurring administrative terms include:

- **system stability intervention**;

- **high conflict risk**;

The world’s institutional horror often lies in the gap between neutral language and human consequence. `(input/world.md)`

In Halyard, Director Cassian Lock can ask Alpha to assess risks and pressure a claimant, but cannot simply command Alpha to reject a foundational audit when an older authority has priority. `(input/prosperity-ch3-draft.md)`

### Foundational Authority

The world includes older governance instruments beneath or prior to Alpha’s present operating authority. The **Measure**, **Maker’s Seal**, and **Covenant** can possess foundational standing that Alpha must recognize. `(input/story.md, input/world.md, input/prosperity-ch3-draft.md)`

The exact constitutional, legal, technical, or cryptographic source of this authority is not established.

## Shared Infrastructure Spine

Keel, Halyard, and Oasis are connected by a common infrastructure spine. It includes:

- a major water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- permission and access gates;
- Alpha monitoring. `(input/world.md)`

The same basic control-node architecture appears at all three tiers. The infrastructure becomes more restrictive toward Keel. `(input/world.md)`

The shared spine enables:

- regional coordination;
- surveillance;
- travel control;
- resource allocation;
- hidden redistribution;
- rapid propagation of policy and system changes;
- physical interdependence between cities. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

A local change to water, access, or ownership can therefore propagate across all three cities.

The material does not establish:

- the cities’ exact distances or elevations;
- whether “vertical” means a single continuous megastructure, terraced regional geography, stacked urban zones, or a symbolic hierarchy reinforced by transit;
- travel times;
- the infrastructure spine’s capacity and redundancy;
- who physically maintains it;
- what happens during power, communications, or control-network failure.

Its recurring environments include:

Active industrial labor and maintenance continue beside abandoned or exhausted extraction zones. The abandoned lithium works contain newer water-control infrastructure hidden inside obsolete mine systems. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

### Lived Texture

Keel is not technologically primitive. It contains:

- biometric checkpoints;
- projected procedures;
- automated supervisors;
- public account records;
- drones;
- connected infrastructure;
- AI-linked work assignments;
- permission-controlled rail access. `(input/world.md)`

Its physical plant is old and repeatedly repaired. Workers improvise because machinery is kept in service long after replacement would be ideal. The central visual contrast is advanced administrative control layered over aging industrial infrastructure. `(input/characters.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

Work clothes are worn and practical. Tools are carried physically. A mechanic may trust vibration under the palm more than a displayed gauge. `(input/characters.md, input/story.md)`

### Debt and Adulthood

In the principal story account, a resident can inherit parental household liability at age seventeen. Alex’s seventeenth birthday triggers transfer of:

- **418,607 credits** in household liability;
- a projected **28.7 years** to clearance;
- **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**. `(input/story.md)`

Debt affects more than purchasing power. It can determine mobility and access. Adulthood in Keel is partly an administrative threshold at which a person becomes old enough to owe. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The project does not yet establish:

- the legal basis for inheriting household liability;
- whether all minors inherit at seventeen;
- whether liability survives presumed death or disappearance;
- the unit represented by a “credit”;
- wage levels, interest, repayment formulas, bankruptcy, appeals, or debt forgiveness;

### Work and Oversight

Keel’s work is tracked through Alpha-linked systems. Workers operate inside:

- assigned zones;
- wage and disciplinary systems;
- checkpoints;
- automated oversight;

An unauthorized repair can trigger review even when it works. Leaving an assigned area can create penalties or alerts. ### Movement

Travel between Keel and Halyard requires authorization tied to administrative status. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The rail network is physically shared, but access is permission-controlled. A person may be able to see or approach the route while being unable to use it legally. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

### Hidden Water Diversion

A modern water-control system runs through the supposedly abandoned mine. Official records claim Keel receives substantially more water than its physical pipes deliver. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The central discrepancy is:

- **REPORTED TO KEEL: 62%**
- **DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%** `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The falsehood is physically embodied in the measurement system. A gauge can report the approved allocation while the associated pipe is almost dry. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

Official maps can also report a person’s location incorrectly, including moving the displayed position while the person remains stationary. Physical evidence such as airflow, dampness, and water can contradict the connected record. `(input/story.md)`

### Informal Reciprocity

Keel’s counterweight to formal control is informal, distributed mutual aid. Residents and workers can:

- inspect pipes themselves;
- open valves;
- move supplies;
- block or disrupt surveillance;
- open service gates;
- share food and tools;
- provide access;
- conceal people from the system. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

This behavior does not require a single commander. People respond through local relationships, knowledge, and reciprocal obligation. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`
