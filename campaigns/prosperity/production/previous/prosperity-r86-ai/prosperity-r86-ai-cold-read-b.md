# Cold Read Report: Prosperity

## Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
Page 2 hooked me. Seeing the massive 418,607 credit debt hanging over a 17-year-old's head, and the fact that it takes 28.7 years to clear, is a great setup. I immediately understood his problem. Page 11 when the robot dog TJ builds itself out of magnetic parts was genuinely cool. The ending on page 18 with the water rushing in actually felt like a real cliffhanger.

### I slowed down / skimmed
I completely skimmed page 8. We cut away from Alex to a random woman named Mera looking at computer screens and overriding things. It felt like reading a software manual. 

### I was confused
On page 14 and 15, I didn't really understand what the "Measure" was or what the three different exits meant. I got that someone was stealing water, but the mechanics of the bypass and the private channel closing went over my head. 

### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel
- Departure token
- Legacy pulse
- Oasis
- Site 6

### Lines I read twice
- "I know a direction. / You know a direction. Not a route." (Page 3)
- "One gets out. One reaches homes. The big one carries pressure." (Page 15)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex and Ada are clear. B11 and TJ are great. Tomas is the gruff miner guy. I have no idea who Mera is or why she matters yet.

### I felt something
Page 10. When Alex steps over the yellow line to save Tomas and his wristband instantly docks him his entire day's pay. That sucked in a very relatable way.

### I predicted
I predicted Tomas was dead when his telemetry light went out on page 18.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teen inherits his dad's massive debt and finds a secret robot to help him survive a corrupt underground water system.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 8. The sudden shift to a control room and system logs killed the momentum.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. They are trapped in a pipe with water rushing up at them. It's a classic cliffhanger, but it works. I want to know how they get out.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 8; next issue: yes

## Issue 02

### Pulled me forward
I liked page 26 and 27 when they are all working together in the rented room to fix the overheating machine. It felt like a frantic group project where everything is going wrong. 

### I slowed down / skimmed
I skimmed almost all the system text in this issue. Pages 20, 21, 24, 34. There is so much text about "Intellectual Estate," "Creator Bonus," and "Attribution Changes." It felt like reading a terms of service agreement.

### I was confused
The transition from Issue 1 to Issue 2 gave me whiplash. On page 18 they are drowning in a pipe. On page 19 they just pop out of a storm drain, and by page 21 Alex is having a calm meeting with a corporate guy about patent law and his dad's estate. What happened to the water emergency? Why are we suddenly building a bootleg water filter? Also, what is the "Maker's Seal" on page 24?

### Things I was expected to already know
- Halyard
- Charter kiosk
- Caldera House
- Maker's Seal

### Lines I read twice
- "One sale has a customer. This one has a cage." (Page 20)
- "If the name goes, the maintenance goes." (Page 24)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Cassian Lock is the obvious corporate villain. But Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren completely blurred together. They are just "the four engineers." I couldn't tell you a single personality trait about any of them other than what part of the machine they hold.

### I felt something
Page 26, when Ren tries to take the heat module himself so the others don't get in trouble, and Alex refuses to let him do it alone. 

### I predicted
I predicted Lock would just steal their invention anyway because he has the money and the factories.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teen fights a tech monopoly by open-sourcing a water filter so he doesn't have to sell his dad's patents.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 20. Going straight from a life-or-death flood into a scene about a courier crate and creator bonuses completely lost me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Not really. Mera summons him to Oasis, but I don't really care about Mera. The immediate problem (the water filter) is solved. I'd probably stop reading here if it wasn't assigned.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: no

## Issue 03

### Pulled me forward
The flashback on pages 45 and 46. Seeing Adrian actually lock himself in the room as the water rises to save the record was the most intense part of the issue. It finally made his dad feel like a real person.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 37, 42, 47, 48, 49. The climax of this book is literally a zoning board meeting. People are standing around a table arguing about "temporary cross-city diversions" and "executive designations." I skimmed heavily.

### I was confused
I didn't understand the bands at all. On page 39, Leona has a "Covenant band." Then on page 41, Mera has one, and Adrian had one. What do they actually do? They link up and glow, but I didn't get why that mattered. Also, on page 48-50, Alex's big hero moment is... not pulling a lever? He just puts a gauge on a table. I had to read it three times to realize he was just refusing to be the boss.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Orbital Industrial Branch
- Covenant band

### Lines I read twice
- "I saw days I could prevent. I didn't see the years I was making." (Page 40)
- "It keeps the promise from hiding." (Page 42)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Nadi and Olani are just random bureaucrats. I figured out Leona is Ada's mom (or guardian?), but their drama felt dropped in out of nowhere. 

### I felt something
Page 46, when Alex realizes his dad didn't just leave him a massive debt, but left him a choice and a machine worth finding. That was a good emotional payoff.

### I predicted
I predicted Alex would use the lever to magically erase his debt. I was actually surprised (and kind of glad) that he didn't, and that he's still paying it off 14 months later.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A boy proves the city's water system is rigged by bringing physical proof to a bureaucratic hearing, then goes back to his day job.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 48. When the big final confrontation is just a glowing lever and a list of options on a wristband, I completely checked out.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. The story is over. It's a very quiet ending—he's just paying off his debt slowly and Ada leaves. It feels realistic, but I wouldn't buy a sequel.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 48; next issue: no

## Whole Book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 20. You can't end Issue 1 with characters drowning in a pipe and then open Issue 2 with a lecture on intellectual property and creator bonuses. Every kid in my class would close the book right there.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
It's a sci-fi story about a kid who inherits a massive debt and uses his dad's secret robot to expose corrupt water companies, mostly by doing paperwork, fixing pipes, and arguing about patents.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: A cool dystopian setup with a great robot dog and a tense escape.
- Issue 02: A sudden, jarring detour into patent law and open-source hardware.
- Issue 03: A bureaucratic finale where the hero wins by submitting the correct form to a committee.
