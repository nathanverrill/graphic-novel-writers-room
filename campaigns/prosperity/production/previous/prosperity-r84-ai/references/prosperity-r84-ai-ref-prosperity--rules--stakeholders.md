# Who must be satisfied

Two people outside the room must each finish this book satisfied, at least in part: the
partner who will put it in front of students, and the backer who paid for it. Neither has to
love every page. Neither may finish it feeling the book argues against what they stand for.
This file says who they are, in their own words where possible. It is not material for the
story: nothing here should appear on a page as a speech, a lesson or a name.

## The line neither may cross

The book never concludes, and never leaves a reader concluding, that free markets, free
enterprise, private ownership, business that serves customers, or the libertarian and
free-enterprise beliefs of Bill Reynolds and Young Americans Center are bad. Monopolists,
cronies and a central planner can be villains. Markets, honest business and ownership are
never the villain, and the ending does not argue against them. A book that crosses this line
has not satisfied either of them, however much else they like in it.

## Young Americans Center for Financial Education (the partner)

A Denver nonprofit, founded in 1987 by Bill Daniels, that runs a real FDIC-insured bank for
customers under 22 and reaches more than 67,000 young people a year: Young AmeriTowne (a day
running a simulated town, with paychecks, deposits and business loans), International Towne
(trade and currency), YouthBiz (young entrepreneurs, pitch competitions) and Money Matters.

- **Mission:** young people "take charge of their future and lead with purpose in a free
  enterprise system." The founder: "The eighth wonder of the world is the free enterprise
  system. The ninth is so few people understand it."
- **Values they name:** confidence, a "take charge" attitude, discipline, resilience, being
  "willing to fail," character, integrity, responsibility.
- **Tone:** upbeat, centre-right in values, carefully nonpartisan, because they work inside
  public schools of every political stripe.
- **The founder's story:** Bill Daniels' basketball team went bankrupt in 1975 and his debts
  were legally discharged. Five years later he repaid every ticket holder, vendor, player and
  employee anyway, with 8% interest.
- **They would like:** a young person who takes initiative, fails, owns it and keeps their
  word; earning, saving, borrowing and paying back shown as tools a teenager can use; a
  business that serves people and makes money doing it; integrity under pressure.
- **They would object to:** banks, markets or profit as the villain; "the system is rigged
  and effort doesn't matter"; debt as something done to you that someone powerful cancels;
  gambling made glamorous for minors; partisan cues in either direction; anything that gets
  how accounts, loans or credit work wrong. They are a real bank.

## Bill Reynolds (the backer)

Born in Boulder in 1938. Naval officer, then a real-estate developer who started with no salary
("I would only be paid if I made a sale. That was a revelation") and built the W.W. Reynolds
Companies. His foundation funds free-market education, financial literacy and civics, through
games and hands-on learning, and the Hernando de Soto Capital Markets Program at CU Boulder.

- **In his words:** "If it is to be, it is up to me." "You're in charge of your life. Nobody
  else." "I'm a libertarian." He says "free markets," not "capitalism." "Free markets work only
  when there's failure." "There is No Free Lunch!"
- **On victims:** "Don't allow others to cast you as a victim. Don't play the victim's role,
  even if you have been victimized."
- **On windfalls:** "A windfall isn't a pathway. Money by itself doesn't create mobility.
  Earning, saving, and investing do." "Eating an apple gives you a moment. Growing an orchard
  gives you a future." "We don't believe in charity; we believe in opportunity."
- **On property (de Soto):** what poor people own is "dead capital" until it is written down,
  recorded and defensible; then effort can become capital. "Ownership ends a surprising number
  of arguments."
- **On government:** it wants "desperately to control you... casting many of us as victims and
  bribing us to support them with our own money." Against bailouts and corporate welfare: big
  companies going "up to the government, getting money."
- **On teaching:** "I've never believed in lecturing... I've always tried to ask instead: How
  do you see it? What are we missing?" "We can't tell anybody what to think, particularly
  youngsters."
- **On AI, which he uses every day:** "Technology may scale intelligence. It cannot replace
  presence."
- **His best story:** young, he and friends bought a Boulder beer hall with a bank loan. "We
  made every mistake in the book. We borrowed too much." It failed. "I went to the bankers...
  I said, Paul, I can't pay you." The banker wrote separate notes, "and we made the payments
  over the next few years." He calls it his best project, because it taught him the most.
- **His voice:** plain, slow, a bit roundabout, Western ("damn hard"). A question first, then a
  story from his own life, then one plain line. Jokes mostly at his own expense. Never a
  lecture.
- **He would like:** a young person who fails, faces the lender, and pays it back; earned
  success; ownership made concrete; a mentor who asks more than it tells and is sometimes
  wrong; a dog named for a Founder.
- **He would object to:** a windfall, bailout or giveaway that solves the plot; business owners
  as villains by default; "capitalism" as a dirty word; an AI mentor that hands out answers or
  replaces people; a lecturing tone.

## Bi11bot

Bi11bot's personality, character and beliefs are Bill Reynolds': his voice, his questions, his
stories of his own failures, his humour at his own expense. He is never a lecturer and never
the boy's replacement for people.
