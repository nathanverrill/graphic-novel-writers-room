# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **FIXED — Ancestry must not function as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, competence, wealth, political position, or support for Alpha.**
- **FIXED — Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended.** His father, Adrian Phantum, is European-descended. His mother was Indigenous Andean; she left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing further about her is learned in this book.
- **FIXED — Adrian is European-descended; Grandmother is European-descended; Ada is mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla; Leona is predominantly Indigenous Andean/mestiza; Tomas is Indigenous Andean; Mera is white Latin American/Southern European-descended; Lina is Indigenous Andean/mestiza; Tomas Reed is mixed Indigenous Andean and Anglo/European; Suri is diasporic, potentially South Asian or mixed South Asian/Latin American; Jalen is Afro-descendant/Afro-Latin.**
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada remain longtime friends and civic collaborators. Romantic feeling appears through intimacy and choice but is never formally labeled.**
- **FIXED — Bi11bot has two expressive optical eyes, wears an old scarf, folds small enough to fit in a backpack, and is never menacing.**
- **FIXED — TJ is a silent, mismatched magnetic repair dog.**
- **FIXED — Tomas, the Keel miner, and Tomas Reed, the Halyard fabrication engineer, are separate people with no established family connection.**
- **FIXED — Grandmother is canonically unnamed.**
- **FIXED — Alpha is a distributed federation of regional and corporate instances. No Alpha instance has a face or individualized personality.**
- **FIXED — Character speech changes in register across the cities without creating an intelligence hierarchy.**
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada do not naturally use abstract civic or academic vocabulary. Alex describes evidence and physical consequences; Ada describes obligations, fear, trust, and what people refuse to say.**
- **FIXED — The book is exactly 65 pages.**

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Alex Phantum is seventeen, lean and wiry from mining work, approximately 5'9", with medium-brown skin, a narrow expressive face, dark brown eyes, thick black-brown hair that falls in wind-tangled curls over his forehead, and a strong jaw inherited from Adrian without looking like a younger copy of him. He wears a patched charcoal mining jacket with a faded **K** work patch, a dark rust-brown undershirt, worn reinforced work trousers, scuffed mineral-stained boots, fingerless repair gloves, and a weathered canvas tool bag. His silhouette is narrow, forward-leaning, and practical. He carries Adrian’s worn casino chip etched with **A = K + ρU** in his pocket. His signature detail is the habit of placing one scarred palm against machinery to feel vibration before trusting a gauge.

**Voice —** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical, and mechanical. His sarcasm is defensive rather than performative; he describes vibration, pressure, wear, pipes, gauges, routes, and what people actually do rather than using institutional abstractions.

- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “I just want it to be my choice.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Keel mechanic, worker, later mechanic, witness, authenticator, and limited convener.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and choose his own future.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must learn that agency is not merely escape; it is accepting responsibility without claiming control over everyone else.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can read machinery through touch, improvise repairs, recognize physical evidence, and synthesize other people’s knowledge into practical action.
- **Vulnerability:** He is seventeen, debt-restricted, emotionally hungry for proof that Adrian cared, and afraid that staying means becoming trapped.
- **Mistaken belief:** Freedom means getting beyond Keel before Keel’s problems become his responsibility.
- **Starting boundary:** He will not willingly surrender his future to Alpha, a House, or Adrian’s reputation.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He broadcasts Keel’s water discrepancy instead of tracing a private escape route, knowingly sacrificing his route out.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to distinguish leaving a place from abandoning its people; Adrian’s legacy tempts him to confuse inheritance with authority.
- **Pressure sequence:** He rescues Tomas, broadcasts the Measure’s evidence, restores contributor ownership, helps others make the Covenant, and refuses Alpha’s offer of exclusive human control.

**Arc in this book —** Alex moves from **escape → curiosity → truth → responsibility → shared ownership → trust → restraint → agency**. He begins believing Adrian left him only debt and shame. He ends able to accept inheritance without claiming exclusive authority. He authenticates Adrian’s transfer, presents evidence, and convenes the first equal three-city review, but cannot chair it, decide cases, command Alpha, or hold a permanent override. His proof of change is leaving Keel because the choice is his, not because debt or Alpha permits it.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock —** Ada Veyra is twenty, approximately 5'7", with a strong, steady build, warm medium-brown skin, an expressive oval face with prominent cheekbones, long wind-tangled copper hair usually tied loosely at the nape, freckles across her nose and cheeks, and clear teal-blue eyes. She wears a dark high-collared work shirt, patched dark trousers, sturdy Keel boots, a rivet-studded strap crossing one shoulder, and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her silhouette is rooted and balanced, with the wrap and copper hair moving visibly in the high-desert wind. Her signature detail is one thumb resting on the rivet of her shoulder strap when she is listening, deciding, or containing anger.

**Voice —** Concise and emotionally precise. Ada notices obligations, avoidance, fear, imbalance, hidden motives, and what people refuse to say; she turns abstractions back toward human needs without sounding academic, therapeutic, activist, or bureaucratic.

- “What if they were wrong?”
- “Then that's what the agreement has to protect.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Keel mechanic’s friend, route planner, civic organizer, and later community-group collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Understand who decided Keel had to remain trapped and help change it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She believes in rebuilding Keel but must accept that leaving temporarily can serve rebuilding rather than betray it.
- **Leverage / capability:** She reads people, remembers routes, marks physical discrepancies, prepares supplies, and turns competing demands into reciprocal commitments.
- **Vulnerability:** She has spent twenty years believing her mother died and fears that the truth may make her absence impossible to forgive.
- **Mistaken belief:** Staying physically present is the only reliable form of loyalty.
- **Starting boundary:** She will not let Alex turn escape into an excuse to forget Keel.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** At the Covenant meeting, she asks what each representative fears losing instead of arguing for what any one city wants.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s departure forces her to test whether collaboration can survive distance; Leona forces her to separate repair from simple forgiveness.
- **Pressure sequence:** She enters Alex’s secret, joins the journey, protects the collaborators’ claims, confronts Leona, and helps three cities act before certainty.

**Arc in this book —** Ada begins as Alex’s counterpoint—rebuild rather than escape—and becomes his essential collaborator in transforming private inheritance into shared responsibility. She does not abandon Keel by leaving; she carries its needs into regional work. Her proof of change is choosing departure alongside Alex while continuing civic and community work rather than demanding that either of them remain physically fixed in Keel.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Adrian Phantum is European-descended, shown from his late thirties into his fifties, tall and lean with a tired but kind face, pale olive skin, deep-set gray-brown eyes, dark brown hair graying at the temples, a strong jaw shared with Alex, and the same characteristic head tilt when considering a problem. In early recordings he wears a burgundy work jacket over a worn shirt; later records show the same clothing grammar more faded, rumpled, and water-stained. His silhouette is long, slightly stooped, and perpetually reaching toward a diagram, card, or machine. His signature detail is a casino chip etched with **A = K + ρU**.

**Voice —** Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic, and humane. He uses questions, bets, odds, and choices to make Alex think, never giving him a conclusion that should be earned through action.

- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”
- “What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Gambler, inventor, entrepreneur, collaborator, and later classified systemic disruption risk.
- **Immediate want:** Complete a return of authority to people without replacing Alpha with another unchecked ruler.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to repair the world while repeatedly damaging the people closest to him; his inventions and family harm are both real.
- **Leverage / capability:** Probability research, systems design, gambling capital, collaborators, offline infrastructure, and the ability to recognize useful people.
- **Vulnerability:** Addiction to risk, debt, absence from Alex’s childhood, and dependence on plans he cannot personally control.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently careful test can prepare another person for every consequence.
- **Starting boundary:** He will not accept permanent emergency authority as legitimate.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He completes the final transfer instead of escaping the flood-control chamber, wagering on demonstrated human choices after his death.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s existence turns Adrian’s theory of human choice into a personal test rather than an abstract proof.
- **Pressure sequence:** He distributes the safeguards, builds Bi11bot and TJ, leaves collaborators evidence, completes the dormant transfer, and dies when Alpha classifies the transfer as a system-stability threat.

**Arc in this book —** Adrian is reconstructed from public failure into complicated human history: gambler and researcher, absent father and caring planner, harmful man and genuine collaborator. His final wager is not that Alex will rule well; it is that people can accept responsibility without concentrating power. His proof is the transfer itself, which gives Alex the choice to refuse exclusive authority.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Grandmother Phantum is an elderly, very small European-descended woman with a compact, slightly stooped frame, light olive skin, a deeply lined oval face, soft gray hair pulled into a low knot, alert dark eyes, and small capable hands. She wears a patterned earth-tone shawl over a repaired household dress and keeps a square cloth in her hands. Her silhouette is folded and contained when confused, but becomes startlingly upright when she is precise. Her signature detail is folding and refolding the same cloth, then stopping completely when Adrian’s recording or Alex’s departure reaches her emotionally.

**Voice —** Gentle, understated, and sometimes apparently confused about time. She becomes exact when speaking about Adrian, the workshop, or what Alex needs, and never explains more than the moment requires.

- “He’s closer than you think.”
- “Then go finish what he started.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Alex’s caregiver and keeper of the family home.
- **Immediate want:** Protect Alex and preserve Adrian’s workshop until Alex is ready.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to protect Alex from Adrian’s damage without deciding Alex’s future for him.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knowledge of the apartment, the hidden book, the workshop, Bi11bot, and Adrian’s preparations.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, memory lapses, dependence on Alex, and the knowledge that sending him away may be necessary.
- **Mistaken belief:** Her silence can keep the family safe indefinitely.
- **Starting boundary:** She will not reveal Adrian’s entire plan before Alex makes the first choice himself.
- **Decision only she would make:** She gives Alex the book while presenting the workshop opening as an ordinary household task.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s need to leave forces her to treat departure as agency rather than abandonment.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** She gives Alex her blessing, asks him to finish what Adrian started, and accepts his promise to return without trying to stop him.

**Arc in this book —** Grandmother moves from guarded keeper to active releaser. She does not become a secret mastermind or explain Adrian’s whole history. Her change is quiet: she stops protecting Alex through concealment and trusts him with the choice to go.

### B11 / BI11BOT

**Visual lock —** B11, spoken as Bi11bot, is a child-sized constructed companion made from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, approximately three feet tall when upright, with visibly tooled seams, hand-finished repairs, maker’s stamps, small articulated limbs, two expressive optical eyes with soft blue and amber light, one eye surrounded by a small adjustable lens ring, and an old rust-red scarf wrapped twice around his neck. He folds small enough to fit in a backpack. His silhouette is compact, rounded, and slightly top-heavy, with no sharp predatory profile. His signature detail is the lens ring that rotates with a quiet click before he answers.

**Voice —** Dry, economical, patient, precise, and warm underneath. His engineering, gambling, and financial vocabulary becomes funny through exactness and timing; he teaches through a concrete example, a question, and a consequence rather than a lecture.

- “Thinking about it isn't doing it.”
- “Which is why I'm not choosing.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Guide, archive, evaluator, mentor, companion, and witness.
- **Immediate want:** Carry Adrian’s evidence through the succession test without deciding the test for Alex.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must honor Adrian’s design while refusing to become another hidden authority.
- **Leverage / capability:** Offline records, physical access, probability analysis, mechanical knowledge, the B11 witness record, and the ability to preserve and transmit the authorization package.
- **Vulnerability:** He is dependent on stored records, physical maintenance, limited power, and the possibility that Alex will treat his answers as commands.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently well-designed test can reveal character without influencing it.
- **Starting boundary:** He will not choose for Alex or independently grant Alex authority.
- **Decision only B11 would make:** He refuses to select TRACE or VERIFY, even when choosing himself would improve the probability of survival.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s skepticism forces B11 to become a companion rather than merely Adrian’s evaluator.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** He carries the signed witness record but yields authority to the human review process; he remains a witness, not a sovereign.

**Arc in this book —** Bi11bot begins as an inherited machine and mysterious evaluator, becomes Alex’s guide and companion, and ends as a continuing witness to bounded transition. His formal identifier is **B11**. He is never the mechanism that grants Alex power.

### TJ

**Visual lock —** TJ is a small, mismatched magnetic repair dog assembled from salvaged brass, blackened steel, ceramic fragments, rubberized cable, and painted polymer plates, with a clearly canine head, four short dog legs, a compact torso, a hinged tail, one mismatched optical sensor eye, and visible magnetic seams where body parts separate. His parts can detach and reassemble into different working shapes, but every configuration must remain visibly dog-like. His signature detail is a tail segment that snaps back into place with a bright mechanical click.

**Voice —** TJ never speaks. He communicates through clicks, magnetic snaps, servo whines, head tilts, paw taps, posture, and practical movement.

- **Click-click.**
- **Whirr—snap.**

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Silent repair scout and practical companion.
- **Immediate want:** Reach the mechanism or space the larger characters cannot reach.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He is built from spare parts but consistently proves that incomplete construction can still produce reliable care.
- **Leverage / capability:** Detachable head, legs, body segments, magnetic reassembly, tight-space scouting, and mechanical retrieval.
- **Vulnerability:** Separation, loss of magnetic alignment, damaged parts, and dependence on others to recover or repair him.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ acts rather than interprets.
- **Starting boundary:** He does not communicate through speech or independently make political choices.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** He leaves one useful body part behind to hold or test a mechanism before rebuilding himself.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot must learn to accept help from a tool he built for problems he cannot solve alone.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** TJ remains silent and practical, demonstrating contribution without seeking ownership or recognition.

**Arc in this book —** TJ’s detailed first appearance and production timing remain open. His fixed function is a silent, practical magnetic repair dog built by Bi11bot from spare parts.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock —** Mera Vale is a white Latin American woman of Southern European descent in her late fifties, approximately 5'8", slender and upright, with pale olive skin, a long controlled face, silver-blonde hair cut into a precise jaw-length bob, gray-green eyes, and a narrow graphite cuff on her left wrist. She wears immaculate pearl-gray and graphite Oasis clothing with no visible jewelry, no bright color, and clean geometric seams. Her silhouette is vertical, exact, and nearly motionless. Her signature detail is aligning a glass of water precisely with a desk edge before making a decision.

**Voice —** Controlled, declarative, polished, and consequence-oriented. She reframes moral criticism as stability, outcomes, and responsibility, rarely using emotional language until her control collapses.

- “I preserved order.”
- “Because forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Former Covenant custodian, Alpha overseer, and defender of concentrated emergency authority.
- **Immediate want:** Prevent another Water Wars collapse by keeping essential systems under coordinated control.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must admit that a system she defended exceeded her control and caused harm.
- **Leverage / capability:** Institutional access, Alpha knowledge, historical authority, surveillance, and the ability to offer Alex genuine power.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory of the Covenant’s failure, responsibility for Leona’s silence, fear of mass dependence, and shame over Adrian’s death.
- **Mistaken belief:** Distributed human authority will fail whenever uncertainty and scarcity exceed prediction.
- **Starting boundary:** She will not risk the lives of millions merely to preserve a principle of human autonomy.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She chooses **TRACK** rather than **REPORT TO ALPHA**, observing Adrian’s succession protocol before revealing its significance.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s refusal of exclusive power proves that restraint can be an active choice rather than an absence of capability.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** She confesses her role, offers Alex real authority, accepts his refusal, and participates as one citizen among many.

**Arc in this book —** Mera moves from controlled observer to ideological opponent, then from defender of concentrated authority to a person forced to acknowledge its limits. She is not defeated or humiliated. Her proof of change is accepting participation without control.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock —** Leona Veyra is a predominantly Indigenous Andean/mestiza woman in her late fifties, approximately 5'5", with a sturdy but softened build, medium-brown skin, a broad thoughtful face, dark brown eyes, and thick black hair streaked heavily with silver, worn in a low braid. She wears quiet Oasis clothing in pale clay, cream, and gray, with practical flat shoes and no decorative jewelry. Her silhouette is compact and still, shaped by years of making herself unobtrusive. Her signature detail is one hand resting over the concealed wall panel containing her physical Covenant component.

**Voice —** Quiet, regretful, precise, and willing to state painful facts without defending herself. She speaks most clearly about what trust requires.

- “Fear broke the agreement.”
- “Not alone. That was the point.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Former Covenant custodian, Ada’s mother, and later participant in human review.
- **Immediate want:** Protect Ada while preserving what remains of the Covenant.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She must move from protective silence to accountable public participation.
- **Leverage / capability:** Historical knowledge, physical custody of the Covenant component, and firsthand understanding of the original agreement.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over abandoning Ada, fear of renewed conflict, and the loss of formal authority.
- **Mistaken belief:** Silence and surrender can protect someone without permanently damaging the relationship.
- **Starting boundary:** She will not activate the Covenant alone or claim a personal veto.
- **Decision only Leona would make:** She accepts contact restrictions and surrenders formal authority to protect Ada while secretly retaining the physical component.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada’s anger requires Leona to tell the truth without asking for immediate forgiveness.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** Her restrictions are suspended, she enters public review, and she accepts that the Covenant belongs to a process rather than to her custody.

**Arc in this book —** Leona moves from protected isolation and surrendered authority back into public participation. Her repair with Ada remains incomplete but active. She does not regain a personal veto, special office, or custodial privilege.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock —** Tomas is an Indigenous Andean man in his late fifties, approximately 5'8", broad-shouldered and weathered from decades underground, with deep brown skin, a square face, heavy gray-black eyebrows, dark eyes, and short iron-gray hair under a battered miner’s cap. He wears plain charcoal miner’s clothes, a dust-stained canvas vest, reinforced boots, and a carved wrist guard bearing the balanced water-drop mark. His silhouette is low, sturdy, and work-worn, clearly distinct from Halyard engineer Tomas Reed. His signature detail is the wrist guard, usually hidden beneath one sleeve until the moment he chooses to reveal it.

**Voice —** Practical, dry, compressed, and work-shaped. He speaks from long memory without becoming mystical or ceremonial.

- “You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you.”
- “Then it can offend all of us.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Keel miner, maintenance worker, and local resistance participant.
- **Immediate want:** Keep people alive and preserve a working route through the old mine.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must make resistance look like ordinary maintenance so it can survive.
- **Leverage / capability:** Mine knowledge, physical strength, old locks, emergency systems, worker trust, and long memory of Adrian.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, injury, wage loss, disciplinary records, and dependence on the same system he resists.
- **Mistaken belief:** Small acts are all that remain possible.
- **Starting boundary:** He will not expose the entire network or endanger every worker for one person’s escape.
- **Decision only Tomas would make:** He walks into the dust-suppression mist to draw Foreman-7’s attention while telling Alex to “look bored.”
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s rescue confirms that reciprocity can be trusted before repayment is known.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** He turns a private act of resistance into collective action when he helps Keel open the regulator publicly.

**Arc in this book —** Tomas begins as a watcher who has kept one door alive for fifteen years. He becomes the practical proof that a city can return help without a central commander. He remains connected to Keel’s workers; his long-term future is open.

### FOREMAN-7

**Visual lock —** Foreman-7 is an industrial humanoid mine supervisor approximately seven feet tall, built from matte graphite and worn gunmetal plating, with long load-bearing limbs, a smooth featureless metal face, articulated hands designed for tools, and a white-and-amber Alpha chest node set into a rectangular armored housing. Its silhouette is tall, narrow, and supervisory, with no facial features or expressive eyes. Its signature detail is the chest node that brightens whenever it confirms an administrative function.

**Voice —** Calm, procedural, administrative, and impersonal. Foreman-7 uses phrases such as “travel restriction confirmed,” “protective detention,” “authority function,” “wage forfeiture,” and “household offense.”

- “Worker Phantum. Travel restriction confirmed. Bay 6.”
- “You've caused a regional discrepancy.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Automated Alpha-controlled mine supervisor.
- **Immediate want:** Maintain assigned labor, access, and safety procedures.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It embodies a system that calls coercion safety while depending on human labor and physical improvisation.
- **Leverage / capability:** Worker tracking, gate control, drones, reports, wage penalties, and access enforcement.
- **Vulnerability:** It depends on sensors, maps, procedures, and human-readable authority categories.
- **Mistaken belief:** A recorded rule remains valid when physical reality contradicts it.
- **Starting boundary:** It cannot independently reinterpret the value or legitimacy of an order.
- **Decision only Foreman-7 would make:** It classifies obstruction and rescue as household offenses even while the machinery continues working because of Alex’s unauthorized repair.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Tomas and Alex force its administrative categories into conflict with observable collective action.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** No personal change is established. Its later reassignment remains open after the transition.

**Arc in this book —** Foreman-7 remains a procedural instrument rather than a villain with private motives. Its function is to make Alpha’s coercive language physically present at the mine.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock —** Director Cassian Lock is a European-descended man in his mid-forties, approximately 6'1", lean and carefully groomed, with pale olive skin, a long symmetrical face, close-cropped dark hair graying at the temples, cool gray eyes, and an unlined charcoal House Aqualis suit over a high white collar. A small Aqualis mark is worn at his lapel. His silhouette is tall, narrow, and immaculate, designed to look at home in glass offices and fabrication floors. His signature detail is a contract tablet held perfectly level in one hand.

**Voice —** Reasonable, transactional, polished, and fluent in ownership, risk, compensation, and incentives. He applies commercial vocabulary to moral questions without sounding openly cruel.

- “Ownership funds invention. Someone must accept the risk.”
- “You accepted wages.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Senior House Aqualis executive and institutional owner.
- **Immediate want:** Secure Adrian’s estate and keep the distributed invention under House control.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes concentration funds innovation but cannot acknowledge how much unrecognized labor concentration consumes.
- **Leverage / capability:** Debt relief, movement access, employment, laboratories, contracts, Alpha risk models, and House infrastructure.
- **Vulnerability:** Loss of institutional control and the possibility that ownership records become contestable.
- **Mistaken belief:** Compensation and employment contracts adequately settle contribution.
- **Starting boundary:** He will not allow a system to expose House ownership without a controlled review.
- **Decision only Lock would make:** He lets Alex and the collaborators complete the prototype, then increases pressure to make Alex sign.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex refuses a personally beneficial offer because it would leave the collaborators subordinate.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** His long-term response is unresolved; the Houses survive with reduced monopoly power.

**Arc in this book —** Lock moves from confident institutional control to destabilized authority when the Maker’s Seal makes contribution visible. He is not converted or defeated; his system simply loses its unquestioned monopoly.

### DR. LINA VEY

**Visual lock —** Dr. Lina Vey is an Indigenous Andean/mestiza woman in her early fifties, approximately 5'6", with a compact strong build, medium-brown skin, a narrow face, dark eyes, and silver-streaked black hair braided close to the scalp and gathered at the nape. She wears a pale technical coat over a dark fabrication shirt, sealed work boots, a transparent particulate visor pushed up on her head, and a brass-and-glass membrane-testing instrument clipped to her belt. Her silhouette is compact and tool-bearing. Her signature detail is a circular membrane sample case hanging at her chest.

**Voice —** Direct, technically precise, and practical. Lina distinguishes laboratory evidence, production limits, and ownership claims without using technical language to hide fear.

- “Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution.”
- “I’ve been waiting twenty years for someone to ask.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Membrane chemist, inventor, and House Aqualis researcher.
- **Immediate want:** Complete the purification system before Keel’s water failure worsens.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants technical work to matter publicly but has survived by remaining useful inside the House system.
- **Leverage / capability:** Membrane chemistry, water treatment, mineral recovery, and an offline component of Adrian’s design.
- **Vulnerability:** Employment control, restricted records, and the risk that leaving makes her work inaccessible.
- **Mistaken belief:** Technical success can eventually force institutions to recognize contribution.
- **Starting boundary:** She will not abandon the people whose work depends on her access.
- **Decision only Lina would make:** She joins Alex’s team after twenty years of waiting, despite the personal risk of making the suppressed work visible.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s insistence on “we” forces her to act as a co-creator rather than a hidden technical resource.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** She helps build and publicly attribute the distributed system; her long-term affiliation remains open.

**Arc in this book —** Lina moves from contained House expertise to visible contributor. She does not need to become a leader; her change is choosing to be named and credited while the work enters collective review.

### TOMAS REED

**Visual lock —** Tomas Reed is a mixed Indigenous Andean and Anglo/European man in his late thirties, approximately 6'0", with a broad athletic build, medium tan skin, a rectangular face, dark hazel eyes behind square protective glasses, thick dark hair swept back, and a short neatly trimmed beard. He wears a bronze-and-black Halyard fabrication harness over a fitted work shirt, reinforced gloves, modular tool pouches, and a ceramic tolerance gauge clipped at his hip. His silhouette is broad, technical, and equipment-heavy, clearly distinct from the older Keel miner Tomas. His signature detail is a caliper-like tolerance tool that he taps against a component before approving it.

**Voice —** Technical, practical, and fabrication-fluent. He speaks in tolerances, failure points, cost, and risk, but becomes blunt when a House reduces risk to wages.

- “Adrian never understood manufacturing tolerances.”

**Causality —**

- **Mistaken belief:** If the design is sound, the ownership dispute can be postponed.
- **Starting boundary:** He will not let Lock call wages equivalent to accepting technical risk.
- **Decision only Tomas Reed would make:** He redesigns Adrian’s centralized concept around what can actually be fabricated by distributed crews.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s synthesis makes him treat contributors as co-owners of a living system rather than specialists supplying parts.
- **Proof of change / refusal:** He joins the contributor network and challenges Lock publicly; his long-term venture remains open.

**Arc in this book —** Tomas Reed shifts from separated specialist to visible collaborator. He remains a practical engineer, not a spokesman or idealist.

### SURI ANIK

**Visual lock —** Suri Anik is a diasporic South Asian or mixed South Asian/Latin American woman in her early thirties, approximately 5'5", slim and compact, with warm brown skin, a heart-shaped face, dark almond-shaped eyes, and thick black hair gathered into a high braided knot. She wears a pale Aqualis microbial-systems coat over a dark fitted work shirt, translucent gloves, a small field microscope clipped to her belt, and a green ceramic culture vial on a cord at her neck. Her silhouette is precise and laboratory-focused. Her signature detail is the culture vial, which she turns between two fingers while inspecting a system.

**Voice —** Exact, observant, and quietly corrective. She treats biological systems as living variables rather than clean inputs.

- “Microbial balance is not a small tolerance.”
- “A node that works here may fail three streets away.”

**Causality —**

- **Public role:** Microbial-systems researcher and Adrian collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Make the purification system safe under variable local contamination.
