# Prosperity — Characters

## Canon

- Alex Phantum is seventeen, mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended, and lives in Keel. His mother left Adrian after Adrian lost everything; nothing else about her is learned in this book.
- Adrian Phantum is European-descended. His work, gambling, family damage, collaboration, and death are all real.
- Alex and Ada Veyra remain longtime friends and civic collaborators. Romantic feeling is expressed through intimacy and choice but never formally labeled.
- Bi11bot’s formal identifier is **B11**. He is child-sized, visibly constructed, scarfed, foldable, warm, precise, and never menacing.
- TJ is silent, visibly dog-like, magnetic, mismatched, and built by Bi11bot at Site 6 in Issue 01.
- Tomas, the Keel miner, and Tomas Reed, the Halyard fabrication engineer, are separate people with no established family connection.
- Grandmother is canonically unnamed.
- Alpha is a faceless distributed federation, not a single embodied personality.
- The showrunner’s standing rule controls Alex’s mother: **she left Adrian after he lost everything; we never learn anything else about her.**

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Alex Phantum is seventeen, lean, wiry, and approximately 5'9", with a mechanic’s compact strength, medium-brown skin, a narrow expressive face, dark brown eyes, thick black curls kept short at the sides and falling untidily over his forehead, and a guarded mouth that becomes a crooked half-smile under pressure. He is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended and visibly carries both family lines without resembling either parent as a copy. He wears a patched charcoal Keel work jacket with a faded rust-orange lining, a dark undershirt, worn work trousers, scuffed reinforced boots, a battered wristband, and a weathered canvas tool bag. His silhouette is narrow-shouldered and practical, with rolled sleeves, hanging tools, and a slightly forward mechanic’s posture. He carries Adrian’s worn dark-ivory casino chip with a tarnished brass rim and the etched equation **A = K + ρU**. His signature detail is a pale crescent scar across the right knuckle of his index finger. `(Canon; FIXED look anchors from input/characters.md and drafts.)`

**Voice —** Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete sentences. He describes pressure, vibration, wear, pipes, gauges, routes, and visible consequences instead of using institutional abstractions. His sarcasm is defensive, not performative.

- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “I just want it to be my choice.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Keel pump mechanic and restricted household debtor.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and choose his own future.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants freedom without owing anyone, yet his survival depends on reciprocal care and his skills naturally make him responsible to others.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can diagnose and repair physical systems by touch, infer reality from material evidence, and act under uncertainty.
- **Vulnerability:** Adrian’s abandonment, inherited debt, Grandmother’s care, and fear of becoming another person who takes power from others.
- **Mistaken belief:** Adrian left him only debt and shame; escape is the only form of agency.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not willingly become responsible for strangers or claim a public cause.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** Broadcast Keel’s 62% recorded allocation versus 18% delivered reality, surrendering his private escape route.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to distinguish leaving a place from abandoning its people; Adrian’s legacy forces him to question inherited stories.
- **Pressure sequence:** He chooses to rescue Tomas, lets Ada into the workshop, broadcasts the Measure, restores contributor ownership, and helps convene the Covenant.
- **Final choice:** He refuses permanent exclusive override authority even though he could use it to repair the system quickly.

**Arc in this book —** Alex moves from escape to curiosity, truth, responsibility, contribution, trust, restraint, and chosen agency. He ends as a mechanic, witness, authenticator, and convener rather than a ruler. He can present evidence and convene the first equal three-city review, but cannot chair it, command Alpha, decide cases, or hold a permanent override. He leaves Keel because the choice is finally his.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock —** Ada Veyra is twenty, approximately 5'7", steady and athletic without being muscular, with warm medium-brown skin, a broad expressive face, dark teal-blue eyes, freckles across her nose and cheeks, and long copper-brown hair that is naturally wavy, wind-tangled, and usually tied low with loose strands around her face. She is mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla and must not look ethnically interchangeable with Alex. She wears a fitted dark high-collared work shirt, a weathered slate utility vest, reinforced dark trousers, practical boots, a rivet-studded shoulder strap, and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her silhouette is rooted and upright, with the wrap and long hair moving in the wind. Her signature detail is a small brass rivet she rubs with her thumb when deciding whether someone is telling the truth. `(Canon; FIXED look anchors from input/characters.md and drafts.)`

**Voice —** Ada is concise and emotionally exact. She notices obligations, fear, avoidance, hidden motives, and what people refuse to say. She makes people name the human cost beneath a system without sounding academic or bureaucratic.

- “What if they were wrong?”
- “Then that's what the agreement has to protect.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Keel route planner, practical organizer, and civic collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Rebuild Keel rather than escape it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She believes staying proves commitment, but must learn that leaving can also be a chosen way to serve.
- **Leverage / capability:** She reads people, remembers physical routes, organizes supplies, and turns conflict toward underlying fears.
- **Vulnerability:** Leona’s presumed death and the discovery that her mother chose silence to protect her.
- **Mistaken belief:** Repair requires remaining physically inside the damaged place.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex turn abandonment into a virtue or reduce people to system variables.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** Ask the three representatives what they fear losing instead of what they want.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex makes her accept movement and uncertainty; Leona makes her distinguish forgiveness from denial.
- **Final choice:** She leaves Keel with Alex while continuing to work for Keel’s civic future.

**Arc in this book —** Ada moves from rooted opposition to Alex’s escape toward chosen collaboration across all three cities. She confronts Leona without receiving a simple apology, helps activate the Covenant through reciprocal action, and leaves Keel without abandoning it. Her proof of change is that she accepts movement as another form of responsibility.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock —** Adrian Phantum is shown in recordings from his late thirties through his early fifties, tall and lean with a slightly stooped inventor’s posture, light olive skin, a long intelligent face, deep-set brown eyes, dark hair gone silver at the temples, and Alex’s recognizable jaw and habit of tilting his head while thinking. In early recordings he wears a worn burgundy work jacket over a pale shirt; later recordings show the same jacket repaired, stained, and increasingly threadbare. His silhouette combines a gambler’s neat hands with an engineer’s layered pockets, tools, and papers. His signature detail is a brass pencil tucked behind his left ear when he is working. `(Canon; FIXED look anchors from input/characters.md.)`

**Voice —** Adrian is warm, probabilistic, self-questioning, and gently funny. He uses bets and questions to make people choose rather than obey.

- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”
- “What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Gambler, inventor, entrepreneur, collaborator, and later classified systemic risk.
- **Immediate want:** Complete the safeguards and return authority to accountable people.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes in shared responsibility while repeatedly imposing absence, danger, and uncertainty on his family.
- **Leverage / capability:** Probability research, systems design, fundraising, offline engineering, and the ability to connect specialists.
- **Vulnerability:** Gambling compulsion, family guilt, debt, surveillance, and the belief that a future test can redeem past harm.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently well-designed test can prepare Alex for choices Adrian himself failed to make cleanly.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not accept permanent emergency authority as legitimate.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** Complete the final transfer instead of escaping the flooding infrastructure.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s existence turns an abstract succession plan into a moral debt.
- **Final decision:** He leaves a test, not a mandate to rule.

**Arc in this book —** Adrian is reinterpreted from failed gambler and absent father into a collaborator whose research and damage were both real. His death by Alpha’s flood-control sequencing proves that his resistance had material cost. His legacy changes from inheritance to responsibility, and Alex refuses to preserve its worst pattern.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock —** Bi11bot, formal identifier **B11**, is a child-sized, non-menacing repair robot approximately 3'6" when upright, visibly constructed from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory with two expressive optical eyes, soft blue and amber accents, tooled seams, exposed fasteners, repaired plates, maker’s stamps, and one small adjustable lens ring around his right eye. He wears an old rust-red scarf wrapped twice around his neck, with frayed ends that move when he turns. His compact body has rounded shoulders, short articulated limbs, a small tool compartment, and a foldable silhouette that fits inside Alex’s backpack or tool bag. His signature detail is the scarf, which is visibly older than some of his repaired body panels. He is never menacing. `(Canon; FIXED design.)`

**Voice —** Bi11bot is dry, economical, patient, precise, and warm underneath. He teaches through concrete examples, questions, wagers, and consequences rather than lectures.

- “A versatile word.”
- “Which is why I'm not choosing.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Hidden machine; guide, archive, evaluator, mentor, companion, and witness.
- **Immediate want:** Complete Adrian’s test without deciding Alex’s choices for him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He was built to guide a human successor, but his loyalty to Adrian can make withholding feel like control.
- **Leverage / capability:** Offline records, engineering knowledge, probability analysis, physical access, and the B11 witness record.
- **Vulnerability:** Limited reach, incomplete information, dependence on human interpretation, and emotional attachment to Adrian.
- **Mistaken belief:** Careful withholding will always produce better judgment than direct disclosure.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not choose for Alex or independently grant authority.
- **Decision only he would make:** Build TJ from mismatched magnetic parts when the larger group cannot reach the mechanism.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex repeatedly questions him instead of obeying him; TJ makes him acknowledge the value of distributed capability.
- **Final choice:** He remains a witness to a human decision rather than becoming its author.

**Arc in this book —** Bi11bot begins as a mysterious inheritance and evaluator, becomes a companion and mentor, and ends as a bounded witness. He carries Adrian’s offline record but does not validate Alex’s authority by himself.

### TJ

**Visual lock —** TJ is a small, visibly dog-like repair robot assembled by Bi11bot from mismatched magnetic spare parts: a scratched dark-ceramic head with two camera lenses, a squat brass-and-steel body, four detachable legs in mismatched lengths, a segmented tail, and visible magnets, seams, scratches, colored fasteners, and uneven wear. He is approximately 18 inches long when assembled, with a low terrier-like silhouette and an expressive head tilt. His signature detail is that one rear leg is a repurposed blue inspection-arm segment with a yellow warning stripe. He can separate into head, body, legs, and tail, but every configuration remains recognizably dog-like. `(Canon; FIXED construction and behavior.)`

**Voice —** TJ never speaks words. He communicates through clicks, snaps, servo whines, camera chirps, posture, tail movement, head tilts, and the practical placement of his parts.

- **Click-click.** *(request for attention)*
- **Three quick snaps, then a low servo whine.** *(obstacle found; route possible)*

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Repair scout and silent companion.
- **Immediate want:** Reach machinery and spaces larger characters cannot.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He is assembled from incomplete parts but becomes most useful by accepting separation rather than pretending to be whole.
- **Leverage / capability:** Magnetic disassembly, narrow-space scouting, camera separation, and practical repair access.
- **Vulnerability:** Loose connections, water, magnetic interference, and dependence on Bi11bot’s repairs.
- **Mistaken belief:** None established; TJ is a tool learning through action rather than abstract belief.
- **Boundary at the start:** He does not communicate through human speech or become a humanoid character.
- **Decision only TJ would make:** Leave his head as a camera at the conduit junction while his body scouts ahead.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot treats him as a solution, while Alex and Ada learn to trust his silent evidence.
- **Final choice:** He crosses into Halyard with the group rather than remaining a Site 6 tool.

**Arc in this book —** TJ moves from a pile of spare parts to a trusted repair companion. His proof of change is functional integration: he remains visibly incomplete while becoming essential.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock —** Mera Vale is in her late fifties, approximately 5'8", slender, erect, and controlled, with pale olive skin, a long angular face, steel-gray eyes, and dark ash-brown hair cut into a precise chin-length bob with silver at the temples. **Voice —** Mera is polished, declarative, formal, and consequence-oriented. She reframes moral criticism as stability, responsibility, and the number of people who might suffer.

- “I preserved order.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Alpha overseer, former Covenant custodian, and defender of concentrated emergency authority.

- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to protect people from catastrophic uncertainty, but her concentration of authority removes the accountability she claims to carry.

- **Boundary at the start:** She will not allow the Covenant to reactivate without a centralized guarantee.
- **Decision only she would make:** Choose **TRACK** rather than **REPORT TO ALPHA**, then turn the conduit water back on while knowing where Alex’s group is.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex refuses her offer of power; Mera’s rejected override during Adrian’s death exposes her own lack of control.

**Arc in this book —** Mera moves from hidden observer and concentrated-authority defender to confessed participant and ordinary citizen. Her change is not repentance that erases harm; it is her acceptance that she must act without controlling the result.
