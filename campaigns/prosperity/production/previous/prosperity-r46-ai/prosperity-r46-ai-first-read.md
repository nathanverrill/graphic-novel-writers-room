# First Read — Prosperity, Audition (Issue 01, pages 1–3)

Who I read as: a 16-year-old who got this as homework, opened it at 11pm wanting it to be bad, and is ready to call anything that sounds like a chatbot wrote it.

**I read A first.** So B was not a cold read. I already knew the debt reveal was coming, and I already knew the chip, the tea and the "everything has a price" exchange. Where B surprised me anyway, I say so.

I only counted what is on the page: captions, balloons, screen text, SFX and the art. B has lines that are clearly notes to the artist ("The human achievement is immense. The human figures are small.", "No principal characters are identifiable at this scale"). A reader never sees those, so I ignored them. I also ignored the part of the art description that calls the chip "Adrian's". A picture of a chip doesn't tell me whose it is.

---

## VERSION A

### Pulled me forward
- **A, page 1.** The pipe. One huge pipe running down through three cities, fat at the top and thin at the bottom. I got it in a second: the people at the top get the water, the people at the bottom don't. Nobody had to explain it. It was the first thing that made me look at the page instead of past it.
- **A, page 2, panel 3.** The scanner screen. "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS." That hit. I did the maths without meaning to. He's 17, he gets paid 40, and he owes almost half a million. On his birthday. That is the moment the book got me, and it's on page 2, which is early enough.
- **A, page 2, panel 4.** The gate staying locked after the screen. Not being allowed to leave is clear without anyone saying it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **A, page 3, panels 1–3.** I skimmed the tea scene. "Thought you might need this." / "It's on me." It's a nice moment, but nothing happens in it, and right after the debt screen I wanted something to happen.
- I nearly skimmed "Everything has a price, Ada." / "Not everything." I've read that exchange in a hundred things. It sounds like a poster.

### I was confused
- **A, page 2, panel 1.** Why are his eyes closed with his hand on the pump? Is he listening to it? Is it a superpower? I couldn't tell if this was a skill or something magic. I was confused but curious. I wanted to know.
- **A, page 2, panel 3.** "PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER." I worked out that his parents' debt is now his. But where are the parents? Dead? Gone? Nothing tells me. I didn't know whether to feel sorry for him because they're dead or angry for him because they left.
- **A, page 2, panel 4.** He reaches into his pocket and his "scarred knuckle tightens." I saw a hand in a pocket. I didn't know what he was grabbing until page 3.
- **A, page 3.** Who is Ada? Sister? Girlfriend? Neighbour? Coworker? I couldn't tell her age or how they know each other. She acts like she knows him well, but I don't know what she is to him.
- **A, page 3, panel 2.** A casino chip. Why does a kid in a mining town have a casino chip? It's obviously important because the panel zooms in on it, but nobody says a word about it.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| The three cities (top / middle / bottom) | A, p1 | **No names at all.** The page only says "2043". I don't know any of the cities' names. Later I can guess the bottom one is the place he lives. I never learn it's called Keel. |
| The white-and-amber boxes on the pipe | A, p1 | No. Small devices. I'd never guess they matter. |
| Credits, the debt system | A, p2 | Partly. I got it's money he owes. I don't know who he owes it to. |
| "Liability transfer" | A, p2 | Half. I guessed it. |
| Why he can't travel | A, p2 | No. It just says RESTRICTED. |
| His parents | A, p2 | No. |
| Ada | A, p3 | Name only. Not who she is. |
| The casino chip | A, p3 | No. |
| The white towers he's staring at | A, p3 | Only that they're "up there" and nicer. |

### Lines I read twice
- **"Just notice it works."** (A, p2) Read it twice. "Just notice"? It's slightly off, like a translated line. I'd say "It works, doesn't it?" I got the attitude but the wording bumped me.
- **"Nobody leaves. And nobody stays. Not really."** (A, p3, last panel) Read it three times and gave up. Nobody leaves *and* nobody stays? Is that about dying? About people being stuck but checked out? It's a riddle, and it's the last line, so I closed the page puzzled and not in a good way.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: yes. The kid with the debt, the hand on the pump, the attitude.
- Supervisor and guard: interchangeable uniforms. Fine, they're background.
- Ada: I can tell her apart visually (green wrap, copper hair), but not as a person. She says kind things. That's all she is so far.

### I felt something
- Yes, at the debt screen on A, page 2. A gut drop. The "Happy birthday" right before it made it worse in a good way.
- "I've had better surprises." I half-smiled, then half-rolled my eyes. It's a Marvel quip. It works once.
- Page 3 didn't make me feel much. It was quiet, but empty quiet.

### I predicted
- He's going to try to get to the white city at the top.
- The casino chip belonged to his dad, and it's how the plot starts.
- His dad either ran off or did something bad to cause the debt.
- Ada is going to be the one telling him to stay and fix the bottom city.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A poor kid in the bottom city inherits a debt he can't pay and wants to escape to the rich city at the top.

### Where I would have put it down
- **A, page 3, panel 4.** If I was in a shop, I'd have closed it after "Nobody leaves. And nobody stays. Not really." Not because it's bad. The debt screen had me, then page 3 slowed down and ended on a line I couldn't decode.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
- Not really. Page 3 is a mood and not a hook. I care a bit about Alex because of the debt, but the final beat is a riddle from someone I don't know yet. I'd keep going because it's homework, not because I need to.

## VERSION B
*(Read second. I already knew the debt reveal, so its punch is partly blunted for me.)*

### Pulled me forward
- **B, page 1.** The city names are on the page: OASIS, HALYARD, KEEL. That alone helped. When the kid is in "Keel" later, I know where I am. The narrowing pipe still does its job.
- **B, page 2, panel 1.** "Then don't approve it." Laughed. That sounds like an actual 17-year-old talking back to a boss. Way better than "Just notice it works" on its own.
- **B, page 2, panel 3.** Other workers in the queue with small numbers, and his number blowing up huge above the scanner. Even knowing it was coming, seeing that he's worse off than everyone around him landed.
- **B, page 2, panel 3: "GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED."** This is the line that grabbed me. Someone with his last name is missing, and the system doesn't even know where. That's a question I want answered. A never gave me that.
- **B, page 3, panel 1.** "It came with a twenty-eight-year warranty." That was a good joke. It's sad and funny at once, and it made Alex likeable rather than just smug.
- **B, page 3, panel 4.** "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." Then the chip stops turning. Once I worked out what she meant, it was the best moment in either version. She cut him, and the story suddenly had a real argument in it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **B, page 2, panel 3.** That screen is a lot of text. Eight lines in capitals. I skimmed the top ("TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY" felt clunky, like a holiday banner) and went straight for the number.
- **B, page 3, panel 3.** "Up there, I could choose what happens next." / "Down here, we could choose what happens next." Too neat. It sounds like two people reading a debate prompt and not like friends talking. I skimmed it. This is the kind of mirrored line my class would screenshot and say "AI wrote this."
- "Everything has a price." / "Not everything." Same reaction as in A. Generic. I'd read it once already, but I think I'd have skimmed it cold too.

### I was confused
- **B, page 2, panel 2.** The gauge needle is in the red, but the pump is steady under his hand. Does that mean the official gauge is wrong and he's right? Or that he's wrong and it's about to break? I wasn't sure which side the book was on. Confused but curious, not lost.
- **B, page 2, panel 4 → page 3, panel 1.** He's at the overlook at dawn saying "One day, I'm leaving", then it's late afternoon at the *same* overlook. Did he sit there all day? Did he go to work and come back? Small bump, but I flipped back to check.
- **B, page 3, panel 4.** "paying for what he did." Who's "he"? I had to connect it back to "ADRIAN PHANTUM" on the screen and decide that's the dad. It worked, but only because I went back.
- **What did he do?** No idea. It's a hook, not a leak. I want to know.
- **Ada.** Same problem as A: I can't tell what she is to him. Friend, sister, older girl from next door? She feels closer to him here, but I still couldn't say.
- **B, page 3, panel 4.** She rubs a "brass rivet" on her strap. I saw it. It meant nothing to me.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Oasis / Halyard / Keel | B, p1 | Names, yes, from the captions. What they *are*, only from the pictures. |
| The three symbols on Halyard's towers (water, energy, mobility) | B, p1 | No. I'd see three logos and move on. |
| White-and-amber boxes | B, p1 | No. |
| "Debt Transfer Day" | B, p2 | Partly. Seems like a thing that happens to everyone at 17. |
| Adrian Phantum | B, p2 | Pretty sure it's his dad by the end of p3. Never actually said. |
| Credits, who he owes | B, p2 | Owes *somebody*. Never said who. |
| Wristband | B, p2 | Clear enough, it's his ID/leash. |
| The chip and the "equation" etched on it | B, p2 | No. But an equation on a casino chip made me curious. It's the dad's, right? I'm guessing. |
| Ada | B, p3 | Name only. |
| The rivet | B, p3 | No. |

### Lines I read twice
- **"Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving."** (B, p3, last panel) Read it twice. The first time I didn't get it. The second time I got it: the dad left, so if Alex leaves he's proving the dad right, or becoming him. Once I got it, it was good. But I had to solve it.
- **"That's not the same as nobody trying."** (B, p3, panel 3) Read it twice. Fine on the second read.
- **"Up there, I could choose what happens next." / "Down here, we could choose what happens next."** I read the pair twice to spot the difference ("I" vs "we"). I saw what it was doing and didn't like being made to see it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: yes, and more of a person than in A. Funnier, angrier.
- Supervisor: a little more there ("Keep moving, Phantum" feels like he's quietly letting it slide).
- Guard: background.
- Ada: clearer than in A. She pushes back, she watches *him*, not the view. I still don't know what she is to him.
- Adrian: not on the page, but he's the most interesting person in it.

### I felt something
- The warranty joke: actual smile.
- The guarantor line: curiosity, a little dread.
- The last panel of page 3: a small sting. She said the thing he didn't want to hear. The chip stopping was the first time either version showed a character *reacting* inside. I noticed it.

### I predicted
- Adrian is the dad. He ran off and left the debt.
- The chip with the equation is a clue to something the dad made or hid.
- Alex will go up the mountain looking for him or for answers.
- Ada stays in Keel and is going to matter more than she looks.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with the debt of a father who vanished has to decide whether getting out of Keel means becoming the man who left.

### Where I would have put it down
- **B, page 3, panel 3.** The "Up there / Down here" lines. That's where my skepticism kicked in hardest, and it's the panel where I'd have flipped to the end to see if it got better. It did, one panel later, so in a shop I'd probably have kept it in my hand.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
- More than A. It's not a cliffhanger, nobody is in danger. But it ends on a question about people I now care a bit about: where's Adrian, what did he do, and is Alex going to turn into him? That's a real question and not a trick. I'd turn the page tonight, not wait a week.

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
B. It was decided twice. First at **B, page 2, panel 3, "GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED"**, which gave me a mystery A never did. Then at **B, page 3, the last panel**, where Ada's line about "people leaving" made the chip stop turning. A had the cleaner, quicker debt shock and the better first read on page 2. But A ended on a riddle I couldn't open, and B ended on a line I could open, and it hurt a bit when I did. Caveat: I read B second, so its debt reveal didn't surprise me, and B's text-heavy screen and its mirrored "up there / down here" lines are where a skeptical reader would sneer.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A good punch on page 2, then a nice quiet moment with someone I don't know that trails off into a line I couldn't decode.
- **B:** A funnier, angrier kid with a missing father and a friend who just said the one thing he can't argue with, though it nearly lost me on a line that sounded written.
