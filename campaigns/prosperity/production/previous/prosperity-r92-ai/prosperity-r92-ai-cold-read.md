# Cold Read — Prosperity

Read in one sitting, Issue 01 to Issue 03, the night before, as a sixteen-year-old who was assigned it. I had only the pages. I read every issue with the same patience. I went in expecting to dislike it.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, the whole page.** This is where the book got me. It's his birthday, he turns seventeen, and the checkpoint moves his dad's debt onto him: 418,607 credits, and he earned 40 today. The guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum." Then **28.7 YEARS**. I did the maths in my head without meaning to. Panel 4, where he says the number out loud, works. I understood the situation straight away: a kid inherits a debt he can never pay because his dad disappeared. That's a real problem.
- **Page 2, panel 3, the casino chip.** Dad gambled. One object and I got it.
- **Page 6, B11.** "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." / "You are Adrian's son." "Problem?" "A fact with a poor record." This was the first character with a personality. I liked the robot right away, and I didn't expect to.
- **Page 10, panel 4: "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR."** He crosses the line to save a guy and loses the whole day's pay, the same 40 from page 2. That one hit, because I already knew what 40 meant to him.
- **Page 11, TJ.** A crooked magnet dog that takes its own head off and sends it through a gap. "That dog yours?" "Recently." Funny, and I'd happily see more of it.
- **Pages 14–15: 62% reported, 18% delivered.** Finally a clear crime. They tell Keel it gets 62% of the water and it really gets 18%, and the rest goes up a pipe marked industrial. This is the first time I understood what the book was about beyond the debt.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1.** It's a fine scene, but I didn't know who the supervisor was or why I should care about a bracket. I read it, then forgot it.
- **Page 8, Mera's control room.** I read the screen text (TRACK, REPORT / IMMEDIATE ISOLATION, OVERRIDE: DENIED, MANUAL INTERVENTION — RECORDED) and took in nothing. Four panels of interface.
- **Pages 15–17, the chamber with three routes.** Private channel, residential-service hatch, main conduit, dry ledge, conduit valve, wall. I gave up on the layout and skimmed until water showed up.

### I was confused
- **Page 3: who is Ada?** She just turns up at the pharmacy tying a pouch shut and then tells Alex what his grandmother needs. Does she work there? Is she his friend, his girlfriend, his cousin? I never found out in this issue. Actually I never found out in the whole book.
- **Page 3, the money.** He has 52. The medicine is 30. Why can't he just pay for the medicine? Why does Inez front it to him? Then Ada pushes the token back to him and Inez writes the token down as "payment pending," so he took both on credit? I read this panel three times and I'm still not sure what he walked out with.
- **Page 3: what's the token for?** "Departure" to where? Nobody says. "You don't enter old works alone": what are the old works? Who said he was going anywhere?
- **Page 3 to page 4, timing.** Ada says the grandmother needs the medicine *tonight*. Page 4 is "the next evening," and on panel 3 he's giving her the medicine. Did he wait a day? Or is the apartment scene a flashback?
- **Page 4: "The assignment took my shift."** What assignment? Nobody assigned him anything on page 3.
- **Page 8: is Mera good or bad?** She tracks the signal, tries to hold the route open, gets denied, and then (I think?) she's the one who reassigns Alex to Site 6. "Reach it before they close it." Who are "they"? Who is she? Where is Oasis? I had nothing.
- **Page 12, Tomas's key.** Dad asked a random miner to keep a door, and Alex happens to get assigned to that exact mine. Okay, I think Mera did that. But I'm guessing.
- **Page 16: what does "SEND" do?** I got that the public screens show the truth now. I don't get why sending it shuts the private channel, or why that matters.
- **Page 17: "Open the segment. Not the homes."** I don't know what Mera is doing or who she's helping. Then Tomas is holding a "worker-status relay" against a wall. Why?
- **Page 18, panel 3: Tomas's light goes dark.** Did he die? Did his tracker die? Did the water get him? Is it their fault?

### Lines I read twice

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear:** Alex (broke, stubborn, fixes things), B11 (dry, funny), TJ (dog), Tomas (tough, jokes while trapped), Grandmother (barely there, but the cloth-folding is a nice touch).
- **Blurry:** Ada. She has lines and does things, but I don't know who she is to Alex or why she's coming. Inez is a pharmacist with a ledger, and that's fine. Mera is a name in a control room.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the 28.7 years.
- Page 10, the −40.
- Page 18, Tomas's light going out. A little, because he'd just been funny. "Enough to complain."

### I predicted
- Dad's dead, and the robot knows something about it. (I was right, but not until Issue 03.)
- Mera is secretly on Alex's side.
- The 62% number would come back. (It did.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his gambler dad's debt finds his dad's robot and proves the city is stealing his neighbourhood's water.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 8.** Up to there I had a broke kid, a dead-ish dad and a cool robot. Then I got four panels of interface screens and a woman I don't know saying "Reach it before they close it." In a shop, this is the page I'd close it on. I kept going because of the assignment, and TJ won me back on page 11.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Kind of. The splash of climbing out with water rising is a good image. But it's an escape, and I'm not worried about anyone in it, because the four people climbing are obviously fine. The real hook is the panel before: Tomas's light going dark. I want to know if he's alive. I'd also want the 18% thing to blow up in somebody's face. I'd read the next one tomorrow, but I wouldn't rush.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 8; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19:** "That was not a route." / "It was a route. The destination objected." B11 is still the best thing in the book.
- **Page 20, panel 2, the kiosk.** A worker puts in a part he fixed and the machine says **OWNERSHIP: CALDERA HOUSE / CREATOR: UNLISTED / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS**. "My correction is in there." I got that instantly: the rich companies steal what workers make. Good scene, and nobody had to explain it.
- **Page 21, panel 4:** Lock offering to clear the debt tonight. That's a real temptation, because I've watched the 418,607 for a whole issue.
- **Page 23, panel 4:** Alex tearing up the transfer document. Satisfying.
- **Page 28:** "I expected a longer failure." / "You can have one later." Funny.
- **Page 33, panel 4:** him reading the four names out loud. I didn't know these people well, but it felt like a stand.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 23–29.** This is where I started skimming hard. Membrane, biofilter, culture, monitored power node, heat shield, recovery module, connector, sleeve, tolerance, casing. Four new people, each with a part, each with one technical line. "The node sends." "The membrane receives." "The casing chokes." I followed the percentages (11%, then 94%, then 91%) and skipped the rest.
- **Page 29:** I got that it's too big to be useful, so they shrink it. That part was fine, but I read it fast.
- **Pages 34–35:** registry, Seal, columns, publish, license. Mostly screen text. Skimmed.

### I was confused
- **Page 19: where are we?** The issue is called Halyard, so I assume this is Halyard. Nobody says it. Is it the city on top? Is that the same place as Oasis? I couldn't tell how many cities there are.
- **Page 19: "keep Keel from being sealed shut."** Who's sealing Keel? Since when? This is the goal for the whole issue and it shows up as one line after one screen notice.
- **Page 20: Mara Morrow.** I had *just* learned Mera. Mera and Mara are one letter apart. For a second I thought it was the same person.
- **Page 21: 312 patents.** Whose? Dad's, I guess? That's huge news (the gambler dad was an inventor with hundreds of patents), and it's dropped as a number on a screen. Alex doesn't react at all.
- **Page 21: who is Lock, and how does he know Alex?** Why is he in a registry hall waiting for him?
- **Page 22: Lock tells Alex to go find the four people.** Why would the bad guy help? Is he a bad guy?
- **Pages 22–23: why do the four people matter to saving Keel?** I didn't connect "build a purifier" to "stop Keel getting sealed" until much later, and I'm still not sure I did.
- **Page 26:** we go from a burning House fixture straight to "a rented maker room." How? Who rented it?
- **Page 31: "Put that in the price."** Didn't get it.
- **Page 32:** Morrow & Sons paid before seeing it, and Ada says "They saw the failure list." So honesty sells? I think that's the point. It went by fast.
- **Page 35:** the collaborators get "reassigned." Is that bad? Are they arrested? They vanish behind shutters and never come back. I never found out what happened to Lina, Suri, Jalen or Ren.
- **Page 36:** Mera says go to Oasis. Alex *turns away from the Oasis lift* and jams the barrier to get back to Ada. Then page 37, all four of them come out at Oasis. So he went anyway? Then what was the choice on page 36 about?
- **Page 36:** "his scarred wrist guard." What scar? I don't remember a wrist guard.
- **Where's the dad plot?** Grandmother said "Then he stopped coming home." That was the question I cared about, and this whole issue drops it.
- **Tomas: "STATUS: UNRESOLVED"** on page 19, then nothing.

### Lines I read twice
- "Take it. Then sign Lock's paper." (p23). Why would she want him to sign?
- "If I take it, who services it?" (p23)
- "Not that valve. The culture dies hungry." (p23)

- "Thin route." / "Still carries." (p31)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Couldn't:** Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren. They arrive on pages 22–25, four names in four pages. I can tell you their jobs: Lina is membrane, Suri is culture, Jalen is power and doors, Ren is heat. I can't tell you anything else about any of them. Ren is the only one who sounds like a person ("Take the part. I take the heat").
- **Lock:** the stylus, the offer. A suit.
- **Ada** does almost nothing this issue except say "Move" and "Alex." On page 35 she catches his wrist and he says "I see you," as if this is a big relationship moment. I didn't know they had a relationship.
- **Mera / Mara:** see above.

### I felt something
- Page 20, the worker and his 14-credit "bonus." Annoyed on his behalf.
- Page 23, tearing the paper.
- Not much otherwise. The barrier separation on pages 35–36 should have been a feeling, but I didn't know what Ada was to him.

### I predicted
- The House would copy the purifier anyway. (It did, page 32.)
- Lock would come back with the offer at the end. (He did.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
I thought this issue was about Alex refusing to sell out his dad's inventions and instead building a water purifier with workers who get their names credited, though I'm not sure how that saves Keel.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 24.** Four new names, a "culture," a "monitored power node," and a bronze Seal pulled out of a uniform that I'm supposed to care about. I'd lost track of what we were doing and why. This is where the phone came out.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. It's a loud noise: sparks, barrier, KRRRNNK. The choice on screen (Oasis or Halyard) didn't mean anything to me, because I didn't know what either one costs. I barely know who Ada is to him, so being separated from her isn't a stake. What I actually want to know (is Tomas alive, what happened to Dad) wasn't touched all issue. I'd only open Issue 03 because it's assigned.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: no

## Issue 03 — Oasis

### Pulled me forward
- **Pages 39–40, Ada and her mother.** This is the best-written scene in the book, and I wasn't expecting it. "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" Then "Did you know where I was?" "Within a year." "And you didn't come." Short and mean, and it hurt. I sat up. If the whole book were written like this, I'd have finished it without being made to.
- **Page 45, Dad's last recording.** "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." The water around his boots. Finally, the dad question gets answered.
- **Page 46:** "He was still a gambler." "Yes." "He built something worth finding." "Also yes." Best B11 moment. It let Dad be both things at once. I liked that.
- **Page 51: Tomas is alive.** I actually said "oh" out loud. "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." Great callback, and it worked because I remembered the numbers from Issue 01. "You made it." "Had work to do." Good.
- **Page 54, the debt: 402,110.** Fourteen months of work and he's paid off about sixteen thousand. That's honest, and kind of brutal. I respected that the book didn't magic it away. The medicine advance to Inez is PAID, and that small thing landed.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37:** two new people (Nadi Ruiz, Clerk Olani) at a table before I'd even seen Oasis. Skimmed.
- **Pages 41–42, the bands.** I skimmed these hard, because I couldn't follow any of it.
- **Pages 47–50:** VERIFY, VERIFY, lever, designation, FLOW SHIFT, CONVENOR OF PROPOSAL, NO EXECUTIVE DESIGNATION, RESCINDED. A wall of screen text. Skimmed.
- **Page 53:** a meeting gets paused and a review date gets written down. I read it, but that's the climax?

### I was confused
- **Page 37: "ORBITAL INDUSTRIAL BRANCH."** Orbital? Like space? That's the first time anyone has said that. Is the water going to *space*? That's a massive thing to learn on page 37 from a form, and nobody reacts.
- **Page 38: Ada's mother lives in Oasis?** So Ada is from the rich city? That would have changed how I read her in Issue 01. It's dropped here as "Her mother," and Mera says it, not Ada.
- **Pages 39–42: the Covenant bands.** What are they? They warm up or stay cold depending on... whether you're being honest? Whether you sit with the poor people? "The band stays cold." "Her band warms only after she sits." Are they magic? Tech? Who made them? Why does Dad have one?
- **Page 41:** "TWO OF THREE — EMERGENCY AUTHORIZATION / EXPIRATION: NONE / INDEPENDENT REVOCATION: NONE." I think this means three people (Leona, Mera, and Dad?) voted to give someone emergency powers and forgot to put an end date on it. I worked that out *after the book was over*, piecing it together. On the page I had no idea.
- **Page 42: "I will release what he left. I will mark what stops."** No idea.
- **Page 42: "It won't approve the request." / "It wasn't built to."** What wasn't built to do what?
- **Pages 43–45: who killed Dad?** The cards say he couldn't buy parts and people stopped coming. A door closed. Mera "reached it" when it was already closing. Water rose. So did the system trap him? Did Mera? Is she guilty? Is this supposed to be a twist? I couldn't tell whether I was supposed to hate her or forgive her.
- **Page 44:** it's labelled MERA VALE's override request, so Mera tried to save him? Then what's the "two of three" about?
- **Pages 47–49: what does the lever do?** Mera says "Take it. You can stop the shift tonight." Ada says "Don't make two people the answer for everybody." So he'd become a sort of dictator, but a good one? I got the *idea* of "don't take the power." I didn't get what the power was.
- **Page 49:** he refuses the lever, and "The flow indicator stops advancing." Why? Because he showed people the reading? Because he refused? Cause and effect lost me here.
- **Page 54:** Alex works at Morrow & Sons in Keel now, so the purifier from Issue 02 was for them? I think so.
- **Page 54: the token.** Alex has a departure token, Ada tears a map, Ada takes the morning departure, and "the route back" is "Mine." Is Ada leaving him? Leaving Keel? Going to her mother? Is Alex staying? Is he going to leave too? I read panel 4 three times.
- **Halyard lights above the basin** on the last panel: so you can see Halyard from Keel? Was Halyard always right there?

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Clear now:** Ada, finally, thanks to pages 39–40. She's the one who got abandoned. Too late, though: she shows up as a real person on page 39 and leaves on page 54. Leona: cold mum trying to make up for it. Tomas: still great. B11: the best character from start to finish.
- **Blurry:** Mera. Three issues and I still can't tell you whether she's a hero, a villain or a guilty bureaucrat. Nadi and Olani are a job title each. Lock disappears completely after Issue 02, and so do the four collaborators. I noticed.
- **Alex** himself is a bit blank. He's always right and always calm, and he says the correct thing in short sentences. I liked him most when he was losing money in Issue 01.

### I felt something
- Pages 39–40. Ada and her mum. Real.
- Page 45. "The rest is yours to choose." A bit.
- Page 46. "Also yes."
- Page 51. Tomas alive. Proper relief.
- Page 54, the 402,110. A quiet "oof."

### I predicted
- Dad died down there. (Yes.)
- Alex would refuse the power. (Yes. I could see it from page 48, panel 2. The book had been setting up "don't take the shortcut" since he tore up the paper.)
- Tomas would come back. (I hoped so, and I wasn't sure. Good.)
- I predicted Ada would stay with Alex. Wrong, and I'm not sure what happened instead.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
I think it's about refusing shortcuts: the debt deal, the power lever, the stolen credit. Fixing things slowly and in public, with everyone's names on it, is the point, even if the debt is still 402,110 at the end.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 41.** The bands, "TWO OF THREE," expiration none, revocation none, bands touching but staying dim. I had just had the best scene in the book (pages 39–40), and it immediately turned into a rules system I couldn't follow. If I hadn't been made to, I'd have stopped here and never reached Tomas on page 51, which is a shame, because 51 is good.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
This is the end of the book, so the question is whether I'd buy a next one. Honestly, no. The last page is quiet, and parts of it are nice (the unfolded cloth, the paid medicine, the debt still huge). But Ada's exit is too cryptic to ache over: I don't know where she's going or what "the route back" means. Alex turning a token at an overlook isn't a question I need answered. It feels finished, in a "the meeting is adjourned" way. I'd read a sequel if it were all B11 and Tomas.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 41; next issue: no

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 41.** Everyone who survived the membrane-and-culture stretch in Issue 02 (pages 23–28, and half of them would already be gone) would quit at the bands. "TWO OF THREE — EMERGENCY AUTHORIZATION / EXPIRATION: NONE / INDEPENDENT REVOCATION: NONE," and two bands that "touch but remain dim." Nobody in my class would know what any of those words are doing, and the book never stops to say. A close second is page 30, where B11 says "I counted the record and missed the people," because that's the line someone would screenshot and call AI.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid gets stuck with his dead gambler dad's giant debt, finds out the rich city is stealing his town's water, and spends the whole book refusing every shortcut to fix it. There's a really funny robot and a magnet dog, and one great scene with his friend and her mum. A lot of it is screens and machines I didn't understand."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A strong hook in the debt and the robot, then lost in tunnels I couldn't map, but 62% vs 18% stuck with me.
- **Issue 02:** Four people I couldn't tell apart build a water filter I didn't understand, and I mostly remember the 14-credit bonus and B11's jokes.
- **Issue 03:** One brilliant mother-daughter scene, one great Tomas callback, and in between a rulebook about magic bands and levers that I skimmed to get to the end.
