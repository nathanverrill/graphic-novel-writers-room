# Cold Read — Prosperity

I read it the way I'd read assigned homework: in one sitting, late, expecting not to like it. I read Issue 01 straight through, then 02, then 03. I didn't go back to earlier pages except where I say so.

---

## Issue 01 — Keel (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, the whole page.** This got me. It's his birthday, the guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum," and the screen hands him his dad's debt: 418,607 credits, 40 earned today, 28.7 years to pay it off. I did the math in my head and felt sick for him. Panel 4, where he says "Twenty-eight point seven" while the number hangs over the checkpoint, is the moment I stopped wanting the book to be bad.
- **Page 2, panel 3, the casino chip.** Dad gambled and the kid pays. I understood that right away.
- **Page 4, panel 4 into page 5.** A book on a shelf opens a secret room. Cheap, and it still worked on me.
- **Page 6.** B11 is the first character I actually liked. "That took longer than expected." / "You were asleep." / "Stored. Sleep is more comfortable." That's funny. Then "You are Adrian's son," and a band with ADRIAN on it in his chest. I wanted to know what happened to the dad.
- **Page 10, panels 2–4.** He steps over the line and loses the day's 40 credits. After page 2 I knew what 40 credits meant to him. "That is today's pay." / "I know." This is the best use of the money stuff in the book.
- **Page 11.** TJ is built out of parts and pops its head off to go through a gap. "That dog yours?" / "Recently." I liked it.
- **Page 15, panel 1.** PHYSICAL FLOW 18%, REPORTED FLOW 62%. This was the clearest the book had been since page 2: they're lying about the water. I got it in one read.
- **Page 16.** He presses SEND and every screen in Keel shows the lie. That felt like a real thing he did on purpose, and it costs him his way out.
- **Page 18, panel 3.** Tomas's light goes dark.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1.** Fine, but it's a man fixing a pump and a supervisor stamping a form. I didn't care until page 2.
- **Page 8.** I read the screen text and skipped working out what Mera was doing.
- **Pages 13–15.** Pipes, bolts, mounting traces, condensation. I skimmed until the 18%/62% numbers.
- **Page 15, panel 3 to page 17, panel 1.** Channel, hatch, conduit, ledge, segment. I stopped trying to picture where anyone was standing. I just went with "they ride the water up."

### I was confused
- **Page 3.** Who is Ada? She shows up tying medicine at the pharmacy. Does she work there? Is she his friend, his girlfriend, his cousin? She never gets introduced, and three panels later she's coming with him.
- **Page 3, panel 3.** "I know a direction." A direction to where? Nobody has said he's going anywhere. Then Ada says "You don't enter old works alone," and I didn't know what old works were or why he'd go there. They're talking about a plan I wasn't told about.
- **Page 3, the token.** He pushes it back, Ada pushes it forward, and then it's "payment pending." So does he have it or not? And what is it for? Leaving Keel for where?
- **Page 3 to 4, timing.** Ada says grandmother needs the medicine "tonight." Then page 4 is "the next evening," and in panel 3 he's handing grandmother the medicine. Did she go a day without it? Or is panel 3 a flashback? I couldn't tell.
- **Page 4, panel 1.** "The assignment took my shift." What assignment? I only understood the reassignment on page 8, after it was already mentioned here.
- **Page 8.** Who is Mera? Is she on his side? She picks TRACK, the system denies her override, she reassigns him to Site 6 (I think?), and then she says "Reach it before they close it." Who is "they"? Isn't she "they"? I genuinely didn't know if she was helping him or hunting him. Confused but intrigued, not detached, but it stayed fuzzy for two whole issues.
- **Page 10, panel 1.** A man is crushed under a frame and his first line is "That's not regulation." I read it as a joke? A complaint? It made me not take the injury seriously.
- **Page 12.** I didn't know who Tomas was until he pulled out a key Adrian gave him. Then he's suddenly risking his life for them. I needed him earlier.
- **Page 17, panel 2.** "Open the segment. Not the homes." I don't know what this means physically. Is she flooding them up the pipe on purpose? Is that saving them or trying to kill them?
- **Page 17, panel 3.** Tomas "braces the worker-status relay." Why does that matter? Is he keeping their tracking alive, or blocking it?

### Lines I read twice
- p3: "I know a direction." / "You know a direction. Not a route."
- p6: "A fact with a poor record."
- p6: "I have a route. I have not yet decided what it means."
- p7: "Bring back the physical reading. Don't trust my conclusion." (I got it on the second read.)
- p12: "He ask you to keep him out?" / "He didn't ask enough." Gave up on this one. Keep who out of what?
- p13: "The survey says straight." / "The bolts say somebody turned."
- p15: "The big one carries pressure."
- p17: "This gets us to the wall, not up it."
- p17: "Open the segment. Not the homes."

There's a pattern of short "X. Not Y." lines that sound clever but I have to stop and decode them. By the end of Issue 01 I'd spotted it, and it started reading like a style, possibly a machine one. That's the first time I wondered if this was AI-written.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex: yes. Ada: yes by voice (short, bossy, practical) but not by who she is to him. B11: yes, easily. TJ: yes. Grandmother: yes. Inez: yes, the "I need the money" pharmacist, and I liked that she wasn't soft. Tomas: yes by p12. Mera: I knew her by location (control room), not by what she wants.
- The supervisor (p1) and the guard (p2) blur, but that's fine.

### I felt something
- p2, the birthday. Real.
- p10, losing the 40 credits.
- p18, Tomas's light going out. A little. I'd known him for six pages, so it was more "oh no" than grief.
- p5, "He built and bet with the same hands." / "Both hurt us." Small, but it landed.

### I predicted
- From p6, that Adrian is dead and B11 carries his last message. (Right.)
- That Mera turns out to be secretly helping. (Kind of right.)
- That Tomas isn't really dead. (Right, though I wasn't sure.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his gambler dad's debt finds out the city is stealing his town's water and goes to prove it, with his dad's old robot.

### Where I would have put it down
I wouldn't have. Page 3 nearly lost me because I didn't know who anyone was or where they were going, but page 2 had already hooked me, and B11 on page 6 kept me going.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 18 is a splash of them climbing up with water rising behind them. The splash itself is a loud noise. The real hook is panel 3, Tomas's light going dark, because Tomas is a person I'd just met who helped them. I'd read the next issue tonight, mainly to find out if he's alive.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — Halyard (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19, panel 1.** "That was not a route." / "It was a route. The destination objected." B11 is still the funniest thing in the book.
- **Page 19, panel 4.** TOMAS — STATUS: UNRESOLVED. That kept me reading.
- **Page 20, panels 2–3.** A worker puts his fix into a machine, it gets stamped OWNERSHIP: CALDERA HOUSE, CREATOR: UNLISTED, BONUS: 14 CREDITS, and then his access gets reviewed for complaining. I got it right away: the rich steal your ideas and punish you for saying so.
- **Page 21, panel 2.** Adrian had 312 patents. That flipped how I saw the dad. He was a genius AND a gambler, and the family is still drowning in debt? I wanted to know how that's possible. (The book never really tells me.)
- **Page 21, panel 4.** Lock's deal is clear and tempting: sign, the debt's gone tonight. I understood what was at stake.
- **Page 28, panel 4.** "I expected a longer failure." / "You can have one later." Made me smile.
- **Page 30.** B11 plans an escape route for "six recorded bodies," Alex says there are more people than that, and B11 says "The others are not recorded." That made the record-vs-people thing concrete for one panel.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 22.** Four names, four jobs, all at once, on a screen. I didn't learn any of them. I skimmed.
- **Pages 23–26.** The heist across the Caldera floor. People handing each other membranes and vials, rerouting power, heat warnings. I followed it panel by panel without knowing what the goal was until page 27. Mostly skimmed.
- **Pages 27–29.** The build. "The node sends." / "The membrane receives." / "The casing chokes." / "Wrong edge." / "Wrong tolerance." It sounds like a puzzle being solved, but I couldn't follow what was actually wrong or fixed. I watched the percentages go 11% → 94% → too hot → smaller → 91% and took that as the story.
- **Pages 33–35.** The signing scene. It read like a civics lesson: names read into a recorder, a seal, "MISMATCH FOUND," "LICENSE: PUBLIC / NONEXCLUSIVE," "SAFETY DOCUMENTATION: REQUIRED." This is where I felt the book was teaching me something instead of telling me a story.

### I was confused
- **Page 19, panel 4.** "Then we find who can keep Keel from being sealed shut." Who's sealing Keel? "Charter containment review" is the only clue, and I don't know what the Charter is. Alex's goal for this whole issue rests on a threat I can't picture.
- **Page 20.** Why are we looking at a Morrow & Sons crate? It only paid off on page 32, and even then barely.
- **Mara Morrow vs. Mera.** Two women named Mara and Mera in the same book. On page 20 I thought Mera had a shop. I went back to check. On page 54 I had to check again.
- **Page 22.** Why is Lock, the guy trying to take the estate, giving Alex a tour? And why does he say "Then find them before the review closes"? It sounds like he wants Alex to find them. I didn't understand Lock's side at all.
- **Page 22, panel 3.** Keel's water is "below safe threshold." Is this because of what Alex did on p16? Is it getting worse because they exposed it? Nobody reacts to it.
- **Page 23, panel 2.** Lina: "Take it. Then sign Lock's paper." Why would she want him to sign? Is she on Lock's side?
- **Page 23, panel 4.** He tears up the transfer document. Then on page 33 Lock is offering it again like nothing happened. Fine, maybe it's a new copy, but it took the punch out of the tear.
- **Page 24, panel 3.** "The bronze Maker's Seal." Where did it come from, what does it do, why does Lina have it? I didn't find out what it does until p34, and even then only "it shows mismatches."
- **Page 26, panel 4.** Suddenly they're in a "rented maker room." How did they get out of Caldera? Who rented it?
- **Page 32, panel 2.** "Someone paid before seeing it." / "They saw the failure list." Who paid? A Keel family? Mara? Why does a failure list make someone pay? I gave up.
- **Page 32, panel 4.** Lock is suddenly on the speaker in a room they're hiding in. Did he know where they were the whole time?
- **Page 33.** How did they end up in the review chamber? Were they caught? Did they walk in?
- **Pages 35–36.** Did they win or lose? The record is public, but they all get reassigned, the House starts building its own version, and security closes the doors. I couldn't tell if it was a victory.
- **Overall:** in Issue 01 the goal was "prove the water is stolen." In Issue 02 it turns into "get four strangers' names on a patent," and I didn't feel how that helps Keel's water or Tomas. The problem switched under me.

- Lina, Suri, Jalen: no. I knew them as membrane, culture and power. If you swapped their lines I wouldn't notice. Suri says one memorable line ("A family cannot keep this alive"), then fades.

- The unit would work but be too big. (Kind of guessed.)

At this point I thought it was about a kid refusing to let a rich company erase who invented things, which felt like a different book from Issue 01's water story.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 24.** After the four-name dump on p22 and the "take it, then sign" scene on p23, page 24 is more people I don't know doing technical things to a vial and a power node. I'd have shut it here. I only kept going because I was told to.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 36: Mera, still a stranger to me, summons him. "Alex Phantum. Appear." It's a mild hook. I'm curious what Mera wants, and the summons sounds threatening. But the thing I actually cared about, Tomas, got one panel at the start and nothing since. I'd read on, but more like next week than tonight, and mostly to find out about the dad and Tomas, not anything from this issue.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 24; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — Oasis (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 37, panel 1.** "You came." / "Your summons had teeth." Good line.
- **Pages 38–40, Ada and her mother.** Ada finally has something of her own. "You're taller." / "That's what you have?" is brutal and good. "Within a year." / "And you didn't come." is the hardest-hitting exchange in the book after page 2.
- **Page 43, panels 2–3.** The record cards: purchase access limited, travel restricted, liability escalating. "He couldn't buy parts." / "And people stopped coming." Now I understood how a genius ended up broke: they shut him out on purpose. Finally.
- **Page 45.** Adrian in the flooding station, recording into B11. "Alex. The rest is yours to choose." I'd guessed he was dead, and it still worked.
- **Page 46.** The best page in the book for me. "He was still a gambler." / "Yes." / "He built something worth finding." / "Also yes." Simple and honest. I'd have kept that page.
- **Page 50.** Debt unchanged, ownership unchanged, travel expired, workshop sealed. I respected that the book didn't hand him a happy ending for doing the right thing. Then B11's feet touch his boot and TJ taps the floor. Cute.
- **Page 51.** Tomas is alive! I was genuinely glad. "Paper says sixty-two." / "Pipe says eighteen." Paying off the Issue 01 numbers worked.
- **Page 53, panel 3.** "The industrial branch loses production." / "Keel loses water." Clear.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 37, panels 2–4.** New people (Nadi, Olani) and a "cross-city diversion" request. I skimmed.
- **Pages 41–42.** The bands. Skimmed after giving up (see below).
- **Pages 47–49.** The Measure, the Seal and the bands on a table, a lever, "designation." I read the screen text and didn't understand the machine. It felt like the end of a video game where you put the three artifacts on the altar.

### I was confused
- **Page 37.** "Orbital industrial branch." Orbital? Like space? Is there a space station taking the water? It's dropped in and never explained. That's huge if it's true, and it's said like it's normal.
- **Page 37.** Nadi and Olani. Who are they, and whose side are they on? Nadi seems to work for the industrial side. Olani is a clerk, I think.
- **Page 38.** Ada's mother is in Oasis and is a government administrator? There was nothing about Ada having a family or being from Oasis in 37 pages. And Mera just tells Alex. Felt like a twist that dropped from nowhere.
- **Page 39, "Covenant band."** What is it? Ada's mom has one, Adrian has one, Mera has one. They "stay cold" and then "warm." I think they warm up when a person accepts accountability? I'm guessing. Nobody says it.
- **Page 41, panel 3.** TWO OF THREE — EMERGENCY AUTHORIZATION, EXPIRATION: NONE. I think this means three people (Leona, Mera, Adrian?) got emergency powers once and nobody ever turned them off, and that's the root of everything. If so, that's the biggest thing in the book and it's explained in one screen of text on page 41. "It's only mentioned in passing, so I don't understand it when it matters." That's exactly what happened.
- **Page 41, panel 1.** "Adrian told me to keep it dry." Keep the band dry? Why?
- **Page 42.** Leona's band warms "only after she sits." Sitting with the affected people activates a magic bracelet? I didn't know the rules, so it didn't feel earned.
- **Page 42, panel 4.** "It won't approve the request." / "It wasn't built to." What wasn't built to do what?
- **Page 44.** Mera tried to help Adrian, too late. OK, but I had to put this together from cards and a map. Is Mera guilty or not? Is she one of the three?
- **Pages 47–49.** What is "designation"? I think the lever would make Alex the one person in charge of everything (debt, travel, water, House review). He refuses and takes the reading to the public table instead. I got the shape: don't take the crown. I didn't get the mechanics.

- **Page 49, panel 4.** "CONVENOR OF PROPOSAL." What does that mean for him?
- **Page 54.** Ada tears the map, gives Alex "the departure half," keeps "the route back to Keel," and then she's the one taking the morning departure. So she's leaving, but she keeps the way back? - **Page 54, panel 4.** "Halyard lights come on above the basin." You can see Halyard from Keel? I never knew where any of these cities were relative to each other.

### Where I would have put it down

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 54 is a quiet ending: fourteen months later, still in debt, a little better off, Ada leaving, Alex standing there with the token. It's not a cliffhanger, and I don't think it's trying to be. What I'm left with is "will Alex ever leave," but the book just showed me he chose not to, so I'm not dying to know. I don't know where Ada's going well enough to want to follow her. If there were a next book I wouldn't buy it. I'd only read it if it got assigned.

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 22.** Lock giving a tour for no reason I could see, four strangers' names flashing past on a screen, and a water gauge falling with no reaction. It's the point where the book stops being about the kid with the debt and the stolen water and starts being about a patent registry. Most of my class would have read Issue 01, maybe liked B11, and checked out here. Whoever survived p22 would go on p41.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A guy turns 17 and gets handed his dead gambler dad's giant debt. He finds out the rich city is stealing his town's water and lying about it, and he goes after the proof with a funny little robot his dad built. The middle is a lot of patent stuff. The end is about not taking power even when you could. The robot's the best part, and the dad scene near the end is actually good. Some of the dialogue sounds like a fortune cookie."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** A real hook (the birthday debt, the 18% vs 62%, the robot), muddied by not knowing who Ada, Mera or Tomas were when they first showed up.
- **Issue 02:** A heist and invention story about four people I never learned to tell apart, which felt like a civics lesson about who gets credit and left Tomas hanging.
- **Issue 03:** Two scenes that genuinely hit (Ada and her mom, Alex and the recording of his dad), surrounded by bracelet-and-lever mechanics I never understood, ending honestly and quietly on a kid who's still in debt.
