# Second Cold Reader Report

## Issue 00

### Pulled me forward
Page 3 to 5. TJ the robot dog taking apart the drone while his own leg gets ripped off was cool. The action was easy to follow and I liked that the dog just kept going. 

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 9. The minute Alex pulled out a casino chip and started talking about "routing code" and "control nodes," I stopped caring. It felt like a sudden info-dump riddle right after a cool action scene.

### I was confused
Page 3. "That's Phantum's place." I didn't realize Alex's last name was Phantum until later, so I thought they were talking about a ghost or some other guy. 
Page 9. "No recognizable control node." I have no idea what a control node is or why it matters that it's missing.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Phantum (as a name/reputation)
- Control node
- Routing code

### Lines I read twice
- "Or he made water pay for it." (Page 3)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I could tell Alex, the granddaughter, and the thieves apart easily. TJ is very distinct.

### I felt something
Page 5. I actually felt bad for TJ when his leg tore loose and sank. 

### I predicted
I thought they were going to use the code from the drone to track the thieves back to their base and fight them.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
An old man and his robot dog defend their lake from water pirates.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 9. The casino chip reveal felt like homework.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. The barge is coming back and Alex is getting on the radio. I want to see the fight.

`VERDICT Issue 00: put down: page 9; next issue: yes`

## Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
Page 12. The scanner showing Alex is 17 and owes 418,607 credits with a 28.7-year clearance. That instantly made sense to me. Being trapped in massive debt is a real problem I can understand.
Page 18. B11 waking up from the dusty case. 

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 14 to 16. The whole sequence of trading rations, talking to Tomas about 10 credits, and then aligning a casino chip with water-drop marks in a notebook. It felt like watching someone play a slow puzzle video game. I just skimmed until the secret door opened.

### I was confused
Page 20. "Legacy protocol detected. Alpha trace initiated." I have no idea what Alpha is. A police AI? A corporate system? 
Page 27. "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% / PHYSICAL DELIVERY: 18%". I get that water is missing, but I don't know who is stealing it or how Alex's "Measure" tool just magically proved it to the whole city.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Foreman-7 (Is he a cyborg? A guy in a suit?)
- Measure (the tool Alex uses)
- Alpha
- Legacy protocol
- Site 6

### Lines I read twice
- "A fact with a poor record." (Page 18)
- "I need money. Not a story." (Page 15)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex and Ada are clear. Tomas is clear as the guy Alex owes money to. B11 is distinct. I got a little mixed up on whether Foreman-7 was a robot or a human boss.

### I felt something
Page 24-25. Alex risking his own money and safety to lift the heavy frame off Tomas, even when the robot told him to leave him. 

### I predicted
I thought Alex was going to find his dad alive down in the old works.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A teen in massive debt finds his missing dad's secret robot and a map to escape a water-starved slum.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 16. The notebook water-drop puzzle was exactly the kind of riddle stuff I have zero patience for.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Tomas just got locked in a cage by enforcement beams so Alex could escape. I need to know what happens to him.

`VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 16; next issue: yes`

## Issue 02

### Pulled me forward
Pages 30 to 32. The flood escape. The water rising, the gate closing, the rail breaking—it was fast and tense. 

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 37 to 41. This whole section was brutal. Alex just walks around meeting four random people (Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren) who are all doing completely different technical things (biofilters, routing shards, heat exchangers). It was just a rapid-fire checklist of introductions and tech jargon. I completely glazed over and stopped reading the dialogue.

### I was confused
Page 33. "House scanner" vs "public registry". 
Page 34. "Caldera House". 
Pages 39-40. "Vector House" and "Orbital". What are these Houses? Are they corporations? Gangs? Government branches? 
Page 46. "Convenor passage required." I don't know what a Convenor is.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Caldera House
- Vector House
- Orbital
- Maker's Seal
- Convenor

### Lines I read twice
- "The destination objected." (Page 30)
- "I'm not signing people I haven't met." (Page 36)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
I could not tell Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren apart at all. They were introduced way too fast and just blurred into "the four tech people." Lock is just a generic corporate suit.

### I felt something
Page 45-46. Alex jamming his wrist into the closing barrier and getting it crushed, and Ada screaming for him from the other side. That was a great, stressful moment.

### I predicted
I thought they were going to build a super-filter, sell it to Lock, and use the money to buy Tomas out of jail.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A group of teen inventors team up to build an open-source water filter to stick it to a greedy corporation.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 39. The montage of meeting the four makers was incredibly boring. I would have closed the book here.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. They are split up by the barrier, Alex is hurt, and Ada is stuck. 

`VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 39; next issue: yes`

## Issue 03

### Pulled me forward
Page 54. The archive recording of Adrian. Finally getting a straight answer about what happened to Alex's dad and seeing his last moments. 
Page 50. Ada confronting Leona about abandoning her. That felt like a real, human conversation.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 57 to 62. The civic review room. This was awful. It turned into a giant bureaucratic committee meeting. People placing bands on tables, signing process fields, talking about "executive designation" and "public allocation sheets." I completely zoned out. I felt like I was reading a textbook on city council procedures.

### I was confused
Page 49. Who is Leona? Is she Ada's mom? Why is she in Oasis? 
Page 53. Who is Mera? She just shows up and apparently has authority. 
Page 57. "Alpha has no face or body." I still don't really get what Alpha is. Just the city's computer?

### Things I was expected to already know
- Leona (her relationship to Ada wasn't explicitly stated, just implied)
- Mera
- How the Oasis civic review process works
- Alpha

### Lines I read twice
- "I made the safer choice. It hurt you anyway." (Page 50)
- "Inventory isn't delivery." (Page 62)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona and Mera blurred together for me. They both just appeared as older women with authority who knew the dads/system. The "temporary representatives" at the tables were totally faceless.

### I felt something
Page 50. Ada gripping the table and asking Leona why she didn't come for her. 

### I predicted
I thought Alex was going to pull the "single authority" lever, take over the system, and become the new boss of the water supply.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A bunch of people hold a giant board meeting to legally distribute water instead of fighting over it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 58. The minute they started previewing debt records and talking about "executive designation" at a big table, I was completely done.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. It flashes forward, they have a shop, Alex is paying off his debt, and they are looking at travel papers. It feels like the story just fizzled out into paperwork and is over.

`VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 58; next issue: no`

***

## The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 38 to 40. The rapid-fire introduction of four different scientists doing completely different technical things. No high schooler is reading that without checking their phone. If they survived that, Page 60 (signing process fields and placing items on a table) would definitely finish them off.

## What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid inherits his dad's robot and tries to fix a water shortage, but it ends up being mostly about signing open-source patents and attending city council meetings.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 00:** Cool robot dog fights water pirates, but the ending is a confusing riddle.
- **Issue 01:** Great setup with the massive debt and the escape, but the puzzle pieces felt like a video game.
- **Issue 02:** The flood escape was awesome, but then it turned into a boring recruitment montage for a tech startup.
- **Issue 03:** I wanted a revolution or a big reveal, and instead I got a bureaucratic committee meeting about water allocation.
