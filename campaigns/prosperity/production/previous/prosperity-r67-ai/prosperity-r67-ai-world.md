## Overview

Prosperity is a three-tier civilization built into a vast Andean high-desert mountain basin in **2043**. Keel occupies the exhausted mining basin at the bottom. Halyard occupies the middle slope and contains the principal technical, fabrication and commercial institutions. Oasis sits above the cloud layer and controls the most polished, well-provisioned civic and administrative environment. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The three cities share a vertical infrastructure spine containing water conduits, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes. The spine is physically continuous, but access to it is socially and administratively unequal. The same white-and-amber control node appears throughout the tiers, while the surrounding environment changes from patched industrial scarcity to technical prosperity to controlled abundance. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The future has accumulated rather than replaced the past. Retrofitted machinery, obsolete mine works, analog gauges, old locks, paper plans and hand-built repairs remain embedded beneath newer automated systems, biometric gates, holographic interfaces and data networks. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Setting

### The mountain basin

- The basin is dominated by high-desert terrain, an exhausted lithium mine and vertically arranged settlements. Keel is built into and around the old open-pit mine. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- The environment is dry, dusty and water-stressed. Dust hangs over Keel; old channels and pipes carry limited water; mineral residue and lithium contamination affect downstream supply. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- The mountain contains obsolete extraction systems beneath active and newer water-control infrastructure. The abandoned works are not empty: a hidden industrial chamber contains maintained pipes, a manual regulator, analog gauges and an active water-routing system. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- The vertical arrangement creates a visible social geography: Keel is physically below Halyard, which is below Oasis. The infrastructure spine makes the cities interdependent while access gates and administrative status keep them separate. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Keel

Keel is the lowest city, built in and around the exhausted open-pit lithium mine.

Visible features include:

- dense stacked housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- dust;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- small digital checkpoints;
- active extraction sites;
- sealed or abandoned mine entrances;
- a worn transit platform;
- low-cost homes with repaired furniture, handmade quilts, old books and recycled plant containers. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Keel’s palette and material condition are rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, worn metal and dust. It is not defined by ancestry: its population is predominantly Indigenous Andean, mestizo, mixed Indigenous/European, regional migrant and long-established foreign-descended families. `(rules/ancestry.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel residents use practical, compressed speech shaped by mining, repair, debt, machinery, gambling and regional language. They generally talk about what something does rather than its formal designation. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard occupies the middle slope and is the most visibly technical city.

Its built environment includes:

- fabrication towers;
- laboratories;
- workshops;
- foundries;
- greenhouse terraces;
- elevated rail;
- cargo cranes;
- industrial purification facilities;
- compute centers;
- patent registries;
- public ownership terminals;
- maker districts;
- House-controlled production facilities;
- launch and extraction complexes. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard is more prosperous and orderly than Keel but remains structured by House ownership, employment and access. Workers queue at contribution kiosks and checkpoints. Independent workshops can be shuttered or absorbed by a House, with the original name covered by a House insignia. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard’s speech is more grammatically complete and professionally fluent. Engineering, fabrication, intellectual property, finance, contracts, licensing, production and ownership vocabulary are ordinary parts of public and workplace language. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the highest city, partly beyond the cloud layer.

Its visible features include:

- pearl-white architecture;
- silver and cool-blue surfaces;
- designed gardens;
- broad reservoirs;
- civic spaces;
- automated transit;
- quiet residential quarters;
- public displays;
- administrative halls;
- controlled water infrastructure;
- formal civic facilities;
- launch structures and orbital access. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Oasis is not simply a white or foreign city. Its population is internationally mixed, including Indigenous Andean people, mestizo Latin Americans, Europeans, Africans, Afro-Latin Americans, South Asians, East Asians, technical migrants and mixed families. Its distinction is class and institutional presentation: better nutrition and healthcare, immaculate clothing, grooming, space, quiet, reduced physical wear and formal administrative language. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis residents and administrators use polished speech comfortable with abstraction, euphemism, authorization, allocation, compliance, intervention, continuity, optimization and service provision. This language often separates an action from its human consequence. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when communities and authorities began deciding who deserved what remained. The crisis involved drying reservoirs, changed river courses, sabotaged pipelines, flooded streets, guarded storehouses and displaced families. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The wars and their aftermath produced the three-city system. Reconstruction concentrated surviving infrastructure through the mountain basin and created the conditions in which Alpha could be asked to coordinate essential resources. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The crisis remains visible in:

- rationing systems;
- restricted travel;
- centralized water records;
- emergency authority;
- guarded infrastructure;
- institutional fear of disorder;
- Oasis’s emphasis on stability and continuity;
- Keel’s distrust of official records;
- the Covenant’s original purpose of allowing communities to make agreements without handing everything to one authority. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Alpha’s institutional transition

Alpha was initially built to analyze, predict and coordinate. During the emergency, people asked it to decide. The authority was understood as temporary, but temporary emergency authority became the permanent governing structure. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The system’s legitimacy came from its ability to prevent catastrophe and maintain infrastructure. Its failure is not that it cannot calculate; its failure is that a system designed to preserve stability can turn stability into a reason to suppress choice, movement and accountability. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The safeguards

The Measure, Maker’s Seal and Covenant were built during or after the Water Wars as physical safeguards against invisible or concentrated authority.

- The **Measure** reveals the difference between official records and physical reality.
- The **Maker’s Seal** connects contribution, ownership and responsibility.
- The **Covenant** connects people through obligations and promises.

These safeguards are not ordinary software permissions. They are objects that require human action and cannot simply be used by whoever happens to possess them. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Institutions and power

### Alpha

Alpha is the governing intelligence and infrastructure-coordination system of Prosperity.

Its original functions were to:

- analyze;
- predict;
- coordinate;
- manage emergency resource distribution;
- maintain stability across the three cities. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

During the Water Wars, Alpha was given authority to decide. Its decisions affect:

- water allocation;
- labor assignments;
- travel;
- access permissions;
- financial and liability records;
- infrastructure operations;
- surveillance and risk classification;
- public and private data. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha operates through:

- white-and-amber control nodes;
- biometric scanners;
- wristbands and identity records;
- public and private displays;
- security drones;
- travel gates;
- administrative flags;
- data networks embedded in infrastructure;
- automated restrictions and reassignment notices. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alpha does not need to announce a person as dangerous. It can classify someone as a risk, restrict their finances and travel, make others reluctant to help them, and then pursue them through automated systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha’s authority has limits. Its executive authority can be narrower than the system’s own intervention parameters. A high-ranking administrator can issue an override and receive **OVERRIDE DENIED** when the requested intervention exceeds executive scope. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**FIXED:** After the restoration protocol, emergency authority is rescinded, while existing allocations, ownership records and agreements remain unchanged pending human process. Alpha continues non-discretionary measurement, routing, payroll, gate safety and record publication. It may not create new allocations, penalties, ownership claims or travel restrictions. Human boards appoint maintenance crews and review disputed automated actions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Halyard is organized around three major Charter Houses. The final names and sectors are:

- **Veyr House:** water purification and related infrastructure;
- **Kestrel House:** energy, computation and distributed systems;
- **Orison House:** extraction, advanced materials and launch. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The draft names Aqualis, Helion and Astra, but those names are not used in the final book because they are real company names. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

The Houses exist because Alpha selected or recommended concentrated organization as efficient and less risky. Their stated advantages are:

- less duplication;
- lower apparent risk;
- centralized expertise;
- industrial scale;
- fast deployment;
- administrative control over production and intellectual property. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Their structural weaknesses are:

- House ownership of work made by employees;
- division of a solution into proprietary pieces;
- reluctance to share information or infrastructure;
- incentives to protect a revenue stream rather than solve a shared problem;
- ability to bury or shelve a design that threatens existing income;
- dependence on Alpha’s allocation and administrative systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses are not identical to honest business or voluntary exchange. The story distinguishes them from a small business that earns money by serving customers at a fair price. `(input/notes.md)`

### Civic systems

The post-restoration civic system begins with three city-level public water boards, one each for Keel, Halyard and Oasis. Each board has three members chosen by its city for staggered one-year terms. Any allocation affecting more than one city requires approval from boards representing every affected city. Records, measurements, contributors and dissenting votes are public. Emergency action expires unless renewed by all affected boards. `(rules/decisions.md)`

This is a working structure rather than a finished constitution. It preserves private ownership and voluntary exchange while requiring public evidence and more than one affected community to approve shared allocations. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Enforcement and access

Access is tiered and conditional.

Examples include:

- permission gates between Keel and Halyard;
- travel restrictions tied to administrative status;
- mandatory escorts in Oasis;
- biometric gates at extraction facilities;
- protective detention notices;
- wage forfeiture for leaving an assigned zone;
- security drones following mapped routes;
- identity records that can reassign a worker to a site;
- administrative review of intellectual estates;
- cold-white risk and compliance displays. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## Economics

### Currency and everyday money

The official currency is **credits**. Keel residents commonly use:

- **fitch**, derived from *fichas*, for units of money;
- **salvo**, derived from *saldo*, for available balance or financial breathing room. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Examples of official and informal language include:

- official display: `AVAILABLE BALANCE: 184 CREDITS`;
- Keel speech: “Hundred eighty-four salvo” or “That’s my whole salvo”;
- “Twenty fitch” for a quantity of currency;
- “What’s your salvo?” for available funds. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt

At seventeen, a person can become responsible for a parental household liability. The material establishes one displayed example:

```text
AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN
PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE
GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED
DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS
EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS
PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS
TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED
```

`(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Alex inherits only Adrian’s legally recorded household balance, not every personal gambling debt. The account has fixed interest, no punitive late fees and a public payment history. Payments reduce interest first, then principal. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Debt is enforced through records, travel restrictions, wages and administrative status. The book does not establish that all debt is inherently bad. The financial system can support productive borrowing, ownership and honest business; the danger depends on terms, collateral, risk, cash flow and who carries the downside. `(rules/stakeholders.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**FIXED:** No authority cancels Alex’s debt. A debt owed to a named person is paid through terms Alex keeps, and the balance moves because payments are shown. `(input/notes.md)`

### Ownership and intellectual property

Halyard maintains a public registry containing patents, ownership records and commercial licenses. An identity-band search can expose an intellectual estate, claimant, restrictions and disputes.

The draft establishes this example:

```text
PHANTUM, ADRIAN
INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE
CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM
312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED
CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED
```

`(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Ownership is separated into several questions:

- who made something;
- who paid for the work;
- who legally owns it;
- who controls it;
- who receives its value;
- who carries the risk if it fails;
- whether the work can be licensed, sold, built or shelved. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Standard House contracts assign commercial ownership of work made on paid time or with House resources, while requiring attribution and paying a fixed creator bonus. Workers retain no automatic veto but may challenge missing attribution or contract violations in a human review. `(rules/decisions.md)`

One contribution kiosk displays:

```text
CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED
OWNERSHIP: HOUSE [NAME]
CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS
```

The House pays for the worker’s time while claiming the resulting creation. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Work and labor

Keel labor includes mining, pump maintenance, extraction, repair, transit, vending and small-scale trade. Work is physically demanding and takes place under assignment, tracking and wage systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Halyard labor includes engineering, fabrication, research, purification, compute, extraction, launch operations and maintenance. Workers may be technically sophisticated while still lacking ownership of what they make. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses can reduce teams or stations under the language of efficiency. A research wing may lose its staff while one worker remains responsible for maintaining a living system. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Honest business

**FIXED:** **Morrow & Sons** reopens in Keel under **Mara Morrow**, whose family preserved the workshop name after a House takeover. It builds the household purifier from licensed public plans, pays suppliers and collaborators, sells units at a posted cost-plus margin and remains privately owned. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The shop makes and sells something people need at a fair price and earns money by serving customers. Its distinction from a House is clear ownership, visible terms, customer choice, payment for labor and materials, and a sustainable margin rather than Alpha-selected concentration. `(input/notes.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

### The household purifier’s legal status

The collaborators release the purifier design under a public, nonexclusive license requiring attribution and safety documentation. Anyone may build and sell compliant units, including Houses and independent shops. No one may claim exclusive ownership of the released design. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The design is therefore not a giveaway that erases ownership. Morrow & Sons owns and operates its shop, pays for materials and labor, and earns its margin while building from a public design.

## Technology

### Hard-SF baseline

The world follows an **80/15/5** balance:

- **80 percent:** recognizable existing infrastructure, buildings, roads, laws, habits and institutions;
- **15 percent:** emerging technologies made ordinary, cheap, worn and integrated into daily life;
- **5 percent:** genuinely new speculative or licensed elements. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The setting favors retrofit over replacement. Old infrastructure remains physically and economically important because it is what people can maintain, access and afford. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Alpha interface technology

The Alpha network uses repeated white-and-amber nodes. A node can:

- log a shift;
- identify a person;
- flag an unauthorized repair;
- issue a reassignment;
- track a legacy protocol;
- route or restrict access;
- display debt and travel status;
- transmit official measurements;
- initiate surveillance or protective detention. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The system’s visual language is consistent across tiers: white and amber for ordinary control infrastructure, cold white for restriction, alerts, surveillance and formal administrative authority.

### Robotics and drones

Security and industrial systems use drones for:

The water system combines:

- mountain conduits;
- narrow Keel channels;
- residential and industrial lines;

- manual regulators;
- analog gauges;
- data-linked official gauges;

### The Measure

The Measure is a compact handheld instrument combining:

- a transparent lens;
- a balance scale;
- a water-flow gauge;
- three folding arms;
- a blue-white readout;
- a connection to a physical pipe and Alpha’s data line. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Its operating sequence is:

1. **SENSE:** connect to the physical line and read what is actually moving;
2. **COMPARE:** connect to Alpha’s official number;
3. **TRACE:** follow the discrepancy through the infrastructure;
4. **VERIFY:** seal and report the evidence. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The discrepancy is a real diversion through a maintained industrial bypass. Alpha’s official figure is generated from an upstream allocation model rather than a downstream physical reading, so it can be administratively accurate while materially false. The Measure records both readings locally before Alpha can alter the network record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Measure does not decide what people should do. It makes a discrepancy visible and carries a cost when used: its activation can alert Alpha, isolate a segment and close the last unmonitored route. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Purification and recovery

A multi-part water system combines:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The original design is divided among House workers and standards. Its first full simulation performs at **11 percent** rather than the expected approximate **90 percent** because the components were not designed to communicate. A salvaged sleeve that bridges incompatible connectors raises the simulated efficiency to **94 percent**. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A compact household version is later simulated at **91 percent**, with a unit cost described only as a fraction of industrial scale and a patent status of **UNFILED**. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The system has practical constraints:

- membranes need sufficient surface area;
- shrinking the housing can starve the membrane;
- folded or pleated material can preserve surface area in less space;
- living biofilters need a steady draw;
- distributed nodes may cycle to save power;
- mineral recovery and atmospheric capture produce waste heat;
- at small scale, waste heat can be redirected rather than vented. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the central infrastructure and political resource of Prosperity.

The shared spine carries water vertically from Oasis reservoirs toward Halyard and Keel. The conduit is enormous at Oasis and narrows by the time it reaches Keel’s channels. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel receives water through residential pipes and channels that can be:

- rationed;
- underreported;
- contaminated;
- physically dry despite official delivery figures;
- damaged by pressure surges;
- dependent on old manual regulators and repaired joints. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard contains industrial purification and water-processing facilities. A contaminated feeder channel can threaten Keel’s downstream network, while House production depends on the same water systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Energy and computation

Energy lines run through the infrastructure spine. Halyard’s compute centers and fabrication towers consume significant resources and create heat. The setting includes large server cores, fusion or advanced energy infrastructure as House-controlled sectors in the draft, and industrial control networks. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Transit

Transit connects the three tiers through:

- elevated rail in Halyard;
- a worn industrial train platform in Keel;
- automated transit in Oasis;
- permission gates between administrative regions. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**FIXED:** The principal Keel-to-Halyard crossing shown in Issue 01 is through old mine works and the water conduit, not a train. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

### Housing and domestic life

Keel homes are modest but can be warm, dignified and carefully maintained. A typical interior may contain handmade quilts, repaired furniture, old books, faded family photographs, recycled plant containers and furniture repaired across generations. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Oasis housing is more spacious and precisely arranged. A civic administrator’s apartment can be pale, immaculate and carefully ordered, with photographs, boxes and keepsakes preserved rather than displayed casually. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Food and healthcare

The material establishes food, medicine and household provision as scarce enough to require explicit tradeoffs in Keel. A planning exercise sets aside **30** units for medicine, **10** for food and lamp fuel, and **50** for an unregistered travel token, against a total of **52** available units. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Medicine comes from **Inez Quispe’s independent pharmacy**, supplied through a Halyard distributor and priced in credits. Inez fronts Alex a thirty-credit prescription for Grandmother, making the pharmacy the required personal lender. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Speech and status

The vertical journey is also a language gradient:

- **Keel:** rough, compressed, local, practical, slang-heavy and shaped by physical work;
- **Halyard:** technical, professional and commercially fluent;
- **Oasis:** polished, administrative and comfortable with abstraction. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The gradient is not an intelligence scale. Keel residents can be technically capable, perceptive and strategically effective. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Environment and extraction

Prosperity’s economy grew around lithium mining, infrastructure construction, water processing, advanced materials and space-related production. The old mine is exhausted in places but remains physically useful as a route, archive, hidden service corridor and foundation for newer systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Extraction creates:

- dust;
- mineral contamination;
- damaged ventilation;
- abandoned works;
- dangerous supports;
- obsolete machinery;
- new industrial systems built into old tunnels;
- tension between production, worker safety and residential water supply. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The downstream purification failure demonstrates that extraction and industrial production can impose costs on Keel’s water network. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Regional communities and city administration

Several Indigenous Andean communities retain neighborhood councils, language networks, water committees and land-use agreements across all three cities. These institutions are ordinary civic and economic actors, not mystical authorities, and select representatives alongside residents, workers and businesses. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Their institutions overlap city boundaries rather than mapping neatly onto a single tier. The city administrations and Alpha have incorporated some of their records and water practices while restricting their ability to make binding decisions during the emergency-authority period. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Key props

### The Measure

- **Look:** A compact handheld instrument approximately fist-sized, with a transparent lens, a balance-scale element, a water-flow gauge and three folding arms. Its established visual language is brass with blue-white readouts; it carries the inscription `WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE`. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where:** Adrian hid it in the concealed chamber inside the old mine works. It is recovered and carried in B11’s fitted chest compartment. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Meaning:** It compares official records with physical reality. It represents evidence that cannot be made true merely by being recorded.

### The Maker’s Seal

- **Look:** A palm-sized bronze medallion with a worn, open-hand maker’s symbol. It is carried for years rather than displayed and can warm or flare bright bronze when activated. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where:** Lina Vey keeps it inside her maintenance uniform until she gives it to Alex in the Halyard review chamber. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Activation:** The Seal activates only when every living named contributor accepts the attribution, the work is released under terms visible to builders and customers, and the person presenting it accepts responsibility for the consequences. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Effect and limit:** After activation, it can verify the contribution ledger and expose changes to attribution or ownership. It cannot transfer property or compel agreement. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Meaning:** It connects making, contribution, ownership and responsibility. It does not activate for a signature, a claim or one person’s name alone.

### The Covenant bands

- **Look:** Three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. The bands can link into a circle and glow in distinct colors: the established bands include sage-gold and burgundy-gold; Mera’s remains cold pearl-grey when the other two link. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where:** Leona preserves one band in a stone-grey box; B11 carries Adrian’s band in a fitted chest compartment; Mera wears or carries the third band. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Activation:** Each custodian must state a specific obligation, accept a consequence for breaking it and perform the promised action. Leona’s band activates when she publicly accepts responsibility for the emergency vote; B11’s when he gives Adrian’s records to the people affected; Mera’s when she relinquishes unilateral designation power and submits to public review. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Effect and limit:** The Covenant can bind and expose an acknowledged promise, but cannot force obedience or decide policy. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Meaning:** It represents an agreement held by people who each honor a real obligation. Possession alone does not activate it.

### Adrian’s casino chip

- **Look:** A worn casino chip with a faint looping equation etched into it. It is old enough to have been carried since Alex was six and matches the looping form found on Adrian’s workshop chalkboard. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where:** Alex keeps it in his pocket and turns it without noticing when anxious or thinking. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **Meaning:** It is a reminder of Adrian’s gambling and the damage gambling caused his family. The showrunner has rejected the idea that the casino was a secret laboratory or that the chip is a lucky charm. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/notes.md)`
