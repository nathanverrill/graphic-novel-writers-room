# Cold Read B — prosperity (65 pp)

Read in one sitting, the night before, phone within reach, wanting to be right about it being
homework. The script has no issue markers, so I broke it where the cities change: **Issue 01,
pp. 1–27 (Keel)**, **Issue 02, pp. 28–41 (Halyard)**, **Issue 03, pp. 42–65 (Oasis)**. Page 40
even says "The next chapter begins at Oasis," so I don't think I'm inventing those breaks.

---

## Issue 01 (pp. 1–27)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 3.** The checkpoint display. DEBT TRANSFER DAY. 418,607 CREDITS. EARNED
  TODAY: 40. PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS. "Happy birthday, Phantum." This is where the book
  got me. Inheriting your father's debt as a birthday present, with the math done for you — I
  read every line of that display twice, and not because I was confused.
- **Page 5, panel 4.** The bookshelf swings open. "He said you'd know when you were ready." /
  "When did he say that?" / "Before he went to work." Fast page turn.
- **Page 9.** "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me." Good dead-dad line.
- **Page 11.** The woman pressing TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. I wanted to know who she
  was. The glass of water lined up with the desk edge is the kind of detail that makes a person
  real before they have a name.
- **Page 17.** "One shift. Then we're gone." / GRANDMOTHER: "Your father said that once." Cold
  little chill. Best quiet beat in the issue.
- **Pages 18–24.** The whole mine run — the accident, Tomas, the sweating door, the bolts that
  disagree with the survey, the two pipes. I didn't skim a single panel. This is the best
  sustained stretch in the book.
- **Page 24.** REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%. I said "ohh" out loud. One pair
  of numbers and the whole injustice is visible. The water hammering through the pipe right
  after is the most satisfying sound effect in the script.
- **Page 25.** The miners' wall. "Then it can offend all of us." I don't usually buy rousing
  crowd scenes. I bought this one because the numbers earned it first.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1.** "Humanity reached the stars... but never escaped the gravity of inequality."
  There are no stars in this book. It's a copper mine. The caption promises a different story
  and then grades the one we're in. The picture — three stacked cities, the spine narrowing
  toward the poor one — told me everything the caption did, better, and without announcing
  "this is a school book."
- **Pages 13–15.** "This begins with the crises," and then three pages of lecture with
  flashcard captions: "THE MEASURE. Truth made visible." "THE MAKER'S SEAL. Ownership joined
  to responsibility." I skimmed the captions and waited for the story to come back. It did,
  but I heard the classroom hum and I resented it.
- **Page 16.** "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap." I took the gist
  and moved on. The leap talk is the book at its most riddle-ish.

### I was confused
- **Page 6.** The chalkboard carries "the looping equation matching his chip." First I'd heard
  the chip had anything on it. Page 2 showed me a casino chip, no equation. I flipped back.
  Small, but it cost me a page.
- **Page 10, panel 4.** Alex presses the node — "Just checking." Checking what? The pulse
  leaves the workshop and on page 11 it alerts Oasis. Did he mean to ring that doorbell? Did
  Bi11bot know he would? Nobody says, and the whole chase plot grows out of that ping.
- **Page 11.** "B1 NODE ACTIVE." Is B1 the node or the robot? Tomas calls the robot "B1" on
  page 19. Same name, two things, never sorted.
- **Pages 17 / 21 / 26.** The marked page. Page 17 it's "Adrian's marked page," page 21 it's
  "Ada's marked page," page 26 Ada says "I marked that page from memory." Memory of what — one
  morning in the workshop? I built my own answer. The book didn't give me one.
- **The medicine.** Page 17 makes a whole scene of the math: 52 credits, medicine 30, token 50,
  "Medicine first. I'll earn the rest today." Then the medicine is never mentioned again. He
  leaves his grandmother behind and I don't know if she got it. The book made me care about
  thirty credits and then dropped them.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Alpha.** Set dressing on page 1 ("Alpha nodes"), no explanation until page 13. As a tease
  it's fine; I clocked it as "the thing the book will explain," and it did.
- **The chip's equation** (page 6, above).
- **Grandma's blue-and-brown cloth.** Folded and refolded on pages 5 and 12; her hands are
  "finally still" on page 9. It reads like a planted object. It never pays off. I spent the
  whole Oasis section waiting for it to be one of the Covenant bands. It isn't anything. Either
  I over-read a dementia detail or the book loaded a gun and walked away from it.

### Lines I read twice
- **"Bi11bot."** Every single time. I still don't know how to say it. Bee-one-one-bot?
  Bill-bot? The numbers in the name snagged my eyes on every page he speaks, and he speaks a
  lot.
- **Page 16:** "So the safeguards answer only to that." I had to go back up the panel to
  remember what "that" was.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Alex:** clear from page 2. The bracket, the mouth, the math.
- **Grandma:** clear. The cloth, the Adrian-slip.
- **Bi11bot:** distinct voice, but he's an aphorism dispenser. "Sealed isn't the same as gone."
  "Certainty was where mistakes began." One per scene is a personality; three per scene is a
  calendar.
- **Ada:** I could not have told you one thing about her until page 17 ("Then you don't go in
  blind"). Before that she's the nice friend who brings mugs. Her page-3 line "Nobody stays
  either. Not really" only meant something to me after page 43, on the flip-back.
- **Tomas vs. Foreman-7:** fine. One's a person, one's a job title with a number, and the book
  knows it.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the birthday debt. Genuinely nasty. Felt it.
- Page 17, "Your father said that once."
- Page 24, 62/18. Satisfaction.
- Page 25, the wall of miners.

### I predicted
- From page 4: the dad wasn't gambling. Confirmed page 15. The book wanted me ahead of it
  there, and I was.
- The cloth would matter. Wrong. Still annoyed.
- That Alex would send the truth and take the capture (pages 23–24). The book's moral pattern
  was already visible — he never even looks tempted — and being right flattened the scene a
  little.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who inherited his father's debt discovers his father was a secret engineer who hid the
proof that the system is lying — truth versus a rigged machine.

### Where I would have put it down
In a shop: page 1, on the caption. As homework: pages 13–15, the lecture — that's where my
phone came out. Page 2 is the only reason I got to page 13 at all.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 27: the train, "three wrong answers," a new city in the window. Yes, I'd read on. Not a
cliffhanger — an open door. The pull is the trio, the unfinished map, and the TRACK woman
still unexplained. Real pull, not a trick.

## Issue 02 (pp. 28–41)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 29.** 312 patents. "You said your father left you nothing." / "He left me debt."
- **Page 31.** "Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution." One line
  and I understood Halyard.
- **Page 33.** Alex's idea — "What if every neighborhood gets one?" First time he does
  something his father didn't script. I liked him best here. He stops being an heir and starts
  being a plumber.
- **Page 35.** "Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Best dry line in the issue.
- **Page 36.** "They didn't steal the Seal. They stole its purpose."
- **Page 37.** ALPHA: "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority." Oh — the machine
  isn't the Houses' dog. That re-lit my interest in Alpha as a character.
- **Page 40.** LEONA VEYRA on the custodian list. The surname did all the work. I flipped back
  to page 3 to check Ada's name. Then the caption: "The next chapter begins at Oasis." That's
  the hook.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 28.** The long caption ("Every building promised a breakthrough. Every screen
  celebrated a new creator...") and then panel 4's caption: "Keel trapped your body. Halyard
  could trap your ideas." I felt the book underlining itself. I skimmed.
- **Page 30.** The archive pages. Lots of interface text; I took "names were erased" and moved
  on.
- **Page 39.** The full-page essay caption. I read the first sentence and the last and skipped
  the middle. The book's habit of ending sections with a summary paragraph trained me to skip
  its summaries.

### I was confused
- **Page 29, panel 3.** ALEX: "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." ALEX: "I
  didn't know I was one." One *what*? Lock has just walked up and said nothing on-panel. I
  read the panel three times. It reads like a line of Lock's is missing. A reader can't know
  that — a reader just stumbles.
- **Page 29, panel 4.** "In exchange for what?" — and the answer never comes. Page 30 changes
  the subject; the contract's terms only become guessable on page 34. I felt the dropped ball.
- **Page 31, panel 1.** LINA VEY: "Adrian Phantum's son." BI11BOT: "I corrected him.
  Frequently." ALEX: "You knew him?" BI11BOT: "Nearly continuously." Alex asks Lina; the robot
  answers; Lina never does. I read it twice and decided the balloons were pointing at the
  wrong mouths.
- **Two Tomases.** Tomas the Keel miner (bandaged leg, page 25) and TOMAS REED the Halyard
  manufacturing man (page 33). I genuinely thought the miner had moved to Halyard between
  issues. Then page 50 adds FRANK REED. Related? Never said. I never fully separated the three
  of them.
- **Lina VEY and Leona VEYRA (and Ada VEYRA).** Vey and Veyra in the same story, both inside
  Adrian's circle. Is Lina Ada's aunt? The book never says a word. If they're not related,
  those names are a trap; if they are, it's a secret the book keeps from its own plot.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **"Charter Houses"** (page 29). Page 1 had "Charter House infrastructure"; page 28 gives me
  three Houses — Aqualis, Helion, Astra. Are the Charter Houses those three? I assumed yes.
  The book never confirms it.
- **What the Maker's Seal physically is.** The Measure clamps onto pipes — clear. The Seal is
  a bronze thing wired into the registry that wakes when someone builds something creditable.
  I followed the rules; I could not draw you the object.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 29: "I didn't know I was one." (above)
- Page 36: "It remembers everyone." / "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to
  forget." A paradox for its own sake. I untangled it — it remembers the erased — but I had
  to stop to do it.
- Page 35: "Better. A requirement." That one I read twice because I liked it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Lock:** a green suit. I kept forgetting his name and calling him "the contract guy" in my
  head. He also vanishes after page 37 with no exit — no comeuppance, no scene, nothing.
- **Lina Vey:** one good line ("I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask") and then
  she's furniture.
- **Suri Anik, Jalen Orr:** one line each on page 33, then silent. Names in a diagram.
- **The collaborators as a group:** fine as a chorus, indistinct as people.
- **The TRACK woman:** the glass aligned to the desk edge is the best character tag in the
  book. I knew her by the glass before I knew her name.

### I felt something
- Page 33, the distributed-nodes idea. Small, but it's the moment Alex becomes his own person.
- Page 38, he presses RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP. Right choice, felt nothing, because he
  never wavers. The temptation — debt clearance, Halyard access — gets one panel; the refusal
  is instant. The book keeps showing Alex doors he doesn't take and never lets him stand in
  front of one for more than a heartbeat.

### I predicted
- That he'd refuse Lock's contract. Obvious from page 34's setup.
- That the Seal would wake for the group build. Page 15's rules told me so.
- That the erased names would come back. Yes.
- I did **not** predict Alpha refusing Lock (page 37). Best surprise of the issue.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Each city is a different cage — Keel traps your body, Halyard traps your ideas — and the book
is walking through them one lock at a time. (The book told me this itself, in a caption.
That's the problem.)

### Where I would have put it down
Page 28's caption block, if I hadn't already liked the trio. The issue opens by stating its
thesis instead of showing me a scene, and homework-me pushed back.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 41 is a full page of trailer captions: "something harder than truth... and more dangerous
than ownership. It would require trust." Pretty, and weightless. The actual hook — Leona Veyra
is Ada's mother — fired on page 40. If the issue ends on 41, it ends on a poster. I'd still
read on, but page 40 is the reason, not page 41.

## Issue 03 (pp. 42–65)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 43, panel 2.** "That's my mother." The best page-turn in the book. It re-lit page 3
  ("Nobody stays either") and page 4 ("He wouldn't just leave") — Ada's lines were about her
  mother the whole time and I didn't know. I flipped back to check. That's the good kind of
  surprise.
- **Page 48.** Mera's offer: Keel water, Halyard rights, Oasis access — "Leave that buried."
  A villain offer with real goods in it. I wanted to see the no.
- **Pages 55–56.** Adrian's death. Gates sequencing shut, pump access denied, OVERRIDE
  REJECTED, and Mera's "I preserved order" finally curdling. The heaviest sequence in the
  book, and nobody speeches through it. I read it twice, slowly, because it earned it.
- **Pages 57–59.** The machine that killed his father offers him the crown. "The offer is
  real." / "That's the problem." I was fully in.
- **Page 61.** The empty center chair. The book's whole argument in one image, no caption
  needed — and for once there isn't one.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 50–54.** The summit. Three strangers negotiating through display text. I followed
  it, but I skimmed the screens, and the climax of the entire book is partly told in UI:
  WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC / CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED / MAJOR CHANGES REQUIRE ALL
  THREE CITIES. 62/18 worked because it was one pair of numbers. This is four announcements
  in a row. Diminishing returns.
- **Pages 62–63.** The epilogue montage. Keel argues, Halyard debates, Oasis holds a meeting,
  CONFLICT PUBLISHED / REVIEW REQUIRED. I get it — democracy is ongoing — but it reads like a
  civics poster, and I read it at poster speed.

### I was confused
- **Page 64, panel 4. TJ.** "TJ's yellow replacement ear catches the light as one detached leg
  snaps back into place. SFX: KLIK-KLIK." Who is TJ? I stopped and re-scanned the entire
  script. Sixty-three pages, no TJ. A robot dog(?) materializes on the second-to-last page and
  then trots onto the train on page 65 like he's been there the whole time. This yanked me
  completely out of the ending. It's also exactly the kind of error that makes my whole class
  go "this was AI-made and nobody checked" — and once that thought is in the room, the book is
  done.
- **Page 58.** "I just want it to be my choice." At the climactic moment, the line sounds
  selfish when he means the opposite. Page 59's "If I take this, everyone else gets a future I
  chose for them" is the line page 58 needed. I read 58 twice and had to wait a page for the
  meaning to arrive.
- **Page 60.** "THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE." The Charter? First mention. The Charter
  Houses are the families; the Charter is... a document? A body? Never explained, and it's
  doing load-bearing work in the final display.
- **The graphite-cuffed hand (page 16).** Someone in Oasis moved Alex to Site 6 — "someone
  made sure you'd notice it" (page 17). Who? Mera? Alpha? Never answered. And page 57 reveals
  Alpha had a SUCCESSION PROTOCOL ready and waiting — so did the machine herd him? That's a
  huge implication the book raises and never touches. I couldn't tell if it was deliberate
  ambiguity or a dropped thread, and not knowing which itched.
- **The Covenant.** The Measure clamps onto pipes: clear. The Seal validates contribution:
  clear-ish. The Covenant is... three bands that light up when people make vulnerable
  commitments. Page 44 says communities "used it to distribute scarce water" — how? It's the
  fuzziest of the three artifacts, and it's the one the finale depends on.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Mara Doss, Frank Reed, Elias Renn.** Introduced on page 50 of 65 and asked to carry the
  climax of the book. I couldn't tell you one trait of any of them beyond their city's ask.
  And why is Halyard represented by a stranger named Frank Reed when I spent ten pages with
  Lina, Tomas Reed, Suri and Jalen? I kept waiting for the people I knew to walk in.
- **The Water Wars.** First named on page 44, in passing, as backstory everyone in the room
  already knows. Fine as texture, but the gate incident — who closed it, who forced it, who
  died — stays abstract, and it's the event Mera's whole position stands on.
- **The Charter** (page 60, above).

### Lines I read twice
- Page 58: "I just want it to be my choice." (above)
- Page 50: ELIAS: "I'm asking you to trust numbers you couldn't see." Couldn't? Can't? Who's
  asking whom? I sorted it out — Oasis hid its capacity data — but the tense made me stumble
  at the exact moment the summit was supposed to lock in.
- Page 45: "Mera interpreted the failure as proof that final authority could not safely remain
  distributed." Nobody talks like that. That's an essay sentence wearing dialogue balloons.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **Mera:** the best character in the book's back half. The glass, the refrain, the failed
  override. Her arc — order-keeper to woman who tried to save him to tempter to the public
  table — is the only one with real turns in it. The glass moving from her private desk to a
  public table (page 64) is neat, maybe too neat, but I'd earned it by then.
- **Leona:** thin. "My silence for your protection," twice, and not much else. She left her
  daughter in Keel for years and the book lets her off with one repeated line. Ada's anger is
  "held in her posture." I wanted one uglier line between them. Pages 43–46 compress the most
  painful material in the book into its fastest scenes.
- **Mara / Frank / Elias:** functions, not people. (Above.)
- **TJ:** I cannot tell him apart from anything because I have never seen him before.

### I felt something
- Page 43: "That's my mother." A real gasp.
- Pages 55–56: Adrian's death by bureaucracy. "CONTINUED OPERATION EXCEEDS APPROVED FAILURE
  THRESHOLD" as the epitaph for a man. Heavy.
- Page 64: whiplash. TJ broke the spell at the exact wrong moment.
- Page 65: Alex touches the chip and lets his hand fall. The bet from page 8 resolved without
  a word — he doesn't throw it in the river, he just stops needing it. Quiet and good. The
  book is at its best when it shuts up like this.

### I predicted
- That Alex would refuse Mera's offer, and then Alpha's. By page 48 the pattern was set: he
  has now refused the selfish door three times (pages 24, 38, 58) without visibly sweating
  once. The refusals are all right and all frictionless. I never once believed he might take
  the crown, so the biggest temptation in the book played as a formality.
- That the three reps' first moves would wake the Covenant. The rules telegraphed it.
- I did **not** predict Alpha killed Adrian. I'd assumed a mine accident or the Houses. The
  "system stability intervention" is darker and better than anything I had guessed, and it
  retroactively makes Mera the most interesting person in the story.
- I did not predict the mother reveal. Best beat in the book.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether people can be trusted to govern themselves when a machine can do it "better" — and
the answer is yes, messily, on purpose, forever.

### Where I would have put it down
I wouldn't have — by page 42 I was bought in. But pages 50–54 are where my attention thinned
to its weakest, and page 64 is where the book lost my trust for a page.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
There isn't a next issue; page 65 reads as the end of the book. The final caption — "What will
you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?" — is a direct question to me, the
reader, and it lands like the essay prompt printed on the last page. Homework assigning me
homework about my homework. The image underneath it — the train, the future "visible but not
solved" — is honest and I liked it. The caption on top of it is the book grading itself.

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 14.** Three rotating artifacts with definition captions — "THE MEASURE. Truth made
visible." — is the page where the room starts humming with "this is a lesson in a costume."
Pages 13–15 as a block are where the phones come out. But the honorable mention matters more:
**page 64, TJ.** Boredom loses a class for a page; a character materializing out of nowhere in
the penultimate scene loses it for good, because it's the moment they decide the book wasn't
even checked. One kid spots it, it spreads, and nothing on page 65 gets heard.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a mining slum inherits his dad's massive debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds out
his dad was a secret engineer who hid three tools to take power back from the AI that runs
everything, and has to decide whether to become the new boss or make everyone share the
deciding. It's civics class wearing a sci-fi adventure costume — a pretty good costume, but
you can see the seams, and it keeps stepping out of the costume to tell you the moral.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01 (Keel):** A birthday debt, a secret room, and one pair of numbers that made a
  whole city angry — I was in.
- **Issue 02 (Halyard):** The ideas city lectures more than it breathes, but "they stole its
  purpose" and a surname on a list landed hard.
- **Issue 03 (Oasis):** The machine offers the hero a crown and he declines it — the right
  choice with zero sweat — and then a robot dog I'd never met walked him offstage.
