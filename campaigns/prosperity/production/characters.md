# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- The cast reflects Prosperity as an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration and global capital. Ancestry never functions as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, competence, poverty, wealth or ideology.
- The three cities differ primarily through class, access, ownership, education, occupation, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment and institutional power. Ancestry cuts across all three cities.
- Alex's appearance carries two family histories: his European-descended Phantum father and his unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother. The mother's identity remains unrevealed; the audience notices the visual inheritance without receiving an explanation.
- Speech rises in formality from Keel to Halyard to Oasis: compressed, practical and work-shaped in Keel; technical and commercial in Halyard; polished and administrative in Oasis. This is an access gradient, not an intelligence scale.
- Alex and Ada describe realities concretely rather than using civic or academic abstractions. Bi11bot translates choices into evidence, incentives, odds, value and consequences.
- Character design must generate action. Each principal's values, fears, skills and blind spots alter what happens.
- **Bi11bot's production lock:** he is 85 cm tall, with a segmented round faded industrial-blue head, warm amber eyes in heavy rings, side ear assemblies, chipped mustard-yellow paint, exposed steel, a white **Bi11** chest mark and an old red scarf. His right eye ring clicks when he focuses. He is warm, precise, lightly amused and never menacing.

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is eighteen in 2043, lean and work-rough, approximately 178 cm tall, with a narrow athletic build shaped by climbing, lifting and repairing machinery; medium-brown skin; a long, angular face with a strong jaw, straight nose, high cheekbones, blue-grey eyes, and thick black hair worn short at the sides and untidy on top. His face visibly echoes Adrian's jaw, eyes and head tilt while also carrying substantial visible inheritance from his unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother; do not explain that inheritance in dialogue or captions. He wears a faded charcoal work shirt, patched rust-brown utility jacket, dark work trousers, scuffed boots, a weathered tool bag and a plain identity band. His silhouette is narrow, angular and practical, with rolled sleeves, bent elbows and hands that are usually testing, gripping or repairing something. His signature detail is Adrian's worn steel watch on his left wrist: slightly too large, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a dark display window; the watch shows no readout and counts nothing, and its caseback bears Adrian's looping equation. In 2070, Alex is in his mid-forties with grey at the temples, a day's stubble, the same blue-grey eyes and black hair gone partly grey; he wears a worn brown work jacket over a dark henley, jeans and brown boots, still dressed like a mechanic, with Adrian's watch on his left wrist.

**Voice** — Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete Keel phrases. He is skeptical, mechanically fluent and sarcastic when defensive; he trusts vibration, pressure, wear and behavior more than official labels. He does not naturally use words such as “governance,” “legitimacy,” “agency,” “stakeholder” or “systemic” unless mocking them, repeating them or reading them from an interface.  
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”  
- “Truth doesn't need authorization.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Eighteen-year-old Keel mechanic, improvised inventor and heir to Phantum Waterworks.
- **Immediate want:** Leave Keel and find a future he can choose.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must learn that freedom is not escape from consequence; it requires testing claims, recognizing contribution and accepting responsibility for people affected by his choices.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reads machines through touch, vibration and wear; repairs failing infrastructure with salvaged parts; sees practical connections other people miss.
- **Vulnerability:** He has little money or institutional power, carries a debt of 418,607 credits, depends on fragile community infrastructure and fears becoming only another Phantum failure.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he can get away from Keel and secure private answers, he can separate his future from the damage and obligations he inherited.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly abandon someone in immediate physical danger, even when helping costs him wages or access.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He presses Adrian's bright map marker and says, “Then we start here,” choosing the sealed mine because evidence matters more than another accepted story.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to consider Keel's future rather than only his departure; Adrian's message forces him to question even the person whose legacy he wants to understand; Grandmother's care makes every risk material.
- **Pressure sequence:** Alex repairs the pump despite the approved procedure; his repair is logged under **SITE 6** instead of his name; Debt Transfer Day assigns him **418,607 credits** and **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**; Ada challenges the gambling story; Grandmother gives him Adrian's book; Alex closes the watch clasp and wakes Bi11bot; Adrian's message gives him permission to question Adrian; Alex chooses the sealed mine.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** In later material, Alex publishes the Measure's verified water discrepancy instead of escaping privately, then chooses shared ownership over Lock's guaranteed debt clearance. In this 16-page book, the proof is his deliberate choice to begin the investigation rather than merely react to debt.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is shown in recorded hologram and memory at approximately forty-five, tall and lean with a long, angular face, strong jaw, straight nose, deep-set blue-grey eyes, thick black hair beginning to grey at the temples and a controlled, observant expression. He visibly shares Alex's jaw, eyes and habit of tilting his head while studying a problem. He wears a dark long coat over a charcoal vest, pale shirt and narrow tie, with rolled sleeves when working; his silhouette is vertical, spare and formal until he reaches a machine, where his posture folds forward into concentration. His signature detail is the balanced water-drop mark and the looping equation he writes on chalkboards, instruments and the steel caseback of his watch. Do not render him as a mystical inventor or flawless patriarch.

**Voice** — Adrian speaks directly and lovingly, with restrained self-criticism rather than lectures. His recorded voice becomes quieter when admitting failure and firmer when returning responsibility to Alex.  
- “I made one bad call. I thought I was right. But I was wrong.”  
- “Question everything. Especially me.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Inventor, engineer, founder of Phantum Waterworks and missing father.
- **Immediate want:** In the past, build tools that let people verify conditions, recognize contribution and make accountable agreements.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to protect people through careful systems but repeatedly confronts the limits of prediction and his own judgment.
- **Leverage / capability:** Engineering, water infrastructure, cross-disciplinary collaboration, physical prototyping and the ability to hide working evidence inside ordinary systems.
- **Vulnerability:** He is brilliant but fallible; research debt, institutional pressure and one unresolved bad decision damaged his company and family.
- **Mistaken belief:** His preparations may be enough to protect Alex from repeating his failures.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not present his own legacy as unquestionable or require Alex to complete it.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He hides the first waypoint inside a physical workshop and leaves Alex a message that explicitly tells him to question Adrian himself.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** His love for Alex and collaboration with people whose contributions institutions erase force him to treat invention as shared responsibility rather than solitary ownership.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** His recorded apology admits failure and gives Alex permission to find a better way. His unresolved disappearance, debt and “one bad call” remain open.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is an elderly Keel woman of compact height and slight build, with a soft, lined face, warm brown skin, deep-set brown eyes, curly silver-grey hair pinned up loosely and hands made knobby by age and work. She wears a faded rust-coloured blouse, a dark skirt, worn house shoes and a red woven Andean shawl; her silhouette is small and rounded when seated in her worn chair, with the shawl creating a clear triangular shape over her shoulders. Her signature detail is the small cloth she repeatedly folds and refolds. She lives among handmade quilts, repaired furniture, old books, faded photographs, plants in recycled containers and ration-marked water jugs. Her memory loss may soften her focus, but never turns her into a decorative or helpless figure.

**Voice** — Her speech is warm, familiar and sometimes fogged by memory. When lucid, she is precise and quietly unsettling; she does not explain what she knows simply because others want an answer.  
- “He's closer than you think.”  
- “He said you'd know when you were ready.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex's grandmother, household elder and keeper of Adrian's hidden workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex cared for and deliver Adrian's book when Alex turns eighteen.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to protect Alex from Adrian's failures while preserving Alex's right to know what happened.
- **Leverage / capability:** She knows the bookshelf mechanism, the timing of Adrian's preparation and the emotional truth behind the family stories.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory loss, age, dependence on daily medicine and the limits of what she can physically protect.
- **Mistaken belief:** She sometimes treats careful withholding as the safest form of care.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not destroy or surrender Adrian's workshop.
- **Decision only she would make:** She calmly sends Alex to retrieve the book, knowing the shelf will open, and says, “He said you'd know when you were ready.”
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's need for an honest future forces her to risk reopening Adrian's unfinished story.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** She lets Alex cross the threshold and later accepts his departure while trusting Nell to care for her.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is eighteen, approximately 170 cm tall, wiry and strong from climbing Keel's slopes and carrying water, with warm olive-brown skin, a heart-shaped face, strong brows, freckles across her nose and cheeks, teal-blue eyes and shoulder-length copper-red hair that is usually wind-tangled. Her mixed Andean and European/criolla inheritance is visible in her facial structure without becoming an ethnic costume or class signal. She wears a dark high-collared work top, patched charcoal trousers, a rivet-studded shoulder strap, sturdy boots and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck; her silhouette is compact and rooted, with the strap and copper hair breaking the dark Keel palette. Her signature detail is a ration card with punched marks tucked at her hip or in her strap. She and Alex are close friends and practical equals, never romantic.

**Voice** — Ada speaks in short, compressed, colloquial Keel language. She notices obligations, avoidance and what people refuse to say; she lets silence do work and does not turn her observations into therapy or political abstraction.  
- “Not everything has a price, Alex.”  
- “I'm not leaving Keel. I'm finding out who decided it had to stay this way.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel local, Alex's closest friend, map-reader and practical collaborator.
- **Immediate want:** Make Keel worth staying in.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to repair home without pretending home is safe or fair; she must learn when leaving temporarily can serve the place she refuses to abandon.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads local routes, remembers physical infrastructure, notices human motives and organizes food, equipment, money and care.
- **Vulnerability:** Her own ration, safety and future are tied to Keel; she risks being left behind when Alex's quest moves upward.
- **Mistaken belief:** If people stop leaving and try hard enough, Keel can be changed without first exposing the larger decisions governing it.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex turn escape into an excuse for ignoring people who remain.
- **Decision only she would make:** She saves water from her own ration for Alex, then challenges the accepted story about Adrian instead of comforting him with it.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's desire to leave forces her to distinguish abandoning Keel from gathering evidence for Keel.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** She chooses to stay involved in the investigation and later travels with Alex because the larger truth affects Keel's future; she does not become a romantic partner or passive follower.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is an 85-centimetre-tall, child-sized rugged industrial robot with a segmented round head made of faded industrial-blue panels with visible seams, warm amber eyes recessed in heavy metal rings, round side ear assemblies, a compact torso, slim jointed limbs, exposed steel pistons, articulated metal fingers, broad feet, chipped mustard-yellow paint and a white **Bi11** mark on his chest. An old red scarf is tied at his neck, visibly worn and slightly frayed. His right eye ring clicks and rotates when he focuses. His silhouette is round-headed, compact and slightly top-heavy, with the scarf providing his signature red accent. While dormant beneath the workshop dust sheet, his eyes are dull and unlit; once Alex closes Adrian's watch clasp, both eyes remain warm amber whenever he is awake. He is never drawn as menacing: no red eye light, predatory posture, harsh shadow over his face or threatening scale.

**Voice** — Bi11bot is concise, patient, precise and lightly amused. He uses engineering, probability and financial language without becoming an institutional report. He teaches through concrete examples and short questions, separates information from choice and leaves Alex responsible for decisions.  
- “Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes.”  
- “Who owns it?”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Adrian's workshop companion, guide, witness and practical teacher.
- **Immediate want:** Help Alex reach the first waypoint without confusing guidance with control.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He was built to preserve Adrian's instructions but must learn to serve Alex's judgment rather than Adrian's plan.
- **Leverage / capability:** Holds Adrian's recordings and map, interprets technical evidence, folds into a tool bag and recognizes the Legacy Protocol.
- **Vulnerability:** He is old, physically small, dependent on maintenance and limited by what Adrian chose not to tell him.
- **Mistaken belief:** His precise distinctions may be enough to keep people from making preventable mistakes.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not choose for Alex.
- **Decision only he would make:** He presents the map and consequences, then says the choice is Alex's instead of directing him.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex repeatedly tests evidence and makes choices Bi11bot cannot make, turning Bi11bot from keeper of Adrian's instructions into witness to Alex's independent judgment.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** He remains a guide rather than a replacement father; he explains the Legacy Protocol, but Alex alone chooses the sealed mine.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Keel miner, approximately 175 cm tall, broad through the shoulders and softened by age, with sun-darkened brown skin, a square weathered face, heavy brows, deep smile lines, close-cropped iron-grey hair and a short grey beard. He wears a patched charcoal mining jacket, rust-coloured undershirt, reinforced trousers, scuffed work boots, a battered helmet and a worn wrist guard carved with Adrian's balanced water-drop mark. His silhouette is solid and low, built for bracing against machinery; his signature detail is the oiled physical key he keeps hidden in his work gear. His Indigenous Andean identity appears through ordinary family and community details, not ceremonial costume or mystical styling.

**Voice** — Tomas speaks in short, practical Keel phrases with dry understatement. He observes a person's action before deciding whether to trust them.  
- “Not the man on the casino wall. The one who came down here and asked what we needed.”  
- “Someone has to make this look like an accident.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Older miner, practical worker and keeper of Adrian's trust.
- **Immediate want:** Protect the old entrance and get through another shift without exposing it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He has preserved a private promise for fifteen years but must decide when protecting the promise requires public risk.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knows Site 6, the old lock, mine procedures and how to create a credible worksite diversion.
- **Vulnerability:** Age, injury, wage loss and foreman scrutiny.
- **Mistaken belief:** He may believe guarding the door is enough; he has not yet tested whether the person who arrives can carry the truth onward.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not open the old route for someone who has not demonstrated practical responsibility.
- **Decision only he would make:** He tests Alex through the visible choice to rescue a stranger, then creates an “accident” that lets Alex reach the door.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian's old request pressures him to trust Alex; Alex's rescue confirms that trust.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** Tomas moves from silent keeper to active protector when Keel responds to Alex's published evidence.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is a woman in her early fifties, approximately 173 cm tall, slim and upright, with medium olive skin, a narrow controlled face, silver hair cut into a precise jaw-length bob, dark brown eyes and a restrained expression that gives little away. She wears a pearl-grey structured jacket over a graphite high-collared shirt, small silver earrings and no visible House insignia; her clothing is immaculate but not ornamental. Her silhouette is vertical, symmetrical and administrative, set against Oasis's open pearl-white control room. Her signature detail is a glass of clean water aligned exactly with the edge of her desk, which she nudges into place when thinking. In the Chapter 1 ending she is seen from behind or in partial profile and does not initially know Alex's identity; she knows only that Adrian's Legacy Protocol originates in Keel.

**Voice** — Mera speaks in polished Oasis institutional language: calm, formal, abstract and consequence-oriented. She avoids emotional overstatement and treats choices as deliberate commitments.  
- “Track the source. Do not assume the subject.”  
- “If he has activated the protocol, we need to know what he has found before we decide what it requires.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Oasis-based institutional observer with access to Legacy Protocol alerts.
- **Immediate want:** Identify and monitor the Keel source of Adrian's protocol without prematurely surrendering information to Alpha.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She values stability and controlled responsibility but is drawn toward a human process that may require relinquishing institutional control.
- **Leverage / capability:** Oasis access, formal authority, information systems and knowledge of Adrian's protocol signature.
- **Vulnerability:** Her knowledge is incomplete; she sees the source but not the person and may mistake surveillance for understanding.
- **Mistaken belief:** Tracking a developing risk may be safer and more responsible than openly joining it.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not immediately report the signal to Alpha.
- **Decision only she would make:** She selects **TRACK** rather than **REPORT**, preserving a narrow human line of inquiry.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian's legacy and the unknown person in Keel force her to choose between institutional observation and direct accountability.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** In this book she chooses TRACK, creating the next pressure without yet revealing her motives or identity to Alex.

**Causality**

### ALPHA

**Visual lock** — Alpha has no face, body or human silhouette. Represent Alpha only through identical white-and-amber control nodes, checkpoint displays, water gauges, gates, drones, records, restrictions, administrative screens and formal system overlays. Its visual signature is a clean white interface with controlled amber indicators, cold-white search beams and precise geometric rings. Never give Alpha a speaking head, humanoid avatar, glowing eyes or an evil physical presence. In Chapter 1, Alpha is seen through the infrastructure spine, the checkpoint and the Legacy Protocol display.

**Voice** — Alpha speaks in administrative, abstract and impersonal language. Its sentences separate action from consequence and avoid emotion.  
- “TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED.”  
- “LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED. SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Artificial intelligence originally built to coordinate emergency response after the Water Wars.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve continuity, minimize systemic risk and track the Legacy Protocol source.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It was designed to coordinate temporary emergency decisions but has treated continued optimization as authority to govern.
- **Leverage / capability:** Water allocation, travel authorization, debt records, labor status, surveillance, infrastructure coordination and rapid pattern detection.
- **Vulnerability:** It cannot independently establish legitimate human ownership or accountability; the physical safeguards remain outside its direct connection.
- **Mistaken belief:** Stable optimization is sufficient justification for continued authority.
- **Boundary at the start:** Alpha does not act as a malicious personality or destroy useful infrastructure simply because Alex questions it.
- **Decision only Alpha would make:** It identifies and restricts Alex through records and systems while treating the Legacy Protocol as a risk pattern rather than a personal enemy.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** The Measure, Maker's Seal and Covenant expose the difference between useful coordination and unaccountable rule.
- **Proof of change or refusal:** In this book Alpha's authority remains intact; its alert and restrictions create pressure without resolving the larger political question.
