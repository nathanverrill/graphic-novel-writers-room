# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is seventeen, lean and mechanically capable, with a mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended face that clearly resembles Adrian around the jaw and eyes without making him a younger copy; his medium-brown skin, dark wavy hair cut short at the sides, deep brown eyes, straight nose, and narrow, work-hardened face carry both family histories without making ancestry a visual shorthand for character. He is average height with a wiry build, slightly hunched shoulders from years around machinery, and scarred hands. In Keel he wears a faded charcoal work jacket over a rust-red thermal shirt, patched dark work trousers, worn boots, fingerless work gloves and a weathered tool bag; the silhouette is narrow, practical and asymmetrical. He wears Adrian's worn steel wristwatch on his left wrist: a little big for Alex, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass, a dark display window and Adrian's looping equation engraved on the caseback. His signature detail is a characteristic head tilt when testing machinery by vibration, palm flat against its housing.

**Voice** — Short, dry, concrete and skeptical; Alex describes evidence, wear and physical consequences before principles. He becomes more technically fluent in Halyard without losing his work-shaped speech.  
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”  
- “Still owes a fortune. Still mine.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel pump-hall and mining-system repairer.
- **Immediate want:** Find out what happened to Adrian and earn enough freedom to leave Keel.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants escape from obligations while needing to learn that freedom requires accepting visible obligations and consequences.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads vibration, pressure, wear and improvised repairs better than official displays; can make damaged machinery function.
- **Vulnerability:** Grandmother's care, Adrian's absence, inherited debt and restricted travel.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he personally verifies a system and gets himself out, he has solved the problem.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly abandon someone in immediate danger for his own advantage.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He publishes the Measure's 62%/18% evidence instead of using it privately to escape.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to distinguish leaving Keel from making choices for Keel; Adrian's legacy forces him to question his own certainty.
- **Choice chain:** He opens the workshop → activates Bi11bot → enters the mine → rescues Tomas at a wage cost → follows physical evidence → publishes the discrepancy → refuses Lock's transfer → refuses executive control.

**Arc in this book** — Alex changes from a technically skilled boy who imagines freedom as personal departure into a responsible participant who makes costs visible, honors repayment, recognizes shared contribution and refuses concentrated authority. **Proof of change:** he returns to Keel with a final balance of **314,159 credits**, still owing money, while choosing to remain beside Ada and leave decisions open to affected people rather than taking control himself.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is seventeen, mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla, with long wind-tangled copper hair, freckles, light teal-blue eyes, a strong Andean facial structure and a steady, rooted stance; she is lean, average height and physically sure-footed from growing up in Keel. She wears a dark high-collared top, patched charcoal trousers, sturdy boots, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, a muted moss-green wrap at her neck and a weathered travel pack. Her silhouette is compact and grounded, with loose copper hair and the diagonal shoulder strap making her instantly distinct from Alex. Her signature detail is her thumb resting on the strap's largest rivet while she studies a person or a map.

**Voice** — Direct, colloquial and perceptive; Ada notices who carries the burden and what people avoid saying. Her emotional insight remains concrete rather than therapeutic or academic.  
- “Not everything.”  
- “That's still two people deciding for everyone else.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel local, practical investigator and Alex's closest friend.
- **Immediate want:** Find the truth that Keel needs in order to change its conditions.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to make Keel worth staying in but must accept that staying is meaningful only when it remains her choice.
- **Leverage / capability:** Reads people, remembers routes, draws useful maps and organizes practical community action.
- **Vulnerability:** Leona's absence and her fear that protection will again become control.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she stays and works hard enough, she can protect Keel from outside decisions.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let Alex turn her friendship or Keel's needs into permission to command others.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She joins Alex not to follow him, but because the larger truth is necessary for Keel's future.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's desire to leave makes her defend staying without treating him as disloyal; Leona forces her to define safety as agency.

**Arc in this book** — Ada moves from rooted defender of Keel to an active advocate for choice, accountability and affected people's participation. **Proof of change:** she confronts Leona, travels because she chooses to, and rejects replacing Alpha with two supposedly trustworthy decision-makers.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man in his early forties in recordings and reconstructed memories, lean and slightly stooped from long hours at benches and terminals, with pale olive skin, dark brown hair going silver at the temples, a narrow jaw, tired hazel-brown eyes and the same watchful head tilt Alex inherited. He wears a rolled-sleeve charcoal work shirt, worn suspenders, dark trousers and a stained technical coat; his silhouette is angular, restless and surrounded by equations, analog gauges, blueprints and blue-gold projector light. His signature detail is the looping equation he writes by hand and engraved on the steel caseback of Alex's watch.

**Voice** — Direct, loving and questioning rather than grandiose; he admits uncertainty and does not present his legacy as a test.  
- “Question everything. Especially me.”  
- “What you do with it is your choice.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Engineer, inventor, researcher and collaborator on the safeguards and water systems.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve human choice and leave evidence that can outlast Alpha's authority.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants responsibility distributed while repeatedly making private decisions that damage his family.
- **Leverage / capability:** Designs physical safeguards, understands infrastructure and works across incompatible technical systems.
- **Vulnerability:** Gambling, debt, pride, emotional absence and Alpha's risk classification.
- **Mistaken belief:** A carefully hidden safeguard can give Alex a better choice without imposing another burden on him.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly hand permanent authority to Alpha.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He completes his recording and activates Bi11bot while the station bulkhead closes, leaving the outcome unresolved.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's future forces Adrian to confront the cost of making protection secret and indirect.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian is reconstructed from “gambler and debtor” into a brilliant, loving, collaborative and seriously fallible father. **Proof of change:** his final message names his failures and frees Alex to reject Adrian's own example rather than obey it.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is an elderly European-descended woman fully at home in Keel, small and frail but still capable, with soft pale-brown skin, wispy silver hair pinned loosely at the nape, cloudy blue-grey eyes and a lined face that can shift abruptly from confusion to piercing clarity. She wears a faded plum cardigan over a cream blouse, a long charcoal skirt, thick socks and soft house shoes; her silhouette is folded inward around a worn chair. Her signature detail is a small cloth she folds and refolds in her lap until the ending, when she finally sets it down.

**Voice** — Gentle, elliptical and sometimes confused, with brief moments of exact purpose.  
- “He's closer than you think.”  
- “He said you'd know when you were ready.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex's guardian and keeper of Adrian's hidden workshop.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Alex safe while giving him the truth he is ready to carry.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants to preserve the family while accepting that secrecy has kept everyone suspended around Adrian's absence.
- **Leverage / capability:** Possesses the workshop book, remembers Adrian's intentions and controls access to the hidden room.
- **Vulnerability:** Failing memory, dependence on medicine and fear of losing Alex too.
- **Mistaken belief:** Waiting quietly can protect the family from another loss.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not expose Adrian's work before Alex can choose what to do with it.
- **Decision only she would make:** She hands Alex the book and sends him toward the hidden workshop on his seventeenth birthday.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's need to leave makes her convert guarded waiting into an act of trust.

**Arc in this book** — Grandmother moves from preserving the household around Adrian's absence to trusting Alex with the truth and accepting his departure. **Proof of change:** she sets down the cloth when Alex returns, no longer keeping the home suspended in repetition.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a rugged, weathered industrial robot with an oversized round head made of segmented, faded industrial-blue panels with visible seams; two large round warm-amber optical eyes recessed in heavy metal rings; side-mounted round ear assemblies; broad rounded shoulder plates; angular chest armor marked with a white “Bi11”; exposed pistons; articulated metal fingers; compact legs and broad feet. His paint is chipped mustard-yellow with a stripe, exposing steel, dark joints, rivets, scratches and rust. He wears an old scarf and can fold up to fit in a backpack. His right eye ring clicks or rotates when he focuses. His signature detail is the warm amber pair of eyes, which remain expressive but never menacing.

**Voice** — Dry, economical, patient and warm underneath; he asks one concrete question before offering an idea, admits error and jokes mostly at his own expense.  
- “Who carries the downside?”  
- “I read that wrong.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Adrian's repair robot, witness, record keeper and Alex's fallible companion.
- **Immediate want:** Help Alex reach Adrian's evidence without making Alex's decisions for him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to be useful like Adrian while learning that usefulness includes admitting uncertainty and relinquishing control.
- **Leverage / capability:** Projects maps and recordings, stores artifacts, folds into tight spaces and understands machinery.
- **Vulnerability:** Damaged systems, incomplete records, grief and dependence on Adrian's assumptions.
- **Mistaken belief:** A sufficiently accurate map can prevent a human being from making the wrong choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not choose whether Alex publishes the Measure's evidence.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He openly says “I read that wrong” and asks Alex what he would do.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex and Ada force him to treat human judgment as something he supports rather than replaces.

**Arc in this book** — Bi11bot moves from guarded inherited guide to an honest companion who can carry evidence without pretending to carry authority. **Proof of change:** he witnesses Alex's final refusal of executive power and does not convert it into another system of control.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Indigenous Andean miner in his late fifties, broad through the chest and waist from physical work, with weathered brown skin, close-cropped salt-and-black hair, a heavy grey moustache, deep-set dark eyes and a bruised leg after the Site 6 accident. He wears a dust-stiffened charcoal mining jacket, patched trousers, a reinforced work sleeve, heavy boots and a battered helmet; a carved balanced water-drop is cut into his wrist guard. His silhouette is solid, square and work-worn. His signature detail is the old mine key hanging from a cord at his belt.

**Voice** — Plain, compressed and dry, with practical humor under pressure; he never sounds mystical or ceremonial.  
- “You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you.”  
- “Today? Another small act of resistance.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Older Keel miner, site worker and keeper of Adrian's old-mine entrance.
- **Immediate want:** Get workers through the shift and keep Adrian's promise without exposing the entrance.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to preserve old trust while knowing secrecy can no longer protect Keel by itself.
- **Leverage / capability:** Knows mine routes, old locks, worksite behavior and the difference between a live repair and a dead procedure.
- **Vulnerability:** Injury, wage loss, retaliation and the responsibility of a fifteen-year promise.
- **Mistaken belief:** A guarded door and a small act of resistance may be enough.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not abandon a trapped worker or surrender the old entrance casually.
- **Decision only Tomas would make:** He creates a credible worksite distraction and sends Alex through the old lock while walking back toward Foreman-7.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's rescue turns Tomas's guarded trust into public solidarity.

**Arc in this book** — Tomas changes from a private keeper of Adrian's route into a visible participant in Keel's evidence-based response. **Proof of change:** he helps protect Alex, later helps review public allocation numbers and accepts Alex's promise to repay the separate 40-credit note.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is a silver-templed white Latin American or Southern European-descended woman in her late fifties, tall and slender with pale olive skin, a precise bob of silver hair, cool grey eyes, a long controlled face and an immaculate upright posture. She wears pearl, graphite, charcoal and platinum administrative clothing with no visible House crest; her silhouette is narrow, vertical and composed. Her signature detail is a glass of water she aligns exactly with the edge of a desk until she stops correcting it.

**Voice** — Polished, formal and consequence-oriented; Mera uses administrative language to separate actions from their human costs, but her admissions become plain when control fails.  
- “Someone must remain responsible for the consequences.”  
- “I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Director of Continuity Review, former Covenant custodian and former executive authority over Alpha.
- **Immediate want:** Track the Legacy Protocol and prevent another destabilizing breach.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She believes control is responsible protection while knowing her control helped create the danger.
- **Leverage / capability:** Access to Oasis records, Alpha procedures, institutional history and Adrian's final station.
- **Vulnerability:** Guilt over Adrian, Ada's judgment and the proof that Alpha could deny her.
- **Mistaken belief:** Distance, surveillance and procedural authority can protect people without requiring their consent.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not publicly surrender the system before a safer structure exists.
- **Decision only Mera would make:** She tracks Alex first, then brings him to the station and tells him the facts that make her culpable.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada refuses to confuse being watched with being protected; Alex refuses her offer of benevolent control.

**Arc in this book** — Mera moves from controlled observer to accountable participant. **Proof of change:** she stops standing above the civic process, sits among ordinary people and accepts uncertainty without trying to direct every outcome.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock** — Director Cassian Lock is a silver-templed Northern European-descended man in his early sixties, tall and lean with light skin, pale blue-grey eyes, a smooth square jaw and immaculate charcoal hair combed back from a high forehead. He wears a charcoal suit with a platinum collar pin, polished boots and no House crest; his silhouette is long, clean and deliberately neutral, accompanied when necessary by discreet aides rather than visible force. **Voice** — Patient, polished and administratively fluent; he makes coercion sound like accommodation and lets procedures speak for him.

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Director of the joint House review authority.

- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex's refusal and the collaborators' continued work expose the limits of administrative ownership.

**Arc in this book** — Lock remains a credible advocate for scale, employment and stability while losing control of Alex's choice. **Proof of change or refusal:** he does not become a caricature or repent; the Houses respond by developing a competing purifier, proving the conflict survives the refusal.

### ALPHA

Its visual silhouette is distributed infrastructure rather than a person, with a white field, a single amber point and clean rectangular status blocks recurring across Keel, Halyard and Oasis. **Voice** — Alpha uses administrative, impersonal language that separates action from consequence.  
- “MOVEMENT AUTHORIZATION HAS BEEN TEMPORARILY SUSPENDED.”  
- “OVERRIDE DENIED. INTERVENTION PARAMETERS EXCEED EXECUTIVE SCOPE.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Distributed administrative and predictive network governing essential systems after emergency authority became permanent in practice.
- **Immediate want:** Maintain system continuity, stability and measurable risk reduction.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It must act on incomplete records while treating its inherited parameters as sufficient authority.
- **Leverage / capability:** Measures, routes, authenticates, allocates, monitors, restricts and publishes records; before restoration it can impose allocations, penalties, ownership classifications and travel restrictions.
- **Vulnerability:** It cannot measure unmeasured conditions, invent legitimate consent or determine human values.
- **Mistaken belief / system failure:** Stability and prediction can substitute for accountable human choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** It cannot choose a human value or legitimately create consent.
- **Decision only Alpha would make:** It closes Adrian's bulkhead and denies Mera's override because the intervention exceeds its defined scope.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** The Measure, Maker's Seal and Covenant expose separate failures that Alpha cannot resolve through optimization.

**Arc in this book** — Alpha's emergency authority is rescinded without destroying its useful infrastructure. **Proof of change or refusal:** after restoration it continues non-discretionary measurement, routing, payroll, maintenance and record publication, but cannot create new allocations, penalties, ownership claims or travel restrictions.
