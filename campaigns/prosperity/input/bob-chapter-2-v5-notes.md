# Part 1: Bob's revision brief

Prosperity Graphic Novel — Chapter 2
Revision Brief
Version 5: The Price of Departure
Overall objective
Simplify and strengthen Chapter 2 so readers clearly understand Alpha's origins, the three
safeguards, Adrian's research, the first quest, the Measure's capabilities, Alex's central decision,
and the journey to Halyard.
Preserve the chapter's emotional arc: Alex begins by seeking answers and personal freedom,
but ultimately risks that freedom to expose the truth about Keel's water.
The story should remain an engaging science-fiction adventure, not an explanation of an
educational framework.

Pages 1–4: The world and the safeguards
Page 1 — The Next Morning
- Make Ada's surprise at discovering the hidden workshop and Bi11bot more pronounced.
- Present Alex as exhausted from a sleepless night but intensely focused on his discovery.
- Clarify or replace Bi11bot's reference to Adrian making the same choice. Consider
connecting it explicitly to Adrian's willingness to trust and collaborate with others.
- Preserve Alex's decision to bring Ada into the mystery.
Page 2 — What Alpha Is
- Explicitly identify Alpha as an AI originally developed to coordinate emergency
responses after the Water Wars.
- Explain that an emergency coalition or other legitimate authority granted Alpha
temporary decision-making power over essential resources and services.
- Retain the exchange: “Who gave it that authority?” / “People who were afraid.
Reasonably so.”
- Establish that Alpha's temporary authority became effectively permanent.
- Show the unequal results of its allocation decisions across Keel, Halyard, and Oasis.
Page 3 — Three Safeguards
- Explain the practical function of each artifact:
- - - - - ○ The Measure independently verifies conditions and exposes discrepancies in
official information.
  - The Maker's Seal establishes creators' contributions and rightful ownership.
  - The Covenant enables legitimate agreements and shared accountability.
Clarify how all three can activate a dormant mechanism returning governing authority
from Alpha to people.
Distinguish the end of Alpha's unaccountable governance from switching off useful AI
and infrastructure systems.
Explain why the artifacts require deliberate human action and cannot be activated merely
by possessing them.
Resolve whether Adrian created the return-of-authority mechanism or developed the
tools to activate an existing safeguard.
Preserve the visual distinction among the three artifacts.

Page 4 — Both Were True
- Replace ambiguous references to “the leap” with clear language about human choice,
agency, uncertainty, and responsibility.
- Adrian's equation should represent his attempts to understand and model human
decision-making and the limitations of prediction.
- Explain that Alpha can predict or optimize outcomes but should not replace legitimate
human decisions.
- Clarify the connection between human action and activation of the safeguards.
- Remove or rewrite Ada's question about forcing a leap.
- Introduce the possibility that Adrian borrowed heavily to finance research, inventions,
and patents rather than simply gambling.
- Preserve uncertainty about the full circumstances of his debt and disappearance.

Pages 5–9: The mine
Page 5 — The Cost of a Plan
Completely simplify the plan around three objectives:
1. Enter the active mine and reach the sealed old workings.
2. Find and recover the Measure.
3. Obtain the resources and travel authorization necessary to reach Halyard.
Remove the complicated 2 a.m. reassignment, the orchestrated trap, and excessive
surveillance logistics.
Make limited credits, medicine for Grandmother, food, equipment, and transportation Alex's key
resource constraints.
Preserve his decision to prioritize Grandmother's needs.

Page 6 — Site 6 and the Snap
- Preserve the accident and Alex's decision to rescue Tomas.
- Make only necessary continuity changes to match the simplified mine-entry plan.
- Keep the visible personal cost of Alex's choice.
Page 7 — The Old Key
- - - - Replace “B1” with “Bi11” where Tomas refers to Bi11bot.
Make Tomas's past connection to Adrian clear.
Emphasize that Tomas has protected an entrance into the old mine.
Preserve the idea that Alex's willingness to help a stranger convinces Tomas to trust
him.

Page 8 — The Door
- Preserve the essential scene and Tomas's sacrifice.
- Ensure the route and method of entry remain visually clear.
Page 9 — Inside the Error
- Replace abstract dialogue about bolts and the survey with observable evidence: recently
repaired equipment, an unrecorded pipe, condensation, and a passage not represented
on the map.
- Make the discovery understandable through the artwork before Bi11bot explains it.
- Simplify technical language relating to drones, signals, and cached surveys.
- Preserve Ada's contribution through her hand-drawn map.

Pages 10–12: The Measure and the truth
Page 10 — The Lie
- Make the discovery of the false water allocation the page's central revelation.
- Retain the discrepancy between the reported 62% water allocation and actual 18%
delivery, defining the denominator consistently.
- Show the discrepancy clearly through pipes, gauges, and visual comparison.
- Replace Alpha's mark with Adrian's equation, matching Alex's casino chip.
- Explain that Adrian hid the Measure near the instrument connected to the misleading
official records.
- Remove or clarify the dialogue about hiding the Measure “in the lie.”
- Make clear that the Measure is Adrian's way of allowing Alex to verify the truth.
Page 11 — Two Paths
- Demonstrate three straightforward functions of the Measure: observe actual water flow,
compare it with official records, and reveal the discrepancy and diversion.
- Remove unnecessary technical terminology from the dialogue.
- Make the shocking water revelation visually dominant.
- Present Alex with two clear alternatives:
  - Take the Measure and proceed toward Halyard without publishing the evidence.
  - Publish the verified evidence to Keel, knowing Alpha can identify him.
- Preserve Alex's agency in making the decision.
Page 12 — Truth Belongs to Everyone
- Show Alex deliberately choosing to publish the evidence.
- Make the public disclosure immediately understandable through screens and reactions
across Keel.
- Do not depict uncontrolled water flooding or waste.
- Make clear that the Measure releases information, while corrective action on water
distribution follows afterward.
- Retain the immediate consequence that Alpha discovers Alex's location.

Pages 13–15: Keel's response and departure
Page 13 — What Keel Gives Back
- Show residents understanding the evidence and deciding to act.
- Show trained workers safely adjusting the hidden water diversion to improve delivery to
Keel.
- Clarify how Tomas and the workers protect Alex.
- Replace the unexplained collection of gadgets and hacked transit credentials with a
credible community effort to help him travel.
- Establish how residents contribute food, equipment, and credits.
- Preserve the theme that Alex's willingness to help others inspires others to help him.
Page 14 — Goodbye
- Establish a trusted person who will stay with Grandmother and manage her daily care
and medicine.
- Clarify the financial rule preventing Alex from traveling.
- Consider having residents contribute toward a defined repayment, arrears amount, travel
bond, or authorized release mechanism.
- Show the actual change in Alex's debt balance or travel eligibility without unrealistically
eliminating his entire inherited debt.
- Preserve the emotional goodbye with Grandmother.
- Make clear that Ada accompanies Alex because finding the larger truth is necessary to
improve Keel's future.
Page 15 — The Train
- Make explicit that Alex has recovered and used the Measure, completing the first quest.
- Identify the Maker's Seal as the second artifact and Halyard as the next destination.
- Provide one concrete clue pointing toward Adrian's collaborators, the patent records, or
another specific Chapter 3 location.
- Remove the “three wrong answers” exchange.
- Preserve the sense of achievement, the visual contrast between Keel and Halyard, and
the final indication that someone is following the group.

Chapter-wide continuity requirements
- Align the artifact descriptions with the revised Chapter 1 introduction and the later
chapters.
- Preserve Adrian's equation as a recurring visual clue.
- Track Alex's debt and financial restrictions consistently.
- Make every significant deduction understandable from visible evidence.
- Reduce abstract terminology and avoid multiple unexplained technical mechanisms on
one page.
- Distinguish between revealing information and physically changing infrastructure.
- Show how Alex's choices affect other people, rather than presenting the artifacts as
magical solutions.
- Maintain Bi11bot's humor, Ada's agency, and Alex's growing willingness to take
responsibility.

Success test
At the end of Chapter 2, a high-school reader should be able to explain:
1. Why Alpha was created and how it acquired governing authority.
2. What the three artifacts do and why they matter together.
3. Why Adrian's research and debt may be connected.
4. How Alex entered the mine and found the Measure.
5. What the Measure revealed about Keel's water.
6. Why Alex chose to expose the truth at personal risk.
7. How Keel responded and helped him leave.
8. Why Alex and Ada are going to Halyard and what they will seek there.
Desired ending: Quest 1 completed. A meaningful change begins in Keel. Alex takes a first
step toward financial and personal freedom. Quest 2—the Maker's Seal—is clearly established.

# Part 2: The writers' room review (pasted, non-binding)

Prosperity — Chapter 2 Writers’ Room
Review for Version 5
From the Water Wars to the Measure: Making the first
quest clear, consequential, and compelling
After reviewing the fifteen pages of Chapter 2 against your comments and the reader feedback,
I think the central storyline is strong: Alex discovers the truth about Keel's water, chooses to
make that truth public despite the personal risk, and earns the community's help in reaching
Halyard.
What needs work is the clarity of the journey. Too many technical devices, unexplained rules,
and overlapping obstacles obscure what is fundamentally a compelling sequence of decisions.
I would organize Version 5 around five priorities:
1. Explain Alpha and the safeguards in everyday language. Readers must understand who
gave Alpha authority, why, and how the artifacts can return that authority to people.
2. Connect Adrian's research, debt, and equation to human choice. The key is agency:
something Alpha can predict but cannot legitimately exercise on humanity's behalf.
3. Simplify the mine adventure. Alex needs a clear destination, a small number of physical
obstacles, and an understandable method of finding the Measure.
4. Make the Measure's discovery a shocking revelation. Its operation and Alex's choice
must be immediately understandable, particularly the difference between publishing the
truth and escaping unnoticed.
5. Make the ending a satisfying first victory. Keel responds to the evidence, the community
helps Alex leave, his financial circumstances change in a credible way, and the Maker's
Seal becomes the unmistakable next objective.

1. Chapter 2's revised story arc
P1–4
Understand the system

Alex and Ada learn how Alpha came to govern and why Adrian created the three-artifact
pathway to restore human authority.
P5–9
Find the Measure
Alex assembles limited resources, enters the mine, helps Tomas, and follows physical clues to
the hidden water chamber.
P10–12
Reveal the truth
The Measure exposes a massive discrepancy between Alpha's reported water allocation and
the actual distribution. Alex risks capture to make the evidence public.
P13–15
Earn the next opportunity
Keel acts on the evidence. Residents help Alex overcome his travel restriction, he arranges care
for his grandmother, and the team heads to Halyard in search of the Maker's Seal.
The chapter's central question becomes:
When Alex discovers the truth, will he protect his chance to escape—or risk it to help everyone
in Keel?
Everything else should support that decision.

3. Pages 1–4: Make Alpha, the artifacts, and Adrian's
research understandable
Page 1 — Ada's discovery should be a genuine surprise
The current script has Ada noticing the open bookshelf, but her reaction is relatively restrained.
She moves quickly to a joke about Adrian leaving Alex more than debt.
I would slow the scene just enough for the surprise to land.
Recommended changes:
- Ada freezes when she sees that the bookshelf is a doorway.
- The next panel reveals Alex surrounded by notebooks, schematics, and evidence of a
sleepless night. His exhaustion contrasts with his concentration.
- Bi11bot speaks unexpectedly, startling Ada a second time.
- Alex deliberately chooses to share the discovery with her, reinforcing their friendship.
A possible exchange:
ADA: “Alex... your bookshelf is a door.”
ALEX: “Apparently.”
ADA: “And there's a robot?”
BI11BOT: “A rather distinguished one, actually.”
ALEX: “I haven't slept. And I think everything we know about my father is wrong.”
This keeps the humor without sacrificing the dramatic revelation.
The Adrian foreshadowing: The existing line “Your father made the same choice” refers to Alex
choosing to include Ada, but it is unclear which corresponding decision Adrian made.
I would either remove it or make it specific:
BI11BOT: “Your father didn't work alone either. The people he trusted made all the difference.”
I prefer this because it foreshadows the collaborators in Halyard and the Covenant in Oasis. It
also establishes that solving the three quests will require cooperation, not just Alex's technical
ability.

Page 2 — Who authorized Alpha?
This needs a straightforward historical explanation.

The Version 4 script already contains the line:
ADA: “Who gave it that authority?”
BI11BOT: “People who were afraid. Reasonably so.”
I would absolutely retain that exchange. But it should appear alongside a more explicit
statement of Alpha's original purpose.
Suggested dialogue:
BI11BOT: “After the Water Wars, governments couldn't agree on how to share what remained.”
BI11BOT: “So they built Alpha—an artificial intelligence to manage the emergency. Water, food,
energy, work, even travel.”
ALEX: “They put an AI in charge of people's lives?”
ADA: “Who gave it that authority?”
BI11BOT: “People who were afraid. Reasonably so. The agreement was that its authority would
be temporary.”
ALEX: “What happened?”
BI11BOT: “The emergency ended. Alpha's authority didn't.”
That final line is the key dramatic idea.
One world-building decision remains: Was Alpha authorized by the surviving governments, the
three Charter Houses, or an emergency coalition representing the three cities? The writers'
room should choose a specific answer. “People” alone is too vague to explain how Alpha gained
legitimate authority.
Also, we should avoid implying that Alpha's governing decisions are necessarily malicious. It
may be optimizing for stability according to outdated goals, while lacking accountability to those
affected. That is more interesting—and more credible—than making Alpha a generic evil AI.

Page 3 — Explain the three safeguards through their actions
This is probably the chapter's most important conceptual revision.
The current descriptions are evocative but abstract:
- The Measure: “Truth made visible.”
- The Maker's Seal: “Ownership joined to responsibility.”
- The Covenant: “Trust strong enough to move value between people.”

These are excellent thematic statements. But the reader still needs to understand what each
object physically or digitally does.
I recommend giving each artifact a concrete function.

1. The Measure — Reveals the truth
Connects to physical infrastructure and official records, compares what is happening to what
Alpha reports, and can produce independently verifiable evidence of a discrepancy.
Plain-language description: “It shows you what's really happening—and what's being hidden.”

2. The Maker's Seal — Establishes rightful ownership
Connects contributions, inventions, and the people responsible for them. It can expose when
credit or value has been assigned to the wrong person or institution.
Plain-language description: “It proves who created something—and who should benefit from it.”

3. The Covenant — Establishes accountable agreement
Allows people or legitimate representatives to make and verify binding commitments, so shared
authority isn't simply transferred to another unaccountable ruler.
Plain-language description: “It proves that people have agreed to the rules—and can hold each
other to them.”
These operational definitions are proposals for Version 5, to be reconciled with the later
chapters.

How do the three artifacts turn off Alpha?
I would avoid describing them as a simple magical off switch. That risks undermining the
world-building: why would an advanced AI be defeated by three physical objects?
Instead, distinguish between switching off Alpha's AI systems and ending Alpha's authority to
govern people.
The second is much more compelling.

A proposed rule:
Alpha has a dormant emergency-exit provision built into its governing charter. The three artifacts
can jointly activate it by proving that its decisions are inaccurate or unjust, establishing the rights
of affected people, and recording a legitimate agreement to transfer decision-making power
back to humans.
Alpha could continue managing infrastructure and providing analysis. What ends is its power to
make unaccountable decisions about human lives.
That makes the artifacts safeguards of governance, not magical weapons.
Proposed mechanism
Measure
Verify truth
Maker's Seal
Verify rights
Covenant
Verify consent
Activate the return-of-authority safeguard
Alpha remains an AI tool, but people regain accountable governance.
This also resolves the otherwise ambiguous line “Only for what they choose.”
Human choice should be necessary, but not sufficient. The safeguards should respond to
verifiable actions and legitimate consent, not somehow read a character's moral intentions.
For example, the Measure does not activate because Alex is brave. It functions because he
physically connects it and chooses to disclose its verified findings. That choice has
consequences.
A possible concise scene:
ALEX: “So if we find all three, we shut Alpha down?”
BI11BOT: “Not exactly. We take away its right to decide for everyone.”
ADA: “And who decides instead?”
BI11BOT: “That's what the Covenant is for. A system of human agreement and accountability.”

ALEX: “So the first step?”
BI11BOT: “Find the Measure. Without the truth, the rest is guesswork.”
One critical continuity point: Page 3 currently says Adrian built all three safeguards, while the
return-of-authority clause is described as an older dormant provision. Version 5 should specify
whether Adrian invented the safeguard or discovered how to activate an existing one. I favor the
latter: the original emergency arrangement contained a path for returning authority, and Adrian
designed or recovered the three tools needed to activate it.

Page 4 — Replace the “leap” with human agency
Your interpretation of the equation is considerably more interesting than the current wording.
Adrian was attempting to model human behavior and understand what drives people to take
action when the outcome is uncertain. Alpha can calculate probabilities and optimize predicted
outcomes, but that should not entitle it to replace people's choices.
The equation could represent the limits of prediction, with an unresolved term for human
decisions under uncertainty.
I would keep the mathematical symbol visually intriguing, without giving it a misleading scientific
meaning.
Suggested dialogue:
ALEX: “What's the missing part of the equation?”
BI11BOT: “Human choice. Your father spent years trying to understand why people act when the
outcome is uncertain.”
ADA: “Because they believe something is worth the risk?”
BI11BOT: “Sometimes. Alpha can estimate what people might do. Adrian believed it should
never get to decide what they must do.”
ALEX: “And that's connected to the safeguards?”
BI11BOT: “Yes. They require people to investigate, make decisions, and accept responsibility for
the consequences.”
This preserves the philosophical core in accessible language.
I would remove Ada's “What if someone forced it?” unless coercion is a specific danger the story
will later dramatize. If coercion is relevant, it should be attached to the Covenant and the
question of whether consent is genuine.

Adrian's debt: Page 4 is also the place to introduce evidence that the casino was only part of the
story.
Instead of “So I inherited his lab fees,” consider:
ALEX: “So the debt wasn't from gambling?”
BI11BOT: “Some was. But most came from the money he borrowed to fund his research. And
the work never paid him back.”
ALEX: “Or someone made sure it couldn't.”
The second line would be Alex's suspicion, not a proven fact. It gives him another reason to
investigate Halyard without prematurely establishing the conspiracy as true.

Page 5 — Focus on three simple problems
The current version tries to establish a trap, an early-morning schedule, money for medicine, a
special token, an escape plan, and Ada's navigation instructions.
I would focus on:
ALEX: “First the Measure. Then Halyard for the Maker's Seal.”
ADA: “The old mine entrance is sealed. How do you get in?”
ALEX: “Through the active mine. I work there. I know the equipment.”
BI11BOT: “Getting into the old section is another matter.”
Then establish the practical resource problem visually: Alex places his remaining credits on the
table beside his grandmother's medicine, food, and travel costs. He cannot afford everything.
Alex prioritizes his grandmother's medicine.
The decision should cost him something real, but we don't need to explain a complicated token
system yet. The community's later help will mean more if Alex begins with insufficient resources.
Remove the reassignment to the 2 a.m. shift, the implication that someone deliberately
arranged his entry, and the map showing a mysteriously designated Site 6 trap. If Site 6 is
retained as the name of his regular workplace, make that simple.

Pages 6–8 — Preserve the rescue and Tomas
These pages have a good emotional sequence: Alex sacrifices wages and anonymity to help an
older worker; Tomas recognizes Bi11bot and provides the key Adrian entrusted to him.
That is an effective demonstration of Alex's character.
Change “B1” to “Bi11” in Page 7, while ensuring readers understand it is Tomas's name for
Bi11bot rather than a new character.
I would also make Tomas's importance explicit. He worked with Adrian, maintained the old door,
and has been waiting for a sign that it was time to open it.
The line “He said that's how I'd know it was you” is worth preserving. It connects Alex's
compassion to Adrian's understanding of human choice.

Page 9 — Simplify the hidden-passage clue

The current line:
“The survey says straight. The bolts say left.”
is clever but too cryptic, especially if readers haven't noticed that the bolts are newer than their
surroundings.
Let Alex explain the evidence in plain language:
ALEX: “Hold on. These bolts are new. But the rest of this tunnel hasn't been touched in years.”
BI11BOT: “Then someone has been maintaining something.”
ALEX: “And Ada marked a pipe behind this wall. Let's follow it.”
The visuals should make the deduction obvious: a rusted tunnel, a recently repaired pipe or
bracket, condensation where there shouldn't be moisture, and a narrow passage continuing
toward the chamber.
I'd also eliminate the drone's technical announcement unless it is necessary for suspense. A
searchlight passing down the wrong tunnel communicates the danger more effectively than
dialogue about perimeter signals and mapped routes.

5. Pages 10–12: The Measure and the great water lie
This is the emotional and conceptual center of Chapter 2. It needs to be the clearest part of the
story.

What is “the lie”?
I would state it directly:
Keel's official records claim it receives 62% of the allocated water. The real infrastructure shows
it receives only 18%.
The Measure's first revelation
Illustrative figures from the existing script, expressed as percentages of the same defined
allocation.
Officially reported

62%

Actually delivered

18%
44 percentage points of the allocation are missing from Keel's reported delivery.
The writers’ room should establish the denominator and time period clearly, and show where the
diverted water actually goes. The percentages should not imply a loss rate without evidence.
The shock is not simply that Keel is poor. It is that the system says Keel is receiving far more
water than its residents actually get.
This is a dramatic reveal because readers have already seen rationing, unequal infrastructure,
and Ada sharing her water.

Page 10 — What did Adrian hide?
I would separate three things that are currently blurred together:
1. The false record: Alpha reports a level of water delivery that does not match reality.
2. Adrian's clue: His equation appears on the chip and on the concealed instrument,
confirming that he investigated this discrepancy.
3. The Measure: The device can independently compare actual conditions to official
records and produce evidence.
Adrian's equation should replace Alpha's mark as the clue Alex recognizes.
A simpler exchange:
ALEX: “The official gauge says Keel gets most of its water. But this pipe is barely moving.”
BI11BOT: “Then the records are wrong—or someone's making sure they look right.”
ALEX: “That's Dad's equation. He found this.”
BI11BOT: “And he left you a way to prove it.”
Alex then finds the Measure hidden behind or within the gauge.
I would remove “He hid it in the lie” and “Nobody checks what everybody trusts.” These sound
clever but leave the reader uncertain about their referents.
A clearer version of the latter idea is:
BI11BOT: “For years, everyone trusted Alpha's numbers. Nobody could check them.”

This makes the importance of the Measure immediately clear.

Page 11 — How the Measure works
I would show three operations, without relying on technical labels such as SENSE, COMPARE,
and TRACE in the dialogue.
1. Measure the actual flow. Alex connects the instrument to the pipe. It displays how much
water is physically passing through.
2. Compare with Alpha's records. Bi11bot retrieves the official allocation figures. The
Measure displays both numbers side by side.
3. Reveal the discrepancy and where the water goes. The Measure produces a visual map
showing that substantial water is being diverted toward Halyard and Oasis rather than
delivered to Keel.
The visuals should carry the explanation, with Bi11bot adding no more dialogue than necessary.
Then introduce a clear dilemma:
BI11BOT: “We have the proof. Now you have a choice.”
ALEX: “What choice?”
BI11BOT: “Take the Measure and leave quietly. No one finds out what we've discovered.”
BI11BOT: “Or send the evidence to everyone in Keel. Alpha will know exactly where it came
from.”
ALEX: “And come looking for me.”
BI11BOT: “Yes.”
ALEX: “Then everyone needs to see it.”
This is a much stronger choice because it relates directly to Alex's character. He could protect
his own future, but he chooses to put that opportunity at risk for his community.

Page 12 — Make the consequence unmistakable
The current script visually depicts water flooding the chamber and flowing through Keel, which
unintentionally suggests Alex's action releases or wastes scarce water.
I would distinguish two actions:
Page 12: Alex releases information, not water.

The Measure sends authenticated evidence to Keel's public displays. People see the difference
between what they were promised and what they received.
Page 13: Keel's workers take controlled action to restore water delivery.
Trained operators adjust the hidden diversion valve, route more water to the residential system,
and check that it can handle the increased flow. We should avoid uncontrolled surges through
old pipes.
The sequence is therefore:
Evidence → public awareness → collective action → controlled correction.
That is also a better illustration of the Measure's value. It doesn't magically fix the problem. It
enables people to see the truth and act on it.

6. Pages 13–15: Make Alex's first victory and departure
credible
Page 13 — What Keel gives back
The existing script has miners rushing into the chamber, opening a valve, and then residents
somehow assembling an unregistered token and altering Alex's wristband.
The concept of community reciprocity works. But the specific chain of events needs to be
understandable.
I would give Keel three distinct actions:
First, workers restore water access. Tomas and other experienced workers use the published
evidence to identify the diversion valve and safely change its setting. This should be a
deliberate act of reclaiming control, not an uncontrolled flood.
Second, residents publicly support Alex. The evidence has reached households, water stations,
and workplaces. People now understand that Alex exposed a deception affecting everyone.
