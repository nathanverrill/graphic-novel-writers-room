# Prosperity High Level Notes

# **Prosperity — Chapter 1 Revision Brief (Version 5\)**

**Editorial objective:** Preserve the strong characters, visual storytelling, and emotional mystery of Chapter 1 while making the world of Prosperity more understandable and Alex's first quest unmistakably clear. Introduce water scarcity and the legacy of the Water Wars through lived experience rather than heavy exposition.

## **Page-by-page revisions**

**Page 1 — The World Alex Inherits**

Retain the full-page illustration and water infrastructure. Remove the “gravity of inequality” narration. Introduce the three agreed questions:

- Keel: “Where does all our water go?”
- Halyard: “Who really owns what we create?”
- Oasis: “What if everyone you've trusted has a secret?”

These foreshadow transparency, ownership, and trust without naming the artifacts.

**Page 2 — Debt Transfer Day**

In Panel 3, delete Alex's two lines: “I've also got his debt” and “It did. Just not for him.” Retain the bouncer's dialogue and allow Alex's reaction to play visually. Preserve the remainder of the page.

**Page 3 — Two Different Dreams**

Integrate water scarcity into Ada's delivery of the mugs. Consider having Ada share water from her ration as a gesture of friendship. Introduce a subtle visual or conversational reference to the Water Wars. Preserve the disagreement between Alex, who wants to leave Keel, and Ada, who wants to improve it.

Consider replacing Ada's closing line, “Nobody stays either. Not really,” with: “That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.”

Keep the conversation natural, warm, and concise.

**Pages 4–6**

Retain without substantive changes.

**Page 7 — What He Left**

Retain the silent discovery of the equation connecting Adrian's chalkboard and Alex's casino chip. Do not explain it yet. Define its meaning and plan a meaningful reveal later in the story.

**Page 8 — The Awakening**

Retain without substantive changes.

**Page 9 — The Robot Who Bet**

Consider replacing Bi11bot's “I'm not everyone. I was in the room” with: “Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes.” Preserve Bi11bot's dry humor and his intimate knowledge of Adrian.

**Page 10 — The Message**

Preserve the emotional reveal of Adrian and Alex's reaction. Refine Adrian's message to emphasize questioning accepted truths, perseverance, learning from failure, and allowing Alex to choose his own path. Retain the principle “Question everything. Especially me.” Consider a brief acknowledgment of the harm and uncertainty caused by Adrian's absence. Avoid lengthy exposition.

**Page 11 — The First Waypoint**

Completely rewrite the page to establish:

1. Adrian's trail involves three artifacts.
2. The first artifact is called the Measure.
3. The Measure is hidden in the sealed mine beneath Keel.
4. Alex cannot yet leave Keel, but he can attempt this first mission.
5. Alex chooses to begin because the trail may reveal the truth about his father.

Show three clearly distinguishable locations on the holographic map. Use concise dialogue and visual storytelling. Do not reveal what the Measure does or the ultimate purpose of all three artifacts.

**Page 12 — Track**

Preserve the surveillance reveal in Oasis. Ensure that the “Legacy Protocol” alert connects to terminology introduced earlier, or simplify the screen language. The activation of the map should be a clear consequence of Alex's first decision.

## **Chapter-wide requirements**

- Make water a visible narrative thread, especially its unequal availability across Keel, Halyard, and Oasis.
- Establish just enough historical context about the Water Wars for readers to understand the present world.
- Differentiate the three cities through concrete details of daily life, resource access, opportunities, and authority.
- Keep the distinction between purposeful mystery and unintentional confusion.
- Ensure Alex makes a clear, voluntary decision to begin the first quest.
- Preserve the emotional arc involving Alex, Ada, his grandmother, Adrian, and Bi11bot.
- Avoid introducing excessive technical terminology before it becomes relevant.

**Success test:** By the end of Chapter 1, a high-school reader should understand what makes life difficult in Keel, why water matters, what Alex has inherited, what he hopes to discover, where his first quest begins, and why someone in Oasis might be interested in his actions. The deeper meaning of the artifacts and Adrian's disappearance should remain mysterious.

**Detailed Feedback**

1. Make water the thread connecting the opening twelve pages. We should experience its scarcity in Keel, glimpse how access differs across cities, and learn just enough about the Water Wars to appreciate their consequences.
2. Give Adrian a clearer philosophy. His legacy should be about questioning accepted truths, learning from failure, and taking responsibility for what we create—not simply handing Alex a scavenger hunt.
3. Rebuild Pages 10–12 as a clear handoff into the larger adventure. By the end of Chapter 1, readers should know that Alex must find three artifacts, that the first is the Measure, and that it is hidden in Keel’s sealed mine. They need not yet know why the artifacts matter or what they will reveal.

The distinction is important: we want mystery about the answers, not confusion about the mission.

## **2\. Recommendations for the key pages**

### **Page 3 — Make water scarcity tangible**

The existing scene offers a natural opportunity: Ada brings Alex two metal mugs, and they joke about the price.

I would use the mugs to communicate something about life in Keel before introducing the Water Wars. The audience should first feel scarcity through an everyday interaction.

Suggested revision for Panels 2–3:

PANEL 2 — THE OFFER

ADA: Thought you might need this.

ALEX: The birthday special? Not sure I can afford it.

PANEL 3 — NOT EVERYTHING

ALEX: Where'd you get enough water for two?

ADA: Saved some of my ration.

ALEX: Ada, you didn't have to.

ADA: Not everything has a price, Alex.

This does more work than the current exchange. It tells readers water is rationed, reveals Ada's generosity, reinforces the difference between her worldview and Alex's, and gives emotional weight to the story's central resource.

Regarding the Water Wars: I would add only one passing reference, preferably linked to visible evidence of the conflict—an abandoned pipeline, a memorial, or an old warning sign. For example:

ALEX: “People still act like the Water Wars never ended.”

ADA: “Maybe they didn't. Maybe we just stopped calling it a war.”

That exchange is intriguing, but it may be too much for this already busy page. An alternative would be to include a weathered WATER WARS MEMORIAL in the background and have Bi11bot explain the historical significance in Chapter 2\.

My preference: show the legacy of the Water Wars visually on Page 3, then give readers a concise explanation in Chapter 2, where the information becomes directly relevant to the Measure.

### **Does “Nobody stays either. Not really” work?**

I understand the intention, but I would change it.

The current exchange is:

ALEX: “Because nobody leaves.”

ADA: “Nobody stays either. Not really.”

It's poetic, but the distinction isn't immediately clear. A teenager may interpret Ada's response literally and wonder where everyone is going. The intended idea—that people remain physically in Keel but have given up trying to change their lives—is too important to leave entirely implicit.

I would test three alternatives:

| Option | Ada's revised line                                                    | Effect                                               |
| ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| A      | “Maybe. But most people stopped believing this place could change.”   | Clearest, but somewhat explanatory                   |
| B      | “Their bodies stay. Their dreams don't.”                              | Memorable and emotionally evocative                  |
| C      | “That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.” | Best connects to Ada's commitment to rebuilding Keel |

I prefer C. It preserves the disagreement between Alex and Ada while introducing an important tension: Alex wants the freedom to leave; Ada wants to create a reason to stay.

The revised closing could read:

ALEX: “Because nobody leaves.”

ADA: “That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.”

It is clearer, character-driven, and not overly moralizing.

### **Page 7 — When should we explain the equation?**

Not on Page 7\.

The matching equation on the casino chip and chalkboard is a satisfying visual discovery. Explaining it immediately would reduce the mystery.

I suggest a three-stage payoff:

1. Chapter 1, Page 7 — Recognition. Alex discovers the chip isn't merely a gambling token. The equation connects him to his father's work. No explanation.
2. Chapter 2, during the Measure discovery — First understanding. Alex recognizes part of the equation in the mine's water-system records, revealing that Adrian was investigating something measurable and potentially hidden.
3. Later chapters — Full meaning. The equation becomes an important link between the three artifacts or Adrian's larger discoveries, provided this is consistent with the established mythology.

The writers’ room needs to define what the equation actually represents before writing its eventual reveal. Is it a technical formula, a model of resource flows, an authentication mechanism, or some combination? That is currently a story-design question, not something we should improvise on Page 7\.

### **Page 9 — Replace “I was in the room”**

Bi11bot's line establishes proximity to Adrian but does not fully capitalize on his personality or their relationship.

The current dialogue:

ALEX: “Everyone in Keel claims they knew my father.”

BI11BOT: “I'm not everyone. I was in the room.”

Three alternatives:

- “Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes.” My preference. Establishes intimacy and foreshadows Adrian's beliefs about failure.
- “Did they spend fifteen years listening to him argue with himself?” More humorous and distinctly Bi11bot.
- “I knew the man who stayed awake fixing what everyone said couldn't be fixed.” Warmer, but risks idealizing Adrian too early.

I'd choose the first. It also sets up the philosophy we want to develop on Page 10\.

### **Page 10 — What was Adrian trying to teach Alex?**

This page carries considerable emotional weight, so I would resist turning Adrian's hologram into a lecture.

The current “Question everything. Especially me” is actually strong. It suggests that Adrian doesn't want blind loyalty—even to himself. That aligns well with the three artifacts: truth must be tested, ownership must be examined, and trust must be earned.

I would keep that principle, while adding one concise thought about failure.

A possible revision:

ADRIAN: “I got things wrong, Alex. More than once. But every failure taught me something.”

ADRIAN: “You don't have to finish what I started. You may find a better way.”

ADRIAN: “And whatever Bi11bot tells you—question everything. Especially me.”

This is a departure from the current “answers have to be earned” language, which can make Adrian seem unnecessarily withholding. We need readers to believe he is preparing Alex to think independently, not manipulating him into completing tests.

One further suggestion: Adrian should acknowledge the cost his disappearance imposed on Alex and his grandmother. Even one brief moment of regret would make him more complex and make Alex's anger more legitimate. He can be brilliant and loving while having made serious mistakes.

### **Page 11 — Rebuild the first quest**

This is the highest-priority rewrite in Chapter 1\.

In Version 4, we see three map nodes, but the first two remain unexplained. Alex knows the mine is sealed, yet neither he nor the reader knows what he is supposed to recover. The line about finding out whether Adrian was “brilliant or completely mad” provides tone but not an objective.

By the end of Page 11, readers should be able to answer four questions:

| Reader question                    | Required answer                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What is Alex supposed to do?       | Find three artifacts Adrian left behind or connected to his work.                                          |
| What is the first artifact?        | The Measure.                                                                                               |
| Where is it?                       | Inside the sealed mine beneath Keel.                                                                       |
| Why does Alex take the first step? | He believes the artifacts may help uncover what his father was working on—and what really happened to him. |

The purpose of the full three-artifact collection may remain mysterious. Readers need to know the objective, not the outcome.

I would also add one explanation of the sealed mine: perhaps it was closed after a Water Wars-related disaster or because its water infrastructure was declared unsafe. That would connect the setting, the scarcity, and the quest. The exact cause needs to be settled in the story bible rather than assumed.

A possible five-panel reconstruction:

PAGE 11 — THE FIRST WAYPOINT · PROPOSED STORY BEATS

P1 — Three locations

Bi11bot projects the mountain map with three distinct markers. The first lights up beneath Keel.

BI11BOT: “Your father left a trail. Three artifacts. Three places.”

P2 — The first objective

Close on the first marker, identified as THE MEASURE.

BI11BOT: “The first is called the Measure. It's somewhere inside the old mine.”

P3 — The obstacle

Alex recognizes the sealed entrance.

ALEX: “That mine's been sealed since before I was born.”

BI11BOT: “Your father knew.”

P4 — The reason

Alex looks back toward the workshop and Adrian's image.

ALEX: “And finding it tells me what happened to him?”

BI11BOT: “It tells us where he wanted you to begin. The rest, we'll have to discover.”

P5 — The choice

Alex touches the first marker.

ALEX: “Then we start here.”

This gives Alex agency. He is no longer simply checking whether a map works; he is choosing to investigate his father's disappearance. The pulse that travels to Oasis on Page 12 becomes the immediate consequence of that choice.

I would not yet explain precisely how the Measure works. That discovery belongs inside the mine, where Alex should have to use it or understand it to overcome a real obstacle.

### **Page 12 — The Legacy Protocol**

The visual sequence is excellent: Alex activates a signal, it travels through the shared infrastructure, and someone in Oasis quietly begins tracking him.

I would retain it almost entirely.

But “LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED” currently introduces unexplained terminology at the end of an already confusing quest setup.

Two options:

Option A — Keep the term, seed it earlier. Bi11bot briefly identifies Adrian's system as the Legacy Protocol on Page 11\. Then the alert on Page 12 gives readers the satisfying realization that someone else recognizes the signal.

Option B — Delay the term. Change the alert to “ADRIAN PHANTUM — LEGACY SIGNAL ACTIVE” or another more immediately intelligible warning, subject to whether the antagonist should know Adrian's identity.

I prefer Option A if Legacy Protocol has a significant later payoff. We needn't explain its mechanics, just establish that it is the name of the system Adrian prepared. If the name serves no important future story function, simplify it.

## **3\. Cross-cutting notes for the writers’ room**

The partner's feedback is important because it comes from someone who will eventually introduce the graphic novel to students. It highlights five areas to address before Version 5 is finalized.

| Priority | Issue                                                                             | Writers’ room action                                                                                                                   |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Critical | Readers cannot explain Alex's quest after Page 11\.                               | Rewrite Page 11 with three artifacts, the first destination, an obstacle, and Alex's motivation.                                       |
| High     | Water scarcity does not yet feel central to life in Keel.                         | Use Page 3 to show rationing and generosity; reinforce water access through environmental details throughout Chapter 1\.               |
| High     | The Water Wars are mentioned without adequate context.                            | Establish one visible legacy of the conflict in Chapter 1, then explain the essential history when it becomes necessary in Chapter 2\. |
| Medium   | The differences between the three cities are visually clear but socially unclear. | Make differences in water access, mobility, and power observable, rather than adding a long explanatory narration.                     |
| Medium   | Adrian's motivations risk feeling like an elaborate test of Alex.                 | Clarify that he is leaving Alex a path toward discovery, while encouraging him to question and improve upon his father's work.         |

### **Strengthen the financial stakes**

Make Alex's inherited debt of **418,607 credits** a recurring and consequential element of the graphic novel.

On Page 2, preserve the prominent debt-transfer display. Emphasize the debt balance, restricted travel status, and the apparent impossibility of repayment.

Establish a recurring visual debt indicator that can reappear at pivotal moments as Alex earns compensation, discovers inaccurate records, challenges unfair charges, or recovers value from his contributions.

Do not award automatic credits simply for finding artifacts. Debt changes must follow established financial rules and carry meaningful consequences.

### **Develop the mystery behind Adrian's debt**

The prevailing belief in Keel is that Adrian accumulated his debt through gambling.

Develop a competing explanation: Adrian may have borrowed to finance research, patents, prototypes, and innovations that never generated the income necessary to repay his loans.

Determine the identity of the creditor, the source and terms of the original financing, the role of interest and penalties, and why the liability passed to Alex.

Explore whether intellectual property, diverted revenue, or unfair lending practices contributed to the outstanding balance.

The truth should emerge progressively through evidence discovered during the three-artifact quest. Avoid revealing the answer in Chapter 1\.

### **Connect Chapter 1 to Mission 1: Follow the Flow**

**Superpower:** Systems Thinker  
**Primary skill:** Research & Analysis  
**Secondary skill:** Empathy  
**Financial literacy domain:** Philanthropy

Use Pages 1, 3, 7, and 11 to demonstrate the process of identifying a water-related challenge, investigating evidence, considering different perspectives, recognizing resource and financial barriers, and questioning initial assumptions.

Add a concrete example of water scarcity to Page 3 through Ada's delivery of water to Alex.

On Page 11, let Alex and Bi11bot model the distinction between forming a theory and investigating it with evidence.

After Chapter 1, introduce Mission 1 with a brief retrospective narration from Alex, inviting students to investigate a real water challenge in their community.

The mission should lead to individual research and reflection with Bi11bot, followed by a team Challenge Statement grounded in evidence and financial or resource realities.

### **Connect Chapter 1 to Mission 2: Your Prosperity Origin Story**

**Superpower:** Systems Thinker  
**Primary skill:** Critical Reflection  
**Secondary skill:** Communication  
**Financial literacy domain:** Goal Setting

Use Alex's inherited circumstances on Page 2, his contrasting aspirations with Ada on Page 3, and his choice to begin the quest on Pages 10–11 to model personal reflection and goal setting.

Introduce Mission 2 after Mission 1 through Alex's retrospective voice. Invite students to connect their community challenge to their own experiences, values, strengths, and future aspirations.

Students should develop their individual Prosperity Origin Stories, identify a future direction and first action, and collaborate on a team vision statement, name, and motto, with an optional logo.

### **Introduce Alex as a retrospective narrator**

Experiment with an older Alex reflecting on key decisions in the story.

Use this voice sparingly in the graphic novel and more explicitly in the mission transitions. His narration should be personal, thoughtful, and occasionally humorous.

Do not reveal future plot twists or turn his reflections into moral lectures.

The transition should communicate that Alex began his journey by questioning assumptions, understanding his community, and choosing the kind of future he wanted to create.

### **Preserve the story's central mysteries**

Do not resolve Adrian's disappearance, the true origin of his debt, the source of Keel's water scarcity, or the complete purpose of the three artifacts in Chapter 1\.

However, by the chapter's conclusion, readers must clearly understand the immediate quest: Alex is searching for three artifacts, beginning with the Measure in the sealed Keel mine.

**Overall editorial goal:** Transform Chapter 1 from the opening of Alex's adventure into the beginning of a shared journey in which students investigate their own communities, question assumptions, reflect on their aspirations, and begin creating their own Prosperity stories.
