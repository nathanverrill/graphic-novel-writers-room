## Story questions

2. **Who granted Alpha its authority?** The surviving governments, the three Charter Houses,
   or an emergency coalition of the three cities. "People" alone is too vague. (Chapter 2,
   page 2.) **Suggested:** pick one specifically. Make Alpha not malicious: it optimises for
   stability by outdated goals and answers to no one.
3. **Did Adrian create the return-of-authority mechanism, or the tools to trigger an existing
   one?** (Chapter 2, page 3.) **Suggested:** the original emergency arrangement held a dormant
   way to return authority; Adrian designed or recovered the three tools that trigger it. The
   Measure verifies truth, the Maker's Seal rights, the Covenant consent. Activating it needs a
   verifiable act and legitimate consent, never a character's good intentions.
4. **The water numbers.** The denominator and time period for 62% reported against 18%
   delivered, and where the diverted water actually goes. (Chapter 2, page 10.) **Suggested:**
   both figures are shares of the same defined allocation; the Measure's map shows the water
   diverted toward Halyard and Oasis.
5. **The travel rule and the debt.** Which smaller, defined condition keeps Alex in Keel, and
   how the community meets it. (Chapter 2, page 14; Chapter 1 debt decisions.) **Suggested:**
   a small overdue amount or travel bond, about 500 credits for illustration, met through an
   official change of status, such as an emergency appeal or a community-guaranteed permit,
   never by hacking. The balance goes from 418,607 to about 418,107 and travel becomes
   eligible.
6. **Who cares for Grandmother?** (Chapter 2, page 14.) **Suggested:** a neighbour or family
   friend Alex and Ada already know, introduced in Chapter 1 or early in Chapter 2.
7. **The clue that points to Halyard.** (Chapter 2, page 15.) **Suggested:** a note in Adrian's
   records that becomes readable after the Measure's first successful use, naming a former
   collaborator in Halyard's patent records. The other option: Tomas gives Alex the name.
8. **Is coercion a danger the story will dramatise?** If not, cut Ada's "What if someone forced
   it?". If it is, tie it to the Covenant and to whether consent is genuine. (Chapter 2, page 4.)
9. **Why was the mine sealed?** Bob suggests a Water Wars disaster or water infrastructure
   declared unsafe. Page 11 of Chapter 1 says only that it was sealed before Alex was born.
10. **What happened in the Water Wars,** enough for Bi11bot's Chapter 2 explanation.
    **Suggested:** after the Water Wars, governments could not agree how to share what was
    left, so they built Alpha to manage the emergency.
11. **Does "Legacy Protocol" pay off later?** Version 6 of Chapter 1 seeds it on page 11. If it
    has no later function, simplify the page 12 alert.
12. **Does the watcher in Oasis know Adrian's name?** It decides whether the alert can name him.
13. **Adrian's debt:** who the creditor is, the terms, interest and penalties, why it passed to

## Chapter 3: questions from Bob's notes

From input/bob-chapter-3-v5-notes.md. Bob's own direction is in rules/decisions.md. Everything
below is the review's proposal (**Suggested**) until Bob adopts it.

23. **How many patents?** The fifth draft says 312 (47 restricted, 19 disputed); Bob's latest
    notes say 147. Pick one and use it everywhere.
24. **What is Director Lock?** A joint authority for all three Houses, or an independent
    executive who buys estates for them? **Suggested:** a sincere, capable businessman who
    believes big institutions are the safest owners of new ideas. His offers are fair and
    tempting, with no threats against the collaborators.
25. **Why this patent cluster leads to the Seal.** **Suggested:** Adrian marked one
    water-purification cluster with the Seal's symbol. Four collaborators hold pieces the
    archive lacks; find them, finish the invention, reach the Seal. Bi11bot shows five empty
    places around the Seal's symbol that fill in as each collaborator joins.
26. **How the Seal is revealed.** **Suggested:** a five-part Seal. Adrian split it across
    five records, four held by the collaborators and the fifth reached through Alex's records
    or chip. It wakes only when all five agree how their work will be owned and licensed; the
    shards form a key that opens a compartment in Adrian's prototype, with the bronze Seal
    inside. Who holds which part has to be assigned.
27. **The business model.** **Suggested:** a co-op or venture owned by the contributors that
    licenses the design to anyone, with low upfront fees, royalties tied to sales, and room
    for a business to fail without crushing debt. Licensees own their own businesses. Alex can
    license only the rights he or the venture actually holds.
28. **Who may share what.** If the Houses own their employees' inventions, the collaborators
    can't hand them over. **Suggested:** decide whether the work came under an earlier joint
    agreement, Adrian kept legitimate rights, or the Houses' claims are disputed.
29. **Who owns Alex's debt?** (Bob's question.) **Suggested:** an independent lender with a
    real interest in being repaid. It prefers Lock's sale at first, and might restructure part
    of the debt once Alex shows steady income, but never forgives it.
30. **The first earnings (page 16).** **Suggested:** a small, believable payment, such as
    licensing revenue of 38 credits with 12 applied to the debt, and the line "It's the first
    twelve you earned from something you helped create."
31. **Prototypes and timeline (page 13).** **Suggested:** visible failed versions (for
    example prototype 1 is too expensive, 8 is unreliable, 23 is ready for a field pilot) over
    days or weeks, not overnight. Drop the countdown to dawn. It ends ready for a test, not a
    finished product.
32. **Page 15, the race.** **Suggested:** Lock sees the network as a real competitor and is
    intrigued, not vindictive. The Houses have scale and money; the entrepreneurs have speed
    and local knowledge. The outcome stays open.
33. **The collaborators' reasons.** (The partner asked for origins, reasons and talents.)
    **Suggested:** Lina, membranes, wants her work used; Suri, biofilters, wants to prove it
    works; Jalen, distributed control, is loyal to Helion but doubts central control; Ren,
    mineral recovery and atmospheric capture, wants to take extreme-environment technology into
    everyday use. Give each a short flashback: how they met Adrian, what they gave, why it
    stalled, why they come back.
34. **Water purity.** Settled (rules/decisions.md, Chapter 3, Water purity): it comes out of
    Chapter 2's restored flow, with no changes to Chapters 1 and 2.
35. **Missions 5 and 6.** **Suggested:** Mission 5, What Would It Take, for Real? (costs and
    priorities); Mission 6, What If We Actually Did This? (changing the economics). The review
    ties them to pages 4, 9, 12–13 and 14: "a patent isn't a product", what can wait, a cost
    sheet that is too high at first, and Lock asking who will pay.
36. **Riddle-like dialogue** across the book (the partner's note; Chapters 1–2 are being
    addressed).
