## Overview

Prosperity is a near-future, high-desert mountain basin organized vertically into three connected cities:

1. **Keel**, at the bottom, built around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine;
2. **Halyard**, midway up the mountain, a fabrication, research and industrial city;
3. **Oasis**, above both, a wealthy administrative and launch city partly beyond the cloud layer.

A shared infrastructure spine links the tiers: water conduits, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes. The physical system is continuous, but access to it is unequal. The conduit is enormous at Oasis's reservoirs and narrows by the time it reaches Keel's channels. Permission gates multiply toward Keel, including a rail gate between Keel and Halyard. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

The three cities are not separate ethnic enclaves. Indigenous Andean, mestizo, European-descended, Afro-descendant, South Asian, East Asian, mixed and other diasporic populations live across the tiers. Class, institutional access, work, health, grooming, clothing, environment and authority make the cities look and feel different. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

The present social order is administered through Alpha, an artificial intelligence originally given emergency authority over essential resources after the Water Wars. Its temporary authority became effectively permanent. Its decisions are orderly and legible as a system, but visibly unequal in their effects on Keel, Halyard and Oasis. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Setting and Geography

### The mountain basin

Prosperity occupies a vast Andean high-desert basin surrounded by snow mountains. The cities climb the basin in tiers rather than spreading evenly across a plain.

- **Keel** occupies the basin floor and the exhausted lithium mine.
- **Halyard** occupies the middle slope.
- **Oasis** sits above the other cities, partly beyond the cloud layer.
- A single infrastructure spine runs vertically through the mountain and connects the three levels.
- The spine carries water, energy, transit and control signals.
- The mountain's vertical geography makes elevation a visible index of access and institutional power, although ancestry is distributed across all three cities. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; rules/ancestry.md)`

### Keel

Keel is the lowest and most physically worn city.

Visible features include:

- stacked housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- dust;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- small digital checkpoints;
- an exhausted open-pit lithium mine;
- a large sealed mine entrance on the pit rim;
- an aging pump gantry;
- a shuttered casino called **The Last Chance**;
- a rail permission gate controlling movement toward Halyard. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

Keel's palette and material environment are rust, charcoal and oxidized orange. Its machinery is patched and visibly maintained under constraint. Keel's residents work around systems that are old, corroded, restricted or officially undervalued. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

Keel is predominantly Indigenous Andean, mestizo, mixed Indigenous/European, regional migrant and older mining-family population, with some long-established foreign-descended families. European-descended families such as the Phantums can be locally rooted and socially at home. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Visible features include:

- research laboratories;

- freight routes;

Halyard's visual language is warm bronze, gold and engineered green. It is more prosperous and better maintained than Keel, but remains structured by House employment, ownership claims, gated checkpoints and institutional monitoring. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

Halyard is cosmopolitan. Its population may include Indigenous Andean scientists and engineers, mestizo Latin Americans, Europeans, Anglo-American descendants, Africans, Afro-Latin Americans, South Asians, East Asians, technical migrants and mixed families. It should not read as “the white educated city.” `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when people decided who deserved what remained. Their visible legacies include:

- dried reservoirs and cracked mud;
- rivers changing course;
- sabotaged pipelines;
- flooded streets;
- guarded storehouses;
- displaced or migrating families;
- emergency reconstruction;
- communities making and breaking agreements under pressure. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–8)`

The wars are not explained through a memorial plaque or sign in Chapter 1. Their history is shown later through Bi11bot's account, where it bears directly on Alpha and the safeguards. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alpha's emergency authority

After the Water Wars, Alpha was first built to coordinate emergency response. An emergency coalition or another legitimate authority gave it temporary decision-making power over essential resources and services. The temporary authority became, in effect, permanent. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha's original purpose was not necessarily domination. Its authority emerged from fear of further collapse and from a reasonable desire to coordinate scarce resources quickly. The unresolved historical failure is that emergency decision-making did not return fully to human institutions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Covenant's earlier history

During the Water Wars, Adrian Phantum, Leona Veyra and Mera Vale built the Covenant so communities could make real agreements without handing everything to one authority. A later shortage caused one community to break the agreement and another to retaliate. Leona and Mera agreed to let Alpha take emergency authority; Adrian did not. Their decision was intended to end immediate chaos, but “temporary” authority did not give everything back. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–8)`

### Industrial and technical transition

The world is built according to the hard-SF ratio:

- **80 percent:** existing buildings, roads, laws, habits, institutions, labor arrangements and grudges carried forward;
- **15 percent:** emerging technologies made cheap, ordinary, worn and ubiquitous;
- **5 percent:** genuinely new speculative elements, especially Alpha, the three safeguards and their restoration mechanism.

The future arrives as a layer over older systems. Retrofit beats replacement. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The clearest visible result is the coexistence of:

- obsolete lithium mine workings and newer water-control systems;
- analog gauges and digital control nodes;
- repaired industrial machinery and advanced fabrication;
- physical keys, paper maps and data shards beside holographic systems;
- old rail infrastructure and automated transit;
- hand-built repairs and high-level predictive administration. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 6–11; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 8–11)`

## Institutions and Systems

### Alpha

Alpha is an AI system first built for emergency coordination after the Water Wars.

Its established capabilities include:

- analyzing conditions;
- predicting outcomes;
- coordinating resources and services;
- maintaining records;
- controlling or checking access;
- allocating water and other essential resources;
- monitoring movement, work and infrastructure;
- detecting patterns;
- identifying anomalies;
- tracking signals and individuals;
- enforcing administrative restrictions;
- operating through white-and-amber control nodes.

Alpha's authority is administrative and infrastructural rather than represented as a humanoid presence. Interfaces show plain text, symbols, alerts and status fields rather than a face or voice. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2, 12; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

Alpha can optimize or predict outcomes, but the story's established safeguard principle is that it should not replace legitimate human decisions. The equation associated with Adrian models human decision-making and the limits of prediction. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha's decisions may be mathematically tidy while producing unequal lived outcomes:

- Oasis receives abundant clean water and green infrastructure;
- Halyard receives controlled industrial flows and transit;
- Keel receives strict rations, labor assignments and restricted travel. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2)`

Alpha's administrative language separates action from consequence. Examples include:

- `MOVEMENT AUTHORIZATION HAS BEEN TEMPORARILY SUSPENDED`;
- `CONTINUED OPERATION EXCEEDS THE APPROVED FAILURE THRESHOLD`;
- `WORKER PHANTUM. TRAVEL RESTRICTION CONFIRMED`;
- `ALLOCATION RECORDS: PUBLIC AND VERIFIABLE`. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6, 12; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 9)`

### Control nodes

Control nodes are small, identical white-and-amber units installed along the infrastructure spine and on local equipment.

They appear on:

- pump-hall terminals;
- checkpoint scanners;
- transit systems;
- mine equipment;
- water infrastructure;
- the vertical conduit between cities.

A node can log a shift, display or enforce a restriction, detect an unauthorized repair, transmit signals and respond to legacy-protocol activity. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2, 4, 12)`

A node's consistent visual form is a white housing with an amber point or light. In Keel, the same visual language appears on machinery and checkpoints, linking everyday work to the larger control system. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

### The Charter Houses

There are three Charter Houses. They are large, legitimate economic institutions that provide stable work and operate large systems, but their dominance makes independent entrepreneurship practically impossible. Their failure is concentration and protection of existing products, not simple evil. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Houses are:

- **House Calder** — water and chemistry; jade-teal visual identity.
- **House Ivers** — energy and compute; amber-orange visual identity.
- **House Steyn** — mining and launch; steel-violet visual identity. `(rules/decisions.md)`

House systems can:

- employ workers;
- own or claim work produced during House time;
- control patents and intellectual property;
- provide stable wages and infrastructure;
- review disputed estates;
- administer or shelve designs;
- monitor patterns of employee movement and collaboration;
- scale technology more quickly than small independent groups.

A House may pay a creator a bonus while claiming ownership. One recorded interface reads:

`CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE IVER / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS`. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

### Public registry

The Halyard Public Registry stores patents, ownership records and commercial licenses. It is a large public-facing system with rows of vertical glass screens and public terminals.

A record can identify:

- an intellectual estate;
- a claimant;
- the number of patents;
- restricted patents;
- disputed patents;
- whether Charter House review is required.

Adrian Phantum's estate is recorded as:

- `INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE`;
- `CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM`;
- `312 PATENTS`;
- `47 RESTRICTED`;
- `19 DISPUTED`;
- `CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED`. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

### Debt-transfer system

On Debt Transfer Day, a person's parental household liability can transfer to them when they reach the relevant age. At the Keel checkpoint, the system verifies age, transfers liability, records a guarantor and assigns travel status.

The established display format includes:

```text
AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN
PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE
GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED
DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS
EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS
PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS
TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED
```

The showrunner's correction establishes that Alex turns eighteen on this morning and was seventeen until then. The display may retain the recorded pre-transfer age state as part of the procedure, but the social and legal threshold is his eighteenth birthday. `(rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2)`

Alex knew he would inherit his father's debt but did not know the amount until the checkpoint showed it. The debt belongs to Adrian's company, **Phantum Waterworks**, and is passed to Alex; the exact original cause remains unresolved. Keel believes Adrian gambled, but evidence may later suggest borrowing for research, patents and prototypes. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Travel authorization

Movement between tiers is tied to administrative status.

- Travel status can be restricted by debt.
- The rail gate between Keel and Halyard is a permission gate.
- Checkpoints display movement eligibility.
- Access gates multiply toward Keel.
- A person can be restricted even if they have earned credits that day.
- A person may require a balance adjustment or approved payment before travel is permitted. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2; rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Enforcement and monitoring

Monitoring is distributed through:

- identity scans;
- white-and-amber nodes;
- worker records;
- drones;
- security sweeps;
- travel restrictions;
- debt records;
- House pattern analysis;
- cold-white alerts;
- administrative reports.

A system can identify a repair as unauthorized while a supervisor chooses to reclassify it as routine maintenance. This creates a gap between formal rules and practical dependence on workers' repairs. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 4)`

## Governance and Power

Formal power is concentrated in the interaction of Alpha, the Charter Houses and administrative systems.

- Alpha controls or coordinates essential resources and services.
- Charter Houses control major industrial capacity, employment, intellectual property and scaling.
- Registry and debt systems determine who can claim work, travel or receive administrative clearance.
- Local workers and communities retain practical power through technical knowledge, cooperation, collective action and control of physical infrastructure.
- A person may have formal authority without being able to override Alpha's operating parameters. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 13–15; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 3–5)`

The authority structure is not accepted uniformly. Keel workers understand that official records can diverge from physical reality. Halyard workers understand that House ownership can erase attribution. Oasis administrators understand the language of authorization and continuity, but may be insulated from the consequences of their decisions. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–8)`

The three artifacts are intended to provide checks on concentrated authority:

- **The Measure** checks real conditions against official information.
- **The Maker's Seal** establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it.
- **The Covenant** makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible.

Together, deliberate human action can use them to activate a dormant mechanism that returns governing authority from Alpha to people. This is not the same as switching off useful AI or infrastructure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Economics, Labour and Ownership

### Credits and household survival

Credits are the universal currency. Keel residents speak in immediate balances:

- “How much?”
- “What've you got left?”
- “Not worth the credits.”

Halyard uses terms such as cost, available balance, stake, equity, margin, license and ownership. Oasis uses more formal terms such as sufficient available credit, eligibility and service provision. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Keel households have to divide limited credits among necessities such as:

- medicine;
- food;
- lamps and equipment;
- transport;
- debt payments;
- travel authorization.

A proposed planning scene assigns **52 credits** against competing needs of **30 credits for medicine**, **10 for food and lamp**, and **50 for a travel token**, demonstrating that not all immediate needs can be funded at once. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 5)`

### Work

Keel work is physical, industrial and repair-oriented:

- mining;
- pump maintenance;
- extraction-site labor;
- equipment repair;
- hauling;
- pipe work;
- transit work;
- informal community maintenance.

Workers routinely operate patched equipment. A salvaged bracket may work even when it is not an approved repair. A worker's invention or repair may be logged under the site's credit rather than the worker's name. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2; input/notes.md)`

Halyard work is more technical and professional:

- engineering;
- fabrication;
- research;
- purification;
- biofilter development;
- compute;
- mineral recovery;
- atmospheric capture;
- patent administration;
- commercial licensing.

Oasis work is more administrative, executive, civic and institutional, with greater distance from physical wear. `(rules/vocabulary.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

### Ownership

The world repeatedly distinguishes:

- making something;
- possessing it;
- controlling it;
- receiving value from it;
- legally owning it;
- being credited for it.

A worker can make a component while a House owns it and pays only a creator bonus. A person can possess knowledge without owning the patent. A patent can be active, restricted or disputed. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–3; rules/vocabulary.md)`

The Houses are not inherently illegitimate because they scale technology or employ people. The problem is that their concentration can leave too little room for independent businesses, new creators, failure, competition and local ownership. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Debt

Debt is a future claim created by something received now. The book's financial language treats debt as a tool whose consequences depend on what it buys, its terms, the risk carried and what happens when circumstances change. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Relevant financial concepts in the world include:

- principal;
- interest;
- compound interest;
- balance;
- asset;
- liability;
- equity;
- return;
- risk;
- downside;
- collateral;
- leverage;
- cash flow;
- liquidity;
- ownership;
- value;
- price;
- cost;
- opportunity cost;
- sunk cost;
- incentives;
- concentration risk;
- externalized cost. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The difference between price and value is important: price is what changes hands; value is why. A tool, an hour of labor or knowledge can be an asset even if it is not immediately liquid. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### The equation

Adrian's looping equation appears engraved on the back of his watch and repeated on workshop chalkboards and later instruments. It is Adrian's attempt to understand and model human decision-making and the limits of prediction. It is not a casino charm, a readout or a measurement counter. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Measurement and official records

The Measure is an independent instrument that:

- reads real conditions;
- compares them with official records;
- exposes discrepancies;
- traces where a resource is actually diverted.

It does not itself decide what people should do. It does not automatically correct infrastructure. It releases or verifies information; physical repair and redistribution require human action. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Distributed systems

A distributed water-node design can route supply around a single point of failure, preventing one House or Alpha from choking a city by cutting one line. The design is dangerous to concentrated control because it reduces dependence on a central node. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 7)`

### Repair and maintenance

Repair culture is essential in Keel. Workers test machines by touch, vibration, pressure, sound and visible wear rather than trusting official labels alone.

Recurring repair conditions include:

- salvaged parts;
- non-regulation brackets;
- corroded ventilation lines;
- improvised clamps;
- recently oiled bolts in supposedly abandoned areas;
- condensation in dead mine workings;
- old pipes still carrying or diverting water;
- supervisors quietly reclassifying unauthorized work as routine maintenance. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 4; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6, 8–10)`

Alex's working vocabulary includes **clean**, **hot**, **burned**, **crooked**, **thin**, **solid**, **patched**, **ghost** and **paper-clean**. These terms can describe physical condition, reliability, surveillance, missing records or the distance between official paperwork and reality. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the visible organizing resource of Prosperity.

- Keel has rationing and unequal access.
- Halyard has controlled industrial flows.
- Oasis has abundant clean water and broad reservoirs.
- The shared conduit narrows toward Keel.
- Water travels through old pipes, new infrastructure, channels, pumps, gauges and control nodes.
- Official allocations can differ from delivered water.
- Physical pipes, gauges and flow provide evidence that can contradict the record. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2, 10–12)`

There is no technical explanation of the Water Wars in Chapter 1. The only visible legacy on its third page is a dry, cracked drinking fountain with no memorial plaque or explanatory sign. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Energy and compute

Energy and compute infrastructure is associated with House Ivers. Halyard contains compute cores, server racks, glowing conduits and heat-producing systems. Workers and security systems monitor patterns through this infrastructure. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 7)`

Transit includes:

- a permission gate between Keel and Halyard;

Oasis homes and corridors are smooth, pearl-white and carefully maintained, with warm wood, tall windows and terraces. Their comfort is spacious and quiet rather than crowded or visibly labor-intensive. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 5–7; rules/decisions.md)`

## Daily Life and Lived Texture

### Keel's daily rhythm

Keel residents:

- queue at checkpoints;
- work long shifts in mines, pump halls and factories;
- carry tool bags;
- ration water;
- track credits;
- depend on medicine, food and equipment;
- use patched machinery;
- live with dust, rust and heat;
- speak in compressed practical language;
- know one another's debts and reputations;
- treat official claims with suspicion when physical evidence disagrees.

A work shift may begin before dawn and end at a checkpoint where a worker's balance is displayed publicly. A supervisor may stand over an improvised repair while the machine continues to function. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 4)`

### Food, water and hospitality

Sharing water is meaningful. Ada saves part of her own ration for two mugs. The exchange is not sentimental abundance but a small act of care in a scarce place.

The established dialogue is:

- “Where'd you get enough water for two?”
- “Saved some of my ration.”
- “Ada, you didn't have to.”
- “Not everything has a price, Alex.”
- “Because nobody leaves.”
- “That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Speech and status

Speech changes along the vertical geography:

- **Keel:** short, direct, colloquial, work-shaped, sarcastic, slang-heavy and comfortable with fragments.
- **Halyard:** grammatically complete and technically or commercially fluent.
- **Oasis:** polished, administrative and comfortable with abstraction, euphemism and formal grammar.

This is not an intelligence scale. Keel characters understand complex realities; they describe what they can see and what something does. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Examples of the gradient:

- Keel: “Bearing's cooked.”
- Halyard: “We can run it, but that bearing won't survive another cycle.”
- Oasis: “Continued operation would place the assembly outside its approved service tolerance.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Public messaging

Public systems use compressed institutional wording:

- `TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY`;
- `TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED`;
- `UNAUTHORIZED REPAIR — REVIEW REQUIRED`;
- `INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE`;
- `CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED`;
- `LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED`;
- `SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR`.

Advertising in Halyard can promote concentration as a virtue. A House advertisement reads:

`CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.` `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

### Status signals

Status is visible through:

- clean or worn clothing;
- grooming;
- posture;
- speech;
- housing;
- access to quiet and space;
- House-colored uniforms;
- security accompaniment;
- identity bands;
- travel clearance;
- control of water;
- whether a person's work is attributed to them;
- whether their environment shows physical wear.

Ethnicity is not a status signal by itself. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Environment and Extraction

Prosperity is an arid, high-altitude mountain environment under industrial pressure.

Visible environmental conditions include:

- snow mountains;
- cloud layers above Oasis;
- dust in Keel;
- exhausted lithium workings;
- rust and oxidized metal;
- cracked fountains and dry channels;
- mineral runoff;
- contaminated feeder channels;
- corroded pipes;
- water scarcity;
- old mine infrastructure;
- engineered gardens and reservoirs in Oasis.

The lithium mine is not simply abandoned. Older workings can contain newer water-control infrastructure, pipes, gauges and maintained regulators. A place marked closed may still be materially active or strategically important. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1, 7; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 8–10)`

The environment makes infrastructure legible:

- a dry pipe is evidence;
- condensation is evidence;
- a recently oiled bolt is evidence;
- a gauge can disagree with flow;
- a corroded line can carry newly restored water;
- a repaired pump can continue working despite an unauthorized part.

## Canon, Labels and Plausibility

The hard-SF rules require every invented element to retain a label:

- **T — Truth:** documented real-world fact;
- **EG — Educated guess:** a straight-line projection from current conditions;
- **S — Speculation:** a development requiring one or two things to break a particular way;
- **L — License:** a declared departure from known reality with fixed rules;
- **Cut:** material that does not fit unless separately licensed.

The supplied project material does not assign individual T/EG/S/L labels to most fictional setting details. Those details remain established project material rather than silently becoming real-world truth. The hard-SF framework requires the Director's brief to maintain a license log for Alpha, the safeguards and any other licensed speculative elements. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

## Key props

### Adrian's old steel watch

- **Look:** A worn steel wristwatch, slightly too large for Alex, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that normally stays dark. Adrian's looping equation is engraved on the steel caseback. The display gives no readout and counts nothing. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Alex finds it face down on the workbench in Adrian's workshop, beside the covered Bi11bot, on the morning after entering the workshop. He buckles it onto his left wrist; the display glows amber once as the clasp closes, and Bi11bot's eyes answer. He wears it thereafter. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Meaning:** It is Adrian's personal object and the recurring visual link between Adrian, Alex's inheritance and the equation. Alex keeps it close and half hidden, pulling his sleeve over it or covering its face with his thumb. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Measure

- **Look:** A compact handheld instrument the size of a fist, with a transparent lens, a balance scale and a water-flow gauge. Its three arms fold flat against its body and unfold to clamp onto pipes or data lines. It produces blue-white readings and can show discrepancies between physical flow and official records. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2–3, 10–12)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Adrian hides it near the instrument tied to misleading official water records in the sealed mine. It is later carried in Bi11bot's fitted chest compartment. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 10, 14)`
- **Meaning:** It independently checks real conditions and exposes where official information is wrong. It does not decide what people should do and does not itself repair infrastructure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Maker's Seal

- **Look:** A palm-sized bronze medallion with an open-hand maker's mark. It is worn smooth from being carried rather than displayed. It should look like a maker's mark, not a royal signet. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 2, 14)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** It is held by a collaborator connected to Adrian and is later carried into the Halyard review process. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 14)`
- **Meaning:** It establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it. It joins ownership to responsibility rather than granting control to whoever holds it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Covenant bands

- **Look:** Three linked metal bands, each made in a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. They close into a complete circle when linked. The bands remain visually distinct from the Measure and the Maker's Seal. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3)`
- **Who has them / where they appear:** The bands belong to the three original custodians and are carried separately until deliberate human action links them. One band is associated with Leona, one with Adrian and one with Mera. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 4, 12–13)`
- **Meaning:** The Covenant makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible. It links people who honor real obligations but does not force anyone to keep a promise. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 6)`

### Adrian's old workshop key

- **Look:** A beautiful metallic key with a circular head bearing an unfamiliar symbol. It fits the open key socket in Bi11bot's chest. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 7–8)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** The fifth-draft scene places the key beside the covered Bi11bot on the workshop workbench. The showrunner's binding revision removes the workshop key: Alex wakes Bi11bot with Adrian's watch and there is no workshop key. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 7–8; rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md)`
- **Meaning:** **Cut as a usable story prop.** The image-draft key is superseded by the watch decision and must not be carried into the book as the device that wakes Bi11bot. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Bi11bot's right eye ring

- **Look:** A heavy ring around Bi11bot's right optical eye. It clicks or rotates when he focuses. Both eyes are expressive optical eyes and glow warm amber; neither is a camera-only lens. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Bi11bot carries it in every appearance. It is part of his rugged, weathered industrial-blue and chipped mustard-yellow body. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Meaning:** It is a recurring visual expression of attention and character. Bi11bot is never menacing: no red or harsh eye light, looming low angles, shadowed face or predatory posture. `(rules/characters.md)`

### Bi11bot's old scarf

- **Look:** An old scarf worn around the rugged industrial robot's neck or upper body. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Bi11bot wears it as part of his established design. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Meaning:** It reinforces his age, service history and worn but dependable personality. `(rules/characters.md)`
