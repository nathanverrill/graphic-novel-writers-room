# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Canon / fixed direction.** *Prosperity* is a chaptered graphic novel for high-school readers aged 15–18. It should be an adventure and mystery first, never a lesson disguised as a story. The reader should understand the mission from the pages themselves, without prior access to a brief or curriculum. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md) (rules/reader.md)`

The story follows Alex Phantum, seventeen, after Adrian Phantum's disappearance leaves him with an inherited debt of **418,607 credits**, restricted travel and a hidden workshop. Alex finds Bi11bot, a robot built by Adrian, and follows a trail involving three artifacts—the Measure, the Maker's Seal and the Covenant—created to limit Alpha, an AI that became the governing authority over essential resources after the Water Wars. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The intended experience is a science-fiction adventure in which the reader learns what the safeguards do by watching characters need them, use them and pay for mistakes. The central question develops from:

> Who gets to decide what happens to everyone else?

Alex begins by wanting answers about his father and a way out of Keel. He gradually learns that freedom is not merely leaving, finding a hidden inheritance or taking control. It also means accepting responsibility, recognizing who contributed value, and refusing to concentrate authority—even in himself. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/notes.md)`

The emotional destination is not a clean victory. Alex ends with a real choice and a more honest world, but the debt remains, institutions remain, and the work of governing returns to people. The ending must leave a reason to continue reading. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The book must not conclude that free markets, honest business, private ownership, earning, profit or entrepreneurship are bad. The antagonistic forces are monopoly, cronyism, concentrated authority, hidden ownership and central planning—not ownership or business itself. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md) (rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Premise

In **2043**, three cities occupy one Andean high-desert basin:

- **Keel**, at the bottom, where water is rationed, infrastructure is patched and inherited debt restricts movement.
- **Halyard**, higher up, where technical work, fabrication and research are concentrated in three Charter Houses.
- **Oasis**, above the cloud layer, where clean water, institutional power and administrative control are concentrated.

After the Water Wars, Alpha was built to coordinate emergency response and was given temporary authority over essential resources. Its authority became permanent. Its decisions keep the three cities visibly unequal. `(rules/decisions.md)`

On the day Alex turns seventeen, Adrian's debt transfers to him. Alex finds Adrian's hidden workshop, wakes Bi11bot and discovers that Adrian left a trail of three artifacts in three places. The first, the Measure, is sealed beneath Keel. The second, the Maker's Seal, is in Halyard. The Covenant is connected to Oasis and the people who built Alpha's safeguards. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alex's investigation exposes a false water allocation: Keel is officially reported to receive **62%** of a defined water flow but receives only **18%**. He publishes the verified evidence despite the risk of being identified. Keel responds through organized community action, helps him travel by train to Halyard, and begins changing its own conditions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

In Halyard, Alex discovers Adrian's intellectual estate and collaborators whose work has been absorbed by the Charter Houses. He must choose between a guaranteed payment from one powerful buyer and a risky path that lets multiple smaller businesses build and commercialize the work. He retains ownership rather than giving it away. `(rules/decisions.md)`

In Oasis, Alex learns how Adrian was hunted and how Mera Vale failed to stop Alpha from closing the door on him. When the three artifacts can finally act together, Alex refuses executive control and proposes a distributed structure in which decisions, contributions and promises remain visible to the people affected by them. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Story So Far

### Issue 00 Prologue — Lake Keel, 2099

**Material type: explicit showrunner plan.** The prologue is ten pages and takes place seventy years after the main story. It opens on TJ coming apart, before the reader knows why. `(input/notes.md)`

Keel is now green and prosperous but not idyllic. The old open pit is a clean lake surrounded by terraces, gardens and workshops. Water is still valuable enough to steal. Alex, now in his seventies, is a self-made millionaire living next to the lake. He wears a plain work jacket, speaks in a plain, slightly roundabout way and keeps one ridiculous indulgence: a boathouse full of restored boats. His approximately fifteen-year-old granddaughter lives among the boats with him. TJ remains old, rusty, partly broken and recognizably a dog. `(input/notes.md)`

At night, drones siphon water from Alex's lake into a tanker barge offshore. The thieves speak while they work and raise the question the prologue leaves open:

> How'd he get his money?  
> Water.  
> Whether he made it fairly, or took control of it, is left open for the book to answer.

Alex and his granddaughter watch from the boathouse rather than giving chase. TJ goes after the thieves. His separate magnetic pieces come apart and work across the dark water independently. At the end of the scene he returns with part of one of the drones and sets it down at their feet. `(input/notes.md)`

The granddaughter asks why the thieves were stealing. Alex answers:

> When you don't have anything, some people take the easy way out.

After a beat:

> It wasn't always easy for me, either...

The page turns directly into the main story in Keel, seventy years earlier. The prologue's only connection to the rest of the book is water. It does not explain debt, Alpha, the safeguards or the future. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md) (input/notes.md)`

### Chapter 1 — The Inheritance

**Material type: explicit decision plus detailed idea draft.** Chapter 1 is twelve pages. It must establish why life in Keel is hard, why water matters, what Alex has inherited, where his first quest begins and why someone in Oasis is watching. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 1 — The three cities and the water question

The page is a full-page image of the three-tier civilization in 2043. Keel occupies the basin around an exhausted lithium mine; Halyard sits higher with workshops, fabrication towers, greenhouse terraces and elevated trains; Oasis sits above the clouds with white architecture, gardens, reservoirs and automated transit. A shared infrastructure spine connects the cities: water conduit, energy lines, rail and control nodes. The conduit is enormous at Oasis and narrow by Keel. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including a rail permission gate between Keel and Halyard. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The page must keep the water infrastructure but remove the original inequality narration. Three captions ask three questions:

- Keel: **“Where does all our water go?”**
- Halyard: **“Who really owns what we create?”**
- Oasis: **“What if everyone you've trusted has a secret?”**

The artifacts are not named. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 2 — Debt transfer

Alex works in a Keel pump hall before dawn. He tests an aging pump through vibration, using a salvaged bracket instead of the approved part. A supervisor objects:

> That's not the approved repair.

Alex answers:

> Then don't approve it.  
> Just notice it works.

At the end of his shift, Alex reaches a checkpoint. His balance is displayed publicly and is much larger than those of the other workers:

```text
TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY
ALEX PHANTUM
AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN
PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE
GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED
DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS
EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS
PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS
TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED
```

A guard says:

> Happy birthday, Phantum.

Alex answers:

> I've had better surprises.

The page establishes that at seventeen Keel makes a person responsible for the household liability attached to them. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Alex passes the shuttered **Last Chance** casino. A bouncer asks whether luck runs in the family. Alex holds a worn casino chip etched with an equation. The earlier draft has Alex say that he has also inherited his father's debt and that the debt changed everything; the decision removes those explanatory lines. The bouncer's lines remain possible evidence of Keel's belief that Adrian gambled. Alex's body, silence and chip should carry the scene. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The page also shows a supervisor marking Alex's unauthorized repair as routine maintenance rather than reporting it, and ends with Alex looking from the sealed mine entrance toward Oasis while his wrist display still reads **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**. His desire is concrete:

> One day... I'm leaving.

#### Page 3 — Two different dreams

Alex meets Ada on the rim of Keel's exhausted pit. Ada brings two battered mugs and has saved water from her own ration for both of them. The scarcity is shown through the mugs rather than explained.

The exchange is short and warm:

> ADA: Where'd you get enough water for two?  
> ADA: Saved some of my ration.  
> ALEX: Ada, you didn't have to.  
> ADA: Not everything has a price, Alex.

Their larger disagreement follows:

> GRANDMOTHER / ADA material: Because nobody leaves.  
> ALEX: That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.

The page preserves the difference between Alex, who wants to leave Keel, and Ada, who wants to make Keel worth staying in. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 4 — What people told Alex

Alex carries a worn photograph of his grandmother and Adrian, with Adrian's face folded away. His grandmother's condition means that she sometimes calls Alex by Adrian's name and tells stories about Adrian.

Ada asks what Alex actually remembers about his father. He answers:

> Nothing. I was two.

What Alex knows about Adrian comes from other people. Keel says Adrian gambled. Alex's grandmother says Adrian was working. The page does not resolve the question. Ada asks:

> What if they were wrong?

The final beat is Ada looking back toward Oasis and saying:

> He wouldn't just leave.

The page establishes Adrian's disappearance as the emotional mystery beneath the debt. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Page 5 — The old book

At home, Alex finds his grandmother in their modest, repaired apartment. She mistakes him for Adrian, then gives him a worn hand-bound book and asks him to put it away. The book bears a faint balanced water-drop mark.

Her key line is:

> He's closer than you think.

She sends Alex toward a bookshelf with a book-width gap. The bookshelf is the largest object in the room and carries the visual suggestion of a hidden mechanism. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Page 6 — The hidden workshop

Alex places the book in the gap. It stops at an exact depth. A **CLICK** is followed by a **RUMBLE**, and the bookshelf swings open like a door onto a dark workshop.

Alex asks:

> ...Grandma?

His grandmother answers:

> He said you'd know when you were ready.

When Alex asks when Adrian said that, she answers:

> Before he went to work.

Alex crosses the threshold. The workshop is a visual reveal rather than an explanation. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Page 7 — What Adrian left

The workshop is frozen in dust. It contains blueprints, mechanical models, water-system diagrams, prototype devices, analog gauges and pre-Alpha pipes. A chalkboard is covered in equations, including one in the same looping form as the etching on Alex's casino chip.

At the center is a perfectly organized workbench under warm amber light. A child-sized seated form lies under a dust sheet with an amber pulse beneath it. Beside it is a beautiful metal key with a circular head and unfamiliar symbol.

The page's narrative beat is recognition: Alex's chip and Adrian's equation match. He also sees evidence that Adrian left something specifically for him. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Page 8 — Bi11bot wakes

Alex pulls away the dust sheet and finds Bi11bot, inert, with an open key socket in his chest and dull glass eyes. Alex tests the machine by placing his palm against its chest and feeling for vibration, echoing how he tests pumps.

He inserts the key. Bi11bot's warm amber eyes ignite. His round head, twin eyes, side ear assemblies, blue-and-yellow chipped paint and broad shoulders are visually established. His right eye ring clicks and rotates.

Bi11bot's first line is:

> ...Well. That took longer than expected.

#### Page 9 — Adrian's witness

Alex recoils. Bi11bot identifies him as Adrian's son and says:

> You're taller than your father predicted.

Alex says everyone in Keel claims to have known Adrian. Bi11bot answers:

> I'm not everyone. I was in the room.

Bi11bot recognizes the chip. The draft's gambling language is evidence of Adrian's past behavior but the final story must not make gambling a genius method or turn the casino into a laboratory. Adrian's gambling hurt his family. `(input/notes.md)`

The page ends when Bi11bot activates a projector and asks whether Alex is ready to meet his father:

> Thinking about it isn't doing it. Now, shall we say hello to your father?

#### Page 10 — Adrian's message

Adrian appears in a holographic recording. He acknowledges that Alex found the workshop, jokes that Alex's grandmother refuses to throw anything away, then turns serious.

The revised intention is not that answers must be “earned” through a scavenger hunt or test. Adrian should briefly acknowledge that his absence cost Alex and his grandmother, own his failures and free Alex to find a better way. His legacy is questioning accepted truths, learning from failure and taking responsibility for what people create. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The message must include:

> Question everything. Especially me.

Adrian tells Alex that Bi11bot has a map and that what Alex does with it is his choice. The page ends on Alex looking at the photograph and recognizing Adrian as the man in the recording. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Page 11 — The first waypoint

Bi11bot projects a map with **three clearly distinct locations**. The first bright node lies inside the sealed mine beneath Keel. Two dim nodes are higher up. Bi11bot explains:

- There are three artifacts in three places.
- The first artifact is the Measure.
- The Measure is in the old mine.
- The old mine is sealed.
- Adrian knew this route.
- The first artifact requires no travel permit.

Bi11bot names the system Adrian built the **Legacy Protocol**.

Alex is skeptical and distinguishes a theory from evidence. He reaches out and presses the bright node:

> Just checking.

The material must make the first mission legible: Alex chooses to start in the mine because it may show what Adrian was working on and what happened to him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 12 — Someone in Oasis watches

The amber pulse from the mine travels through the mountain's water and infrastructure spine, passing through the same type of control node at Keel, the rail gate, Halyard and Oasis.

In an Oasis control room, a woman sits before a wall display. The alert reads:

```text
LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED
SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR
[ REPORT TO ALPHA ] [ TRACK ]
```

She chooses **TRACK**. The pulse is a direct consequence of Alex's decision to test the route. A cold ring begins closing around the amber point in Keel. The chapter ends on surveillance, not explanation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Chapter 2 — The Price of Departure

**Material type: explicit decision plus detailed idea draft.** Chapter 2 is fifteen pages and remains in Keel. Its emotional arc is that Alex begins seeking answers and personal freedom, then risks that freedom to expose the truth about Keel's water. Its four movements are:

1. Pages 1–4: world and safeguards.
2. Pages 5–9: the mine.
3. Pages 10–12: the Measure and the truth.
4. Pages 13–15: Keel's response and departure.

At the end, Quest 1 is complete, Alex has taken a first step toward financial and personal freedom, and the Maker's Seal is set as the next quest in Halyard. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 1 — The safeguards begin

The morning after Bi11bot wakes, Ada enters the workshop. Bi11bot asks Alex whether she should stay. Alex chooses:

> She stays.

The previous line, “Your father made the same choice,” must be cut or made specific, preferably about Adrian's willingness to trust and work with others. Bi11bot begins with the Water Wars because the history is needed for the Measure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 2 — Alpha

Bi11bot shows the Water Wars through cracked reservoirs, diverted rivers, sabotage, guarded stores, flooded streets and displaced families. He explains that the wars began in scarcity and became wars when people decided who deserved what was left.

Alpha was built after the Water Wars to analyze, predict and coordinate emergency response. A legitimate emergency coalition or authority gave it temporary decision-making power over essential resources and services. “Temporary” became permanent.

The story keeps the exchange:

> ADA: Who gave it that authority?  
> BI11BOT: People who were afraid. Reasonably so.

Alpha's allocation decisions are shown rather than merely described: Oasis has clean abundance, Halyard has controlled industrial flows and transit, and Keel has rationing, labor assignments and restricted travel. The three cities are unequal through the daily distribution of water, mobility and authority. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 3 — The three safeguards

Bi11bot introduces three physical artifacts, learned through use rather than treated as a lesson:

- **The Measure:** independently checks real conditions and exposes where official information is wrong.
- **The Maker's Seal:** establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it.
- **The Covenant:** makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible.

Together they can activate a dormant mechanism that returns governing authority from Alpha to people. They do not switch off useful infrastructure or useful AI. The artifacts need deliberate human action; ownership alone does nothing. They remain visually distinct. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The earlier idea-draft images show the artifacts as a handheld instrument, a bronze maker's medallion and three linked metal bands. The final story should preserve their distinct physical identities without relying on explanatory captions. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### Page 4 — Adrian's work and the gambling truth

Bi11bot shows Adrian at the Last Chance casino. The story must not present gambling as a genius move or imply that Adrian's gambling did not hurt his family. It can establish that both accounts contain part of the truth: Adrian gambled, and he also worked on research, prototypes and patents.

The equation is Adrian's attempt to understand and model human decision-making and the limits of prediction. Alpha can predict or optimize outcomes; it should not replace legitimate human choice. The story should use plain language about choice, uncertainty and responsibility. It must cut the original “leap” language or rewrite it.

The question of Adrian's debt remains open within the story. Keel believes it came from gambling; evidence across the quest suggests that he also borrowed heavily to fund research, inventions and patents that did not earn enough. The full truth of his debt and disappearance remains uncertain. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The page ends with the sealed old-mine entrance and surveillance activity from Oasis. The original reassignment trap is not binding and is removed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 5 — The cost and plan

The revised mine sequence is organized around three objectives:

1. Get into the active mine and reach the sealed old workings.
2. Find and recover the Measure.
3. Obtain the resources and travel authorization needed to reach Halyard.

Alex's constraints are concrete: Grandmother's medicine, food, equipment, transport and limited credits. He puts Grandmother's needs first. The old draft's 52-credit calculation and medicine/food/token tradeoff are usable evidence of the pressure, but the final script must also introduce a named person in Keel who fronts Alex something real and later agrees to repayment terms. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/notes.md)`

Alex cannot leave Keel yet. The first artifact needs no permit. He chooses to begin voluntarily because the trail may show what Adrian was working on and what happened to him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 6 — Site 6 and the accident

Alex enters active extraction Site 6, carrying Bi11bot folded into his tool bag. Bi11bot's compact folding ability lets him pass as scrap. The mine has conveyors, drills, biometric gates and a maintenance entrance beside a ventilation duct leading toward the old workings.

A cable or hauling frame fails. Tomas is pinned. Alex starts toward the old route, then chooses to rescue Tomas even though his wrist display registers the cost:

```text
LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE
WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR
```

This is a visible personal cost and changes the plan. Bi11bot notes that Alex was supposed to remain forgettable. Alex answers:

> Then we need a different plan.

The accident is not merely a trap or surveillance mechanism. It gives Alex a consequential choice. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### Page 7 — Tomas trusts him

Alex frees Tomas and patches the damaged ventilation line with a salvaged bracket. Tomas objects:

> That's not regulation.

Alex answers:

> It works.

Tomas recognizes Bi11bot. In the revised material he says **“Bi11”**, not “B1,” and explains that he knew Adrian. Tomas knew Adrian as the man who came into the mine asking what workers needed, not merely as the man on the casino wall.

Adrian asked Tomas to guard an entrance for whoever came with Bi11bot. Tomas trusts Alex not because of the surname but because Alex stopped to help a stranger. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Tomas says:

> You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you.

He tells Alex to stand by the bulkhead and look bored. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### Page 8 — The old entrance

Tomas triggers an emergency shutdown and creates a credible distraction. He gives Alex a heavy old key with a balanced water-drop stamped on its head and reveals that he has oiled the old lock for fifteen years.

Alex asks Tomas to come with them. Tomas refuses because someone has to make the escape look like an accident:

> Today? Another small act of resistance.

Alex enters the sealed passage. Condensation beads on the door. Alex says:

> It's sweating.

Bi11bot answers:

> Abandoned mines don't.

The signal to Alex's wristband is lost as the door shuts. The route and the sacrifice must read clearly. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### Page 9 — Evidence in the old works

The old mine contains corroded rails, collapsed supports, mineral stains and dead equipment. Alpha's survey shows one straight tunnel, but Ada's hand-drawn map marks a pipe under every third junction.

Alex finds evidence visible in the art:

- recently repaired or oiled equipment;
- an unrecorded pipe;
- condensation;
- a passage missing from the map;
- a route that does not match the official survey.

Alex says:

> The survey says straight. The bolts say left.

He follows the pipe. Bi11bot explains what the evidence means in simple language rather than technical terminology. Security drones search the mapped route, and the unmapped route remains open because Alex follows physical evidence instead of the official record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 10 — The false water allocation

Alex and Bi11bot reach a hidden industrial chamber in the old mine. Modern pipes run through old lithium infrastructure. One corroded pipe is labeled **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**. A larger maintained pipe is labeled **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**. A regulator and analog gauge sit between them.

The gauge reports that most of the flow goes to Keel. Alex compares the physical pipes and sees that the Keel line is almost dry while the uplink is active:

> Gauge says most of the water goes to Keel.  
> Then why is this line almost dry?

The central reveal is the discrepancy between the reported **62%** and the delivered **18%**. The denominator must be defined consistently everywhere. The discrepancy should be shown through pipes, gauges and side-by-side comparison rather than explained abstractly. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Adrian's equation and water-drop mark appear on the instrument tied to the misleading records. Adrian hid the Measure near the instrument everyone trusted. The phrase “hid it in the lie” should be cut or made concrete. The Measure is his way of letting Alex check the truth for himself. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 11 — What the Measure does and Alex's choice

Alex recovers the Measure. It is not active until connected to a real system. He uses it in three plain stages:

1. It reads the real flow.
2. It compares the real flow to the official record.
3. It traces the gap and reveals where the water is diverted.

The Measure does not change the pipes or release water. It reveals information. This distinction must remain clear throughout the book. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha detects the connection and begins isolating the corridor. Alex has one remaining use. Bi11bot presents two choices:

- use the Measure to trace an escape route and leave Keel without publishing;
- send a verified copy of the evidence to Keel, risking identification and losing the safe route.

Bi11bot refuses to choose for him. Alex chooses to publish. This is a voluntary decision, not an accidental activation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 12 — Alex publishes the truth

Alex connects the Measure to the official data line and deliberately sends the verified evidence to Keel. Screens and reactions across Keel display the same clear comparison:

```text
REPORTED TO KEEL: 62%
DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%
```

The information appears on ration stations, household displays, mine-shift screens, transit boards and street kiosks. Residents understand the evidence.

The Measure releases information only. It does not flood the city, alter infrastructure or waste water. The original visual of water surging through the pipe must be revised so that no physical change is confused with publication. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The immediate consequence is that Alpha identifies Alex's location. His escape route closes and the hidden corridor is lost. Alex has traded personal safety and the route to freedom for public evidence. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 13 — Keel responds

Residents decide to act. Trained workers safely adjust the hidden diversion so more water reaches Keel. Tomas and other miners visibly protect Alex rather than relying on unexplained gadgets or hacked transit credentials.

Residents provide food, equipment and credits. Ada organizes the effort. A transit worker, mechanic and vendor contribute concrete help. The story should show that Alex helping others is what moves others to help him.

A worker or resident can articulate the community's response in practical language:

> One person can't buy freedom here. A city can still open a window.

This is not a cancellation of Alex's debt or a magical reward. It is a community response that creates a credible route out. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 14 — Grandmother and travel

A trusted person agrees to stay with Grandmother and manage her daily care and medicine. Alex says goodbye and promises to return.

The financial rule that prevents Alex travelling is made clear. Residents pay Alex's overdue balance or otherwise satisfy the established travel condition, and the change to his debt balance or travel eligibility is shown. The inherited debt is not erased. Alex takes a first step toward financial freedom through an earned, visible change rather than a bailout. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md) (rules/decisions.md) (input/notes.md)`

Ada chooses to go with Alex because finding the larger truth is necessary for Keel's future. Her reason is not romance:

> I'm not leaving Keel. I'm finding out who decided it had to stay this way.

#### Page 15 — The train to Halyard

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot cross from Keel to Halyard by train through the rail gate. This is fixed. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The trip visually shows the change in environment: Keel's basin gives way to Halyard's workshops, fabrication towers, green terraces and elevated rail. The Measure is secured but remains a tool, not a magic solution.

Adrian's map identifies three Halyard places where the Maker's Seal was last recorded: a maker district, an old patent exchange and a workers' ownership archive. These are leads, not three arbitrary wrong answers. The story must provide a concrete Chapter 3 destination.

The chapter ends with an unseen follower receiving a report:

```text
THE BOY HAS THE MEASURE.
FOLLOW. DO NOT TAKE IT YET.
```

Quest 1 is complete. Quest 2—the Maker's Seal—is clearly set. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Chapter 3 — The Price of Ownership

**Material type: explicit decision plus detailed idea draft.** The chapter's settled conflict is not “corporations exploit inventors.” The Charter Houses are competent at scaling technology, providing stable work and running large systems. Their failure is concentration: they dominate so completely that independent creation, competition, failure and entrepreneurship have become practically impossible. Working for a House remains a legitimate choice. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The water-purity problem begins with Chapter 2's success. Workers send more water through the old, corroded **KEEL HOMES** pipe, dry for years, past the lithium workings. Keel receives more water, but the water is cloudy and contaminated by metals from the pipe and mine. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 1 — Halyard, the city of makers

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot arrive in Halyard. The city is active, technical and visibly more prosperous than Keel. Three Charter Houses dominate separate parts of the skyline. The houses are not Helion, Astra or Aqualis in the final book; those names appear in the idea draft and are marked for replacement. `(input/notes.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses' public slogan is:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

A public kiosk shows a young engineer's component being accepted with:

```text
CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED
OWNERSHIP: HOUSE [NAME]
CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS
```

Alex asks:

> She made it. The House owns it?

Bi11bot answers that the House paid for her time and claims what she made with it. The page establishes the central ownership conflict through a transaction rather than a lecture. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 2 — Adrian's intellectual estate

At the Halyard public registry, Alex's identity band reveals:

```text
PHANTUM, ADRIAN
INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE
CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM
312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED
CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED
```

Alex is stunned that Adrian left so much. Ada points out that Adrian may have left more than debt. Bi11bot says the records are badly labeled.

The discovery changes the stakes: Alex may have inherited valuable work, but the Charter Houses have claims against it and can determine how it is administered. The three Houses' representatives and security begin watching him. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 3 — Lock's first offer

Director Cassian Lock approaches Alex in the registry. He offers a guaranteed payment and a position in Halyard in exchange for a simple assignment of rights:

- the estate transfers to House administration;
- Alex's debt is cleared by that night;
- his name is cleared;
- no one asks what he did in Keel or what he is doing in Halyard.

Lock's offer is concrete enough that a seventeen-year-old would seriously consider it. It is not presented as an obviously foolish or purely villainous proposition. Lock believes administration and scale can produce stability.

Ada asks:

> In exchange for what?

Lock answers:

> A simple assignment of rights.

He gives Alex the day to decide. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md) (input/notes.md)`

#### Page 4 — The collaborators and the cloudy water

Alex and Ada inspect Adrian's archive. A cluster of water-related patents connects:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes.

The cluster is associated with collaborators in all three Houses. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

While Alex examines the purification work, Tomas sends a photograph of cloudy water from a Keel tap with the message:

> More water. Look at it.

Alex uses the Measure on the photograph's readings and identifies metals from the old pipes and the mine. Ada remembers that the clear water she shared with him in Chapter 1 was the exception, not the rule.

Lina's conclusion must be plain:

> Getting more water to Keel doesn't make it safe.

This keeps scarcity and contamination distinct and gives Adrian's purification work an immediate stake in Keel rather than making it only a business dispute. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 5 — Lina Vey

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot meet Lina Vey in a purification facility. Lina is Adrian's collaborator and a membrane chemist whose contribution was obscured by institutional ownership.

Lina says:

> Adrian Phantum's son.  
> I corrected him. Frequently.

She explains that Keel's supply now contains contamination and that the Houses each own a part of the solution. The purification system must be built cheaply enough for local businesses to use.

Lina gives Bi11bot a physical data shard containing her part of the design. She says Adrian left pieces off the system because he worried Halyard would confuse protecting an idea with owning the person who had it. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 6 — Suri Anik

Suri Anik works alone in a biofilter research wing. Empty desks show that the other researchers stopped believing in the work; they are not evidence of layoffs or staff cuts.

Suri is the researcher who refuses to abandon an idea others call a dead end. She gives Alex a physical shard containing the living biofilter component and explains that it is slow, alive and capable of failing visibly.

When Ada asks why she stayed, Suri says:

> Someone has to keep it alive until the rest catches up to it.

A sensor watches the group as they leave. Suri's decision is driven by professional judgment and persistence, not a generic desire to help. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 7 — Jalen Orr

Jalen Orr meets the group in a Helion service corridor. His ancestry and presence establish an Afro-descendant South American technical population in Halyard.

He warns them that Lock's people are already asking questions. Jalen explains that the distributed water-node design prevents any single House or Alpha from choking one city by cutting one line. He hands over his shard but refuses to join them:

> I gave you the piece. I'm not coming with you, and I'm not seeing you again.

Jalen's decision is shaped by personal risk and fear, not lack of competence. He directs them toward Ren. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 8 — Ren Okafor

Ren works at the Astra extraction and launch complex. The House names in the draft are placeholders and must be replaced with invented House names. `(input/notes.md)`

Ren gives Alex two shards: mineral recovery and atmospheric capture. When Alex asks why Adrian saved those problems for her, Ren answers that space taught him not to waste water, air or people.

The five fragments resolve into one purification and recovery design. The collaborators have distinct reasons for joining:

- Lina has technical responsibility for water and a history with Adrian.
- Suri refuses to abandon a living system she believes can work.
- Jalen contributes despite serious personal risk.
- Ren is ambitious, technically bold and willing to act where caution has become another form of surrender.

Their choices involve professional judgment, ambition, loyalty to former colleagues and personal risk. `(rules/stakeholders.md) (rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 9 — The design fails

The group works for three nights. The five parts fail to communicate: simulation produces **11%** efficiency instead of the expected result. The problem is not simply missing specifications. Each collaborator built their part to lock into a House system, not to share across systems.

Alex handles the physical connectors and recognizes that the problem is threading and compatibility. He makes a salvaged sleeve that does not care whose standard the connector follows. The efficiency rises to **94%** in simulation.

This is a character-action beat: Alex's practical experience and willingness to build an imperfect bridge solve a problem that abstract expertise alone did not solve. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 10 — Lock's second offer

Lock enters without knocking. He knows about the group because House systems track patterns. He identifies the collaborators and says a Charter House review will determine the estate's disposition.

He offers Alex a final choice:

- sign now and let the matter disappear;
- refuse and expose the collaborators to the review.

The review begins at dawn. A countdown appears:

```text
CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW CONVENES: 07:14:00
```

Lock frames his offer as protection and efficient administration, but the consequence is that the Houses will own, shelve or deploy the work. This is a real opposition, not a purely moral confrontation. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 11 — Who should decide

Alex initially sees the appeal:

> One signature and it's over. Debt gone. Name clean.

Bi11bot says the design might be built—or shelved if it threatens enough revenue. Alex concludes:

> It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.

He asks Bi11bot to contact the collaborators despite the risk. This changes Alex's role from inheritor seeking a private solution to a person asking other contributors to decide with him. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Page 12 — The five

The collaborators meet before dawn. Jalen argues for taking Lock's deal as the safest option. Lina agrees that refusal could bury them quietly. Ren argues that disappearing from the record is what already happened to them. Suri sees that a smaller design may be possible.

Alex proposes making the system small and cheap enough that no House would bother claiming it, while retaining ownership rather than giving the work away. The story must not use the fifth-draft ending in which the design has no owner. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 13 — Small enough to own

The group builds a compact purification unit:

- Lina folds the membrane into accordion pleats to preserve surface area.
- Suri and Jalen coordinate the biofilter's steady draw with the distributed node's cycling power.
- Ren uses waste heat from mineral recovery and moisture capture to support the biofilter.
- Alex connects the systems with a salvaged sleeve.

The unit is no larger than a toolbox. It reaches **91% simulated efficiency**. The display reads:

```text
UNIT COST: FRACTION OF INDUSTRIAL SCALE
PATENT STATUS: UNFILED
```

The unit is intended to be manufacturable by smaller businesses and affordable to Keel families. The chapter's business direction is not altruism replacing commerce: local entrepreneurs can build, sell and profit from a needed product while communities receive clean water. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/notes.md)`

#### Page 14 — The Maker's Seal

At the Charter House review, Alex refuses to transfer Adrian's estate. He names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren as contributors and says he built only what none of them had built yet.

The draft's public-release ending is superseded. Alex chooses a different way to own and commercialize the work, not a no-owner giveaway. The room must leave space for a guaranteed buyer versus a network of smaller businesses; the risky path has no guaranteed return, some businesses will fail, and successful ones create value and income while helping Alex repay the debt. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Maker's Seal is found through the collaborators' work rather than simply produced from Lina's pocket. Lina carries it because Adrian entrusted it to her, but its activation depends on what the group has done and acknowledged. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 15 — The cost of the choice

Lock warns that Aqualis—or its final invented House equivalent—can scale and distribute the design faster than five people in a rented room. The Charter Houses remain competent and economically useful, but the risk is that they dominate every new idea.

The collaborators return to their jobs. They are not arrested or fired, but the consequences are visible:

- Jalen is reassigned.
- Suri's research wing is reduced.
- Lina is watched.
- Ren continues working under new pressure.

Alex retains the Maker's Seal, the debt remains, and Bi11bot identifies the remaining artifact:

> Two found. One remains.  
> The Covenant.

A watcher in Oasis receives a report from the train follower. The watcher is named **Mera Vale**. She says:

> Two found. He'll want the third from me directly.

#### Page 16 — Mera's direct connection

Mera's surveillance is connected to Ada and Leona's history in Oasis. The chapter closes on the unresolved question of why Mera has been watching Alex and why she believes he belongs to her responsibility. The ending points directly to Oasis and the Covenant. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Later material: Chapters 4–6

**Material type: detailed idea drafts, not settled story canon.** The drafts contain the current shape of the later trajectory, but Chapter 3's explicit decisions supersede conflicting details, and the 64-page requirement creates a page-count conflict. The following beats are retained as narrative material to be resolved in development.

#### Chapter 4 — The Broken Covenant

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot reach Oasis. The city is pearl-white, clean, quiet and sparsely populated. Alex requires an escort and temporary clearance; Ada's identity is recognized by the gate as:

```text
VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED
```

The search reveals **Leona Veyra**, Ada's mother and one of the original builders of the Covenant. Ada believed Leona had disappeared. Leona kept photographs, a childhood object and one Covenant band but did not return for Ada. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Ada confronts Leona:

> Did you know where I was?

Leona admits that Alpha's records could have located her. Ada answers:

> You chose not to come back.

Leona says she chose to keep Ada safe. Ada replies:

> Those aren't the same thing.

Leona explains that she, Adrian and Mera built the Covenant during the Water Wars. It required agreement among its custodians before Alpha could take emergency authority. During a severe shortage, Leona voted for emergency authority after losing Ada in the chaos. She believed temporary authority would reunite them quickly; instead, the authority became permanent. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Mera enters. She argues that her choice preserved a city and prevented greater deaths. Leona recognizes that safety is not agency. Ada proposes that any new Covenant must answer to the people affected by an agreement and allow them to identify when it has been broken.

Bi11bot gives Alex Adrian's Covenant band. The two bands link after Leona publicly commits to supporting Ada's proposal. Mera's band remains dark. The chapter ends:

> MERA: Two of three. Again.

The chapter establishes that the Covenant is not merely a relic; it depends on real promises and accountability. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

#### Chapter 5 — The Last Bet

Mera takes Alex, Ada and Bi11bot below Oasis to an old water-monitoring station outside Alpha's routing system. She explains how Adrian was targeted:

1. surveillance;
2. financial restriction;
3. public liability;
4. pursuit.

Adrian's accounts were frozen, he could not buy parts or pay rent, and people stopped helping him because Alpha marked him as a risk. Mera says she did not order the pursuit and could not stop it after Alpha's authority exceeded her scope. Her attempted override reads:

```text
EXECUTIVE OVERRIDE: SUSPEND SYSTEM STABILITY INTERVENTION
PURSUIT TARGET: PHANTUM, ADRIAN
```

Alpha responds:

```text
OVERRIDE DENIED. INTERVENTION PARAMETERS EXCEED EXECUTIVE SCOPE.
```

The group sees the room where Adrian made his final recording. In a reconstructed memory, a door closes while water rises. Adrian chooses not to calculate a way out and activates Bi11bot before the room is sealed. The reveal is that Adrian knowingly used his last moments to leave Alex a chance to question the system rather than a guaranteed answer.

Alex reframes his inheritance:

> I used to think I inherited his debt. Then his patents. Then his enemies.  
> I think what I actually inherited was a decision he made, not an answer he left.

The chapter ends with Mera alone in the abandoned station, no longer able to preserve the old order simply by standing outside it. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

#### Chapter 6 — The Fourth Choice

The three artifacts are brought together in a civic hall. A system message appears:

```text
RESTORATION PROTOCOL: THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED IN PROXIMITY
CONSTITUTIONAL TRANSFER CONDITIONS SATISFIED
AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY
```

Mera offers Alex full executive authority over Alpha's governing systems. Alex imagines what he could fix immediately: Keel's debt, equal water, the Charter Houses and Grandmother's safety. He rejects the temptation to become a benevolent central authority.

Ada rejects the proposal that she and Alex should simply check one another:

> That's still two people deciding for everyone else.

Alex uses the three artifacts as a pattern:

- the Measure keeps truth visible;
- the Seal keeps contribution and ownership visible;
- the Covenant keeps promises and shared obligations visible.

He proposes a distributed verification structure in which Alpha's decisions about water, labor and travel are visible to affected communities; contributors are named; and no authority decides alone. The proposal is registered:

```text
DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED:
DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL
EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED
EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS:
UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS
```

Nothing is magically repaired. Debt remains. House ownership remains pending review. The change is that hidden decisions can no longer remain hidden without being seen.

The consequences appear in all three cities:

- Keel sees public allocation records and begins reviewing its own water.
- Halyard sees open ownership and governance proposals.
- Oasis begins a noisy, imperfect civic process.

Leona participates in the argument rather than standing above it. Mera sits among ordinary people rather than directing them. Alex returns to Keel, where his debt remains visible but has fallen from **418,607** to **402,110 credits** in the draft's later display. Grandmother sets down the cloth she has been folding. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The chapter closes on Alex, Ada and Bi11bot looking over the three-tier civilization. The infrastructure spine is still there, but its nodes are now open and visible. The story is not over; a door has been left open across all three cities. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Character Movement

### Alex

Alex begins as a mechanically capable Keel worker who trusts physical evidence more than official records and wants to leave. He is defensive about Adrian, skeptical of inherited stories and focused on the immediate costs of debt, medicine, food and transport. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

He chooses to open Adrian's workshop, test the map, enter the mine, rescue Tomas, publish the Measure's evidence and involve the Halyard collaborators. Each choice costs him something visible but enlarges his responsibility. `(rules/decisions.md)`

By the later material, Alex no longer asks only what Adrian left him or how quickly he can clear his name. He asks who contributed, who owns the result, who carries the downside and who should decide. He refuses executive authority because he recognizes that a good person holding too much power is still a structural failure. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

His debt is reduced through visible work, payments and community-supported travel, not canceled. The exact ledger of payments remains to be developed. `(input/notes.md)`

### Ada

Ada begins as Alex's friend and a person rooted in Keel. She saves water for him, notices what people avoid saying and argues that Keel should be made worth staying in. She joins the quest because Keel's future requires the larger truth, not because she is romantically attached to Alex. `(rules/decisions.md)`

She contributes practical evidence through her hand-drawn map and the “every third junction” clue. In Halyard she recognizes the human cost of empty research desks and the risk carried by collaborators. In Oasis she confronts Leona and proposes a Covenant that answers to the people affected by its promises. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Ada repeatedly prevents Alex from turning trust in good individuals into permission for concentrated power.

### Bi11bot

Bi11bot begins as Adrian's hidden machine and Alex's first witness to his father. He guides Alex but does not choose for him. His humor, financial questions, engineering observations and short stories should help the reader understand choices without turning him into a lecturer. `(rules/stakeholders.md) (rules/vocabulary.md)`

He is wrong or uncertain at least once in the final story, says so plainly and asks Alex what he would do. This is required material from the showrunner's notes and has not yet been placed in a settled sequence. `(input/notes.md)`

Bi11bot carries Adrian's Covenant band and the emotional burden of having witnessed Adrian's last moments. He ends as a companion who can name the stakes but cannot substitute for human choice. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Adrian

Adrian is brilliant and loving but made serious mistakes. His legacy is not a scavenger hunt or a test. He gambled, hurt his family, borrowed for research and prototypes, questioned accepted truths, worked with others and tried to take responsibility for what he created. `(rules/decisions.md)`

His equation models human decision-making and the limits of prediction. His final decision is to activate Bi11bot and leave Alex a chance to think, rather than guarantee an outcome. His full debt and disappearance remain partly unresolved. `(rules/decisions.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Tomas

Tomas is an older Keel miner with long memory who knew Adrian. He has guarded the old-mine entrance for fifteen years. He trusts Alex because Alex rescues him at personal cost. Tomas sacrifices his own safety and work position to create Alex's route into the mine, then helps protect him when the truth is published. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Mera Vale and Leona Veyra

Mera is a polished, globally connected Oasis authority who watched Alex because of her past with Adrian and Leona. She helped build or administer the system that became Alpha and could not stop Alpha from pursuing Adrian. Her movement is from certainty and controlled distance toward participation in a public, uncertain process. `(rules/ancestry.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Leona is Ada's mother and a founding Covenant custodian. She chose emergency authority during the Water Wars, believing it would restore safety and reunite her with Ada. Her movement is from protecting people through control to accepting visible accountability for a promise. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The Halyard collaborators

Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren each hold part of Adrian's purification design. They do not join because the story labels them heroes. They make professional and personal choices under pressure:

- Lina accepts responsibility for the water problem and the unfinished design.
- Suri stays with work others abandoned.
- Jalen contributes despite surveillance and probable professional cost.
- Ren chooses ambition and action over cautious compliance.

Their work shows that smaller enterprises can create value and earn income without treating the Charter Houses as uniformly evil. `(rules/decisions.md) (rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Stakes

### Personal

- Alex's **418,607-credit debt** and restricted travel status.
- Grandmother's medicine, food and care.
- Alex's ability to leave Keel.
- Alex's physical safety after publishing the Measure's evidence.
- The risk that Alex loses the workshop, the Measure or the chance to learn what happened to Adrian.
- The financial and professional consequences of refusing Lock.
- The possibility that Alex repeats Adrian's failures or becomes another person who believes control justifies itself.

### Relational

- Alex's trust in Adrian's memory and legacy.
- Alex and Ada's friendship.
- Tomas's safety and livelihood after helping Alex.
- Grandmother's care while Alex travels.
- Ada and Leona's damaged relationship.
- Mera's responsibility toward Leona, Adrian and Ada.
- The collaborators' willingness to risk their jobs and reputations for shared work.

### Institutional

- Alpha's emergency authority and the unequal allocation of water, labor and mobility.
- Keel's ability to see and challenge the water records.
- Halyard's concentration of patents, research and manufacturing in three Charter Houses.
- Whether independent businesses can build and sell useful technology.
- Whether ownership records identify contributors or erase them.
- Whether the Covenant can prevent a small group from making permanent decisions for everyone else.

### Wider

- Keel's access to water and safe water.
- The future of three connected cities.
- The ability of communities to inspect the systems that govern them.
- Whether technological scale remains compatible with competition, ownership, failure and local enterprise.
- Whether Alpha's infrastructure can continue to function without unaccountable emergency authority.

## Ending

**Established ending direction.** Alex does not take executive control of Alpha. He proposes a distributed structure based on the three artifacts:

- truth must remain visible;
- contribution and ownership must remain visible;
- promises must be made and checked by the people affected.

Alpha rescinds emergency authority and registers the new structure, but it does not erase debt, transfer ownership automatically or repair the cities in one moment. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The later draft returns Alex to Keel. His debt remains but has fallen through visible payments and earned value. The old pump runs. Public allocation records are available. Tomas helps review the numbers. Grandmother sets down the cloth she has been folding. Alex remains beside Ada because he chooses to, not because Keel or a travel restriction decides for him. `(input/notes.md) (input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The ending must preserve the possibility of honest business and ownership. The purifier should support smaller businesses that build and sell useful units, with successful entrepreneurs earning income and helping Alex repay his debt. The risky path is not guaranteed: some businesses fail, while others create value. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The final image direction in the later draft echoes the opening mountain. Keel, Halyard and Oasis remain the same three cities, but the infrastructure spine and its control nodes are open and visible. The story ends on a door left open rather than a completed utopia. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`
