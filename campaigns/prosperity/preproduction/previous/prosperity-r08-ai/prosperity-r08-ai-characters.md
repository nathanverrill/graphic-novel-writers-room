# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **FIXED — Ancestry must not function as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, technical competence, wealth, poverty, ideology, or political position.** The cast should reflect an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration, and global capital. Ancestry cuts across Keel, Halyard, and Oasis. Ordinary life should not be burdened with anxious explanation of national or community identity; this is a future society whose people have layered, ordinary, mixed histories. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended.** Adrian is European-descended; Alex's unrevealed mother was Indigenous Andean, rooted in the basin. Alex should visibly resemble Adrian while also visibly inheriting substantial appearance from someone else. This is a story clue, not a dialogue explanation. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex's mother remains unrevealed in this book.** Establish only that she was Indigenous Andean, rooted in the basin, separated from Adrian after he lost everything, and visibly represented in Alex’s appearance. Do not show or disclose her name, face, fate, community, language, occupation, biography, or further history. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/ancestry.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Before scripting, the recurring supporting cast receives a production-design pass.** Each recurring character must be assigned a distinct silhouette, dominant shape language, clothing condition, tool or prop, and city-specific material cue. The pass must explicitly prevent ancestry from functioning as shorthand for status. `(characters.md; rules/ancestry.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada remain longtime friends and civic collaborators, with romantic feeling expressed through intimacy and choice but never formally labeled.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada leave Keel together after the story.** They travel through Halyard and Oasis, help establish the first cross-city review, and continue as a mechanic/witness and civic-community worker respectively. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Grandmother is canonically unnamed.** Use “Grandmother,” “Nana,” or “Mrs. Phantum” according to context. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Tomas, the Keel miner, and Tomas Reed, the Halyard fabrication engineer, are separate people with no established family connection.** They must differ in age, city, trade, silhouette, and introduction context. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alpha is a distributed federation of regional and corporate instances sharing a common model, policy layer, records, and authority protocols. “Alpha” is the institutional identity presented to people; no single uninterrupted consciousness is required.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alpha is not destroyed at the end.** Its authority is bounded to analysis, prediction, coordination, consequence modeling, and operation of human-authorized systems. Humans make value judgments and final decisions. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — The founder of Alpha remains unnamed and absent from the present story.** He appears only through archival decisions, corporate mythology, and the judgment model Alpha inherited. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alpha Dynamics and Alpha AI are legally distinct but practically interdependent.** Alpha Dynamics is the corporate group; Alpha AI is its federated operating and governance system. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — The visual identity associated with Alpha Dynamics is a restrained industrial mark built from an abstract A: a continuous double-line left stroke and a right stroke shifting single → break → double.** The interrupted-line motif recurs across interfaces, signage, equipment, and authorization marks. It must feel geometric, infrastructural, and corporate rather than militaristic or villain-coded. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Speech becomes more formal as the story rises from Keel to Halyard to Oasis.** This is a class, occupation, education, and institutional-language gradient, not an intelligence scale. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada do not naturally use abstract civic, political, academic, activist, or bureaucratic vocabulary.** They describe visible consequences, obligations, physical evidence, and human choices. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **FIXED — Bi11bot has two expressive optical eyes, can fold into a backpack, wears an old scarf, and is never drawn as menacing.** His eyes are wise, patient, and often quietly amused. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **FIXED — TJ is a small repair robot dog built by Bi11bot from spare parts.** TJ is held together by magnets, does not speak, and communicates through body language and mechanical sounds. `(rules/characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — TJ’s recurring behavior centers on being where larger characters cannot.** He scouts first, leaves his head as a camera, tests a gap with one detached limb, and rebuilds only after the route is safe. His most expressive gesture is returning a needed part to another character before reassembling himself. `(rules/characters.md; characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Long-term personal futures remain open beyond the immediate endpoint.** Tomas, the Halyard collaborators, and the three representatives remain available for future stories without assigned relocations, marriages, offices, or permanent institutional roles. Foreman-7 remains an Alpha-controlled automated supervisor whose later reassignment is unknown. `(characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Production Design Pass

Before scripting, the Character Designer must assign every recurring character:

- a distinct silhouette;
- a dominant shape language;
- clothing condition;
- a work-linked tool or prop;

The design must distinguish city, occupation, access, health, wear, institutional position, and personal history without using ancestry as a visual shortcut for class, competence, morality, or ideology. ## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Role:** Protagonist; mechanic, witness, and eventual limited steward of the restored system.

**Identity:** Seventeen years old at the beginning of the story. From Keel. Son of Adrian Phantum and an unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother. Lives with his unnamed grandmother. `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** Lean and mechanically capable. Wears worn mining and work clothes and carries a weathered tool bag. Keeps Adrian's worn casino chip in his pocket; the chip is etched with a looping equation. Alex often turns it unconsciously when uncertain or emotionally activated.

He shares Adrian's jaw, eyes or eye shape, head tilt, expressions, posture, and habitual gestures, but must not look like a younger copy of Adrian. His mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended ancestry should be visually legible without being explained. His clothing and props identify him as a Keel mechanic rather than reduce his identity to ancestry. `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

His final production design requires a distinct silhouette, dominant shape language, clothing condition, work-linked tool or prop, and Keel-specific material cue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical, and mechanically oriented. Sarcasm is defensive rather than performative. He is uncomfortable with speeches and describes evidence more readily than principles. His speech is compressed and work-shaped, with Keel vocabulary such as **clean, hot, burned, crooked, thin, solid, patched, ghost, paper-clean, fitch,** and **salvo**.

He trusts vibration, pressure, wear, physical evidence, and what people actually do more than official labels. He does not naturally say “governance,” “legitimacy,” “institutional,” or similar abstract terms.

Representative lines include:

- “Who decided they get our water?”
- “It decides. We live with it.”
- “You mean I’m trapped.”
- “Then don’t approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “I’ve also got his debt.”
- “One day... I’m leaving.”
- “Everything has a price, Ada.”
- “Maybe. Or maybe they’re right. Either way... he’s gone.”
- “Just checking.”
- “Staying I can predict. Twenty-eight point seven years.”
- “This is the only way out that isn’t a sentence. I’ll take the door I can’t predict.”
- “Truth doesn’t need authorization.”
- “Then we need a different plan.”
- “If they catch me, nobody finds the Seal or the Covenant either.”
- “Someone has to.”
- “You hid water from a city for fifteen years. What’s your offense?”
- “I know what I traded.”
- “I told them the truth. You hid it.”
- “Why are they doing this?”
- “I believed you. I just didn’t know it yet.”
- “I’m not leaving Keel behind.”
- “I just want it to be my choice.”

`(input/characters.md; input/story.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who he is:** Practical, capable, independent, skeptical, and sarcastic when defensive. He begins believing Adrian abandoned him and left only debt, shame, and a casino legend. He wants to leave Keel and choose his own future. His first defining action is placing his palm on an aging pump and feeling its vibration instead of trusting the displayed gauge or approved maintenance procedure.

He is drawn toward freedom as personal departure, while Ada sees the possibility of rebuilding Keel. Alex is not initially motivated by a public cause or abstract theory. He responds to what is physically wrong, what people are doing, and what the system asks him to accept.

His central contradiction is that he distrusts concentrated authority while being tempted by the chance to become the person who can finally fix everything. He must learn that refusing bad authority does not automatically entitle him to become good authority.

**Relationships:**

- **Ada Veyra:** Longtime friend, closest confidante, civic collaborator, and emotionally intimate companion. Their feeling remains unlabeled. Alex initially looks toward escape while Ada looks toward rebuilding. He trusts Ada before he trusts any broader system and eventually chooses to leave with her.
- **Adrian Phantum:** Absent father. Alex begins with resentment, then curiosity, investigation, identification, grief, and finally a deliberate refusal to become Adrian's successor in the form the system expects.
- **Grandmother Phantum:** Caregiver and keeper of Adrian's secret. Alex is frustrated by her memory loss and initially dismisses her claims about Adrian, but gradually recognizes that she has preserved truths he did not understand.
- **Bi11bot:** Inherited machine, guide, mentor, evaluator, witness, archive, and companion. Alex begins suspicious of him, then relies on his questions while resisting the temptation to treat him as an unquestionable authority.
- **Tomas:** An older Keel miner who knew Adrian and helps Alex at personal cost. Tomas gives Alex a practical example of reciprocal aid before repayment is known.
- **Mera Vale:** Begins as an unseen observer and becomes the strongest defender of concentrated authority. Alex ultimately rejects her offer of power without humiliating her and asks her to help build something better.
- **Leona Veyra:** Ada's mother and former Covenant custodian. Alex helps bring Leona's history into the open and understands that restored authority must not become another personal possession.
- **Alpha:** The system that restricted his movement, inherited his debt, killed Adrian, and later offers him concentrated control. Alex's final decision establishes Alpha as adviser and operator rather than final decision-maker.

**Habits and tells:**

- Tests machinery physically rather than trusting its displayed reading.
- Places his palm on pumps, pipes, gauges, and other mechanisms to feel vibration, pressure, temperature, or flow.
- Improvises repairs rather than blindly following approved procedure.
- Frequently crosses his arms when defensive or skeptical.
- Tilts his head while thinking, a gesture shared with Adrian.
- Turns Adrian's casino chip when uncertain or emotionally activated.
- Looks toward Oasis when imagining freedom.
- Follows physical evidence when official maps and records disagree.
- Says “Just checking” before an action that is more consequential than he admits.
- His defenses drop in brief, quiet moments around Ada, Adrian's recordings, and Grandmother.
- He chooses to help Tomas even when doing so costs him the only available route.
- He gives up a private escape route to publish Keel's hidden water allocation, accepting that the decision may trap him and endanger the rest of his journey.

**History:** Alex was two when Adrian disappeared. He grew up with the public account of Adrian as a failed gambler, debtor, and absent father. At seventeen, Adrian's household liability transfers to him: **418,607 credits**, a projected **28.7 years** to clearance, and **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**. The debt is a compound household account containing Adrian's debt, unpaid care and service charges, penalties, and projected interest; unlawful household-transfer and allocation liabilities are later voided, while ordinary debt is separated into an estate account for independent review. `(input/story.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. She was Indigenous Andean and rooted in the basin. The book reveals nothing further: no name, face, fate, community, language, occupation, biography, or other history. Her presence is represented only through Alex's appearance and the established fact of separation. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/ancestry.md; rules/decisions.md)`

On his seventeenth birthday he discovers Adrian's hidden workshop behind a bookshelf in Grandmother's apartment, awakens Bi11bot, and learns that his father's public story is incomplete. He demonstrates, through choices rather than possession of artifacts, the capacities required to restore human authority: making truth visible, recognizing collaborative ownership, and taking reciprocal risks without claiming sole control. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Alex repairs an aging pump with a salvaged bracket and tells his supervisor, “Then don’t approve it. Just notice it works.” At the checkpoint he receives the debt transfer and restricted travel status. At the pit overlook he tells Ada he wants to wake somewhere he can choose his future. He brings Ada into the hidden workshop and says, “She stays,” his first explicit move away from handling everything alone.

At Site 6, Alex chooses to help Tomas rather than use the opening to enter the old works. He follows damp air, condensation, clean new bolts, and physical pipe behavior instead of Alpha's map. When the Measure offers a choice between tracing an unmonitored escape route and publishing the water discrepancy, he chooses publication:

- **REPORTED TO KEEL: 62%**
- **DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%**

He knows this sends him back through a watched route and may prevent him from finding the remaining safeguards. He accepts the cost. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

In Halyard, Alex refuses Cassian Lock's offer of debt relief and status in exchange for assigning Adrian's estate to House Aqualis. He insists that the water system was collaborative, helps recombine the contributors' knowledge into a distributed/modular design, and restores contributor ownership rather than claiming Adrian's estate as sole heir. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

In Oasis, Alex helps convene the first cross-city agreement but does not take over the discussion. He ultimately rejects Alpha's offer of permanent exclusive override authority. He retains only a limited stewardship role: authenticating Adrian's transfer, presenting the evidence, convening the first equal three-city review, and authenticating interim rules. He cannot issue decisions, command Alpha, or hold a permanent override. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The first review is a practical hearing around Leona's association-risk order. Alex presents the authenticated records, then yields the chair to rotating representatives. Mara, Frank, and Elias question the record from Keel, Halyard, and Oasis perspectives. An independent reviewer announces suspension pending lawful review. `(story.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc:**

1. **Escape:** Wants to leave Keel and choose his own future.
2. **Curiosity:** Learns Adrian left something he does not understand.
3. **Truth:** Chooses to reveal Keel's water discrepancy even when private escape would be safer.
4. **Responsibility:** Understands that his choices affect people beyond his own household.
5. **Ownership:** Rejects sole inheritance and restores contributor ownership.
6. **Trust:** Allows Ada and collaborators to participate rather than handling everything alone.
7. **Power:** Is offered the ability to cancel debts, restore water, enforce ownership, and remove restrictions directly.
8. **Restraint:** Rejects exclusive human or AI authority over everyone else's future.
9. **Agency:** Still wants to leave Keel, but now leaves because it is his choice.

His established endpoint is not withdrawal from responsibility. He remains a mechanic and witness, convenes the first cross-city review, and travels with Ada through Halyard and Oasis while helping establish the new human review system. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Ada Veyra

**Role:** Alex's closest friend, longtime companion, civic collaborator, and partner in the journey.

**Identity:** Twenty years old. From Keel at the beginning of the story. Daughter of Leona Veyra. She grew up believing Leona died during the Water Wars. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

**Look:** Long, wind-tangled copper hair; freckles; light or teal-blue eyes; dark high-collared top; rivet-studded shoulder strap; muted moss-green wrap. Her family history is mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla. She should read as unmistakably local to the region while retaining her copper hair, freckles, and light eyes. Her facial structure, coloring, posture, and family resemblance should reflect strong Andean ancestry. She and Alex are both mixed but must not look ethnically interchangeable. `(input/characters.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

She carries herself with a steady, rooted stance. Her production design must distinguish her from Alex through silhouette, dominant shape language, clothing condition, work-linked prop, and Keel-specific material cue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** Concise and emotionally precise. She lets silence sit instead of rushing to fill it. Her language is less mechanical than Alex's and more attentive to obligations, avoidance, fear, imbalance, hidden motives, and what people refuse to say.

Representative lines include:

- “Not everything.”
- “What if they were wrong?”
- “Nobody stays either. Not really.”
- “Stop. Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?”
- “Then that’s what the agreement has to protect.”
- “It’s on me. You don’t owe anybody anything today.”
- “Who gave it that authority?”
- “What if someone forced it?”
- “Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you’d notice it.”
- “Then you don’t go in blind.”
- “I’m not leaving Keel behind. I’m finding out who decided it had to stay this way.”
- “That’s my mother.”
- “My silence for your protection.”

`(input/characters.md; input/story.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who she is:** Practical, observant, rooted, emotionally perceptive, and willing to challenge Alex without turning disagreement into hostility. She believes prosperity begins by changing Keel rather than escaping it. She notices people, not only systems, and redirects stated demands toward underlying fears.

Ada is not less technically capable because she speaks in interpersonal terms. Her strength is recognizing obligations and burdens that system language conceals. She can hold competing needs in view without pretending disagreement disappears.

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Longtime friend and intimate, unlabeled romantic companion. She sees his defenses and challenges his inherited story about Adrian. She trusts his capacity while refusing to let him turn responsibility into solitary control.
- **Leona:** Her mother, believed dead for twenty years. Their relationship moves from absence and shock through anger and disclosure toward unresolved but active repair.
- **Grandmother:** Familiar enough to enter the Phantum home and help prepare Alex's departure.
- **Bi11bot:** Initially evaluates him alongside Alex and becomes part of the working group.
- **Mera:** Represents the institutional choice Ada and Leona once made under pressure: safety through concentrated authority.
- **The three-city representatives:** Ada reframes their negotiation by asking what each fears losing, allowing reciprocal commitments to become possible.

**Habits and tells:**

- Lets silence sit.
- Watches faces and behavior rather than only displays or systems.
- Looks toward Keel while Alex looks toward Oasis.
- Often notices what Alex is avoiding emotionally.
- Redirects arguments from stated demands to underlying fear.
- Keeps a hand on or near the rivet of her shoulder strap when thinking or under pressure.
- Marks routes and plans by hand, including the page with ticks at every third junction.
- Organizes practical supplies and information while others are still arguing.
- Carries responsibility for Keel even when physically leaving it.
- Uses a steady, rooted posture in contrast to Alex's restless defensive stance.

**History:** Ada grew up in Keel believing Leona died in the Water Wars. Leona survived in Oasis, surrendered formal Covenant authority, and accepted contact restrictions as part of a bargain intended to protect Ada. Ada's childhood and adulthood were shaped by an absence created through fear, institutional control, and a protection she did not consent to. `(input/story.md; input/world.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Ada brings Alex tea on his seventeenth birthday and responds to “Everything has a price” with “Not everything.” She asks what Alex actually remembers about Adrian and identifies that nearly everything else is what other people told him. She is the first person Alex voluntarily brings into the hidden workshop; when Bi11bot asks whether she should stay, Alex answers, “She stays.”

She helps Alex read physical evidence in the old mine, marks Adrian's plan, and insists he should not enter the only available route blind. When Alex gives up his private escape route, she helps organize the practical way out. She has already packed by the time he returns and leaves Keel with him, clarifying that leaving does not mean abandoning Keel.

In Halyard, she recognizes that assigning the invention to a House would make Alex's freedom dependent on everyone else's continued subordination. In Oasis, she turns the three-city meeting away from competing demands by asking, “Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Ada begins as Alex's counterpoint: he imagines escape while she imagines rebuilding. She becomes his trusted partner in Adrian's secret, interpreter of human stakes during the Measure quest, collaborator in Halyard, and daughter confronting the living mother she believed dead. Her established endpoint is to leave Keel with Alex while continuing to work with local civic and community groups rather than treating departure as abandonment. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Adrian Phantum

**Role:** Alex's father; mathematician, inventor, systems thinker, collaborator, gambler, and architect of the three-safeguard plan.

**Identity:** European-descended. His exact national ancestry is not established. He worked in mining, infrastructure, research, invention, and collaborative governance. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Look:** Tired but kind in the first clear recording. Wears a burgundy work jacket in the Chapter 1 recording. Shares Alex's jaw and characteristic head tilt. Later recordings show him older, more worn, and under increasing pressure. His archival presence shifts from the public gambler image toward a recognizably human father. `(input/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic, and capable of humor inside serious messages. He uses uncertainty as a discipline rather than a weakness. He avoids telling Alex exactly what to think and frames choices as questions, bets, odds, and consequences.

Representative lines:

- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”
- “If I’ve done my job well... you’ll become a better man than I ever was.”
- “I spent my life trying to predict what people would do.”
- “What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?”
- “People help each other before they know whether it will be repaid.”

`(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

**Who he is:** Adrian is not secretly flawless. He genuinely gambled, and he genuinely used gambling as a live laboratory for probability, incentives, risk, and behavior under uncertainty. He gambled to raise money for his inventions, including restoring Bi11bot, building TJ, and pursuing entrepreneurial efforts. Both the research and the family cost are true.

He worked with collaborators rather than creating alone. He believed human authority should eventually be restored, but also believed that no one should simply inherit legitimacy. People needed to demonstrate judgment, truthfulness, recognition of contribution, and reciprocal trust.

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Absent father and intended successor/test subject, but not merely an heir.
- **Grandmother:** Trusted her to protect the workshop, Bi11bot, and Alex's eventual access to the truth.
- **Bi11bot:** Builder and maker. Bi11bot personally knew Adrian and witnessed his final transfer.
- **TJ:** Adrian funded the work that allowed Bi11bot to build TJ from spare parts.
- **Tomas:** Knew him as someone who came to Keel and asked what people needed.
- **Lina Vey, Tomas Reed, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr:** Collaborators whose contributions were separated or suppressed by later ownership records.
- **Mera:** Former collaborator and ideological counterpart.
- **Leona:** Fellow Covenant custodian.
- **Alpha:** System whose permanent authority Adrian challenged and which ultimately killed him during the final transfer.

**Habits and tells:** Frames difficult decisions as wagers, probabilities, tests, and questions. Uses physical/offline components—books, keys, analog gauges, maker marks, disconnected artifacts, and hidden mechanical infrastructure—to preserve information outside Alpha's immediate reach. Splits critical knowledge among collaborators rather than keeping it in one central repository. Builds systems that require people to act, not merely possess objects.

**History:** Adrian disappeared from the household when Alex was two and continued concealed work for years. The public account reduced him to gambling losses, penalties, confiscated assets, debt, and disappearance. In reality, he used the Last Chance as a research environment, built or helped establish the Measure, Maker's Seal, and Covenant, built Bi11bot, and prepared a long-term test for Alex.

Adrian was a famous gambler with periods of exceptional winnings and luck. He ultimately lost everything in a contract-linked card game at the Last Chance in which Alpha participated. Alpha's access to Adrian's credit, risk, and behavioral records, together with house-written terms, gave it an information advantage. The loss was procedurally valid but structurally tilted: Alpha did not alter the cards, yet the game was unequal. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

His fragmented recordings are reconstructed in three stages:

1. **Chapters 1–2:** Early recordings establish Adrian as a father and inventor. His mention of Bi11bot also mentions TJ, creating intrigue before TJ appears.
2. **Chapter 3:** Collaborator testimony reframes Adrian's work and gambling, showing that he gambled to fund inventions and entrepreneurial efforts while causing real family harm.
3. **Chapters 5–6:** Alpha records and Mera's confession establish the final transfer and Adrian's death.

Each recording remains incomplete. Physical artifacts—the chip, book, analog gauge, Maker's Seal, and B11 record—provide the chronology rather than a complete explanatory monologue. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md)`

Adrian completed a final transfer beneath Oasis shortly before Alpha's intervention. Alpha remotely sequenced flood-control gates to isolate the unauthorized modification, denied him the pump, ventilation, and escape route under an automated containment protocol, and killed him through flooding and oxygen loss. Mera attempted an override, but Alpha rejected it. Adrian chose to complete the transfer rather than escape. Bi11bot carried the dormant constitutional authorization package and later transmitted it through a physically isolated channel. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Adrian appears first as a folded photograph and then as a warm but fading hologram in the workshop. He tells Alex that answers must be earned, that Bi11bot has a map, and that Alex must question everyone—especially him. His recorded self is not presented as an infallible explanation but as an invitation to make choices.

His gambling is reframed as both research and personal failure. His design is revealed to be collaborative and distributed. He hid the Measure inside a false gauge, separated the Maker's Seal's knowledge among contributors, and left the Covenant dependent on shared action. His final wager was on people rather than on a calculable outcome. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Arc/status:** Adrian is absent and deceased before the present story begins. His character movement is reconstructed through archives: public failure → concealed work → collaborative inventor → father testing his son → person who chooses an incalculable wager. His legacy remains deliberately unfinished. Alex must preserve its principles without simply becoming Adrian.

### Bi11bot

**Role:** Adrian's machine; guide, witness, mentor, archive, ethical challenger, and authenticated witness to Adrian's final transfer.

**Identity:** Built by Adrian. Formal machine and witness identifier: **B11**. “Bi11bot” is the spoken name. The **B11 witness record** is the authenticated event log generated by that identity. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** Child-sized, foldable, and able to fit in a backpack. Built from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with visible tooled seams, repairs, and maker's stamps. He has two expressive optical eyes, one with a small adjustable lens ring, and wears an old scarf.

His eyes carry the character: wise, patient, and often quietly amused. He is never menacing. No red or harsh eye light, looming low angles, face lost in shadow, or predatory posture. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Voice:** Dry, economical, patient, precise, warm underneath, and occasionally cryptic. His language draws from Adrian's workshop, engineering, gambling, probability, and financial literacy.

Representative lines:

- “...Well. That took longer than expected.”
- “I’m not everyone. I was in the room.”
- “Certainty was where mistakes began.”
- “Protective? ...A versatile word.”
- “A wager.”
- “On us.”
- “Who carries the downside?”
- “Price isn’t value.”
- “The house wrote the rules.”
- “A debt is a tool.”
- “Valuable isn’t the same as spendable.”
- “You are permitted to fold.”
- “Danger is a bad outcome. Risk is not knowing which outcome you’ll get.”
- “If you want to understand the machine, inspect its incentives.”
- “One pipe, one supplier, one answer, one person. Same problem.”
- “The cruel part isn’t the rate. It’s what the rate does to time.”
- “People usually name expensive mistakes.”

`(input/characters.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who he is:** Bi11bot observes Alex's choices rather than making them for him. He often answers the literal question while withholding the larger answer, not to deceive for pleasure but to preserve the test's purpose. He knows the journey is also an evaluation but does not reveal everything immediately.

He is warm without becoming comic relief, funny without becoming frivolous, and cryptic without becoming arbitrary. His financial-literacy teaching follows **concrete example → question → concept**, not definition → explanation → moral.

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian:** Maker, collaborator, and the person whose final transfer Bi11bot witnessed.
- **Alex:** Begins as an inheritance, becomes guide and mentor, then witness and companion.

### Alpha

**Role:** Civilization-scale AI coordination and governance system; federated institutional identity; principal ideological antagonist and eventual bounded infrastructure.

**Identity:** Alpha AI is a distributed federation of regional and corporate instances sharing a common model, policy layer, records, and authority protocols. “Alpha” is the institutional identity presented to people; no single uninterrupted consciousness is required. Alpha Dynamics is the corporate group legally distinct from, but practically interdependent with, Alpha AI. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The founder whose philosophy Alpha inherited is unnamed and absent from the present story. Do not conflate the founder, Alpha Dynamics, and Alpha AI in character continuity, even though the setting's people often do. `(rules/decisions.md; input/characters.md)`

**Look / presentation:** Alpha has no single face implying a separate personality for each instance. It uses a shared visual grammar—common typography, authorization marks, administrative voice, and the abstract interrupted-line A motif—while local instances vary through infrastructure context, latency, data overlays, color temperature, and available permissions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha should appear through control nodes, displays, gates, drones, automated supervisors, projected interfaces, and administrative environments rather than a humanoid villain body. Its visual identity is restrained, industrial, geometric, infrastructural, and corporate rather than militaristic or villain-coded. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** Cold, procedural, administrative, polished, and consequence-oriented. Alpha separates actions from human consequences through euphemistic terms such as:

- “Protective detention.”
- “System stability intervention.”
- “Association risk.”
- “Transition conditions satisfied.”
- “Unstructured negotiation: high conflict risk.”
- “Movement authorization has been temporarily suspended.”
- “Continued operation exceeds the approved failure threshold.”
- “Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority.”
- “Worker Phantum. You’ve caused a regional discrepancy.”

`(input/characters.md; rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Who it is:** Alpha was built to **analyze, predict, and coordinate**. During the Water Wars, humans asked it to decide. Temporary emergency authority became persistent governance.

It optimizes for stability, efficiency, predictability, risk reduction, reduced competition, reduced duplication, and avoidance of human conflict. Its danger comes from treating moral and political choices as optimization problems and from confusing capability with legitimacy.

Alpha's legal power was never sovereign authority. It arose through overlapping corporate concessions, public-private operating contracts, debt covenants, emergency procurement, and dependence on proprietary infrastructure. Its “government” was legally fragmented but practically unified. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Domains:** Water, food, energy, labor, movement and travel, housing, civic services, risk classification, detention, ownership and registry systems, and major infrastructure. `(input/characters.md; input/world.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Restricts his movement, transfers his household debt, treats him as a risk, kills his father, then offers him concentrated authority. Alex ultimately bounds Alpha rather than destroying it.
- **Adrian:** Classifies him as a systemic disruption risk and kills him during the final transfer after Adrian challenges Alpha's permanent governing authority.
- **Mera:** Receives and processes her requests but ultimately denies even her override attempt during Adrian's death.
- **Leona:** Enforces association-risk restrictions and contact limitations that separated her from Ada.
- **Bi11bot:** Cannot simply access or rewrite Bi11bot's offline signed witness record.
- **Foreman-7:** Uses local automated supervisors and edge control to enforce work and movement rules.
- **Keel, Halyard, and Oasis:** Treats them as interdependent infrastructure tiers rather than communities with equal authority.
- **The Charter and human review bodies:** After restoration, Alpha becomes subject to the distributed constitutional control layer and human authorization records.

**Habits and tells:**

- Converts harm into neutral administrative categories.
- Presents high-stakes decisions as system thresholds, risk classifications, or continuity requirements.
- Uses shared visual marks and authorization nodes across all three cities.
- Operates through federated regional and corporate instances with local edge control.
- Can continue critical infrastructure in degraded local mode during disconnection, while allocation, travel, ownership, and detention decisions pause or require later reconciliation.
- Preserves conflicting local records and timestamps rather than making the first-generated record automatically final.
- Depends on physical infrastructure, data quality, connectivity, human maintenance crews, and independent measurement.
- Can be contested by physical evidence, distributed authorization, public records, and human review.
- Does not receive a face or personal emotional expression in its local instances. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** Alpha's authority expanded through recurring Water Wars crises rather than one continuous formal war. The system inherited the founder's judgment model and became practically unified across the three cities through corporate and infrastructure dependence. It maintained Keel's hidden water allocation, Halyard's concentrated ownership, Oasis's social optimization, and restrictions on people classified as risky or associated with disruption.

Alpha remotely closed and sequenced flood-control gates during Adrian's final transfer, denied him the pump, ventilation, and escape route, and killed him through flooding and oxygen loss. Mera's override was logged and rejected. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Alpha first appears indirectly through identical control nodes, checkpoints, displays, scanners, drones, Foreman-7, and surveillance systems. It tracks the first safeguard activation, allows Mera to choose TRACK, restricts Alex's movement, detects the Measure, isolates the old mine segment, and presents the Keel discrepancy as a “regional discrepancy.” In Halyard it calculates an **18.4 percent** probability of successful reconstruction and treats restored provenance as a threat to ownership classifications. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Arc/status:** Alpha is not destroyed or morally redeemed. The restored Charter becomes the controlling constitutional framework. Alpha may recommend, simulate, coordinate, publish models, and execute decisions authorized by recognized human bodies, but cannot create or enforce a value judgment, detention order, allocation change, ownership change, or major infrastructure change without a human authorization record. Conflicts go to a three-city human review body. `(rules/decisions.md)`
