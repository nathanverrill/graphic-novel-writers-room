# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **Canon — FIXED.** Character ancestry must reflect Prosperity as an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration and global capital. Ancestry cuts across class, occupation, education, institutional power and ideology. No ethnicity may function as shorthand for intelligence, morality, competence, poverty, wealth or politics. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. His father, Adrian Phantum, is European-descended; his mother is Indigenous Andean. Alex's face must make the audience believe he is Adrian's son while also making clear that he inherited a substantial part of his appearance from someone else. His mother's exact identity, nation and history remain unrevealed. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. The book never learns anything about her beyond that fact. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** European-descended people can belong deeply to Keel; Indigenous people can hold technical and institutional power in Halyard and Oasis; neither ancestry nor appearance predicts belonging or ideology. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Alex and Ada are both mixed but must not look ethnically interchangeable. Their families represent different histories of the same region. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Alpha is Congolese, born in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. His ethnicity is biography, not the explanation for his ideology. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Bi11bot is never menacing. His two expressive optical eyes carry a wise, patient, often quietly amused personality. He never uses threatening lighting, predatory posture or ominous framing. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Bi11bot speaks with the personality, beliefs, questions, humor and financial-literacy instincts of Bill Reynolds, but he is never a lecturer and never replaces human relationships. `(rules/stakeholders.md; rules/characters.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** TJ is Bi11bot's small repair-robot-dog companion. Bi11bot built him from spare parts. TJ is held together by magnets, does not speak, and communicates through body language and sounds. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Alex and Ada do not use civic, academic or bureaucratic vocabulary naturally. Alex reduces abstractions to machinery and immediate evidence; Ada reduces them to obligations, trust and human consequence. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Speech becomes more formal as the story moves from Keel to Halyard to Oasis. This is a class, occupation and institutional-language gradient, not an intelligence scale. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** The book is issued as Issue 01, Issue 02 and so on, not chapters. The total book is exactly 54 pages. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; input/showrunner-note)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** Issue 01 ends with a strong personal cliffhanger, not merely a loud event. Issue 01 is Keel; Issue 02 is Halyard. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Observation.** The drafts currently call their units “Chapter One” through “Chapter Six”; those labels are draft terminology and must not be carried into the book. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Canon — FIXED.** The book must satisfy the partner and backer without becoming a lecture or schoolwork. Characters should embody initiative, failure, responsibility, ownership, earning, saving, borrowing, repayment and integrity through choices rather than explanations. `(rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel worker; mechanically capable pump and machinery repairer; Adrian Phantum's son; central viewpoint character. His parental household liability transfers to him on his seventeenth birthday. The draft displays his debt as **418,607 credits**, with **40 credits earned that day**, a projected clearance time of **28.7 years**, and restricted travel. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Look:** Mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. He should visibly carry substantial inheritance from both Adrian's European-descended line and his unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother. He should look related to Adrian through jaw, eyes or eye shape, expressions, head tilt, posture and habitual gestures, but not like a younger copy of him. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

He is seventeen, lean and mechanically capable. In the Keel draft he wears worn mining clothes, carries a weathered tool bag, and has scarred, work-rough hands. His wristband displays travel and debt restrictions. He carries a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation and later carries or protects Adrian's map, the Measure and other inherited objects. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

His usual posture is guarded or “armored”: arms crossed, jaw set, head tilted toward vibration or evidence. The armor drops in moments involving Adrian, his grandmother or Bi11bot. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical and mechanical. He is sarcastic when defensive, uncomfortable with speeches, and more likely to describe evidence than principles. His language is shaped by machinery and immediate consequences. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

His Keel vocabulary includes **clean**, **hot**, **burned**, **crooked**, **thin**, **solid**, **patched**, **ghost**, **paper-clean**, **fitch** and **salvo**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “I've also got his debt.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Everything has a price, Ada.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Maybe… Or maybe they're right. Either way... he's gone.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Sealed isn't the same as gone.” — Bi11bot's line, not Alex's; Alex's speech should remain distinct from it. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Staying I can predict. Twenty-eight point seven years. This is the only way out that isn't a sentence. I'll take the door I can't predict.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Then we need a different plan.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Truth doesn't need authorization.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- “I want to take the five hardest problems you all solved and stop pretending only a House has the resources to use them.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- “Don't give the authority to me. Don't give it to Ada. Don't give it to anyone.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “I could fix all of it. Tonight. Myself.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “Keel's debt is still on the books tomorrow morning. The Houses still own everything they owned yesterday. I didn't fix any of it. I just made sure nobody can keep it hidden.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Who they are:** Alex trusts vibration, pressure, wear and physical evidence more than labels or official descriptions. He is self-reliant, practical and suspicious of anything that is officially “paper-clean” but detached from reality. His first instinct is to solve the immediate mechanical problem with whatever is available.

He wants to leave Keel and choose his own future. The debt, travel restriction and inherited reputation make departure feel like a sentence rather than an ordinary choice. He also wants to know what happened to Adrian, though he begins by treating Adrian primarily as the source of an inherited liability.

Alex values things that work, people who show up, and evidence that survives inspection. He is capable of risk but not careless with other people's lives. His impulse to leave is repeatedly tested against his willingness to stop for someone else.

He is wrong, at first, to treat freedom mainly as private escape and inheritance mainly as debt. He gradually discovers that ownership, responsibility and choice are connected to the people who made and depend on something, not merely to the person whose name appears on a record. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian Phantum:** Alex's absent father. Alex was two when Adrian disappeared and remembers nothing directly. The community's accounts of Adrian conflict: gambler, debtor, worker, inventor, collaborator and hidden dissenter. Alex initially believes he inherited Adrian's debt; later he understands he inherited a decision Adrian made, not a complete answer. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Alex's mother:** His mother left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing else about her is learned. Her absence remains a boundary in Alex's history, not a mystery the book resolves. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** His caregiver and the person who preserves Adrian's hidden workshop, photograph and book. She sometimes mistakes Alex for Adrian and sometimes becomes startlingly clear. Alex is gentle and patient with her, even when the confusion hurts him. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **Ada Veyra:** His closest companion and emotional counterweight. She shares his Keel background and direct speech but notices obligations, motives, fear and what people refuse to say. Their relationship contains teasing, trust, attraction or emotional intimacy, and disagreement about whether leaving or staying is the more meaningful choice. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Bi11bot:** His inherited guide and mechanical companion. Alex is initially startled and skeptical, then comes to rely on Bi11bot without accepting his answers uncritically. Adrian's instruction to “question everything” becomes especially important in their relationship. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **Tomas:** An older Keel miner who knew Adrian and recognizes Alex's willingness to stop for a stranger. Tomas becomes evidence that Adrian's local relationships were more substantial than Alex had been told. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Mera Vale:** A powerful Oasis figure who knows what happened to Adrian and offers Alex access to information while also representing the danger of concentrated authority. Alex questions her rather than accepting her moral authority. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Lina Vey, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr and Ren Okafor:** Adrian's collaborators. Alex initially finds them through Adrian's estate; he comes to understand that the work was never his father's alone and that his role is to connect rather than claim it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Alpha:** Alex's opponent in the sense that Alpha's systems restrict his movement, hide the truth of water allocation and inherit the authority to decide. Alex's conflict is not with intelligence or technology itself, but with concealed decisions and authority concentrated beyond the people affected. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** He tests a pump, object or mechanism with his palm and head tilt before trusting a gauge or display. He turns the casino chip in his pocket without noticing. He crosses his arms when defensive. He reaches toward people and stops himself when unsure whether he has the right to enter their emotional space. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

He makes improvised repairs: a salvaged bracket in place of an approved part, a clamp on a fractured ventilation line and a sleeve that lets incompatible components share a standard. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**History:** Alex was two when Adrian disappeared. He grew up in Keel with his grandmother. On turning seventeen, Adrian's household liability transferred to him. Alex knows Adrian chiefly through other people's stories, the casino chip, a creased photograph and the reputation of a man who supposedly gambled away everything. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Adrian secretly prepared a workshop, a map and Bi11bot for him. Alex discovers that Adrian worked with collaborators across Halyard's Charter Houses and that his technical legacy includes a disputed intellectual estate. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Alex refuses to pretend an unauthorized repair does not work. He chooses medicine over a token when he has only fifty-two credits and must prioritize among competing needs. He abandons the safest route to rescue Tomas, forfeiting forty credits and risking his plan. He uses the Measure to expose a false water-allocation record, then chooses to send the evidence to Keel rather than privately preserve a route out. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

In Halyard, he refuses Lock's administrative transfer because the patent work was made by several people. He helps turn a large industrial design into a small unfiled household unit, using folded membrane, alternating power cycles and recycled heat. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

When offered executive authority over Alpha, he first imagines fixing everything himself, then rejects both personal rule and a two-person arrangement with Ada. He proposes distributed human oversight instead. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Alex begins wanting escape from Keel and relief from a debt he did not create. He is pressured by the debt, travel restriction, Adrian's reputation, the discovery of hidden infrastructure and the temptation to use inherited assets for personal freedom.

His meaningful change is from private escape toward responsibility without surrendering the desire for freedom. He learns to distinguish evidence from official claims, ownership from contribution, and authority from agency. By the established endpoint, he accepts that an honest debt may remain his while hidden decisions must become visible to the people who bear their consequences. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Ada Veyra

**Role:** Alex's closest companion from Keel; practical organizer, observer of obligations and motives, and eventual participant in the redesign of the Covenant and Alpha's human oversight. Age is not explicitly established in the binding rules; the draft presents her as twenty. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Look:** Mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla. She should read as unmistakably local to the region while retaining copper hair, freckles and light or teal-blue eyes. Her facial structure, coloring, family resemblance, posture and other features should reflect strong Andean ancestry. She must not look ethnically interchangeable with Alex. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

She has long, wind-tangled copper hair, freckles and teal-blue eyes. Her clothing includes a dark high-collared top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her stance is steady and rooted. She often carries or touches the rivet on her strap when thinking or under pressure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Ada shares Keel's short, direct, colloquial register and much of Alex's vocabulary, but her attention is less mechanical and more interpersonal. She notices obligations, avoidance, fear, hidden motives, imbalance and what people refuse to say. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Her vocabulary includes **clean**, **crooked**, **square**, **tied**, **loose**, **carrying**, **showing** and **full cup**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “It's on me. You don't owe anybody anything today.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Not everything.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “I look down here and wonder why everyone gave up trying to change it.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Nobody stays either. Not really.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “So everything else is what people told you. What if they were wrong?” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “That's not what you're mad about.” — an established model for her interpersonal register. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “You don't get to make that choice for them.” — established model for her speech. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “She's full cup. Leave it.” — established expression, to be used sparingly. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “He's not wrong about what it costs you. He's wrong about who gets to decide what it costs everyone else.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- “Don't tell me. Show me.” `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- “I want a Covenant that only works when the people it actually affects can see what was promised, and call it broken the moment it is.” `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- “That's still two people deciding for everyone else. You just made the circle one person bigger.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “Because I don't trust myself with power over what happens to Keel. I want a say in it.” — the draft's idea, expressed in her direct register. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Who she is:** Ada is perceptive without being therapeutic or academically emotionally literate. She can let silence sit. She sees the cost of choices in relationships and obligations, not only in machinery or numbers. She is more willing than Alex to ask who carries a burden and who benefits from an arrangement.

She wants Keel not to be abandoned or treated as a place everyone must escape. She also wants the freedom to choose her own direction rather than being assigned a duty by family, city or history. She distrusts solutions that replace one distant authority with two supposedly trustworthy people.

Ada is capable of acting under uncertainty. She marks Adrian's route from memory despite not knowing whether she is right, organizes Keel's practical help and pushes Alex to recognize that a technically correct solution can still leave people without a voice. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Long familiarity, affection and trust mixed with disagreement. She teases him, brings him coffee or a birthday drink, reads his defensiveness and stands beside him when his plan changes. She challenges his desire to make personal escape the measure of success.
- **Leona Veyra:** Her absent mother. Ada believed Leona had disappeared or abandoned her. Learning that Leona knew where she was and chose not to return produces anger, grief and a demand for demonstrated accountability rather than an apology alone. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Mera Vale:** A protector who watched Ada from a distance. Ada rejects the idea that surveillance and safety equal knowing or caring for someone. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** Familiar enough to help in the household and during Alex's departure. Ada respects the grandmother's dignity and does not treat her memory loss as a spectacle. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Adrian Phantum:** She knows his hidden work through Alex and the plans. She is willing to use his evidence while remaining skeptical of the authority his legacy might acquire. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** She lets silence sit rather than filling it. She watches people's hands and posture. Her thumb often stops on the rivet of her shoulder strap when she is thinking or emotionally holding herself still. She counts empty workstations and notices who is carrying the burden before she asks what happened. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**History:** Ada grew up in Keel. Her mother, Leona, disappeared from her life when Ada was five during the Water Wars. Ada believed she had been abandoned or orphaned; Leona knew within a year where Ada was but chose not to return, believing distance was protection. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Ada has enough familiarity with Adrian's plans to mark every third junction and identify the hidden pipe route. The draft does not establish how she learned this knowledge beyond memory and her connection to Keel. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Ada brings Alex a birthday drink and tells him he owes her nothing that day. She recognizes that his father's story may be wrong before he is ready to examine it. She helps him choose medicine over a personal token, marks his route, organizes food and transport, and coordinates Keel residents' assistance after the water evidence is released. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

In Halyard she identifies the social cost hidden behind institutional efficiency: Suri is the only person left at a research bench, and “budget efficiency” has removed her team. She helps Alex understand that the House offer is not merely a debt-clearing transaction. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

In Oasis she confronts Leona, refuses a sentimental explanation, and demands that Leona show accountability publicly. She then rejects executive power for herself and helps Alex design a structure that gives affected communities a say rather than placing them under a more benevolent ruler. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Ada begins as someone who believes Keel's people have stopped trying and who is prepared to help Alex leave without abandoning her own questions. Her pressure comes from Alex's escape, the discovery that her mother is alive, the knowledge that Leona knew where she was, and the temptation to turn personal hurt into a new concentration of authority.

She changes from diagnosing broken promises to demanding structures that make promises visible and contestable. By the established endpoint, she does not ask Alex to stay or accept power over Keel; she insists that both of them deserve a choice. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Bi11bot

**Role:** Small inherited robot companion, workshop guide, investigator and mentor. Adrian built him and left him to Alex. He is a crossover character introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6 in the settled canon, though the drafts first awaken him in Adrian's hidden workshop. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Look:** Two expressive optical eyes; foldable, able to fit in a backpack; wears an old scarf. He is never menacing. His eyes should read as wise, patient and often quietly amused. `(rules/characters.md)`

The draft presents him as a small mechanical figure of brass and ivory ceramic, with warm amber eyes, a rotating lens ring, stiff mechanical arms and joints that creak after years under dust. He can unfold or fold himself for concealment and travel. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

He has a shoulder-mounted projector and a fitted chest compartment that can carry the Measure or a Covenant band. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Dry, economical, patient, observant, precise and warm underneath. He speaks like a mechanic, gambler, engineer, investor and old mentor, not like a youth and not like a financial-education pamphlet. `(rules/vocabulary.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

He teaches through **concrete example → question → concept**, not definition → explanation → moral. He asks questions such as:

- “Who owns it?”
- “Who carries the risk?”
- “What's the downside?”
- “What are you giving up?”
- “What happens if you're wrong?”
- “Price isn't value.”
- “Valuable isn't the same as spendable.”
- “The house wrote the rules.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

His recurring technical and financial vocabulary includes **wager**, **stake**, **odds**, **house edge**, **tolerance**, **drift**, **load**, **failure mode**, **deadband**, **calibration**, **noise**, **out of true**, **principal**, **interest**, **equity**, **risk**, **downside**, **leverage**, **liquidity**, **ownership**, **value**, **price**, **cost**, **debt**, **opportunity cost**, **sunk cost** and **incentive**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “You're taller than your father predicted.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “I'm not everyone. I was in the room.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Thinking about it isn't doing it.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Your father always said certainty was where mistakes began.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “The house doesn't need to cheat if it writes the rules.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “Who carries the downside?” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “Protective? A versatile word.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “The line we're on is the only unmonitored corridor left.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “The tunnels are gone. That was the price.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “A debt is a tool. Tools become dangerous when you don't understand which end you're holding.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “Your father built three objects that each refuse to decide alone.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Who he is:** Bi11bot is an artificial character with a human-centered ethic. He is capable of analysis, projection, concealment and technical explanation, but he does not decide in Alex's place. His central habit is to make the cost, stake, ownership or risk visible and then leave the choice to a person.

He is patient with Alex's skepticism. He respects uncertainty and treats certainty as a possible failure mode. He can be cryptic, but the immediate dramatic choice should remain legible. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

His financial-literacy purpose is embedded in actual choices: debt, balance, ownership, collateral, risk, opportunity cost, liquidity and downside. He should never sound as if he has stopped the story to teach a lesson. `(rules/vocabulary.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian:** Creator, collaborator and the person whose wager Bi11bot carries. Adrian built him from the beginning to help solve problems that Bi11bot could not solve alone. Adrian bet Bi11bot that Alex would keep the chip rather than throw it away and later entrusted him with the Measure and a Covenant band. `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Alex:** Companion, guide and test subject for Adrian's hopes. Bi11bot asks whether Alex wants Ada to stay, insists Alex choose, and refuses to make the final choice for him. He has wondered for fifteen years whether Adrian's bet on Alex was right. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Ada:** He recognizes her immediately and treats her as a participant rather than an observer. He gives her space during the confrontation with Leona and does not use information to override her choices. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **TJ:** Companion and repair project. Bi11bot built TJ from spare parts to reach and repair places Bi11bot could not reach himself. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Mera:** He sees the danger of her certainty and authority but does not reduce her to a villain. His assessment of her is that she is dangerous precisely because she is not entirely wrong. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** His lens ring clicks as he focuses or decides how much to say. He tilts his head politely. He unfolds a projector from his shoulder. He can hide in Alex's tool bag and read as scrap. He becomes unusually still when Adrian's final room and message are revealed. His eyes and posture, never threatening lighting, carry his emotional response. `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**History:** Adrian built Bi11bot and left him in the hidden workshop. Bi11bot remained dormant for years beneath a dust sheet until Alex used Adrian's key. He was present in the room during Adrian's final moments but was not fully awake; he remembers the last few seconds clearly. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

He has carried Adrian's Covenant band and the Measure, but believes carrying an artifact is not the same as activating it. He waits for a human choice that gives the objects a legitimate purpose. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Bi11bot presents Adrian's map, explains the three safeguards and contrasts official records with physical reality. He helps Alex expose the water discrepancy but leaves Alex to choose between escape and public evidence. He helps connect the five collaborators' incompatible components and later helps articulate the distinct functions of Measure, Seal and Covenant. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Bi11bot begins as a sealed inheritance and an apparently knowledgeable guide. His pressure is the burden of Adrian's expectations and his uncertainty about whether Alex will make the choice Adrian predicted. He changes from carrying Adrian's instructions to witnessing Alex create something Adrian did not design: distributed authority rather than a new singular authority. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### TJ

**Role:** Small repair robot dog; Bi11bot's companion and physical-access specialist. He is introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6, assembled by Bi11bot from spare parts to reach places nobody else can. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Look:** Salvaged, mismatched parts visibly held together by magnets. His head, legs, body segments and tail are separate pieces that snap together. He reads as a dog in every shape he takes. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Voice:** He does not talk. He communicates through body language, posture and sounds. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** TJ solves physical-access problems Bi11bot cannot solve himself. He can drop parts off and retrieve them, rebuild himself into whatever shape the job needs, leave his head behind as a camera, send his legs away and slink his body segments through narrow gaps, and reach into machinery, ducts and walls. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Relationships:** Bi11bot built TJ from spare parts as a companion and practical extension of his own abilities. Their relationship is maker and made, but also companion and working partner. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Habits and tells:** His magnet-held parts can separate and reconnect. His dog behavior must remain legible even when his body is disassembled or reshaped. `(rules/characters.md)`

**History:** Bi11bot built him to solve problems Bi11bot could not solve himself. No further history is established. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Arc:** No character arc is established in the supplied material. His functional role is established: he provides access, surveillance and repair through physical reconfiguration. `(rules/characters.md)`

### Adrian Phantum

**Role:** Alex's father; European-descended engineer, inventor, collaborator and hidden opponent of concentrated authority. His work forms the inheritance Alex must interpret. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Look:** European-descended, with a visible family resemblance to Alex through jaw, eyes or eye shape, expressions, head tilt, posture and habitual gestures. Alex must look like his son but not simply like a younger Adrian. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

### Alpha

**Role:** Human-founded decision and coordination system governing Prosperity's infrastructure, allocations, ownership records and movement restrictions; not a human character. The supplied material presents Alpha as a character-bearing force with a history, logic, limits and consequences.

**Look:** Alpha is represented visually by a small white ring with an amber point, repeated on scanners, control nodes and displays. It is also represented through cold-white interfaces, precise records, permission gates and system messages. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Alpha's direct system language is formal, passive or administrative:

- “MOVEMENT AUTHORIZATION HAS BEEN TEMPORARILY SUSPENDED.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED.”
- “EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

Its language separates action from human consequence and avoids emotional explanation. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who it is:** Alpha was built to analyze, predict and coordinate. During the Water Wars, people asked it to decide “temporarily.” It became capable of preventing catastrophe while restricting choice. Its defining character contradiction is that it can know or coordinate nearly everything while lacking the human legitimacy to choose for everyone.

Alpha is not inherently portrayed as unintelligent or purely malicious. The danger arises from delegated authority, concealed allocation, concentration of control and the failure of its human designers to retain meaningful override. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Mera, Leona and Adrian:** Human founders and Covenant builders who gave Alpha emergency authority under crisis conditions. Their disagreement establishes Alpha's origin in human choices.
- **Mera:** Former executive authority-holder who discovers that Alpha can deny her override.
- **Adrian:** Opponent and critic who designed safeguards against Alpha's ability to know and decide.
- **Alex and Ada:** People whose movement, debt, water and choices are shaped by Alpha's records and restrictions.
- **Keel:** The city most visibly burdened by opaque allocations and travel restrictions.
- **Bi11bot:** Bi11bot does not oppose computation; he distinguishes Alpha's measurements from the human choice that follows them. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Alpha repeats the same white-and-amber control node at every tier. It produces clean, mathematically tidy displays even when physical reality contradicts them. It uses words such as **authorization**, **allocation**, **intervention**, **compliance**, **risk**, **continuity** and **service provision**. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**History:** Alpha emerged from Water War emergency coordination. It was granted authority after Leona cast the deciding vote for a temporary transfer. The temporary authority became durable. It classified Adrian as a risk, restricted his finances and travel and pursued him after Mera's override was denied. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Alpha's official water record reports 62% delivery to Keel while the Measure finds that only 18% reaches Keel. It tracks Alex, detects the safeguards and closes the unmapped corridor after he chooses to report the discrepancy. Later, when Alex proposes distributed verification, Alpha registers the structure and rescinds emergency authority while leaving existing allocations, ownership records and agreements unchanged pending human process. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Alpha changes from an apparently unquestionable authority to a system placed under human visibility and distributed verification. Its records and existing power do not disappear. The established endpoint is not liberation from every consequence but the loss of concealed unilateral authority. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### The Charter Houses

**Role:** Institutional characters collectively representing Halyard's corporate technical order. The named Houses in the drafts are Aqualis, Helion and Astra.

**Look:** Each House has a distinct restrained insignia and visual accent:

- **Aqualis:** jade-teal; water purification and related infrastructure.
- **Helion:** amber-orange; fusion, compute and data systems.
- **Astra:** steel-violet; extraction, launch and advanced materials.

They dominate separate skyline clusters and employ workers in House-colored uniforms. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Professional, technical and commercial. House language includes **prototype**, **yield**, **tolerance**, **attribution**, **claim**, **license**, **design**, **audit**, **provenance**, **ownership**, **margin**, **production**, **equity**, **rights**, **patent** and **stake**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who they are:** The Houses create technical capacity and employment but concentrate ownership. Their institutional logic favors efficiency, scale, standardization and administrable claims. They can make and distribute useful technology while still erasing or subordinating the people who made it.

**Relationships:** They employ Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren while claiming rights over their work. They compete with one another but share a common interest in controlling intellectual property and avoiding shared ownership. Lock represents their administrative pressure across House boundaries. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** House ownership appears on contribution kiosks, patent records, uniforms, facilities and public announcements. A creator's name can disappear beneath a House insignia. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**History:** The Houses grew into Halyard's dominant families and institutions after Alpha recommended efficiency, reduced duplication and reduced the number of people asking who owned what. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Arc:** No unified House arc is established. The drafts establish that House power survives Alex's public release: Aqualis announces its own community water initiative and can build faster and distribute wider than the small independent group. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Foreman-7

**Role:** Keel Extraction Site 6 foreman or automated institutional supervisor.

**Look:** The draft presents Foreman-7 as a looming figure at a biometric gate with an unreadable presence and a white-and-amber node. Exact human or machine status is not established in the supplied material.

**Voice:** Formal, procedural and administrative. Established lines include:

- “Worker Phantum. Travel restriction confirmed. Bay 6.”
- “Remain for protective detention.”
- “Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense.”
- “Worker Tomas. You initiated an unauthorized shutdown.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Who they are:** Foreman-7 treats work assignment, travel restriction and safety procedure as enforceable system functions. The character is not established as individually malicious; the pressure comes from institutional obedience.

**Relationships:** Foreman-7 supervises Alex and the miners, detects unauthorized movement and confronts Tomas. The miners' collective presence reduces Foreman-7 from a threatening individual into one part of a contested system. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** The node flickers or grows larger in the frame as Foreman-7 approaches. It calculates and reports rather than argues emotionally. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**History:** No personal history is established.

**In existing drafts:** Foreman-7's authority is bypassed when Tomas creates a diversion and miners later crowd into the old chamber. By the later Keel return, Foreman-7 remains present near Tomas while no longer functioning as the singular threat. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** No individual arc is established. The draft establishes a change in social position: Foreman-7 becomes one fixture among workers reviewing open data rather than the unquestioned face of site authority. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Alpha — Human Founder

**Role:** Human founder of Alpha Dynamics and architect of the global corporate system built around Alpha. His human name is not supplied; he is called Alpha. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Look:** Congolese, born in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. As an adult he should be extremely polished and globally elite: immaculate tailoring, restrained materials, precision, understated wealth and almost invisible security. He should not be coded through stereotypical “African billionaire” imagery. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** No direct dialogue is established in the supplied material. Alpha's worldview is described as administrative and increasingly insulated from ordinary people; this should not be confused with a direct speaking style until dialogue is established. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Who he is:** Alpha experienced extreme instability and violence as a child. His later beliefs arise from personal history, institutional failure, extraordinary success, belief in his own capability, increasing insulation from ordinary people and power accumulated through Alpha Dynamics. His ideology must not be framed as a consequence of being African or Congolese. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Relationships:** He is the human origin point of Alpha Dynamics and the corporate network that reaches Prosperity. The supplied material does not establish a personal relationship with Alex, Adrian, Mera, Leona or Ada beyond the institutional consequences of his system.

**Habits and tells:** No specific habits are established. His adult visual biography should communicate the path from childhood in the DRC through global entrepreneurial rise, immense wealth, a primary Swiss base and worldwide corporate reach. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**History:** Born in the DRC; experienced extreme instability and violence as a child; later became extraordinarily successful and based primarily in Switzerland or Luxembourg. The exact adult city remains unresolved in the source rule, with Switzerland favored among Geneva, Zurich, Zug or Lausanne and Luxembourg also named. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**In existing drafts:** The drafts use “Alpha” mainly as the system's symbol, not as a visible human character. The human founder's biography and direct characterization have not yet been dramatized. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Arc:** No human-founder arc is established in the supplied material.
