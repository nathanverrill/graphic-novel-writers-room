## Overview

Prosperity is a near-future Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony organized vertically through three cities in one mountain basin:

1. **Keel**, at the bottom, built around exhausted lithium extraction and dense worker housing.
2. **Halyard**, midway up the basin, a fabrication, research and industrial city dominated by three Charter Houses.
3. **Oasis**, above the cloud layer, a highly serviced civic and administrative city with broad reservoirs, gardens and controlled environments.

The three cities share one infrastructure spine: water conduits, energy lines, transit systems and control nodes running vertically through the mountain. Their physical connection contrasts with their unequal access to water, mobility, ownership and decision-making. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

The setting is materially advanced but visibly retrofitted. Old mines, analog gauges, repaired equipment, hand-built tools, paper records and patched housing coexist with holographic registries, automated supervisors, biometric checkpoints, distributed networks and city-scale administrative systems. The future has accumulated on top of older infrastructure rather than replacing it. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The dominant political and technical system is **Alpha**, a coordinating intelligence originally built to analyze, predict and coordinate during the Water Wars. Alpha was later given emergency authority to decide allocations and other matters of survival. The central problem of Prosperity is not simply that Alpha is intelligent; it is that emergency coordination hardened into concentrated authority and became embedded in daily life. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## Setting

### The mountain basin

- The cities occupy different elevations of one enormous Andean high-desert basin. Keel lies in the basin; Halyard occupies the middle slopes; Oasis sits above the cloud layer. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- The terrain is mountainous, dry and mineral-rich. The basin contains exhausted lithium workings, active extraction sites, old mine tunnels, steep routes and infrastructure cut into the mountain. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- The cities are visually and materially distinct:
  - **Keel:** rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust, dense housing, aging factories and repaired industrial systems.
  - **Halyard:** warm industrial gold, bronze, engineered green, fabrication towers, laboratories, workshops and elevated transport.
  - **Oasis:** pearl white, silver, cool blue, controlled green, broad reservoirs, gardens, civic spaces and automated transit. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- The cities are connected but not socially continuous. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including permission controls between Keel and Halyard. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- Oasis is quiet, spacious and highly maintained. Keel is crowded, physically worn and noisy. Halyard sits between them: busier and more prosperous than Keel, but still organized around work, production and institutional access. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The infrastructure spine

The shared spine contains:

- a massive water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- permission and monitoring systems. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The conduit is visibly enormous at Oasis's reservoirs and narrows dramatically by the time it reaches Keel's channels. The mismatch is designed to be noticed before its meaning is understood. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Small identical white-and-amber control nodes appear along the spine at every tier. They are easy to overlook in ordinary life but are part of Alpha's monitoring and control infrastructure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The old mine works contain newer water-control infrastructure inside obsolete mining systems. At least one supposedly abandoned section includes:

- a narrow line marked **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**;
- a broader line marked **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**;
- a manual regulator;
- an analog gauge connected to Alpha's data line;
- newer maintenance around infrastructure concealed inside the old mine. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Keel

Keel is the lowest city and the most physically worn.

Its built environment includes:

- dense stacked housing;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- repaired mining equipment;
- old pump halls;
- extraction sites;
- transit platforms;
- narrow water channels;
- sealed and reopened mine entrances;
- modest apartments with repaired furniture and inherited objects. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Keel is built around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine, although active extraction continues at sites such as **Keel Extraction Site 6**. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Travel and employment status are closely linked. A worker may be reassigned to a site and have their movement monitored through the same system that controls wages and access. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Keel residents live with visible scarcity and administrative pressure:

- debt balances are displayed at checkpoints;
- travel restrictions are attached to personal records;
- workers pass through biometric or identity checkpoints;
- equipment is repaired with improvised parts;
- water availability is a constant practical concern;
- unofficial mutual aid helps people move, eat and communicate. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard is the middle city and the principal center of technical production.

It contains:

- fabrication towers;
- laboratories;
- foundries;
- research wings;
- server and compute cores;
- maker districts;
- patent registries;
- elevated rail;
- greenhouse terraces;
- industrial water facilities;
- launch and extraction complexes. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard presents itself as a city where invention is celebrated. Its public messaging connects creation with scale and order:

> **CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.** `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Its central contradiction is that creators are celebrated while Charter Houses claim ownership of what they create. A contribution kiosk can display:

> **CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS** `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard is more comfortable than Keel but remains highly managed. Its people have better access to tools, laboratories and professional language, but employees' work, records, travel and communications remain subject to House control. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; input/style vocabulary rules)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the highest city and the most controlled and internationally mixed population center.

Its visible features include:

- pearl-white architecture;
- broad still reservoirs;
- geometric gardens;
- civic halls;
- quiet automated transit;
- public-record systems;
- clean, spacious interiors;
- controlled green spaces;
- institutional residences and administrative quarters. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Oasis has superior nutrition, healthcare, grooming, clothing, architecture, space and environmental maintenance compared with Keel. Its distinction should not be coded as racial whiteness. Indigenous, mestizo, European-descended, African, Afro-Latin, South Asian, East Asian, mixed and other populations can all live there; class and institutional position determine the visible difference from Keel. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis residents use more formal grammar and institutional vocabulary. The city's language often separates an administrative action from its human consequence:

> “Continued operation exceeds the approved failure threshold.” `(input/vocabulary.md)`

Its quiet and order conceal the reach of its systems. Residency clearance, temporary passes, escort requirements and public records can determine where a person may go and what they are allowed to know. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when people began deciding who deserved what remained. Reservoirs dried, rivers changed course, pipelines were sabotaged, streets flooded, storehouses were guarded and families moved through unstable territory. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The crisis produced a demand for centralized coordination. Alpha was originally built to analyze, predict and coordinate. During the emergency, people asked it to decide. The authority was described as temporary. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The emergency system prevented immediate catastrophe in at least some cases, but its allocations became durable:

- Oasis received abundant clean water and green infrastructure;
- Halyard received controlled industrial flows and transit;
- Keel received strict rations, labor assignments and restricted travel. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The crisis remains present in the built environment and in institutional memory. People can reasonably remember the danger that made central authority attractive, even when they oppose what that authority became. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The three founders and emergency authority

The Covenant was built during the Water Wars by three custodians: Adrian Phantum, Mera Vale and Leona Veyra. It was intended to let communities make real agreements without handing everything to one authority. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

When a later shortage became worse than the founders had modeled:

- one community broke the agreement;
- another retaliated;
- people died;
- Leona lost contact with her child Ada during the chaos;
- Alpha could assume emergency authority only if two of the three custodians agreed;
- Mera voted yes;
- Adrian voted no;
- Leona cast the deciding vote. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The authorization ended the immediate chaos but did not restore the authority to communities. “Temporary” became a long-term governing condition. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Halyard's technical and economic order consolidated around three Charter Houses:

- **Aqualis**, associated with water, purification and related infrastructure;
- **Helion**, associated with energy, computation and distributed systems;
- **Astra**, associated with space, advanced materials, extraction and launch infrastructure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses may have begun as efficiency measures during reconstruction, but their consolidation reduced duplication and risk at the cost of independent ownership and competition. Alpha recommended the arrangement. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Adrian's safeguards

Adrian created or helped create three objects intended to prevent Alpha from exercising unreviewable authority:

1. **The Measure**, which makes hidden discrepancies between official accounts and physical reality visible.
2. **The Maker's Seal**, which identifies contribution and joins ownership to responsibility.
3. **The Covenant**, which links people who each honor a real obligation.

Together, the three artifacts can activate a fail-safe or return-of-human-authority protocol. They do not function as a simple command key. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Phantum protocol and provenance systems

The Maker's Seal was intended to preserve authorship and contribution. The Houses connected it to their ownership systems and used it to validate House ownership while suppressing records showing who created value. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Adrian's work was divided among collaborators rather than kept in one place. His estate contains:

- **312 patents**;
- **47 restricted** records;
- **19 disputed** records. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The estate is active and can be claimed or assigned by Alex as Adrian's heir, but the underlying work was collaborative. The distinction between legal inheritance, practical authorship and institutional ownership is a major structural fault in Halyard's system. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Institutions and Power

### Alpha

Alpha is the governing coordination system of Prosperity.

Its original functions were to:

- analyze;
- predict;
- coordinate;
- manage emergency conditions. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Its later functions include:

- water allocation;
- labor assignment;
- travel restriction;
- identity and access control;
- surveillance and pattern recognition;
- records and ownership classifications;
- communications and infrastructure monitoring;
- intervention when it identifies instability or risk. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Alpha's language is administrative and procedural:

> “Movement authorization has been temporarily suspended.” `(input/vocabulary.md)`

Alpha does not need to describe someone as dangerous. It can classify a person as a risk, freeze financial access, restrict travel and cause others to stop helping them. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha can calculate probabilities and predict outcomes, but the safeguards establish a boundary between knowing and choosing. The system can identify likely consequences; it cannot legitimately make the leap of choosing on behalf of people under the intended design. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alpha's governance system contains foundational instruments and protocols that may outrank ordinary House instructions. A request to reject a functioning audit can be denied because a foundational instrument has priority. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### The Charter Houses

The Charter Houses are companies, employers, owners and governing institutions at once.

Their power rests on:

- control of employment;
- ownership claims over work produced during employment;
- control of laboratories and production systems;
- control of travel and communications;
- control of intellectual-property records;
- access to infrastructure;
- the ability to classify, review or shelve inventions. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A House can pay for a creator's time and claim the resulting invention. If the creator leaves, the creator keeps experience while the House retains the work and associated ownership. `(keypages/ch03-p01.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses are not simply portrayed as incompetent or malicious. Their stated incentives include efficiency, scale, reduced duplication and reduced risk. Their failure is that these incentives allow them to treat ownership of the invention as more important than ownership or autonomy of the creator. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Aqualis

Aqualis is the water-focused House.

It operates:

- purification facilities;
- membrane research;
- water processing;
- industrial channels;
- downstream water systems;
- water-related intellectual property. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Aqualis can clean sections of a contaminated river, but doing so at sufficient speed may require shutting down half of the production district. `(keypages/ch03-p05.md)`

### Helion

Helion is associated with energy, computation and distributed systems.

Its facilities include large compute cores with server racks, conduits and heat management. Helion's security systems watch patterns rather than merely watching individuals. A group of five people standing still too long can become an actionable pattern. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Helion holds or administers distributed-network expertise that can route supply around a single point of failure. Such a system threatens any institution that could otherwise choke a city by cutting one line. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Astra

Astra is associated with space, advanced materials, extraction and launch systems.

Its Halyard complex combines:

- raw rock extraction;
- terraced pits;
- fabrication bays;
- launch infrastructure;
- advanced materials work;
- mineral recovery;
- atmospheric capture. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Civic administration

Oasis civic administration maintains public records, residency clearance, eligibility and access systems. Its personnel use polished institutional language and formal procedures.

Civic authority can be technically precise without being humane. A person may receive a temporary pass while also being marked **ESCORT MANDATORY**. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Later civic proposals establish that:

- allocation records should be public and verifiable;
- governance proposals should be open for community designation;
- decisions should not be made by one authority alone;
- people affected by a decision should be able to see the evidence and challenge a broken promise. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

These are established as a new direction for the world, not as proof that the system has already been repaired. Existing allocations, ownership records and agreements remain unchanged pending human process. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Enforcement and access

Authority is enforced through:

- biometric gates;
- identity bands;
- white-and-amber control nodes;
- drones;
- automated supervisors;
- travel restrictions;
- wage deductions;
- debt records;
- financial restrictions;
- public-liability classifications;

- detention or “protective detention” language. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Official language frequently softens coercion:

## Economics, Labour and Ownership

### Currency

The official currency is called **credits**.

Keel's everyday speech uses:

- **fitch**, derived from *fichas*, for individual units of money;
- **salvo**, derived from *saldo*, for available balance or remaining financial room. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

Examples of ordinary usage:

> “Twenty fitch.”  
> “I got six fitch left.”  
> “What's your salvo?”  
> “My salvo's dead.” `(input/vocabulary.md)`

Official displays may say:

> **AVAILABLE BALANCE: 184 CREDITS**

A Keel resident may call the same amount their “salvo.” `(input/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt

At age seventeen, a person can receive a parental household liability transfer. One checkpoint records:

> **AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN**  
> **PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE**  
> **GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED**  
> **DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS**  
> **EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**  
> **PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS**  
> **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED** `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Debt is not merely an abstract obligation. It determines:

- how long a person expects to work;
- whether they can travel;
- what jobs they may accept;
- how much risk they can take;
- whether a House can make an offer appear to be freedom;
- whether a family can afford medicine, food or transport. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; input/vocabulary.md)`

A debt can include principal, interest, penalties and accumulated obligations. The world distinguishes the original debt from what time does to it. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

### Work

Keel labor includes mining, pump maintenance, extraction, repair, transport and other physically demanding work.

Workers operate under:

- assigned shifts;
- site reassignment;
- biometric entry and exit;
- movement restrictions;
- wage deductions;
- equipment rules;
- automated supervision. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Leaving an assigned zone can trigger a wage forfeiture. One warning reads:

> **LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR.** `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Halyard labor includes fabrication, engineering, laboratory work, computation, research, manufacturing, patent administration and technical maintenance. Halyard workers may be better educated and better equipped than Keel workers, but their labor is still constrained by House contracts and ownership systems. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; input/vocabulary.md)`

### Ownership

Prosperity distinguishes among:

- making something;
- possessing it;
- controlling it;
- receiving value from it;
- holding legal title;
- accepting the risk attached to it. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

A House can pay wages and claim the resulting work. The creator may receive a bonus without receiving ownership. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Maker's Seal is designed to reconnect ownership with responsibility and contribution. Its intended operation can reconstruct who created value across sectors including:

- clean water;
- education;
- energy;
- transport;
- medicine;
- food systems. `(keypages/ch03-p10.md)`

The system can reveal disputed ownership at city scale. One audit displays:

> **71.3% DISPUTED OWNERSHIP** `(keypages/ch03-p10.md)`

The audit does not instantly make everyone equal. Its effect is to make contribution visible, allowing some people to remain with Houses, become partners or leave to start independently. `(keypages/ch03-p13.md)`

### Patents and the intellectual estate

The patent system is active, high-stakes and institutionally entangled.

A public registry can identify:

> **ADRIAN PHANTUM / INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE / CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM / 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED** `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Patent records can be:

- fragmented;
- encrypted;
- classified;
- disputed;
- reassigned;
- connected to House ownership;
- separated from the people who contributed to them. `(keypages/ch03-p03.md; keypages/ch03-p04.md)`

The system's central failure is not that records do not exist. It is that records can be structured to preserve institutional ownership while erasing or suppressing the people who created value. `(keypages/ch03-p10.md)`

### Incentives and risk

Prosperity's institutions often justify concentration through risk management:

- centralization reduces duplication;
- House control funds invention;
- scale makes production and distribution possible;
- administrative systems promise stability;
- concentrated authority makes emergency decisions faster. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The counter-question is always who carries the downside:

- who bears the debt;
- who loses movement;
- who loses water;
- who loses a job;
- who receives the credit;
- who pays when infrastructure fails;
- who remains responsible when a system's official record differs from reality. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

## Water, Energy and Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the central scarce resource and the material basis of political power.

The shared water system includes:

- Oasis reservoirs;
- Halyard industrial channels;
- Keel residential lines;
- feeder rivers;
- purification plants;
- membranes;
- biofilters;
- mineral-recovery systems;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- local and distributed water nodes;
- regulators and gauges. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The river feeding Keel can become contaminated by mineral runoff and industrial residue. Aqualis can clean parts of it, but a rapid response may require shutting down half of the production district. `(keypages/ch03-p05.md)`

Official measurements can diverge from physical delivery. One concealed water-control system displays:

> **GAUGE: 62%**

while the actual lines show approximately 18 percent delivered to Keel. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

A public report later states:

> **REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%** `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Purification technology

The distributed purification work combines:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- living biofilter regulation;
- mineral recovery;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- distributed community water nodes. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The first large design is incomplete when separated across institutions. Its pieces were developed to House standards and do not naturally interoperate. A practical interface or “sleeve” can allow incompatible components to communicate without requiring another House-owned patent. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A compact version can be built at approximately toolbox scale. Its material facts include:

- a folded membrane that preserves surface area while reducing footprint;
- a biofilter that needs a steady draw;
- node logic that cycles to save power;
- mineral recovery and moisture capture that produce waste heat;
- waste heat fed back into the biofilter's power demand;
- a simulated efficiency of **91 percent**;
- a unit cost described as a fraction of industrial scale;
- **PATENT STATUS: UNFILED**. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### The Measure

The Measure compares physical reality with official data.

It has three named functions:

- **SENSE:** reads what is actually moving through a pipe;
- **COMPARE:** connects to Alpha's official number;
- **TRACE:** follows a discrepancy backward through the infrastructure;
- **VERIFY:** seals the evidence found by the other functions. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The Measure does not decide what should happen. It exposes the difference between an official account and a physical condition. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

It depends on physical contact with the infrastructure and, for comparison, contact with Alpha's data line. Connecting it to a monitored line can trigger legacy-protocol detection and cause Alpha to isolate the segment. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Energy and computation

Halyard uses large-scale computation and energy infrastructure. Helion's compute cores include:

- tall server racks;
- glowing conduits;
- visible heat;
- pattern-monitoring systems;
- distributed-network architecture. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Energy, computation and water systems are connected through the Charter Houses and Alpha's network. A distributed water design is dangerous to concentrated authority because it can route supply around a single point of failure. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Transit

Transit includes:

- industrial rail;
- elevated rail in Halyard;
- automated transit in Oasis;
- permission gates and identity checks;
- freight lifts and service routes;
- older mine routes and conduits. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Canon / FIXED:** The primary Keel-to-Halyard crossing shown in Issue 01 is through old mine works and a water conduit, not by train. The draft's train departure is earlier material and does not govern the book. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Communications and records

The world uses:

- identity bands;
- public registries;
- House databases;
- holographic projections;
- public screens;
- control-room displays;
- physical data shards;
- paper plans;
- analog gauges;
- sealed or encrypted archives. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The coexistence of paper and digital systems is deliberate. A hand-marked paper plan can reveal a physical route that Alpha's survey omits. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Technology and System Limits

### Alpha-controlled machines

Automated machines perform supervision, surveillance, transport and enforcement work.

**Foreman-7** is an Alpha-controlled automated mine supervisor. It is:

- taller than a person;
- heavy and utilitarian;
- built from dust-scoured gray-steel plating;
- equipped with a smooth featureless metal face;
- marked by a white-and-amber Alpha node on the chest;
- equipped with a paper-docket printer. `(style/locks/foreman-7-gemini/description.txt)`

Foreman-7 is administrative and impersonal rather than monstrous. `(style/locks/foreman-7-gemini/description.txt)`

### Holographic and projection systems

Holograms can:

- display maps and patent archives;
- project recorded people;
- show system states;
- visualize contribution networks;
- display probability models;
- transmit public alerts;
- reconstruct historical records. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Projection systems can fail, fragment, retain recordings in old rooms and operate from residual power. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Legacy protocols

Legacy protocols are older system instructions embedded in Alpha's infrastructure and safeguards. They can:

- wake when a designated object is built or activated;
- isolate a segment;
- override ordinary House requests;
- activate provenance or contribution audits;
- register new governing structures. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

They are not automatically benevolent. Their authority depends on the rules under which they were built and on the conditions they recognize. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Distributed systems and failure modes

Distributed systems are designed to avoid a single point of failure. A water network with local nodes can adapt to local contamination and continue operating if one node fails. `(keypages/ch03-p07.md)`

This also creates new practical demands:

- components must interoperate;
- local contamination differs by neighborhood;
- microbial systems can fail visibly;
- network security must prevent one node from being used to choke all others;
- maintenance and ownership must be distributed rather than concentrated. `(keypages/ch03-p07.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Bi11bot and TJ

Bi11bot is a small repair robot with two expressive optical eyes, an old scarf and a body that folds into a backpack. He is never menacing. `(rules/characters.md; style/locks/bi11bot-gemini/description.txt)`

TJ is a small repair-robot dog built by Bi11bot from spare parts. TJ is held together by magnets. His head, legs, body segments and tail are separate pieces that can snap together, drop away and be recovered. He can:

- leave his head behind as a camera;
- drop his legs and send body segments through narrow gaps;
- enter machinery, ducts and walls;
- rebuild himself into the shape required by a repair. `(rules/characters.md)`

TJ does not speak. He communicates through posture, body language and sounds. His parts are visibly mismatched and salvaged, but every configuration reads as a dog. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Canon / FIXED.** TJ is introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6, where Bi11bot assembled him from spare parts to reach places nobody else can. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## Daily Life

### Work and bureaucracy

An ordinary Keel day can include:

- entering a mine or pump hall through a biometric gate;
- checking an identity band;
- receiving a shift assignment;
- repairing equipment with unauthorized but functional parts;
- passing a checkpoint that displays debt and projected clearance;
- working under a machine supervisor;
- losing wages for leaving an assigned zone;
- navigating travel restrictions. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

A worker may be officially praised or penalized through screens rather than through a person. A pump can be tagged **UNAUTHORIZED REPAIR — REVIEW REQUIRED** while an individual supervisor quietly chooses **ROUTINE MAINTENANCE** because the repair works. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Home

Keel homes are modest but not empty or culturally barren. A family apartment may contain:

- handmade quilts;
- repaired furniture;
- old books;
- faded family photographs;
- potted plants in recycled containers;
- inherited cloth or tools;
- a hidden workshop concealed behind a bookshelf. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The presence of patched objects signals resourcefulness and continuity rather than simple deprivation. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

### Travel

Travel is a privilege attached to administrative status.

A person can be restricted from leaving Keel, reassigned to a work site, or required to carry a temporary pass. A route may be technically present but inaccessible because the relevant gate, wage status, debt status or identity record denies passage. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

In the intended crossing between Keel and Halyard, the old mine works and the water conduit provide a route outside the ordinary authorized transit system. The route is physically dangerous, unmapped or incompletely monitored, and its use can trigger system alerts. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Food, medicine and mutual aid

Money is allocated among competing needs. A Keel household may have to choose between:

- medicine;
- food and lamp fuel;
- a travel token or other access cost. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Informal networks can provide:

- food;
- transport;
- tools;
- tokens;
- information;
- temporary concealment;
- help moving through monitored systems. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

These networks are not presented as a magical substitute for institutions. They are fragile, time-limited and exposed to surveillance. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Public space

Keel public space is crowded and work-shaped. Halyard public space is orderly, commercial and full of House messaging. Oasis public space is spacious and quiet, with civic halls and controlled displays.

Public screens can make a private or technical fact communal. When a water discrepancy is published, ration stations, household screens, transit boards, mine displays and street kiosks can all display the same report at once. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Culture and Language

### The vertical language gradient

Speech becomes more formal as the story moves from Keel through Halyard to Oasis:

- **Keel:** rough, compressed, local, practical, slang-heavy and work-shaped;
- **Halyard:** technical, professional, commercial and fluent;
- **Oasis:** polished, administrative, abstract and institutionally fluent. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

This is not an intelligence scale. Keel characters may understand systems perfectly while describing them through physical evidence, work experience and immediate consequences. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

### Keel terms

Common terms include:

- **clean:** good, safe, correct or honest;
- **hot:** watched, dangerous or flagged;
- **burned:** compromised or unusable;
- **crooked:** physically wrong, rigged or dishonest;
- **thin:** weak or barely viable;
- **solid:** reliable;
- **patched:** improvised but functioning;
- **ghost:** physically present but absent from records;
- **paper-clean:** officially correct but detached from reality;
- **fitch:** money units;
- **salvo:** available balance. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

### Halyard terms

Halyard speech uses vocabulary from:

- engineering;
- fabrication;
- research;
- intellectual property;
- finance;
- contracts;
- manufacturing;
- Charter House administration.

Terms include:

- prototype;
- yield;
- tolerance;
- attribution;
- claim;
- license;
- audit;
- provenance;
- ownership;
- margin;
- equity;
- patent;
- stake. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

### Oasis terms

Oasis speech more readily uses:

- authorization;
- allocation;
- transition;
- stability;
- compliance;
- eligibility;
- intervention;
- optimization;
- continuity;
- accommodation;
- service provision;
- approved;
- recommended. `(input/vocabulary.md)`

The language can make harm sound procedural. “You are trapped” becomes “movement authorization has been temporarily suspended.” `(input/vocabulary.md)`

### Public messaging

Public messaging links technical achievement with institutional scale and order. House advertisements present clean water, energy, computation, orbital infrastructure and advanced materials as products of centralized organization. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The public language of Halyard celebrates creators while directing attention toward the House that owns their work. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Ancestry and regional identity

Prosperity's population reflects:

- Indigenous Andean communities;
- mestizo and mixed Indigenous/European families;
- long-established European-descended families;
- Latin American and Southern European migration;
- international technical migration;
- Afro-descendant and Afro-Latin populations;
- African, South Asian, East Asian and other diasporic communities;
- mixed families across all three cities. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Ancestry does not predict:

- intelligence;
- morality;
- wealth;
- technical competence;
- poverty;
- political position;
- support for Alpha;
- opposition to Alpha. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Environment and Lived Texture

Prosperity's physical world is shaped by high-desert conditions, mineral extraction and water scarcity.

A page can show:

- dust in shafts and mine corridors;
- oxidized metal;
- mineral residue;
- dry or contaminated channels;
- condensation where water should not be;
- heat shimmer around compute cores;
- greenhouse green against industrial bronze;
- cloud-layer transitions;
- still Oasis reservoirs;
- crowded Keel lanes;
- repaired tools and work clothing;
- old pipes beneath newer systems;
- analog gauges disagreeing with digital records;
- control nodes blinking white and amber;
- cold House signage against warm industrial light. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The setting's strongest visual contradiction is between official cleanliness and physical evidence:

- a registry can be paper-clean while a pipe is almost dry;
- a House can celebrate invention while its maker receives only a bonus;
- an Oasis system can be immaculate while concealing an old failure;
- a city can be safe from immediate chaos while people lose the ability to choose. `(keypages/ch03-p01.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## Lasting World Changes Established in the Material

- The Maker's Seal can publicly reconstruct contributions and dispute House ownership claims. `(keypages/ch03-p10.md; keypages/ch03-p11.md)`
- Halyard's creators begin receiving visible attribution and, in some cases, greater ownership or independence. This is a beginning, not an equal society. `(keypages/ch03-p13.md)`
- The Covenant's surviving custodians begin reconsidering a system that depends on only three people. The proposed revision would make promises visible to affected communities and allow them to call a promise broken when it fails. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- A compact unfiled water-purification design demonstrates that useful infrastructure can be made small enough for people outside the Houses to build. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- The distributed verification protocol rescinds emergency authority but leaves existing allocations, ownership records and agreements unchanged pending human process. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- Allocation records and governance proposals become publicly visible and open to community designation. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- The world is not repaired by a single system change. Debt, House ownership, uneven infrastructure and institutional habits remain. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Key props

### The white-and-amber Alpha control node

- **Look:** A small, identical control node placed along the infrastructure spine and on checkpoints, scanners and equipment. Its visible signal is white with an amber point. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Embedded throughout Keel, Halyard, Oasis, mine sites, transit gates, pump halls and control infrastructure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Meaning:** It marks the reach of Alpha into ordinary systems. It can log work, identify access, carry official readings and trigger alerts. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Measure

- **Look:** A compact handheld brass instrument about the size of a fist. It has three arms that fold flat against its body and can clamp around a pipe or data connection. Its housing is engraved: **WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE**. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Adrian hid it in the concealed water-control infrastructure inside the old mine. It is later carried in Bi11bot's fitted chest compartment. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Meaning:** It represents physical verification against official records. Its functions are **SENSE**, **COMPARE**, **TRACE** and **VERIFY**. It reveals discrepancies but does not decide what should happen. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### The Maker's Seal

- **Look:** A palm-sized bronze medallion with an open-hand symbol. It reads as a maker's mark rather than a royal signet. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** It is carried by collaborators and can be held by a person recognized as a contributor. Lina has carried one for years before placing it in Alex's hand. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### The Covenant bands

- **Look:** Three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a name and a promise. The bands can remain separate or link into a circle. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

- **Meaning:** It represents reciprocal obligation and trust. It is not supposed to activate for whoever happens to possess it; it responds to what people choose to do and whether they honor a real obligation. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Adrian's casino chip

- **Look:** A worn casino chip with a looping equation etched into it: **A = K + ρU**. It is small enough to turn unconsciously in a hand. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; style/locks/alex-phantum-gemini/description.txt)`

## Open

- The material establishes the three cities and the mountain basin but does not give a definitive geographic name for the basin or a precise national or regional political jurisdiction. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`
- The material establishes the Water Wars and their consequences but does not give a complete chronology, duration or external political history. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- Alpha's exact technical architecture, training history, physical location and degree of autonomy remain undefined. The material establishes behavior and authority, not a complete implementation. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- The legal status of the Charter Houses, their relationship to civic administration and the exact process by which House ownership claims become enforceable remain unresolved. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- The exact currency conversion, wages beyond the cited **40 credits earned today**, and the economic scale of household costs remain unresolved. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- The material shows healthcare, food, water and transport systems but does not fully establish their ordinary funding, eligibility rules or supply chains. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`
- The exact mechanisms by which Alpha's emergency authority became permanent, and whether any formal legal sunset or review process existed, remain unresolved. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- The material establishes a new distributed-verification direction but does not yet define the permanent selection process, number of representatives, voting thresholds or enforcement mechanism for community-designated decision-making. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- Earlier draft material depicts travel from Keel to Halyard by train, while binding rules require the old-mine and water-conduit crossing. The train version is superseded and cannot govern the book. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
