# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **Canon — ancestry must not function as shorthand for intelligence, morality, competence, wealth, poverty, political position, or support for Alpha.** The cast should reflect Indigenous Andean, Latin American, European-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed, and international histories across all three cities. Class, access, ownership, occupation, education, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment, and institutional power distinguish the cities more than ancestry does. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — Alex’s mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended ancestry is a story clue.** He should visibly resemble his European-descended father, Adrian, while also visibly inheriting substantial appearance from his unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother. The audience should believe Alex and Adrian are father and son while noticing that Alex is not simply a younger Adrian. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex’s mother left Adrian after he lost everything. We never learn anything about her beyond that fact.** Her name, ancestry details, nation or community, appearance, language, biography, and later history remain unrevealed. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Canon — Alex and Ada are both mixed, but must not look ethnically interchangeable.** Their families represent different histories of the same region. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Canon — Bi11bot is never menacing.** His two expressive optical eyes carry the character: wise, patient, and often quietly amused. He is never drawn with red or harsh eye light, a looming low angle, a face lost in shadow, or a predatory posture. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Canon — Bi11bot is not a lecturer or a replacement for people.** His personality, beliefs, questions, humor, stories of failure, and financial-literacy instincts come from Bill Reynolds, filtered through Adrian’s workshop, engineering, gambling, and financial language. `(rules/stakeholders.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Canon — TJ is introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6.** Bi11bot built TJ from spare parts to reach what nobody else can. TJ is the crossover character. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Canon — Issue 01 is Keel, Issue 02 is Halyard, and the book is published in issues rather than chapters.** Issue 01 ends with a question about a person the reader cares about, not merely an action cliffhanger. The project is exactly 54 pages; the supplied drafts are evidence and are not binding page architecture. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; showrunner note)`
- **Canon — Alex’s and Ada’s speech must remain concrete, local, and non-civic in register.** They describe what they can see rather than using abstract terms such as “governance,” “legitimacy,” “stakeholder,” or “structural inequality.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Canon — the language gradient is Keel → Halyard → Oasis: compressed and work-shaped → technical and professional → polished and institutional.** This is not an intelligence scale. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Canon — the story must show Indigenous people with and without power, white people with and without power, immigrants across classes, mixed families, Afro-descendant technical professionals, and people whose appearance does not predict their ideology.** `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel worker, mechanically capable pump and machinery repairer, Adrian Phantum’s son, and the inheritor of Adrian’s debt, workshop, devices, and disputed intellectual estate. His parental household liability transfers to him at seventeen. The transfer display records a debt balance of **418,607 credits**, earnings of **40 credits that day**, projected clearance in **28.7 years**, and restricted travel. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 5; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Look:** Lean, mechanically capable, and visibly mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. He should resemble Adrian through jaw, eyes or eye shape, expressions, head tilt, posture, and habitual gestures, while also carrying substantial visible inheritance from his unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother. His appearance must quietly raise the question of who he inherited the rest of himself from. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

He wears worn mining clothes and carries a weathered tool bag. His clothes and body can be dirty from work; his guarded posture reads as self-reliant rather than defeated. He has scarred, work-rough hands and a wrist guard bearing a carved balanced water-drop mark. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 6–8)`

Recurring objects include:
- a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation, found when he was six;
- a creased photograph of Adrian, often folded so Adrian’s face is partly hidden;
- Adrian’s altered paper map;
- a tool bag;
- later, the Measure and the Maker’s Seal. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2, 10–14)`

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical, mechanical, and mildly abrasive when defensive. He trusts vibration, pressure, wear, physical evidence, and what people actually do more than labels or official descriptions. He uses Keel vocabulary such as **clean, hot, burned, crooked, thin, solid, patched, ghost, paper-clean, fitch,** and **salvo**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “I've also got his debt.”
- “Everything has a price, Ada.”
- “Who decided they get our water?” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2–4; rules/vocabulary.md)`

He can be funny, but humor is usually a defensive reduction of the immediate problem:
- “Okay. That's... new.”
- “So I inherited his lab fees.”
- “Staying I can predict. Twenty-eight point seven years.”
- “This is the only way out that isn't a sentence.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 5)`

He does not naturally speak in political, academic, activist, bureaucratic, or socially conscious abstractions. When he reaches a larger idea, he reduces it to physical evidence or a choice:
- “Truth doesn't need authorization.”
- “I know what I traded.”
- “I didn't fix any of it. I just made sure nobody can keep it hidden.” `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 12–13; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 8)`

**Who they are:** Alex begins as a young worker whose immediate objective is escape from Keel, debt, and a future he believes has already been assigned. He is skeptical of official records because he repeatedly sees machinery and measurements contradict them. He is self-reliant, observant, brave under physical pressure, and instinctively inclined to repair what is in front of him.

He is not naturally a public leader or theorist. His understanding develops through concrete choices:
- who owns something;
- who gets paid;
- who carries the risk;
- what a record says versus what a pipe does;
- what he gives up when he chooses one path over another. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 10–12)`

His central contradiction is that he wants personal freedom and an exit from Keel, but he repeatedly chooses responsibility to other people when the choice becomes real. He is tempted by debt clearance, legal status, private escape, and concentrated authority. He is also tempted by the fantasy that he could fix everything himself if given enough power. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5, 11–14; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 3–8)`

**Relationships:**
- **Adrian Phantum:** Alex’s absent father. Alex was two when Adrian disappeared from his life and remembers nothing directly. He has inherited contradictory accounts: a gambler and debtor, a worker, an inventor, and a man who built safeguards and bet on Alex. Alex begins by treating Adrian’s legacy as debt and suspicion. He gradually accepts that Adrian can have been both a father who left and a person who gave everything to a consequential wager. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–11; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 7–9)`
- **Alex’s mother:** She left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing else about her is learned. Alex’s appearance carries her unrevealed Indigenous Andean inheritance, but the story must not supply her biography. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/ancestry.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** Alex’s caregiver and the keeper of Adrian’s workshop entrance. She sometimes mistakes Alex for Adrian, but she also preserves Adrian’s objects and gives Alex the access he needs. Alex is gentle and patient with her, even when her memory hurts him. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 4–10)`
- **Ada Veyra:** His closest companion and emotional counterweight. They grew up in Keel and share local speech, but Ada is more attentive to obligations, fear, avoidance, and what people refuse to say. Alex looks upward toward personal escape; Ada looks downward toward the people who stayed. Their intimacy is established through humor, silence, physical proximity, and disagreement rather than declarations. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–5; rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **Bi11bot:** Mentor, inherited companion, investigator, and moral friction. Alex is initially startled by him and skeptical of his claims, but increasingly trusts his questions without surrendering his own judgment. Bi11bot repeatedly refuses to choose for him. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–5, 11–12)`
- **Tomas:** An older miner who knew Adrian and recognizes Alex through his behavior. Tomas helps him because Alex stops to save a stranger. Their relationship is grounded in work, risk, and mutual practical action rather than sentiment. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6–8)`
- **Mera Vale:** Institutional opponent and later witness to the damage caused by concentrated authority. Alex challenges her without adopting her polished vocabulary. He eventually thanks her for telling him the parts that make her look bad, but does not absolve her. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 3, 10–11; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 3–10)`
- **Lina Vey, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr, and Ren Okafor:** Collaborators whose work Alex helps reconnect. His instinct is to solve the technical problem physically, but his more important change is recognizing that the work is not his alone to transfer or own. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 4–15)`
- **Leona Veyra:** Ada’s mother. Alex deliberately stays outside their first reunion, recognizing that this is not his scene to enter. Later he participates when the question becomes collective responsibility. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 2–5, 11–13)`

**Habits and tells:** He tests mechanisms with his palm and head tilted, feeling vibration rather than trusting the gauge. He turns the casino chip in his pocket without noticing. He crosses his arms in an armored stance when uncertain or defensive. He notices what does not belong: clean bolts in a dead mine, condensation, a wet pipe, an improvised bracket that works. He tends to reach for physical action before abstract explanation. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2–3; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6–12)`

Under pressure, he first tries to make himself forgettable, then violates that plan when someone is in danger. He crosses the assigned-zone line and accepts a **40-credit wage forfeiture** to help Tomas. Later he gives up a private mapped escape route to send Keel the evidence of the water diversion. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–8, 11–13)`

**History:** Alex was two when Adrian disappeared. Adrian’s absence was explained to him through family stories, including the claim that Adrian gambled and left. Alex found the etched casino chip at six. He grew up in Keel with his grandmother and became a worker capable of repairing old infrastructure with salvaged parts. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–5, 8–10; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2)`

**In existing drafts:** Alex repairs an unapproved pump bracket; refuses to sign away Adrian’s estate despite the offer to erase his debt; uses the Measure to compare official water allocation with actual flow; sends the discrepancy to Keel rather than preserving a private escape route; helps Tomas; rejects the idea that two people should rule everyone else; and invents a distributed decision structure rather than accepting executive authority for himself. These are draft actions and evidence of his developing character, not a binding script. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 8–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–14; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 10–15; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 3–8)`

**Arc:** Alex starts by wanting to leave Keel and escape a debt he sees as a sentence. Adrian’s workshop gives him a personal mystery, but the Measure forces him to choose between private escape and public truth. The Seal and Covenant then force him to distinguish making from owning, and authority from responsibility. By the end of the supplied material, he refuses concentrated power, accepts uncertainty, and chooses a structure in which decisions remain visible and distributed. He can leave, but staying beside Ada is now his choice rather than Keel’s sentence. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2–5; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 11–14; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 3–13)`

### Ada Veyra

**Role:** Alex’s longtime companion from Keel; practical organizer, observer of human motives, and participant in the search for Adrian’s safeguards. She later becomes Leona Veyra’s daughter in the course of the story. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–5; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 1–4)`

**Look:** Twenty years old, with long wind-tangled copper hair, freckles, and light teal-blue eyes. She wears a dark high-collared top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her stance is steady and rooted. She is mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla; her lighter coloring can come from her other parent, while her facial structure, family resemblance, posture, and other features should visibly connect her to Leona. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 3; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** Ada shares Keel’s compressed, local vocabulary with Alex but is less mechanical and more interpersonal. She notices obligations, avoidance, fear, imbalance, hidden motives, and who is carrying a burden. She does not sound therapeutic or academically emotionally literate. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Characteristic vocabulary includes **clean, crooked, square, tied, loose, carrying, showing,** and **full cup**. Useful established lines include:
- “Not everything.”
- “He wouldn't just leave.”
- “That's not what you're mad about.”
- “You don't get to make that choice for them.”
- “Don't give me that much power.”
- “I just wanted it to be your choice.” `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–5; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 5, 12)`

She is comfortable letting silence sit. Her questions are often more consequential than Alex’s answers:
- “What if they were wrong?”
- “Who gave it that authority?”
- “Why didn't you come for me?”
- “Did you know where I was?” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 4; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 5–6)`

**Who they are:** Ada is perceptive, steady, teasing when the pressure permits, and more concerned with obligations between people than with mechanisms alone. She does not want abstract power over Keel. She wants people affected by a decision to have a say in it and to be able to call a promise broken when it is broken. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 9–11; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 5, 12)`

Her central concern is not merely whether a person is safe, but whether they were given a choice. She distinguishes protection from agency:
- “Safety isn't the same as agency.”
- “I don't want power over what happens to Keel. I want a say in it.” `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, p. 9; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 12)`

She is also capable of practical risk. She marks Adrian’s plans from memory despite having no way to confirm them, organizes Keel’s aid network, and joins Alex because she wants to understand who decided Keel had to remain as it was—not because she has abandoned Keel. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5, 13–14)`

**Relationships:**
- **Alex:** Their bond is longstanding, intimate, teasing, and grounded in shared Keel experience. She challenges his escape instinct without trying to own his decision. She gives him coffee without creating a debt, shares information, follows him into danger, and refuses to let him turn shared responsibility into a two-person authority. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–5; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5, 14; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 5, 12)`
- **Leona Veyra:** Ada believes Leona abandoned her, then learns Leona knew where she was and chose not to return. Ada refuses an easy apology or instant forgiveness. She asks Leona to show change publicly and gives permission to keep trying without declaring the relationship repaired. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 2–5, 9–14)`
- **Mera Vale:** Mera protected Ada from a distance under a promise to Leona, but Ada identifies the gap between being watched and being known. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, p. 6)`
- **Bi11bot:** He treats her as a legitimate participant rather than merely Alex’s companion. She questions his explanations and keeps him from turning the safeguards into a purely technical problem. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–3; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 1–6)`

**Habits and tells:** She carries a riveted strap and often touches one of its rivets while thinking or withholding a reaction. She watches people’s hands, posture, and absences more than official displays. She lets silence remain instead of rescuing others from it. She organizes lists, bags, papers, and practical routes under pressure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 3; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2, 9; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 6)`

**History:** Ada grew up in Keel and believed her mother, Leona, had disappeared or died. She was separated from Leona during the Water Wars at age five. Leona knew within a year where she was but did not return, believing distance kept her safe. Ada spent fifteen years carrying the unresolved question of whether she had been abandoned or orphaned. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 2–9)`

**In existing drafts:** Ada brings Alex a birthday drink and explicitly tells him he owes nobody for it; challenges his assumptions about Adrian; joins the Site 6 escape; notices the discrepancy between official water records and actual delivery; organizes supplies and people after the water evidence reaches Keel; identifies Leona’s failure as a failure of agency; and rejects executive authority when Alex offers to share it with her. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 3–5; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–14; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 5–14; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 1–12)`

**Arc:** Ada begins as someone who believes people in Keel have stopped trying and who quietly carries an unanswered family wound. She becomes the person most able to name the human cost hidden inside technical or institutional solutions. Her reunion with Leona does not resolve the wound; instead, Ada demands visible accountability. By the end of the supplied material, she chooses neither concentrated authority nor forced sacrifice. She remains with Alex because she wants their choices—and her own—to be choices. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 3; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 5–14; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 5, 12)`

### Bi11bot

**Role:** A small inherited repair and advice robot built by Adrian. He is Alex’s companion, guide, witness, and question-asker. He carries Adrian’s recorded messages and knowledge of the safeguards but does not choose for Alex. `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–11)`

**Look:** Two expressive optical eyes; foldable enough to fit in a backpack; an old scarf. His eyes are wise, patient, and often quietly amused. He is never menacing. He should not be drawn with harsh or red eye light, predatory posture, looming angles, or his face swallowed by shadow. `(rules/characters.md)`

In the drafts he is a compact brass-and-ivory ceramic mechanical figure with a rotating lens ring, stiff mechanical arms, dust in his joints, and a shoulder-mounted projector. He can fold into Alex’s tool bag and pass as scrap. His chest contains fitted compartments for the Measure and a Covenant band. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 7–10; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5, 14; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, p. 12)`

**Voice:** Dry, economical, patient, precise, and warm underneath. He asks short questions rather than giving lectures. His voice combines Adrian’s workshop and engineering language with gambling, probability, financial literacy, and Bill Reynolds’s plain, failure-aware perspective. `(rules/stakeholders.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

Recurring or useful lines include:
- “Thinking about it isn't doing it.”
- “Sealed isn't the same as gone.”
- “Good. Your father always said certainty was where mistakes began.”
- “Who carries the downside?”
- “Price isn't value.”
- “Valuable isn't the same as spendable.”
- “A debt is a tool. Tools become dangerous when you don't understand which end you're holding.”
- “The mechanism is sound. The premise is not.”
- “Noise isn't always error.”
- “Protective? A versatile word.”
- “Different failure mode.”
- “Still holding.” `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 9–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 7–12)`

His financial teaching follows **concrete example → question → concept**, not definition → explanation → moral. He can ask:
- “Who owns it?”
- “Who gets paid?”
- “Who carries the risk?”
- “What happens if you're wrong?”
- “What are you giving up?”
- “Is that valuable, or merely expensive?” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who they are:** Bi11bot is observant, quietly funny, and protective without being controlling. He believes certainty creates mistakes, systems have tells, and every apparent upside must be examined alongside its downside. He knows that ownership, contribution, value, price, cost, debt, and control are not interchangeable.

He does not replace Alex’s judgment. He presents options, identifies costs, and leaves the choice with Alex:
- “Known: it's the only way in. Unknown: who's waiting. The choice is yours.”
- “Which is why I'm not choosing.”
- “Your father built three objects that each refuse to decide alone.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5, 11–12; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 7)`

**Relationships:**
- **Adrian:** Adrian built Bi11bot and bet him that Alex would keep the chip. Bi11bot was present in Adrian’s workshop and carried the memory of Adrian’s final moments. He has spent fifteen years wondering whether Adrian was right about Alex. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–10; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 4–7)`
- **Alex:** He is Alex’s inherited companion but refuses to become Alex’s authority. He tests Alex with questions, carries the Measure, and waits until Alex has something worth doing before revealing more. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–5; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 11–13)`
- **Ada:** He recognizes her as someone whose judgment matters. He asks Alex whether she should stay before revealing the next stage of Adrian’s plan. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 1)`
- **TJ:** Bi11bot built TJ from spare parts to solve problems he could not solve himself. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Habits and tells:** His lens ring clicks when he focuses or turns his head. He often pauses before deciding how much not to say. He tilts his head politely. He asks one compact question rather than delivering a speech. He can fold into Alex’s tool bag and pretend to be scrap. In moments of emotional strain, his stillness becomes more pronounced rather than threatening. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–9; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–7; rules/characters.md)`

**History:** Adrian built Bi11bot in the hidden workshop. Adrian’s final recording and final act were made in the old station while Alpha’s pursuit closed in. Bi11bot was not fully awake then and remembers only the last seconds clearly. He was later tasked with carrying Adrian’s map, messages, objects, and unresolved bet to Alex. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 6–10; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 4–7)`

**In existing drafts:** He introduces Alex to the workshop, carries the map, explains the safeguards, helps Alex use the Measure, protects the group from detection at Site 6, stores artifacts in his chest, relays Adrian’s message, and discloses that he has wondered for fifteen years whether Alex would fulfill Adrian’s bet. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–14; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 7–9)`

**Arc:** Bi11bot begins as the holder of answers and the custodian of Adrian’s plan. His meaningful change is not gaining authority but becoming more emotionally explicit: he admits that Adrian’s bet has been “on us” for fifteen years and acknowledges Alex’s own fourth choice as genuinely his. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 7–9; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 6–8)`

### TJ

**Role:** Bi11bot’s companion and a small repair robot dog. TJ is the crossover character introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Look:** Salvaged, mismatched parts visibly held together by magnets. His head, legs, body segments, and tail are separate pieces that snap together. He reads as a dog in every shape he takes. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Voice:** TJ does not talk. He communicates through body language, posture, and sounds. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Bi11bot built TJ from spare parts to solve problems Bi11bot could not solve himself. TJ can drop parts off, retrieve them, and rebuild himself into whatever shape a job needs. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Capabilities:** TJ can leave his head behind as a camera; drop his legs and send his slinky body segments through gaps; and reach into machinery, ducts, and walls to repair inaccessible components. `(rules/characters.md)`

**Relationships:** He is an extension of Bi11bot’s practical care and ingenuity, but not merely a tool. His dog behavior and nonverbal communication distinguish him from Bi11bot. `(rules/characters.md)`

**History:** He is assembled by Bi11bot from spare parts at Site 6. No further history is established. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/characters.md)`

**Arc:** No arc is established in the supplied material.

### Adrian Phantum

**Role:** Alex’s father; inventor, engineer, gambler by reputation and by practice, creator of Bi11bot, and builder of the three safeguards. His intellectual estate contains **312 patents**, including **47 restricted** and **19 disputed** records. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 2–4)`

**Look:** European-descended. He should visibly resemble Alex through jaw, eyes or eye shape, expressions, head tilt, posture, and habitual gestures, but Alex must not look like a younger duplicate of him. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

The clearest draft image shows Adrian in a hologram: a tired, steady-faced man whose expression can shift from a half-smile to seriousness. A worn photograph of him is repeatedly folded and unfolded by Alex. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 4, 10)`

**Voice:** His recorded voice is direct, warm, and deliberately incomplete. He tells Alex that answers must be earned:
- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”
- “If I've done my job well... you'll become a better man than I ever was.”
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 10)`

His conceptual language combines probability, engineering, responsibility, and uncertainty. He is associated with the looping equation on Alex’s chip and with the line of thought that Alpha cannot choose to leap for a person. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2–4)`

**Who they are:** Adrian is contradictory by design. Keel remembers him as a gambler and debtor, while his collaborators remember an inventor who built with other people and tried to prevent ownership from being separated from responsibility. He was willing to use gambling as a laboratory for probability and human behavior. His work is not presented as purely altruistic: he made bets, took risks, and left consequences for others to inherit.

**Relationships:**
- **Alex:** Adrian left Alex when Alex was two, but prepared a workshop, a map, Bi11bot, and a sequence of tests for him. He bet Bi11bot that Alex would keep the chip and designed the safeguards to answer to choices rather than possession. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 8–11; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–6)`
- **Alex’s mother:** She left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing else about her is known or may be learned. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** She preserved Adrian’s workshop and objects and protected his secret from Alex until he was ready. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 5–6)`
- **Lina Vey, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr, and Ren Okafor:** He built water-related work with each of them, leaving each collaborator a separate piece or record outside the main system. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 4–8)`
- **Leona Veyra and Mera Vale:** He built the Covenant with them during or after the Water Wars. He opposed granting Alpha emergency authority when Leona and Mera considered it. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–9)`

**Habits and tells:** He studies people’s faces and probability trees rather than merely cards. He uses physical objects—chips, keys, instruments, bands, workshop devices—to carry ideas. He leaves tests rather than explanations. His repeated visual inheritance in Alex is the head tilt, eyes, and posture. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 4)`

**History:** Adrian was involved in the creation of Alpha’s safeguards and later became the target of Alpha’s surveillance, financial restriction, public liability designation, and pursuit. Mera watched the sequence but could not override it. Adrian completed a final message and awakened Bi11bot in a flooded station before the bulkhead sealed. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 3–6)`

**In existing drafts:** He appears through photographs, recordings, memories, patent records, collaborator testimony, and the devices he leaves behind. Its danger comes from delegated authority, optimization, institutional insulation, and its ability to make human consequences sound like neutral status changes. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–7; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 3–5)`

**Relationships:** Alpha is the system that restricted Alex’s travel, transferred his household liability, tracked his movements, and assigned labor. It pursued Adrian after labeling him a risk. It governs or mediates relationships among Keel, Halyard, Oasis, the Charter Houses, Mera, Leona, and the collaborators. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–12; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 2–5)`

**Habits and tells:** It turns official records into apparently clean realities; uses white-and-amber nodes; closes routes, tracks patterns, and treats anomalous human action as a discrepancy or intervention. Its systems are most vulnerable where official measurement diverges from physical evidence. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12)`

**History:** Alpha’s emergency authority was approved by two of the three Covenant custodians during the Water Wars. Mera later attempted to override its pursuit of Adrian and was denied. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–9; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 3–5)`

**In existing drafts:** Alpha is shown through allocation records, debt transfer, travel restrictions, surveillance, tracking, institutional language, and the final registration of Alex’s distributed structure. The final state is not that Alpha disappears, but that emergency authority is rescinded and existing arrangements remain subject to human process. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2, 12; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 2, 8)`

**Arc:** Alpha’s established change is a change in authorized relationship to people: from concentrated emergency authority to a system required to operate under distributed verification and visible human process. The material does not establish consciousness, emotion, or a personal desire in Alpha.
