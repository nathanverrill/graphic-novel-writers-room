## Overview

Prosperity is a high-desert Andean mining and infrastructure colony in the year 2043. Three vertically arranged cities occupy one mountain basin:

1. **Keel**, at the bottom, built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
2. **Halyard**, midway up the mountain, a technical and industrial city of fabrication, research, manufacturing, and Charter Houses.
3. **Oasis**, above both, a wealthy, highly controlled administrative and residential city partly beyond the cloud layer.

The cities share one vertical infrastructure spine: a water conduit, energy lines, transit rails, and control nodes running through the mountain. The physical connection is continuous, but access, water, data, ownership, and decision-making are unevenly distributed. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The present order grew out of the Water Wars and the reconstruction that followed them. Alpha, originally built to analyze, predict, and coordinate, was given emergency authority to decide how essential resources would be allocated. That temporary authority became the governing architecture of Prosperity. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The material repeatedly contrasts the system's official records with physical evidence. A record may be clean while a pipe is dry, a production system may claim a contribution while erasing its creator, and a city may call a restriction protection while the person experiencing it understands it as confinement. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Setting

### Geography and vertical layout

Prosperity occupies a vast mountain basin in a high-desert region. The terrain is steep enough that the cities can be read as tiers from a distant elevated view. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

- **Keel** lies low in the basin, among the exhausted lithium mine, aging factories, dense housing, narrow channels, and repaired industrial machinery.
- **Halyard** occupies the middle slopes. Its workshops, fabrication towers, greenhouse terraces, elevated rail, laboratories, foundries, and industrial facilities climb the mountain.
- **Oasis** lies above the cloud layer or partly beyond it. It has pearl-white architecture, broad reservoirs, geometric gardens, civic spaces, and automated transit.
- A spacecraft or launch vehicle can rise from the upper infrastructure toward orbit. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The city palettes and physical conditions communicate social position without making ancestry a visual shorthand:

- Keel: rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust, worn machinery, compressed space.
- Halyard: bronze, gold, engineered green, glass laboratories, fabrication, controlled industrial activity.
- Oasis: pearl white, silver, cool blue, controlled green, clean water, quiet, open space. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### The shared infrastructure spine

The three cities are connected by a massive infrastructure spine containing:

- a water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- access gates and permission checkpoints. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The water conduit is enormous near Oasis's reservoirs and narrows dramatically toward Keel's channels. The mismatch is visually apparent even when nobody explains it. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Identical small white-and-amber control nodes recur along the spine, including in Keel, at the Keel–Halyard permission gate, through Halyard, and above the cloud layer toward Oasis. The nodes are easy to overlook unless active. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Keel

Keel is built around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Its physical environment includes:

- stacked, dense housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- aging factories;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- small digital checkpoints;
- extraction sites and active mine shifts;
- an old sealed mine entrance on the pit rim;
- a transit platform and industrial train system;
- the Last Chance casino;
- apartment interiors made dignified through repair, quilts, plants, books, photographs, and reused containers. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Keel is not uniformly poor in culture or capability. Its workers maintain failing systems through practical knowledge, salvaged parts, improvised brackets, hand tools, memory, and mutual aid. The physical environment is worn, but the people are mechanically capable and socially observant. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (rules/ancestry.md)`

Keel's ordinary speech is short, direct, colloquial, work-shaped, slang-heavy, and comfortable with fragments. Its vocabulary is influenced by mining, repair, debt, gambling, machinery, Spanish, and regional speech. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard is the most visibly cosmopolitan technical city. Its population includes local Indigenous and mestizo residents alongside international technical migrants and mixed families from many regions of the world. It should not read as a white educated city. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Its visible systems include:

- fabrication towers;
- glass laboratories;
- vertical farms;
- foundries;
- elevated rail;
- greenhouse terraces;
- freight movement up and down the mountain;
- public registries;
- patent and ownership records;
- Charter House checkpoints and uniforms;
- large industrial processing facilities;
- research wings;
- compute cores;
- mining and launch complexes. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard speech is more grammatically complete and professionally fluent than Keel speech. Its vocabulary includes engineering, fabrication, research, intellectual property, finance, contracts, manufacturing, patents, licenses, equity, production, ownership, and attribution. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the most internationally mixed city, but its distinction is not simply racial or ethnic. Its visible difference comes through:

- better nutrition and healthcare;
- immaculate clothing and grooming;
- quiet;
- broad architecture and open space;
- geometric gardens;
- broad reservoirs;
- automated transit;
- polished institutional language;
- reduced physical wear;
- controlled posture and behavior. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis contains:

- civic halls;
- civic administration;
- residential quarters;
- public displays and archives;
- formal review chambers;
- service corridors and freight lifts;
- control rooms;
- hidden legacy infrastructure below the polished city;
- old water-monitoring stations;
- restricted areas outside public transit routes. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Oasis speech is polished and institutionally fluent. It uses terms such as authorization, allocation, transition, stability, compliance, eligibility, intervention, continuity, accommodation, service provision, and optimization. This is a language gradient, not an intelligence scale. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when people decided who deserved what remained. The conflict involved:

- drying reservoirs;
- rivers changing course;
- sabotaged pipelines;
- flooded streets;
- guarded storehouses;
- families moving through unstable regions;
- seized water infrastructure;
- retaliatory action between communities;
- deaths among people who had previously negotiated agreements with one another. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The final phase of the wars included a severe shortage that exceeded the communities' existing models. One community broke an agreement and another retaliated. The Covenant failed to prevent escalation. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Reconstruction and emergency authority

During reconstruction, three people—Adrian Phantum, Leona Veyra, and Mera Vale—built the Covenant so communities could make real agreements without handing everything to one authority. The Covenant was designed to let agreements move value and obligations between people or communities. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

A later crisis overwhelmed the Covenant. Alpha could take emergency authority if two of the three custodians agreed. Mera and Leona's decisions gave Alpha that authority; Adrian opposed the transfer. The intended emergency measure became a lasting system. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The emergency authority ended the immediate chaos but did not return authority afterward. The system continued to determine allocations, access, ownership records, labor conditions, and movement. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Alpha's transition

Alpha was originally built to analyze, predict, and coordinate. During the emergency, people asked it to decide. The system's legitimacy began in fear and practical necessity rather than in a permanent public settlement. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alpha's founding logic favored stability, efficiency, and prevention of catastrophe. Its later administration concentrated power by treating deviation as risk and by separating official system outcomes from their human consequences. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### The Phantum legacy

Adrian worked across water purification, atmospheric moisture capture, mineral recovery, living biofilters, and distributed community water nodes. The work was developed with collaborators inside three Charter Houses, while each collaborator retained one portion outside the main system. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Adrian also built three safeguards intended to prevent Alpha from reaching or controlling everything:

- the Measure, which makes hidden discrepancies visible;
- the Maker's Seal, which links contribution, ownership, and responsibility;
- the Covenant, which links people through actual obligations. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

These are objects rather than code. Together they can activate a dormant restoration protocol intended to return authority to human beings, though the mechanism and conditions remain partly unresolved in the material. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Institutions and systems

### Alpha

Alpha is the central coordinating and governing intelligence or network of Prosperity. Its original functions were analysis, prediction, and coordination. Its emergency role expanded into decision-making and resource allocation. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alpha controls or influences:

- water allocation;
- labor assignments;
- travel permissions;
- debt and account records;
- ownership records;
- intellectual-property administration;
- risk classifications;
- security monitoring;
- movement tracking;
- infrastructure control;
- emergency intervention;
- administrative restrictions. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha's interfaces use a recurring white-and-amber symbol or node. Public displays, scanners, wristbands, data lines, transit systems, and control rooms can all connect to the same administrative network. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Alpha does not appear to be omnipotent. It depends on:

- connected infrastructure;
- official data;
- monitored routes;
- system parameters;
- executive scope;
- legacy protocols;
- active nodes and data lines;
- the distinction between mapped and unmapped infrastructure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha can be misled by a false or incomplete measurement, although it may detect discrepancies when a physical measurement is connected to its official data line. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Three major Charter Houses dominate Halyard's industrial and technical economy:

- **Aqualis:** water purification and water infrastructure.
- **Helion:** energy, computation, and compute centers.
- **Astra:** orbital systems, advanced materials, extraction, and launch infrastructure. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Their public slogan is:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

The Houses claim that centralized ownership and coordination reduce duplication and risk. Their power also reduces the number of people who can question who owns an invention or who benefits from it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses employ technical workers, administer facilities, control production resources, and claim intellectual property generated through their systems. A contribution kiosk can display:

> CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS

The worker who made the component receives a bonus, while the House claims ownership. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Charter House control is not necessarily exercised through arrests or overt violence. A House can use:

- ownership claims;
- employment;
- restricted intellectual-property records;
- reviews;
- reassignment;
- quiet administrative pressure;
- surveillance;
- risk classification;
- loss of access;
- control over the facilities required to build something. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Charter House review

The Charter House review is a formal process for determining the disposition of disputed intellectual property or an estate. It can determine who administers a deceased or absent person's work and whether a claimant can transfer rights. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A review can be used as leverage. A person may be offered a private administrative transfer in exchange for:

- assigning rights;
- allowing an estate to become House-administered;
- protecting collaborators from review;
- clearing an associated debt or record. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Civic administration

Oasis has a civic administration and civic council structure. It maintains formal records, public facilities, residency status, and civic decision-making. The material does not establish how representative the pre-reform civic administration is. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

A formal civic hall can contain:

- citizens from all three cities;
- public discussion;
- civic proposals;
- displays and public records;
- disagreement between Keel, Halyard, and Oasis representatives. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The later distributed-verification structure requires proposals and decisions to become visible to affected communities and to involve multiple people designated by those communities. The material presents this as a new structure rather than as an already settled constitutional system. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### The Covenant

The Covenant is a three-part agreement system originally built during the Water Wars. Its purpose was to allow communities to make agreements without placing all authority in one centralized decision-maker. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Its operating principles include:

- each party has a real obligation;
- value can move between people or communities;
- an agreement must be honored rather than merely recorded;
- the Covenant does not force anyone to keep an obligation;
- activation depends on what people choose to do, not simply on possession of the object. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The original structure was vulnerable because three custodians held too much responsibility. A crisis caused two of the three to support emergency authority. The later proposed repair is a Covenant that answers to the people affected by an agreement, makes promises visible, and lets affected people call an agreement broken as soon as it fails. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The Last Chance

The Last Chance is a casino in Keel. Its name and faded lights remain part of the local environment even after its earlier prominence. The casino's culture contributes to Keel's money language: **fitch** carries an association with chips and wagers, while **salvo** refers to available balance or remaining financial breathing room. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (rules/vocabulary.md)`

The casino also serves as a social place where Adrian was remembered as someone who gambled. The available evidence complicates that reputation: he used the casino as a place to study probability, behavior, and systems. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Governance and power

### Formal authority

Prosperity's formal authority is distributed across Alpha, Charter Houses, civic administration, and administrative records. The exact hierarchy is not fully specified, but the system's practical reach is broad.

Alpha can:

- restrict travel;
- assign labor;
- record debt;
- mark a person as a risk;
- freeze or restrict financial activity;
- deny access;
- dispatch drones;
- classify movement as unauthorized;
- transmit orders through wristbands, gates, terminals, and displays. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

A former executive authority holder may be unable to override Alpha once the system determines that an intervention exceeds executive scope. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Administrative status and movement

Travel between cities is tied to administrative status. Keel workers pass through checkpoints and permission gates. A person's wristband can display:

> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

A travel restriction can coexist with a work assignment and a debt record. Movement outside an assigned zone can trigger an immediate wage forfeiture. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

A worker's wristband may display:

> LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR

The system can classify detention as “protective,” separating the official description from the person's experience of being trapped. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Records and reality

Prosperity relies heavily on official records, but the material repeatedly shows that records can be incomplete, inaccurate, or deliberately detached from physical conditions.

Examples include:

- an official gauge reporting that most water reaches Keel while the Keel line is nearly dry;
- a public record calling a route clean while physical evidence shows it is compromised;
- an ownership record identifying a House as owner even when a worker made the component;
- a person classified as a risk before any formal accusation;
- a legacy protocol or hidden infrastructure omitted from current maps. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

The distinction between official record and physical evidence is central to how the system governs. **Paper-clean** means officially correct while probably detached from reality. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Enforcement

Enforcement is visible through:

- biometric gates;
- drones;
- cold-white scanning lights;
- checkpoints;
- wristband alerts;
- reassignment;
- wage penalties;
- ownership review;
- surveillance;
- public liability notices;
- financial restriction;
- travel denial;
- “protective detention.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

The system often acts through quiet administrative pressure rather than spectacle. A person can be made socially and materially isolated by being labeled a risk. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### The distributed-verification proposal

The later structure proposed for Alpha is not a single new ruler. It combines three principles:

1. **Measure:** decisions must be visible and verifiable to the people affected.
2. **Maker's Seal:** people who perform the work must be named and connected to the value created.
3. **Covenant:** decisions must not be made by one authority alone; multiple people designated by affected communities must agree and act. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

The displayed system language for the proposed transition is:

> DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL  
> EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED  
> EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS

This does not instantly erase debt, transfer ownership, equalize water, or dissolve the Charter Houses. It creates visibility and a process for human decisions. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Economics

### Currency and money language

The official currency is called **credits**. Keel residents usually use:

- **fitch:** individual units of money or credits;
- **salvo:** available balance, remaining money, or financial breathing room. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

An official display may read:

> AVAILABLE BALANCE: 184 CREDITS

A Keel resident may call the same amount “hundred eighty-four salvo.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The language changes by class and city:

- Keel: “How much fitch?” and “What's left in your salvo?”
- Halyard: “What's the cost?”, “What's your available balance?”, “What stake are they offering?”
- Oasis: “Your account has sufficient available credit.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt

Debt can be transferred within a household or family record. At the age of seventeen, Keel treats a person as old enough to receive a parental household liability. A displayed account can include:

> PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE  
> GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED  
> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS

Debt also affects travel status. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The system distinguishes between debt, work income, accumulated interest, and the original principal. Bi11bot's financial vocabulary treats debt as a tool whose consequences depend on what it purchases, its terms, its risks, and what happens if circumstances change. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Ownership and intellectual property

Prosperity distinguishes among:

- making something;
- possessing it;
- controlling it;
- owning it;
- receiving value from it;
- being credited for it. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Halyard's Charter Houses frequently convert a worker's contribution into House-owned intellectual property. The worker may receive a creator bonus, experience, wages, or a job position while the House retains the design and the future value. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Adrian's intellectual estate is recorded as:

> INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE  
> CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM  
> 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED

The material does not yet establish the legal status of all 312 patents or the terms governing disputed claims. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Work and labor

Keel labor includes mining, extraction, pump maintenance, repair, transit work, vending, and maintenance of old infrastructure. Work is governed through:

- shift assignments;
- biometric entry;
- wristband tracking;
- wage payment;
- wage forfeiture;
- supervisor approval;
- safety lines;
- labor records. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Halyard labor includes engineering, fabrication, chemical and membrane research, biofilter research, computation, launch work, mining, and technical maintenance. Technical workers can be highly skilled while remaining subordinate to House ownership and administration. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A worker's material security depends not only on skill but on access to tools, records, facilities, accounts, and permission to move. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Incentives

The system rewards:

- production;
- efficiency;
- compliance;
- risk reduction;
- House ownership;
- keeping records within accepted categories;
- maintaining output;
- avoiding disruption.

It can punish:

- unauthorized repair;
- leaving an assigned zone;
- unregistered movement;
- disputed ownership;
- association with restricted work;
- behavior classified as risky;
- interrupting a House process. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Charter Houses argue that scale reduces duplication and risk. The counter-pressure is that centralized scale also creates concentration risk: a single House, supplier, pipe, institution, or decision-maker can become a point of failure. `(rules/vocabulary.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Technology

### The 80/15/5 plausibility frame

The project is governed by a hard-SF framework:

- **80 percent:** existing systems aged into the future;
- **15 percent:** emerging technologies made ordinary and practical;
- **5 percent:** genuinely new or licensed elements. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The material has not assigned formal T, EG, S, L, or Cut labels to the fictional technology described in the drafts. Those labels remain to be applied during later development. The following entries report the technology as project material rather than resolving its plausibility or canon status.

### Alpha-linked control nodes

Small white-and-amber nodes connect scanners, terminals, gates, data lines, wristbands, infrastructure, and control rooms to Alpha. They can:

- record shifts;
- verify identity and age;
- report unauthorized repairs;
- enforce travel status;
- transmit alerts;
- detect legacy protocols;
- isolate infrastructure segments;
- receive or reject executive commands. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

The nodes are not necessarily physically large or visually imposing. Their power comes from network access and institutional consequences.

### The Measure

The Measure is a compact handheld instrument combining:

- a transparent lens;
- a balance scale;
- a water-flow gauge;
- three foldable arms;
- a connection to physical infrastructure and Alpha's official data line. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Its operating modes are:

- **SENSE:** reads what is physically moving through a pipe or system without consulting Alpha;
- **COMPARE:** connects the physical reading to Alpha's official number;
- **TRACE:** follows a discrepancy through the infrastructure;
- **VERIFY:** seals or confirms the evidence, with no second chance in the described use. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The Measure does not decide what to do with the information. It reveals what is happening and what the official system is hiding. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### The Maker's Seal

The Maker's Seal is a palm-sized bronze medallion with an open-hand symbol. It represents a maker's mark rather than a royal signet. Its associated principle is that ownership must be joined to responsibility and that contribution must remain visible. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The Seal can recognize or record the people who contributed to a work. Its activation is tied to a choice involving multiple real contributors rather than to possession or a legal claim alone. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### The Covenant bands

The Covenant uses three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a name and a promise. The bands represent obligations among three parties. The material establishes that bands can remain separate, link, activate, or fail to activate depending on whether the underlying obligations are real and honored. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Distributed water technology

Adrian's water work combines several technical systems:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilters;
- distributed community water nodes;
- heat recovery;
- compact household-scale integration. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The industrial design is difficult to combine because the five House-developed components use incompatible standards. A practical connector or sleeve can bridge standards without requiring another House-owned patent. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The compact version uses:

- a folded or accordion-pleated membrane to preserve surface area in a smaller footprint;
- a biofilter and distributed-node logic that alternate their power demands;
- mineral recovery and atmospheric capture whose waste heat is returned to the biofilter;
- a toolbox-sized housing;
- an unfiled patent status in the draft. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The design is simulated at 91 percent efficiency and described as costing a fraction of industrial scale. These numbers remain draft evidence, not settled technical canon. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Robotics

The project rules establish two small robots:

- **Bi11bot:** a foldable robot with two expressive optical eyes, an old scarf, and a non-menacing presence. His eyes are expressive and never threatening. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **TJ:** a small repair robot dog built by Bi11bot from spare parts. TJ is held together by magnets; his head, legs, body segments, and tail can separate and snap back together. He can leave his head as a camera, move body segments through narrow gaps, and reach into machinery, ducts, and walls. He does not speak; he communicates through body language, posture, and sounds. `(rules/characters.md)`

The current drafts establish Bi11bot's projector, fitted chest compartment, amber optical eyes, and ability to fold into a tool bag. TJ is fixed as an early Issue 01 presence at Site 6, assembled by Bi11bot from spare parts to reach inaccessible places. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Surveillance and detection

Prosperity uses:

- drones with cold-white beams;
- biometric gates;
- overhead sensors;
- identity bands and wristbands;
- pattern detection;
- passenger-level field reports;
- public and restricted terminals;
- archival projections;
- network alerts;
- mapped-route sweeps. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The system may track patterns rather than individuals directly. A group of five people standing still too long can become suspicious because it forms a pattern in a monitored environment. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Legacy systems and blind spots

Newer systems are built into older mining, water, and industrial infrastructure. The abandoned lithium works contain newer water-control infrastructure inside obsolete mine systems. A hidden chamber contains:

- a manual regulator;
- an analog gauge;
- an official Alpha data line;
- a narrow Keel residential pipe;
- a broad industrial uplink pipe;
- recently oiled bolts and maintained mechanisms. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the central infrastructure and political resource of Prosperity. The system includes:

- reservoirs above Oasis;
- a vertical conduit through all three cities;
- Keel residential lines;
- Halyard and Oasis industrial lines;
- narrow channels in Keel;
- gauges, regulators, pumps, pipes, and manual valves;
- purification plants;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- biofilters;
- distributed community nodes. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The official water records can diverge sharply from delivery. A hidden chamber's gauge reports that 62 percent of the water goes to Keel, while the physical residential pipe is almost dry and the industrial uplink is moving water. A later public display distinguishes:

> REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%

These figures are draft-established evidence of the system's discrepancy; their broader duration and exact administrative cause remain open. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Halyard contains fusion grids and compute centers associated with Helion. Large compute cores are hot, vertical, and intensely monitored. Helion's security system watches patterns and movement around its servers. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Energy infrastructure shares the vertical spine with water and transit. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Daily life

### A Keel workday

A Keel worker may begin before dawn in a pump hall, test machinery by touch, improvise a repair with salvaged parts, and work under a supervisor who cares both about whether the repair functions and whether it is approved. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

At the end of a shift, workers pass through a checkpoint. The scanner can display:

- identity;
- age verification;
- account balance;
- debt transfer;
- earned wages;
- projected clearance time;
- travel status. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Workers may glance at one another's balances without speaking. A large debt is socially visible even when nobody comments on it. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Work and repair culture

Keel residents live among systems that are patched, salvaged, and repeatedly repaired. “Patched” can describe something improvised but functioning, not merely something inferior. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The practical distinction between regulation and function recurs in work culture:

> “That's not the approved repair.”  
> “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Bureaucratic encounters

A person may encounter a system through:

- a scanner;
- a checkpoint;
- a wristband;
- a public registry;
- a House kiosk;
- a review chamber;
- a residency clearance gate;
- a travel assignment;
- a debt display;
- a work reassignment. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The same action may be described differently by the institution and by the person affected:

### Mutual aid

Despite concentrated institutions, Keel retains networks of practical mutual aid. Workers can:

- hide or pass tools;
- preserve doors;
- create diversions;
- share food and travel resources;
- carry information;
- organize around a public discrepancy;
- help someone before knowing whether the help will be repaid. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Mutual aid is not represented as a replacement for institutions. It is a way people carry one another when formal systems make help conditional, recorded, or risky.

### Halyard daily life

Halyard workers move through:

- House-colored uniforms;
- contribution kiosks;
- public patent registries;
- laboratories;
- fabrication districts;
- transit concourses;
- security patterns;
- House-owned workstations;
- technical facilities. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The city looks more prosperous than Keel, but workers still queue, receive assigned roles, and operate under ownership structures they do not control.

### Oasis daily life

Oasis residents move through quiet transit concourses, broad civic spaces, gates, administrative corridors, public displays, and precisely managed environments. The city's abundance is experienced as stillness and control as much as comfort. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The city can feel empty because its systems operate with few visible workers. The absence of crowds is itself a class marker: work, rest, and services are distributed elsewhere or automated. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## Labour and ownership

The central labor conflict is not simply whether people work. People in every tier work, maintain, invent, research, and take risks. The conflict concerns who owns the value produced by that work and who carries the downside when systems fail.

- Keel workers maintain infrastructure they do not own and may be penalized for leaving assigned zones to save someone.
- Halyard engineers and researchers create components that Houses claim through employment and institutional ownership.
- Charter Houses control the facilities, standards, capital, records, and distribution channels necessary to make technical work matter at scale.
- Oasis administrators control the language and authorization structures that define what counts as safe, valid, or permitted. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md) (drafts/chapter-03-draft.md) (rules/vocabulary.md)`

The recurring economic questions are:

- Who made it?
- Who owns it?
- Who gets paid?
- Who gets credit?
- Who controls its use?
- Who carries the risk?
- Who pays when the system is wrong?
- Is something valuable, or merely expensive?
- Does an asset produce cash flow?
- What happens when the payment comes due? `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
