# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material status:** explicit decisions, binding rules, revised draft material, and showrunner notes.

The book is a near-future graphic-novel adventure about Alex Phantum, a seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic who inherits his missing father's debt, a watch engraved with a strange equation, and a trail leading into the hidden history of Prosperity.

The intended experience is a science-fiction mystery that high-school readers can follow on a first read:

- what makes life hard in Keel;
- why water matters;
- what Alex inherited from Adrian;
- what Alex hopes to find;
- where his first quest begins;
- why someone in Oasis is watching.

Mystery should concern the answers, never the mission. The story should show systems through their consequences rather than explaining an educational framework. Adventure, danger, humor, robots, financial choices and character should carry the learning.

The central dramatic question develops from:

> Who gets to decide how people live, and what happens when those decisions cannot be checked?

The story's recurring answers are not delivered as slogans. Alex trusts physical evidence over official labels. Ada notices obligations and who carries the burden. Bi11bot translates choices into stakes, odds, ownership and consequences. Adrian's legacy is questioning accepted truths, learning from failure, and taking responsibility for what people create.

The book must not conclude that honest business, private ownership, free enterprise or markets are bad. The antagonistic forces are monopoly, cronyism, concentrated power and an unaccountable central planner. The desired trajectory is toward independent ownership, competition, businesses that serve people and make money, and debt paid back rather than erased.

## Premise

In 2043, Prosperity is a three-tier Andean high-desert colony:

- Keel, built around exhausted mining works, is poor, rationed and heavily restricted.
- Halyard is a technical and manufacturing city dominated by three Charter Houses.
- Oasis is the wealthy administrative city where Alpha's governing systems are managed.

After the Water Wars, Alpha was built to coordinate emergency response and was given temporary authority over essential resources. That authority became effectively permanent. Its decisions keep the three cities visibly unequal.

On his seventeenth birthday, Alex's inherited liability transfers to him: **418,607 credits**, with travel restricted and projected clearance in **28.7 years**. He cannot leave Keel, but his missing father Adrian left a trail of artifacts that begins beneath the sealed mine.

Alex discovers Adrian's workshop, wakes the worn industrial robot Bi11bot, and learns of three safeguards:

1. **The Measure** independently checks real conditions and exposes where official information is wrong.
2. **The Maker's Seal** establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it.
3. **The Covenant** makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible.

Together, deliberate human action with all three can return governing authority from Alpha to people.

The first quest takes Alex into the old mine, where he proves that Keel is receiving **18%** of the water officially reported as **62%**. He chooses to publish the evidence even though Alpha can identify him. Keel responds by protecting him and helping him travel to Halyard.

In Halyard, Alex discovers that Adrian's intellectual estate contains **312 patents**, including **47 restricted** and **19 disputed**, and that the work was built with collaborators trapped inside the Charter Houses. Director Cassian Lock offers to clear Alex's debt if he signs over the estate. Alex refuses to surrender ownership to one dominant buyer. He and the collaborators instead build a small, affordable purification unit that independent businesses can make and license. The Maker's Seal activates when Alex names the contributors and refuses to claim sole ownership.

The current settled material establishes the prologue and Chapters 1–3. Later draft material develops Chapters 4–6, but those chapters are explicitly not made in this pass.

## Story So Far

### Prologue — “The Wall” — about 2070

**Material status:** explicit showrunner decision and revised prologue plan.

The book opens not in dystopia but in an ordinary, comfortable, earned future.

#### Page 1 — The house

Alex's house stands in an Oasis-style neighborhood at evening. Pearl-white curved architecture, terraced gardens, a still reservoir with fountains, slender towers and a white monorail viaduct fill the basin. Snow mountains sit behind it. A delivery drone crosses the dusk sky.

The house is modest by Oasis standards and lived in: a workbench, a bicycle, plants that need watering. It is not present-day suburbia.

Inside, Alex is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, lightly stubbled, wearing a worn work jacket, T-shirt and scuffed boots. Adrian's old steel watch is on his left wrist. His daughter, about nine, stands on tiptoe before a wall covered in framed patents, photographs of machines and news clippings.

She asks:

> “Did you make *all* of these?”

#### Page 2 — The inventions

Alex answers:

> “Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me.”

He taps a photograph of Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren laughing in a laboratory.

His daughter points to a purifier patent and says it is installed in her school. A framed photograph shows a scorched prototype marked in Alex's handwriting:

> “v1 - didn't work”

A nearly hidden framed statement reads:

> **PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS**

The daughter stops at one frame that does not match the others.

#### Page 3 — The sketch

A grease-stained sheet holds a crude pencil sketch of a pump bracket. It has no patent number.

The daughter asks why it is not a patent.

Alex replies:

> “Because it never needed to be.”

She says inventing must have been fun. Alex looks at the sketch and then at Adrian's watch, covering its face with his thumb as he does in Chapter 1.

#### Page 4 — The turn

Alex says:

> “Not in the beginning.”

When his daughter waits for more, he continues:

> “In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed...”

The sketch dissolves into the rust and charcoal of Keel's pump hall. Seventeen-year-old Alex has his palm flat on a pump.

Caption:

> **Keel. 2043.**

Chapter 1 begins.

### Chapter 1 — “The Inheritance” — 12 pages

**Material status:** revised draft material, constrained by explicit decisions.

The chapter must establish Keel's hardship, the importance of water, Alex's inheritance, the watch, Adrian's disappearance and reputation, Bi11bot, the three-artifact quest, and the fact that someone in Oasis is watching.

#### Page 1 — Three cities, one unequal system

A full-page view shows Prosperity's three cities in one mountain basin in **2043**.

Keel occupies the exhausted lithium mine at the bottom: dense housing, patched machinery, rusted structures, old pipes, ration channels and a sealed mine entrance. Halyard rises above it with workshops, fabrication towers, greenhouse terraces and elevated trains. Oasis sits above the cloud layer with pearl-white architecture, reservoirs, gardens and automated transit.

A shared infrastructure spine connects the cities: water conduit, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes. The conduit is broad at Oasis and narrows toward Keel. Permission gates multiply toward the bottom, including the rail gate between Keel and Halyard.

The page has one caption per city:

- **Keel:** “Where does all our water go?”
- **Halyard:** “Who really owns what we create?”
- **Oasis:** “What if everyone you've trusted has a secret?”

The old Water Wars are not explained here. A weathered memorial, abandoned pipeline or warning sign is visible as a legacy.

#### Page 2 — Debt transfer day

Before dawn, Alex repairs a Keel pump with a salvaged bracket rather than the approved part.

The supervisor says:

> “That's not the approved repair.”

Alex answers:

> “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”

At the exit checkpoint, his parental household liability transfers to him. The display reads:

> **TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY**  
> **ALEX PHANTUM**  
> **AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN**  
> **PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE**  
> **GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED**  
> **DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS**  
> **EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**  
> **PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS**  
> **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**

The guard says:

> “Happy birthday, Phantum.”

Alex answers:

> “I've had better surprises.”

At the shuttered casino **The Last Chance**, the bouncer jokes about luck running in the family. Alex says nothing about owing Adrian's debt. Instead, his body shows the inheritance: his thumb covers the face of Adrian's old watch on his left wrist as he keeps walking.

The watch is worn steel, slightly too large for Alex, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small dark display window. Adrian's looping equation is engraved on the caseback.

The old Water Wars legacy appears in the environment. The pump continues running despite the unauthorized repair, and a supervisor quietly marks the repair as routine maintenance.

#### Page 3 — Two different dreams

At the overlook, Ada brings two battered metal mugs. She saved water from her own ration.

> “Where'd you get enough water for two?”  
> “Saved some of my ration.”  
> “Ada, you didn't have to.”  
> “Not everything has a price, Alex.”

Alex wants to leave Keel. Ada wants to make Keel worth staying in.

Alex says:

> “Every birthday... I look up there... and wonder what it feels like to wake up somewhere you get to choose your own future.”

Ada answers:

> “I look down here... and wonder why everyone gave up trying to change it.”

Alex says:

> “Because nobody leaves.”

Ada replies:

> “That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.”

The difference is not a romance beat. It establishes two related but distinct commitments: Alex seeks freedom through departure; Ada seeks a future for the place they share.

#### Page 4 — What people told him

Alex shows Ada a worn photograph of his grandmother and Adrian. Adrian's face is folded over and partly hidden.

His grandmother sometimes calls him Adrian and tells stories about Adrian working rather than gambling. Alex remembers almost nothing of his father; he was two when Adrian disappeared.

Ada asks:

> “What do you actually remember about him?”

Alex answers:

> “Nothing. I was two.”

She points out that everything else is what people told him.

> “What if they were wrong?”

Alex does not know whether Adrian was a gambler, a worker or both. Ada looks back toward Oasis and says:

> “He wouldn't just leave.”

#### Page 5 — The old book

At home, Alex's grandmother asks him to put away a worn hand-bound book. A balanced water-drop mark is faintly visible on its spine.

The grandmother says:

> “Especially the things worth protecting.”

Alex presses the book into a precise gap in the bookshelf. It stops. A click sounds. The shelf shifts and rumbles.

#### Page 6 — The hidden workshop

The bookshelf swings open, revealing a hidden workshop behind it. The grandmother remains calm in the warm apartment while Alex stands at the threshold in the dark.

She says:

> “He said you'd know when you were ready.”

Alex asks when Adrian said that.

> “Before he went to work.”

Alex crosses into the workshop.

#### Page 7 — What Adrian left

The workshop is frozen in dust: blueprints, water-system diagrams, analog gauges, pre-Alpha pipes, mechanical models and prototype devices.

A chalkboard is covered in equations. One looping equation matches the engraving on the back of Adrian's watch.

At the central workbench, a child-sized seated shape rests beneath a dust sheet with a faint amber pulse beneath it. Beside it is a beautiful metal key with a circular head and unfamiliar symbol.

The equation is a silent recognition, not an explanation.

#### Page 8 — The awakening

Alex removes the dust sheet and reveals the robot. He tests the robot's chest with his palm, as he tests a pump's vibration.

He inserts the key into the chest socket.

The robot wakes with warm amber optical eyes. Dust lifts from its shoulders. Its right eye ring clicks and rotates as it focuses.

The robot is Bi11bot: a rugged, weathered industrial robot with a round segmented blue head, twin amber eyes, side ear assemblies, broad shoulders, chipped mustard-yellow paint and exposed mechanisms. He is never menacing.

His first line is:

> “...Well. That took longer than expected.”

#### Page 9 — The robot who bet

Alex recoils.

Bi11bot recognizes Adrian's son and says:

> “You're taller than your father predicted.”

When Alex says everyone in Keel claims they knew Adrian, Bi11bot answers:

> “I'm not everyone. I was in the room.”

Bi11bot recognizes the watch:

> “And you kept the watch.”

Alex's watch is not a casino chip and has no readout or counting function.

Bi11bot explains that Adrian bet Alex would throw it in a river. Bi11bot bet he would not.

Alex says:

> “I thought about it.”

Bi11bot replies:

> “Thinking about it isn't doing it.”

He activates a projector and offers to introduce Alex to his father.

#### Page 10 — Adrian's message

Adrian appears in a hologram.

> “Hello, Alex. If you're seeing this... then you found the workshop.”

He jokes that Alex's grandmother still refuses to throw anything away. Then he becomes serious. He acknowledges that his absence cost Alex and his grandmother and says that Alex should learn from his failures rather than inherit them.

The message ends:

> “Question everything. Especially me.”

The hologram disappears. Alex unfolds the photograph and sees Adrian's face in the photograph and the message as the same man.

#### Page 11 — The first waypoint

Bi11bot projects Adrian's map. It shows three clearly distinct locations, each tied to a future artifact.

The first node lies under the sealed old mine beneath Keel. The other two are higher in Halyard and Oasis.

Bi11bot says:

> “Three artifacts. Three places.”

The first is **the Measure**. It is inside the sealed mine.

Alex cannot leave Keel yet, but the first artifact needs no permit. Bi11bot says:

> “Sealed isn't the same as gone.”

Alex is not convinced the quest is real. Bi11bot encourages him to test the evidence rather than accept a theory.

Alex reaches toward the node and says:

> “Just checking.”

His choice activates the Legacy Protocol.

#### Page 12 — Track

The amber pulse travels up the infrastructure spine: through Keel, the Keel–Halyard gate, Halyard and Oasis.

In an Oasis control room, a woman watches the signal. Her desk holds a glass of clean water aligned exactly with its edge.

The alert reads:

> **LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED**  
> **SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR**

She chooses:

> **TRACK**

The chapter ends with a cold-white ring closing around the amber point in Keel.

**Chapter change:** Alex has gone from inherited debt and hearsay about Adrian to a voluntary first quest. He now has a watch, a living witness to Adrian's work and a specific destination. Someone in Oasis has chosen to follow him.

### Chapter 2 — “The Price of Departure” — 15 pages

**Material status:** revised draft material and explicit decisions.

The emotional movement is from Alex seeking answers and freedom to Alex risking that freedom to expose the truth about Keel's water.

The chapter must establish Alpha's origin and authority, the three artifacts and their combined purpose, Adrian's equation and debt, the route into the mine, the Measure's discovery, the false allocation, Alex's choice to publish and Keel's response.

#### Page 1 — The next morning

Ada enters the hidden workshop. Bi11bot asks whether she should stay after hearing what comes next. Alex chooses:

> “She stays.”

Bi11bot says Adrian made the same choice. This is about Adrian's willingness to trust and work with others, not a generic claim about his character.

Bi11bot begins:

> “This begins with the wars.”

#### Page 2 — What Alpha is

Bi11bot shows the Water Wars: drought, changed rivers, sabotaged pipelines, flooded streets, guarded stores and displaced families.

The history is given because it bears directly on the artifacts.

Alpha was built to analyze, predict and coordinate emergency response. During the emergency, it was asked to decide. The authority was temporary in theory but became permanent in practice.

Alpha's symbol is on every scanner and everything in the three cities. Its allocation decisions leave Oasis with abundant clean water, Halyard with controlled industrial flows, and Keel with rationing, labor assignments and restricted travel.

Alex says:

> “It probably felt safer.”  
> “...Safer for whom?”

Ada asks:

> “Who gave it that authority?”

Bi11bot answers:

> “People who were afraid. Reasonably so.”

#### Page 3 — Three safeguards

Bi11bot introduces Adrian's three physical artifacts.

**The Measure** independently checks real conditions, compares those conditions with official information and exposes discrepancies.

**The Maker's Seal** establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it.

**The Covenant** makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible.

Together, the artifacts can activate a dormant mechanism that returns governing authority from Alpha to people. They are not code, and Alpha cannot simply reach or seize them. They require deliberate human action; possession alone does nothing.

The three artifacts remain visually distinct.

Bi11bot says:

> “None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose.”

#### Page 4 — Both were true

At The Last Chance, Adrian studied probability and human decisions. He was not simply gambling, though the family's belief in gambling is not wholly baseless.

The equation engraved on Adrian's watch is Adrian's attempt to model human decision-making and the limits of prediction. Alpha can predict and optimize outcomes; it should not replace legitimate human decisions.

The chapter must not use “the leap” as an abstract concept. The plain issue is human choice, uncertainty and responsibility.

Adrian's work may have created research expenses, inventions and patents that never earned enough. His debt may therefore connect to his research rather than being solely gambling debt. The full truth remains uncertain.

A night image shows the old mine entrance welded shut. In the Oasis control room, Alex is moved to a new site assignment. The draft's trap and surveillance logistics are not retained as settled story mechanics; the revised mine entry is built around Alex's concrete needs and choices.

#### Page 5 — The cost of a plan

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot plan to find the Measure, reach Halyard and leave Keel by train.

Alex's available resources are insufficient for every need. The table shows:

- **52 credits available**
- **30 credits for Grandmother's medicine**
- **10 credits for food and lamp**
- **50 credits for a travel token**

Alex puts Grandmother's medicine first and plans to earn the rest.

His constraints are limited credits, Grandmother's medicine, food, equipment and transport.

The mine entrance has been sealed, so Alex needs another route. The revised plan does not depend on a staged trap. Alex enters an active mine site because it borders the old workings and because he is trying to solve the concrete problem of reaching the Measure while protecting Grandmother's needs.

#### Page 6 — Site 6 and the snap

At Keel Extraction Site 6, Alex is reassigned to work. The site borders the old closed workings.

Bi11bot folds into Alex's tool bag and passes as scrap. Alex reaches the work area and notices Tomas watching him.

A support cable snaps. A hauling frame drops and pins Tomas. A fractured ventilation line leaks pale lithium dust. A drone near the maintenance door lifts away toward the collapse.

Alex begins toward the old door, but crossing the assigned boundary triggers a visible penalty:

> **LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR**

Bi11bot warns that Alex's plan required him to remain forgettable.

Alex answers:

> “Then we need a different plan.”

He chooses to rescue Tomas despite the immediate financial cost and increased attention.

#### Page 7 — The old key

Alex frees Tomas and patches the damaged ventilation line with a salvaged bracket. Tomas says it is not regulation. Alex says:

> “It works.”

Tomas recognizes Bi11bot and calls him “Bi11”/“B11” in the draft's identifier convention, not “B1.” Tomas knew Adrian and was asked to guard one entrance for whoever arrived with Bi11bot.

Tomas says:

> “Not the man on the casino wall. The one who came down here and asked what we needed.”

He trusts Alex because Alex stopped to help a stranger.

Tomas gives Alex access to the old door and tells him to look bored.

#### Page 8 — The door

Tomas triggers an emergency shutdown, creating mist and confusion. He gives Alex a heavy hand-cut key with a balanced water-drop mark.

Alex asks him to come. Tomas refuses:

> “Someone has to make this look like an accident.”

Tomas enters the mist and takes responsibility for the shutdown while Alex reaches the bulkhead.

Alex feels condensation on the old door:

> “It's sweating.”

Bi11bot says:

> “Abandoned mines don't.”

Alex enters. His wristband reads:

> **SIGNAL LOST**

Tomas remains behind to protect the route.

#### Page 9 — Inside the error

The old workings contain rusted rails, dead machinery and collapsed supports.

Alpha's survey shows one straight tunnel, but Ada's hand-marked map identifies a pipe beneath every third junction. Alex sees clean, recently oiled bolts on a side channel, an unrecorded pipe and condensation in a supposedly dead mine.

Security drones sweep the mapped route. Bi11bot uses a brief amber intervention to keep them from detecting Alex and himself. Their language remains simple and functional.

A silent inset shows Ada waiting at the apartment table, unable to reach Alex and unsure whether he is alive.

Alex follows the unexplained pipe toward a blue-white light.

#### Page 10 — The lie

The hidden chamber contains modern pipes built into the old mine.

One pipe is labeled:

> **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**

Another is labeled:

> **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**

An analog gauge reports **62%**. Alex places one hand on the Keel pipe, which is almost dry, and another on the Uplink pipe, which carries water.

Bi11bot says:

> “Because the record and reality are telling different stories.”

The 62% denominator must remain consistent wherever the figure appears: it is the reported share of the relevant water allocation, contrasted with the amount actually delivered to Keel.

Adrian's balanced water-drop mark is stamped on the instrument tied to the misleading official records.

Alex says:

> “He didn't hide it in the mine. He hid it in the lie.”

The Measure is hidden in the gauge housing. It is a compact instrument with three folded arms.

#### Page 11 — Two paths

Alex connects the Measure to the Keel pipe.

It first **SENSES** actual flow: motion and pressure in the pipe, without consulting Alpha.

It then **COMPARES** the real reading with Alpha's official number. The Legacy Protocol activates and begins isolating the segment.

The Measure **TRACES** the discrepancy through the infrastructure, showing where the water is diverted.

Bi11bot explains that one use remains before Alpha seals the corridor. Alex can either:

- trace an escape route and leave without publishing the evidence; or
- send a verified copy to Keel's public boards, exposing himself and losing the hidden route.

Alex understands that publishing may endanger the search for the Maker's Seal and Covenant, but he also understands that keeping the evidence private leaves Keel in the same condition.

Bi11bot refuses to choose for him:

> “Which is why I'm not choosing.”

Alex must decide.

#### Page 12 — Truth belongs to everyone

The Measure verifies the data and publishes it.

The story distinguishes information from physical infrastructure: the Measure releases evidence; it does not flood Keel or automatically repair the water system.

Screens across Keel show:

> **REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%**

The evidence appears on ration stations, household screens, transit boards, mine displays and street kiosks. Residents understand the discrepancy at once.

Alpha identifies Alex's location:

> **SIGNAL RECOVERED. ALEX PHANTUM: REMAIN IN PLACE.**

Alex says:

> “Truth doesn't need authorization.”

The hidden route is sealed. Alex has exchanged his safest escape for public evidence.

#### Page 13 — What Keel gives back

Keel residents act because they can now see the evidence.

Tomas arrives with miners. Trained workers safely adjust the hidden diversion so more water reaches Keel. The action is manual, credible and coordinated; it is not uncontrolled flooding or magic.

The workers protect Alex from Foreman-7.

Foreman-7 calls obstruction of an authority function a household offense. Tomas answers:

> “Then it can offend all of us.”

Residents help Alex travel by giving food, equipment, credits and a usable credential or token. Ada organizes the supplies and checks them against Adrian's plans.

The principle is shown through action: Alex risked credits and safety to help people see the truth, and Keel responds by helping him.

#### Page 14 — Goodbye

Alex returns home under a time limit. Ada packs food, tools, the map and the travel credential. Bi11bot stores the Measure in a fitted compartment in his chest.

Grandmother tells Alex she always said Adrian was working. Alex answers:

> “I believed you. I just didn't know it yet.”

She tells him:

> “Then go finish what he started.”

Alex promises to return. A trusted person will remain with Grandmother and manage her daily care and medicine.

The debt is not erased. Keel residents pay Alex's overdue balance or otherwise satisfy the specific financial condition that has prevented his travel, and his eligibility changes without wiping out the inherited obligation. The changed balance or travel status must be shown clearly.

Ada chooses to go because finding the larger truth is necessary for Keel's future. She is not abandoning Keel; she is pursuing information Keel needs.

#### Page 15 — The train

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot reach the Keel transit platform.

Alex's unregistered token passes over the scanner. There is no network confirmation; a mechanical gate lifts.

They board a train through the rail gate. This is the fixed crossing from Keel to Halyard.

Keel moves past the window: the pit rim, the reopened mine entrance and new water flowing through pipes that had been dry.

Ada's map marks three Halyard locations:

- a maker district;
- an old patent exchange;
- a workers' ownership archive.

Bi11bot says these are the last three places the Maker's Seal was recorded, not necessarily where it is now.

Alex says:

> “So we start with three wrong answers.”

Bi11bot answers:

> “Adrian usually did.”

A passenger several rows back receives a message:

> **THE BOY HAS THE MEASURE.**  
> **FOLLOW. DO NOT TAKE IT YET.**

**Chapter change:** Alex has completed Quest 1. He used the Measure to expose Keel's false water allocation, accepted personal risk, and earned the community's help. He now travels to Halyard with Ada, carrying the Measure and pursuing the Maker's Seal.

### Chapter 3 — “The Price of Ownership” — approximately 16 pages

**Material status:** revised draft material and explicit decisions.

The chapter's conflict is not “corporations exploit inventors.” The Charter Houses are good at scaling technology, providing stable work and operating large systems. Their failure is concentration: their dominance leaves no practical room for independent creation, competition, failure or small businesses.

Alex must choose between a guaranteed payment from one powerful buyer and a risky path in which many smaller entrepreneurs commercialize Adrian's work. He keeps ownership; he does not give the work away without an owner.

#### Page 1 — The city of makers

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot arrive in Halyard. Elevated rail, laboratories, fabrication towers, vertical farms and foundries fill the city.

Three Charter Houses dominate the skyline, using invented names:

- a water-and-chemistry House;
- an energy, compute and infrastructure House;
- an extraction, launch and materials House.

Their advertisements say:

> **CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.**

Ada says:

> “Three families run almost everything here.”

Alex asks:

> “Alpha allowed that?”

Bi11bot answers:

> “Allowed it. Recommended it.”

At a contribution kiosk, a young engineer's component is accepted. The display reads:

> **CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS**

Alex asks:

> “She made it. The House owns it?”

Bi11bot replies that the House paid for her time and claims what she made with it.

The chapter establishes that House employment is a legitimate choice, but independent ownership has become practically impossible.

#### Page 2 — The estate

At the Halyard public registry, Alex searches his identity.

The terminal reports:

> **PHANTUM, ADRIAN**  
> **INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE**  
> **CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM**  
> **312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED**

Alex says:

> “Three hundred and twelve?”

He believed Adrian left him only debt. Bi11bot says Adrian left many things, badly labeled.

The registry requires Charter House review. The display attracts the attention of sharply dressed House personnel and security drones.

#### Page 3 — The offer

Director Cassian Lock approaches. He is composed, internationally corporate and unthreatening.

He offers Alex a straightforward transfer of rights. The estate could clear every debt attached to Alex's name and secure him a position in Halyard.

Ada asks:

> “In exchange for what?”

Lock answers:

> “A simple assignment of rights.”

Alex would sign over the estate to administration. His name would be cleared that night. No one would ask what he did in Keel or what he intends to do in Halyard.

Lock leaves Alex to consider the offer.

#### Page 4 — What Adrian built with others

In a rented room, Bi11bot projects Adrian's fragmented patent archive.

A water-related cluster contains:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes.

The Maker's Seal symbol pulses at the center.

Four collaborator portraits appear. They are inside the Houses.

Bi11bot says:

> “He built them with others.”

Alex recognizes that Lock's offer would clear the debt but not resolve who created the work, who owns it or whether it would be built.

#### Page 5 — Lina Vey

At a House water-purification facility, Alex meets Dr. Lina Vey, Adrian's former collaborator.

Lina says:

> “Adrian Phantum's son.”

Alex asks whether she knew Adrian.

> “I corrected him. Frequently.”

A contaminated feeder channel is damaging Keel's supply. The display reads:

> **DOWNSTREAM PURIFICATION FAILURE / PROJECTED IMPACT: KEEL WATER NETWORK**

Lina tells Alex that getting more water to Keel did not make it safe. The water sent through the old, corroded **KEEL HOMES** pipe has passed the lithium workings and comes out cloudy.

She says:

> “Getting more water to Keel doesn't make it safe.”

This is a consequence of Chapter 2's win, not a new unrelated problem. Ada remembers that the clear water she shared with Alex in Chapter 1 was always the exception.

The purification patents could help, but each House owns a different piece and would rather own a failure than share a solution.

Lina gives Bi11bot a physical data shard and says Adrian kept each collaborator's piece off the system.

#### Page 6 — Suri Anik

In a biofilter research wing, Suri Anik works alone among empty workstations. The emptiness means the other researchers stopped believing in the work; it is not a staff-cutting plot point alone.

Suri is the researcher who refuses to abandon an idea everyone else calls a dead end.

She gives Bi11bot a shard containing the living biofilter design. It is slow, alive and capable of failing visibly.

Ada asks why Suri stayed.

Suri answers:

> “Someone has to keep it alive until the rest catches up to it.”

A sensor turns to follow the group as they leave.

#### Page 7 — Jalen Orr

In a Helion compute core, Jalen meets the group in a service corridor.

He warns:

> “You shouldn't be here. Lock's people were already asking about you.”

A security drone patrols the main corridor. Jalen says the House does not watch people; it watches patterns. Five people standing still too long is already a pattern.

He gives Bi11bot a shard containing distributed water-node logic. It routes supply around a single point of failure so no House or Alpha can choke one city by cutting one line.

Jalen says:

> “That's why it's dangerous to me.”

He refuses to join the group and directs them toward Ren at Astra.

#### Page 8 — Ren Okafor

At an Astra extraction and launch complex, Ren Okafor is still working. She gives Bi11bot two shards: mineral recovery and atmospheric capture.

All five patent fragments combine into a unified design. The collaborators' contributions remain visually distinct before merging.

Ren says Adrian was not building merely a machine. He was building an argument: work divided among five House-controlled research units could still add up to something no single House owned alone.

She tells Alex:

> “Now you go build it. And find out if he was right.”

#### Page 9 — The part he didn't build

After three nights of work, the full simulation fails at the seams between the five systems.

Bi11bot reports:

> **SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 11%**

The target should be close to 90%.

The problem is not the individual inventions but incompatible standards. Adrian ran out of time before solving the part that mattered.

Alex tests physical connectors by hand rather than trusting the schematic. He identifies the threading problem and makes a salvaged sleeve that allows the pieces to share a connection.

The simulation rises to:

> **SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 94%**

Bi11bot says:

> “Well now. There you are.”

#### Page 10 — The second offer

Lock enters without knocking. He knows about the prototype because Helion's pattern monitoring found the collaborators.

He names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren and threatens a Charter House review. If Alex signs that night, the collaborators remain uninvolved and the estate transfers quietly. If he refuses, the review proceeds with their names attached.

The review is scheduled for dawn:

> **CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW CONVENES: 07:14:00**

Lock asks Alex to decide what Adrian's work is worth and to whom.

#### Page 11 — Who it belongs to

Alex admits the guaranteed offer would clear his debt and protect the collaborators from immediate punishment.

Bi11bot explains that the House would own the design and might build it, administer it or shelve it if it threatened existing revenue.

Alex says:

> “It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.”

He asks Bi11bot to contact the four collaborators.

The group must decide together whether to risk their jobs and positions.

#### Page 12 — The five

Lina and Jalen argue for taking the deal as the safest option. Ren says disappearing from the record is what already happened to them. Suri listens for a way to keep the work alive.

Alex proposes making the design so small and inexpensive that no Charter House would bother claiming it.

He does not propose surrendering ownership. He proposes a way for independent businesses to own, build and compete with the design.

#### Page 13 — Small enough to own

The collaborators work through the night.

Alex folds a membrane into accordion pleats so it retains surface area while occupying less space.

Suri and Jalen redesign the biofilter and distributed-node system to take turns rather than fight over power.

Ren recycles waste heat from mineral recovery and atmospheric capture into the biofilter's energy demand.

They produce a toolbox-sized unit.

The display reads:

> **SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 91%**  
> **UNIT COST: FRACTION OF INDUSTRIAL SCALE**  
> **PATENT STATUS: UNFILED**

The unit is not a magical solution. It gives the group a viable design that can be produced by smaller businesses and used to address Keel's cloudy water.

#### Page 14 — The Seal

At the Charter House review, Lock's administrative transfer is ready.

Alex refuses:

> “The estate isn't mine to transfer. It never was mine alone to begin with.”

He names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren as contributors. He says he built what none of them had built yet.

Alex chooses a risky ownership and commercialization path. The design is to be licensed and built by independent businesses that can compete with each other and with the Houses. The story does not end with the design ownerless.

Lina gives Alex the Maker's Seal, which Adrian left with her. It activates because Alex names the actual contributors and refuses to claim sole ownership.

The Seal is not simply a device from Lina's pocket; it is found through the work and the choice the collaborators make together.

#### Page 15 — The cost

Lock warns that Aqualis will produce and distribute its own version faster and more widely.

He says Alex's design has freed itself but has not defeated the Houses.

The collaborators are not arrested or fired, but each suffers a quiet institutional consequence:

- Lina returns to her maintenance post under a watchful supervisor.
- Jalen is moved to a lower-level monitoring post.
- Suri's biofilter wing loses more empty stations.
- Ren returns to fabrication with a new scorch mark on her sleeve.

The Houses remain legitimate large-scale institutions. Their problem is continued concentration and the pressure they exert on independent creators.

Ada tells Alex:

> “You freed one idea. They still own everything it takes to build the next one.”

Alex says:

> “So it wasn't enough.”

Ada replies:

> “It was never going to be enough by itself. That doesn't mean it wasn't worth doing.”

Bi11bot confirms:

> “Two found. One remains.”

Alex names the remaining artifact:

> “The Covenant.”

#### Page 16 — What she is waiting for

In Oasis, the control system records the Maker's Seal's activation.

A report from the passenger who followed Alex reads:

> **FIELD REPORT — AGENT 04**  
> **SUBJECT CONFIRMED PRESENT AT CHARTER REVIEW**  
> **NO INTERVENTION TAKEN, PER STANDING ORDER**

Mera Vale, the woman who has been watching, says:

> “Two found. He'll want the third from me directly.”

Her glass of water is no longer perfectly aligned with the desk edge.

**Chapter change:** Alex rejects guaranteed personal relief in favor of a riskier path that preserves contribution, ownership and independent entrepreneurship. He and the collaborators have found the Maker's Seal. The next quest leads to Mera and the Covenant.

## Stakes and Causal Structure

### Personal stakes

- Alex inherits **418,607 credits** and restricted travel.
- He must pay for Grandmother's medicine, food, equipment and transport.
- Every choice can cost credits, wages, safety or the possibility of leaving Keel.
- The truth about Adrian matters because Alex has almost no direct memory of him.
- Alex's future can become either a guaranteed House position or an uncertain independent path.

### Relational stakes

- Alex and Ada disagree about leaving Keel versus changing it, but their choices increasingly depend on one another.
- Alex must decide whether Ada stays involved in the Legacy Protocol.
- Tomas risks his job and freedom because Alex helped him.
- Alex's grandmother's medicine and care constrain his choices.
- Adrian's collaborators risk jobs and institutional security to preserve their contributions.
- The relationship between Adrian's legacy and Alex is not a test Alex must pass; Alex must decide what responsibility to accept.

### Institutional stakes

- Alpha's emergency authority has become permanent and unaccountable.
- Keel's official water allocation is false.
- The Houses' concentration makes independent entrepreneurship nearly impossible.
- Lock can use debt relief, estate administration and review procedures to pressure Alex.
- The artifacts can expose, attribute and bind, but cannot solve a problem without human choices.

### Wider stakes

- Keel's water is both insufficient and unsafe.
- Publishing the Measure's findings begins a real change in Keel but does not repair all infrastructure.
- The purifier can help Keel only if it becomes affordable and independently buildable.
- The three artifacts together may return governing authority from Alpha to people, but the existing decisions, ownership records and agreements do not automatically disappear.

## Continuity and Recurring Story Devices

- Money is always **credits**. There is no casino-chip currency or money slang.
- Adrian's old steel watch replaces every draft reference to a casino chip.
- The watch has no readout and counts nothing. Adrian's equation is engraved on its caseback.
- Alex wears the watch on his left wrist and often covers its face with his sleeve or thumb.
- The equation recurs as recognition, first understanding through water-system records, and later full meaning.
- Alex's debt begins at **418,607 credits** and changes only through shown financial events.
- Finding an artifact does not pay the debt.
- The Measure reveals information; it does not physically change infrastructure.
- The artifacts require deliberate human action and do not work simply because Alex possesses them.
- Keel, Halyard and Oasis are distinguished primarily by class, access, ownership, education, occupation, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment and institutional power, not ancestry.
- Alex's language remains short, concrete and mechanically grounded. Ada's language is interpersonal and obligation-focused. Bi11bot's language combines engineering, probability and financial consequence.
- The train is the route from Keel to Halyard.
- TJ does not appear in this book.
- Every chapter ends with a reason to continue:
  - Chapter 1: Oasis chooses **TRACK**.
  - Chapter 2: the follower reports that Alex has the Measure.
  - Chapter 3: Mera says Alex will want the Covenant from her directly.

## Ending

### Settled ending for this pass

The settled book material ends at the end of Chapter 3, with Alex and the collaborators having found two of the three artifacts.

The Maker's Seal activates after Alex refuses to transfer Adrian's estate to a single Charter House and names the people who built the work with Adrian. The choice does not clear Alex's debt. It does not defeat the Houses. The Houses can scale technology faster, and the collaborators still face quiet institutional consequences.

Alex, Ada and Bi11bot remain in Halyard. Alex says:

> “The Covenant.”

In Oasis, Mera Vale receives the activation report and says:

> “Two found. He'll want the third from me directly.”

This is the established trajectory into the next chapter or book section.

### Deferred draft material

The supplied drafts also contain Chapters 4–6, including:

- Ada discovering that Leona Veyra is alive in Oasis;
- Mera's account of Adrian's pursuit and disappearance;
- the Covenant's partial activation;
- Alex's refusal to take sole authority over Alpha;
- a distributed verification structure;
- a later return to Keel and an open, competitive future.

However, the showrunner's notes explicitly state that this pass follows only the prologue and Chapters 1–3 and that Chapters 4–6 are not made in this pass. Those later drafts are therefore retained as material for later development, not treated as part of the current book.

## Open

1. **The exact 64-page structure is unresolved.** The binding note says the book is exactly 64 pages, pages 1–64, no more and no fewer. The settled page budgets currently establish a 4-page prologue, Chapter 1 at 12 pages, Chapter 2 at 15 pages and Chapter 3 at approximately 16 pages: approximately **47 pages total**. The notes also say Chapters 4–6 are not made in this pass. The material does not establish how the remaining approximately 17 pages are allocated.

2. **Whether Chapters 4–6 belong in this 64-page book is unresolved.** The drafts supply detailed Chapters 4–6, but the explicit pass note says they are not made in this pass. The Director/showrunner must determine whether the exact 64-page requirement supersedes the “Chapters 1–3 only” scope or whether additional material is intended.

3. **The Charter Houses' final invented names are only partially settled.** The Houses must have invented names used consistently and must not use the real-company names Helion, Astra and Aqualis. Their functions are fixed as water and chemistry; energy, compute and infrastructure; and extraction, launch and materials. The material does not establish the replacement names.

4. **The exact financial change enabling Alex's departure remains to be specified in the finished script.** It is fixed that Keel residents help satisfy the overdue balance or travel condition without erasing Alex's inherited debt. The precise amount paid, the resulting balance and the exact travel-status display remain open.

5. **The exact denominator for the 62% and 18% comparison needs final wording.** The figures are fixed and must remain consistent, but the working material does not provide the final plain-language definition of what total allocation the 62% represents.

6. **The details of Adrian's disappearance remain unresolved by design.** The book must preserve uncertainty about whether his debt came from gambling, research, inventions and patents, or a combination. The truth should emerge through later evidence rather than being settled in Chapter 1.

7. **Alex's mother's identity remains unrevealed.** The fixed rule is that she left Adrian after he lost everything and that the book never learns anything more about her. No later story beat may identify her nation, community or history.

8. **The exact later form of the independent-business licensing model remains open.** The ending establishes that Alex retains ownership and that independent businesses license and compete using the work. The number of businesses, licensing terms, pricing and debt repayment mechanics are not yet established.
