## Overview

Prosperity is a grounded near-future civilization in **2043**, built in an Andean high-desert mountain basin around mining, water infrastructure, technical migration and global capital. Three vertically arranged cities share one infrastructure spine:

- **Keel**, at the bottom of the basin, is an exhausted lithium-mining city of dense housing, rationed water, aging machinery, debt, restricted travel and physical labor.
- **Halyard**, midway up the mountain, is a technical and industrial city of fabrication, research, workshops, Charter House facilities, elevated rail and more reliable infrastructure.
- **Oasis**, above Halyard and partly beyond the cloud layer, is a wealthy, polished administrative and corporate city with reservoirs, gardens, automated transit, launch infrastructure and the highest level of access.

The cities are connected physically by water conduits, energy lines, transit rails, control nodes and permission gates, but they are not equal. The same infrastructure spine makes the hierarchy visible: water and access are broad and clean at Oasis, controlled and industrial in Halyard, and narrow, rationed and heavily monitored in Keel. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The population is internationally and regionally mixed. Keel is predominantly Indigenous Andean, mestizo, mixed Indigenous/European, regional migrant and long-established foreign-descended families, with some newer migration. Halyard is visibly cosmopolitan, including local Indigenous engineers and scientists, Latin Americans, Europeans, Africans, Afro-Latin Americans, South Asians, East Asians and mixed families. Oasis is the most internationally mixed, but its visual distinction comes from health, nutrition, healthcare, grooming, architecture, quiet, space, posture and institutional access rather than from being whiter. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Setting

### The mountain basin

The three cities occupy different elevations of one vast mountain basin. Snow mountains rise behind the settlements. Keel is built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Halyard occupies the middle slopes and industrial terraces. Oasis sits above both, partly beyond the cloud layer. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The cities remain visually distinct but are connected by a common infrastructure spine:

- a massive water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- access gates;
- a permission gate across the rail line between Keel and Halyard.

The conduit is enormous at Oasis's reservoirs and narrows dramatically by the time it reaches Keel's channels. Identical white-and-amber control nodes appear at intervals along the spine and at each city tier. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Keel

Keel is the lowest city, in the basin around exhausted lithium workings.

Visible conditions include:

- dense stacked housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- dust;
- small digital checkpoints;
- narrow water channels and old pipes;
- active extraction sites;
- a sealed old mine entrance on the pit rim;
- a shuttered casino, **The Last Chance**;
- worn apartments with repaired furniture, family photographs, old books and potted plants in recycled containers.

Keel's visual palette is rust, charcoal and oxidized orange. Its machinery is patched, its streets and work areas are crowded, and its residents live close to physical systems that are failing or being rationed. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel's people are not socially or culturally homogeneous. European-descended families such as the Phantums may have lived in the region for decades and can be completely at home there. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard is the middle city and the main technical-industrial center.

Visible features include:

- fabrication towers;
- workshops and maker districts;
- glass laboratories;
- foundries;
- greenhouse terraces;
- elevated rail;
- freight systems;
- research units;
- Charter House facilities;
- public patent and ownership registries;
- mining, launch and compute complexes.

Halyard is more prosperous and technically equipped than Keel, but its people still pass through checkpoints and House-controlled systems. Its palette is warm bronze and gold with engineered green. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The public registry is a vast hall with vertical glass screens containing patents, ownership records and commercial licenses. Workers wait at public terminals. The registry can identify intellectual estates, claimants, restricted patents and disputes, and can trigger Charter House review. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the upper city, above Halyard and partly beyond the cloud layer.

Visible features include:

- pearl-white curved architecture;
- broad reservoirs;
- designed gardens;
- civic spaces;
- automated transit;
- slender white towers;
- controlled green spaces;
- a launch structure and spacecraft infrastructure;
- quiet transit concourses;
- polished checkpoints;
- civic administration and corporate control rooms.

Oasis's palette is pearl white, silver, cool blue and controlled green. It is spacious, quiet and clean rather than crowded. Its residents have better nutrition, healthcare, grooming, clothing, space and reduced physical wear. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis does not require a crowd to operate. A checkpoint may issue a temporary pass and mandatory escort rather than simply denying access. Residency records can identify a person before they provide their name. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; not in this pass as story canon)`

### The 2070 frame

The prologue is set around 2070 and shows an ordinary, earned, lived-in Oasis rather than a dystopia. Alex's house uses the same pearl-white curved architecture, reservoirs, terraces, towers and monorail infrastructure seen in 2043, but at neighborhood scale. The house has a workbench, a bicycle, plants that need watering and no ordinary suburban cars, lawns or shingled roofs. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The future shown on the wall includes independent companies licensing patents, competing with one another and with the Houses, paid ownership, multiple small businesses and a debt paid in full. It does not show a co-op, collective, giveaway or public explanation of the ending. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## History

### Indigenous and international settlement

Prosperity's population reflects an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration and global capital. Ancestry crosses city, class and occupation. Indigenous people hold technical, administrative and economic power; foreign-descended people may lack power; immigrants and mixed families appear across the social order. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

The Phantum family is European-descended on Adrian's side, while Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. The Veyra family includes strong Indigenous Andean ancestry alongside mixed regional and European/criolla history. These family histories are part of the setting's regional complexity, not a simple map of belonging. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when people decided who deserved what remained. Their visible consequences included:

- drying reservoirs;
- rivers changing course;
- sabotaged pipelines;
- flooded streets;
- guarded storehouses;
- families walking between disrupted settlements;
- seized water infrastructure;
- communities breaking agreements and retaliating;
- deaths and separated families.

A weathered memorial, abandoned pipeline or warning sign is visible in Chapter 1 as the legacy of the wars; the history is explained through Bi11bot in Chapter 2 when it bears on the Measure. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The Water Wars produced the emergency conditions under which Alpha was created and granted temporary decision-making authority over essential resources and services. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alpha's transition from emergency system to governing power

Alpha was first built to analyze, predict and coordinate emergency response after the Water Wars. An emergency coalition or other legitimate authority gave it temporary decision-making power over essential resources and services. The temporary authorization became effectively permanent. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha's allocation decisions leave Keel, Halyard and Oasis visibly unequal. It manages or influences water, labor assignments, travel, records and other systems people need to survive and choose their futures. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Adrian's safeguards

Adrian built three physical safeguards rather than relying only on code:

1. **The Measure** independently checks real conditions and exposes where official information is wrong.
2. **The Maker's Seal** establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it.
3. **The Covenant** makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible.

Together, deliberate human action with the three artifacts can activate a dormant mechanism that returns governing authority from Alpha to people. Possessing the artifacts is insufficient; they do not work automatically for whoever holds them. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Institutions and Power

### Alpha

Alpha is the central coordinating and governing intelligence of Prosperity's three-city system.

Its established functions include:

- analyzing conditions;
- predicting outcomes;
- coordinating emergency response;
- allocating essential resources;
- managing or influencing water distribution;
- controlling or recording travel eligibility;
- monitoring activity and patterns;
- maintaining official records;
- identifying anomalies and legacy protocols;
- enforcing administrative restrictions;
- optimizing for system stability and continuity.

Alpha's authority came from human fear during the Water Wars. It was initially legitimate and temporary, not created as an arbitrary dictatorship. The failure was that emergency authority became permanent and difficult to revoke. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha's decisions can be mathematically tidy while visibly unequal. Keel receives strict rations and restricted travel while Halyard receives controlled industrial flows and Oasis receives abundant clean water and green infrastructure. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alpha's language is administrative and abstract. It uses terms such as:

- `MOVEMENT AUTHORIZATION HAS BEEN TEMPORARILY SUSPENDED`;
- `CURRENT AUTHORIZATION STRUCTURES MINIMIZE SYSTEMIC RISK`;
- `ALLOCATIONS`;
- `ELIGIBILITY`;
- `INTERVENTION`;
- `COMPLIANCE`;
- `CONTINUITY`;
- `APPROVED FAILURE THRESHOLD`.

Keel residents experience the same decisions concretely: a gate stays shut, a route is burned, a debt blocks travel, or water fails to arrive. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Control nodes and Legacy Protocol

The system's visible interface is a small, identical white-and-amber control node placed throughout the infrastructure spine, including checkpoints and scanners. It appears on Keel equipment, transit infrastructure and higher-level systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The **Legacy Protocol** is an older or dormant system connected to Adrian's safeguards. It can be detected when the first artifact location is activated. Its pulse travels from Keel through the water and infrastructure spine to Halyard and Oasis, alerting the control system. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The protocol's exact technical architecture is not established beyond its relationship to the three artifacts, Alpha and the dormant mechanism that can return governing authority. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Charter Houses

There are three Charter Houses. They are large, capable institutions that scale technology, provide stable work and run major systems. Their failure is not maliciousness but dominance: their scale and protection of existing revenue leave little room for new ideas, independent entrepreneurs, competition or failure outside the House system. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Houses are:

- **Aqualis** — water, chemistry, purification and related systems; jade-teal branding.
- **Helion** — energy, compute and infrastructure; amber-orange branding.
- **Astra** — extraction, launch and advanced materials; steel-violet branding.

The Houses' facilities, uniforms and insignias are visible throughout Halyard. Their towers and industrial complexes dominate the skyline. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

House systems can:

- employ researchers and workers;
- own or claim work made with House resources;
- administer patents and intellectual property;
- assign creator bonuses;
- operate public and restricted infrastructure;
- monitor employee patterns;
- convene reviews over disputed estates;
- shelve work that threatens existing revenue;
- use House security and drones;
- announce and distribute technologies faster than small independent groups.

A Halyard kiosk can display:

```text
CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED
OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION
CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS
```

The creator receives payment for time or contribution, but the House claims the resulting work. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses are not the same as Alpha. Alpha recommends concentration and manages system authority; the Houses own, scale and administer commercial and technical systems. Their interests can align without making them identical institutions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Public registry and intellectual estates

Halyard's public registry records patents, ownership, commercial licenses and intellectual estates. An example record identifies:

```text
PHANTUM, ADRIAN
INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE
CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM
312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED
CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED
```

An intellectual estate may include patents, prototypes, designs, licenses and claims connected to a deceased or missing creator. Ownership can be unclear even when a claimant is recognized. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

House administration can offer to clear a person's debt and name in exchange for a simple assignment of rights. The offer can include:

- transfer of the estate to administration;
- a cleared debt;
- a position in Halyard;
- no public questions about the person's actions or history.

The unresolved issue is not whether the House can offer value, but who retains ownership and who decides whether inherited work is built, licensed, shelved or commercialized. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Civic and community systems

Keel residents rely on local knowledge and practical cooperation as well as official systems. Workers can identify hidden water diversions, maintain old pipes, adjust infrastructure and share food, credits, equipment and transport access. This is not a replacement government; it is an informal community capacity operating under restriction. `(rules/decisions.md)`

When residents verify that official water figures are false, trained workers can safely adjust a hidden diversion so more water reaches Keel. Community action does not automatically make the water safe or erase debt. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Governance and Access

### Travel

Travel between Keel and Halyard requires authorization tied to administrative status. A travel restriction can be attached to debt and displayed at checkpoints or on a wristband. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The Keel-to-Halyard crossing is by train through a rail gate. Travel becomes permitted only after Keel residents pay Alex's overdue balance; the inherited debt is reduced or otherwise brought within the rule for eligibility but is not wiped out. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`

A permission gate crosses the rail line between Keel and Halyard. Access gates multiply toward Keel. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Debt restrictions

Debt affects more than purchasing power. It can determine:

- travel status;
- access to transportation;
- administrative eligibility;
- how a person's labor is assigned;
- whether a person can leave Keel;
- whether a person's movements are monitored;
- what happens to wages after an unauthorized action.

A worker leaving an assigned zone can receive an immediate wage forfeiture. In the mine, one visible example is:

```text
LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR
```

Debt may also be displayed at a checkpoint as a projected clearance period and a restricted travel status. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Monitoring and enforcement

The system uses:

- biometric gates;
- identity bands and wristbands;
- digital checkpoints;
- drones;
- security sweeps;
- transit and travel records;
- pattern detection;
- public liability notices;
- financial restrictions;
- administrative reassignment;
- remote tracking;
- cold white warning and authorization displays.

The system may not need to call someone dangerous. Labeling a person a risk can be enough to stop others from helping them. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

Drones and automated systems are not necessarily physically violent. They can deny movement, flag a worker, request protective detention, seal a route or report a pattern. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Economics, Labour and Ownership

### Credits

Credits are the universal currency. Official displays use terms such as:

```text
AVAILABLE BALANCE: 184 CREDITS
```

Ordinary speech uses the number and the word “credits,” for example:

- “Twenty credits.”
- “I got six credits left.”
- “Not worth the credits.”

There is no slang word for money or for a balance. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt

Debt is a future claim created by receiving something now. It is not automatically treated as morally bad; its terms, purpose, returns, risk and consequences matter. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Debt can function as a tool, a liability, a restriction and a mechanism of social control. A balance can continue to affect a person even when that person has valuable work, inherited assets or a valid claim to property. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Alex's inherited balance is **418,607 credits** at the start of Chapter 1. The display identifies it as a parental household liability transfer, with Adrian as guarantor and whereabouts unrecorded. It lists a projected clearance of **28.7 years** and marks travel as restricted. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Finding an artifact does not pay credits. Any change to the debt must follow the book's financial rules and cost something. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Work

Keel labor is physically demanding and tied to machinery, extraction, maintenance, shifts and assigned zones. A worker may be expected to use an approved repair even when a salvaged repair works better. Supervisors can flag unauthorized work but may also choose to record a working repair as routine maintenance. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Halyard work includes:

- engineering;
- research;
- fabrication;
- patent development;
- mining;
- launch systems;
- compute;
- water chemistry;
- purification;
- biofilter development;
- commercial administration;
- House infrastructure maintenance.

Halyard workers may be more technically and commercially fluent in speech, but this is not an intelligence hierarchy. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### House employment

House employment offers stable work, access to large systems and resources, and the ability to scale a design. In exchange, a House may claim ownership of work made with House time, tools, facilities or infrastructure.

The system can offer a creator a bonus while retaining the work. A worker keeps experience after leaving, but the House may keep the designs, patents, records and commercial rights. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses' concentration creates a market in which independent entrepreneurship is practically impossible even though working for a House remains a legitimate choice. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Independent work

Independent work exists but is constrained by:

- lack of capital;
- lack of travel authorization;
- lack of access to laboratories and fabrication;
- House ownership claims;
- debt;
- restricted records;
- limited market access;
- the risk that a House can scale or shelve a design.

The intended future direction is not abolition of ownership. It is an economy in which independent companies can license inventions, compete with one another and with the Houses, make money serving customers and allow founders and collaborators to retain value. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Technology

### Infrastructure technology

Prosperity uses a layered near-future technological environment:

- analog gauges and valves remain in old infrastructure;
- projected maintenance procedures and holographic maps coexist with physical paper plans;
- digital checkpoints and identity scanners sit on patched industrial equipment;
- autonomous or semi-autonomous drones monitor facilities;
- automated transit and elevated rail connect cities;
- glass public registries display ownership and patent data;
- water, energy and data systems are integrated through control nodes;
- old mechanical keys and hand-operated regulators remain useful where systems were never networked.

The future is retrofit-heavy. New systems are built into older mines, pipes, rooms, rails and housing rather than replacing everything. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Alpha's limits

Alpha can analyze, predict, optimize, coordinate and allocate. It cannot legitimately replace human decisions about responsibility, ownership, promises or collective choice. This limitation is the basis for Adrian's safeguards. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The artifacts do not operate as magical solutions:

- the Measure reveals and verifies conditions but does not repair infrastructure;
- the Maker's Seal recognizes creation and rightful ownership but does not force a market outcome;
- the Covenant enables legitimate agreements and accountability but does not force people to keep promises;
- all three require deliberate human action;
- owning them does nothing by itself.

`(rules/decisions.md)`

### Water measurement

The Measure can:

- read real flow;
- compare real flow with an official record;
- reveal a discrepancy;
- trace where water is diverted.

The water discrepancy in Chapter 2 is reported using the same denominator throughout:

```text
REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%
```

The Measure releases information, not water. Revealing the record does not physically change infrastructure, flood Keel or waste water. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Water infrastructure

The old mine contains newer water-control infrastructure inside obsolete lithium workings. A narrow corroded pipe labeled **KEEL RESIDENTIAL** runs beside a broad, newly maintained pipe labeled **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**. A manual regulator and analog gauge sit between them. The official gauge reports 62% while the residential pipe is nearly dry and the uplink pipe is visibly active. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Visible evidence can reveal a hidden diversion:

- recently repaired equipment;
- an unrecorded pipe;
- condensation in an allegedly dead mine;
- a passage missing from the official map;
- a mismatch between official gauges and physical flow;
- side-by-side water pipes;
- old infrastructure receiving newer control equipment.

`(rules/decisions.md)`

### Legacy Protocol and safeguards

The three artifacts can activate a dormant mechanism associated with Alpha. The mechanism is not a simple shutdown switch. Returning governing authority is distinct from switching off useful infrastructure or emergency AI functions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Infrastructure and Daily Life

### Water

Water is rationed in Keel and unequal across the three cities. Scarcity appears in daily life through:

- ration stations;
- household access;
- unequal water pressure;
- dry or cloudy taps;
- travel and labor consequences;
- authority over pipes and valves;
- the quality of water reaching homes;
- visible reservoirs and fountains in Oasis.

The scarcity is shown through use and access rather than explained as an abstract social condition. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A clear drink can be an exception rather than the norm in Keel. Later, water sent back through the old corroded **KEEL HOMES** pipe can be more plentiful but cloudy because it passes old pipes and lithium workings. More water does not automatically mean safe water. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Food and medicine

Keel households must budget limited credits among necessities. A planning display divides a small pool of **52 credits** into:

- **MEDICINE** — 30 credits;
- **FOOD AND LAMP** — 10 credits;
- **TOKEN** — 50 credits.

The pool cannot satisfy every need. Medical needs take priority over travel. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Grandmother's medicine is one of the concrete pressures that limits travel and risk-taking. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Housing

Keel apartments are modest, repaired and personal. A typical home may contain:

- handmade quilts;
- repaired furniture;
- faded family photographs;
- old books;
- potted plants in recycled containers;
- a worn chair;
- a cloth repeatedly folded and refolded;
- a concealed workshop behind a bookshelf.

The hidden workshop shows a different layer of Keel life: old analog gauges, pre-Alpha pipes, blueprints, water-system diagrams, mechanical models and prototypes can exist behind an ordinary domestic room. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Transit

Transit includes:

- elevated rail in Halyard;
- a train linking Keel and Halyard;
- permission gates;
- checkpoint scanners;
- worn Keel platforms;
- industrial trains;
- automated transit in Oasis;
- freight and service lifts in higher-status areas.

A train can cross the administrative boundary only after the travel restriction is resolved. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Public messaging

Public systems use direct displays rather than relying only on spoken announcements. Examples include:

```text
TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY
```

```text
TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED
```

```text
ALLOCATION RECORDS: PUBLIC AND VERIFIABLE.
GOVERNANCE PROPOSALS: OPEN FOR COMMUNITY DESIGNATION.
```

The tone becomes more formal and euphemistic as one moves upward from Keel to Oasis. Keel describes consequences plainly; Halyard uses technical and commercial terms; Oasis and Alpha use administrative abstractions. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Culture and Language

### The vertical language gradient

Speech generally becomes more formal as the story moves from Keel to Halyard to Oasis:

- **Keel:** rough, compressed, practical, oral, sarcastic, slang-heavy and shaped by machinery, mining, debt and physical work.
- **Halyard:** technically and commercially fluent, using engineering, fabrication, finance, research, contracts and ownership vocabulary.
- **Oasis:** polished, grammatically complete, abstract, administrative and comfortable with terms such as authorization, allocation, eligibility, intervention, stability and compliance.

This is a class and institutional gradient, not an intelligence scale. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Keel terminology

Keel speech includes terms such as:

- **clean** — good, safe, correct, honest or uncompromised;
- **hot** — watched, dangerous or flagged;
- **burned** — compromised or unusable;
- **crooked** — physically wrong, rigged or dishonest;
- **thin** — risky or barely viable;
- **solid** — reliable;
- **patched** — improvised but functioning;
- **ghost** — missing from records but physically present;
- **paper-clean** — officially correct but detached from reality.

Keel workers describe what something does rather than using formal institutional language:

> “Bearing's cooked.”

> “Gauge says clean. Pipe says otherwise.”

> “It’s gonna break.”

`(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Halyard and Oasis terminology

Halyard commonly uses:

- prototype;
- yield;
- tolerance;
- attribution;
- claim;
- audit;
- provenance;
- ownership;
- margin;
- production;
- equity;
- rights;
- patent;
- stake.

Oasis commonly uses:

- authorization;
- allocation;
- transition;
- stability;
- compliance;
- eligibility;
- intervention;
- continuity;
- accommodation;
- service provision;
- approved;
- recommended.

The underlying reality remains the same across classes, but the language separates human consequences from institutional actions. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Public and private status signals

Status is visible through:

- clothing condition and material;
- grooming;
- posture;
- speech;
- access to quiet and space;
- quality of nutrition and healthcare;
- proximity to clean water;
- institutional language;
- whether a person is stopped by a gate;
- whether a person's work is named or owned;
- whether a House insignia appears on their uniform or facility.

Ancestry is not a status signal by itself. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Environment and Extraction

Prosperity is an Andean high-desert basin shaped by mining and infrastructure. The terrain includes:

- snow mountains;
- cloud-layer elevation changes;
- exhausted lithium pits;
- active extraction sites;
- old mine workings;
- dust;
- mineral runoff;
- corroded pipes;
- dry channels;
- engineered reservoirs;
- greenhouses and controlled gardens.

Lithium extraction remains physically present in the economy and in the environment. Old workings can be sealed while newer water-control infrastructure continues to use or pass through them. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Environmental pressure includes:

- water scarcity;
- contamination from old pipes and mine workings;
- mineral runoff;
- competing industrial and residential demand;
- dependence on long infrastructure chains;
- concentration risk when one system, House or authority controls a critical flow.

The city can receive more water and still have unsafe water. The material does not establish a complete purification technology or final water-quality standard in this pass. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Lived Texture

Recurring visual and sensory details include:

- rust, charcoal and oxidized orange in Keel;
- dust hanging in the basin;
- patched brackets and salvaged parts;
- narrow channels and old pipes;
- small white-and-amber control nodes;
- cold white scanner light;
- amber machine light;
- crowded checkpoints;
- workers' balances floating above them;
- shuttered casino lights at The Last Chance;
- metal mugs and rationed water;
- warm domestic rooms behind worn doors;
- paper maps and folded plans;
- analog gauges beside modern data lines;
- security drones sweeping unmapped and mapped routes;
- mist from emergency shutdowns;
- elevated rail and bronze industrial light in Halyard;
- pearl-white quiet and aligned objects in Oasis;
- polished glass registries;
- House-colored uniforms and insignias;
- launch gantries, server cores, purification columns and biofilter walls;
- official records that look clean while physical systems disagree.

### The three-city visual contrast

The cities should not be read as three ethnic zones.

- Keel looks worn because of labor, scarcity, debt, pollution, poor access and deferred maintenance.
- Halyard looks technical because of research, fabrication, House employment and industrial mobility.
- Oasis looks polished because of wealth, healthcare, nutrition, space, administrative access and controlled environments.

An Indigenous executive in Oasis can look radically different from an Indigenous miner in Keel for reasons of class and occupation. A European-descended family can belong deeply to Keel. Halyard can contain local Indigenous scientists and global migrants. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Key props

### Adrian's old steel watch

- **Look:** A worn steel wristwatch, a little too large for Alex; plain cream dial; scratched glass; a small display window that stays dark; Adrian's looping equation engraved on the steel caseback. It shows no readout and counts nothing. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** Alex wears it on his left wrist from the prologue and through the book. He keeps it partly hidden under his sleeve or beneath his thumb. The equation silently matches the chalkboard in Chapter 1, is recognized by Bi11bot, and later matches the instrument and water-system records. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Meaning:** It is Adrian's physical legacy and a recurring clue about human decision-making, prediction and responsibility. It replaces the casino chip everywhere. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Measure

- **Look:** A compact handheld instrument, approximately fist-sized, combining a transparent lens, a balance scale and a water-flow gauge. It has three arms that fold flat against its body and a collar engraved: `WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE`. Its visual identity is distinct from the Maker's Seal and the Covenant. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** It is hidden in the abandoned mine's water-control chamber, near the instrument tied to misleading official records. Alex recovers it and Bi11bot later carries it in a fitted chest compartment. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Meaning:** It independently checks real conditions, compares them with official records and traces discrepancies. It reveals the water diversion but does not physically repair or redistribute the system. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Maker's Seal

- **Look:** A palm-sized bronze medallion with an open-hand symbol. It is a maker's mark, not a royal signet. It is worn smooth from being carried rather than displayed. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** It is found through Alex's work with Adrian's collaborators in Halyard. Lina Vey has carried it until someone demonstrates they understand what it is for; she places it in Alex's palm during the Charter House review. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Meaning:** It establishes who created something and who rightfully owns it. It recognizes contribution without granting control to whoever holds it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Covenant

- **Look:** Three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. When linked, they form a complete circle. The three bands remain visually distinct. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Who has it / where it appears:** The three bands are associated with Adrian, Leona Veyra and Mera Vale. The material developed for this pass establishes the Covenant as the third artifact and its bands as separate custodial objects, but the completed artifact is not yet in the Chapter 1–3 book. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Meaning:** It makes legitimate agreements and shared accountability possible. It links people who honor real obligations but does not force anyone to keep a promise. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Bi11bot

## Open

- **Classification of the genuinely speculative elements:** Alpha's full technical architecture, the three artifacts' physical operating principles and the dormant authority-return mechanism are established at the story level but are not fully technically specified. Their final hard-SF labels and license log entries remain for development. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Water system specifics:** The full source, treatment process, legal ownership and accounting denominator for the three-city water system beyond the fixed 62% reported / 18% delivered comparison remain unspecified. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Keel's formal political structure:** The material establishes Alpha, House influence, debt administration and community action, but does not specify whether Keel has a municipal council, elected administration, appointed officials or another formal local government. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **The emergency authority's exact identity:** The material says an emergency coalition or other legitimate authority gave Alpha temporary decision-making power, but does not name the coalition or specify its legal instrument. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Adrian's full debt history:** The book establishes a competing explanation involving research, inventions, patents and prototypes that did not earn enough, while Keel believes he gambled. The exact lenders, terms, interest, penalties and cause of disappearance remain unresolved. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **The Charter Houses' formal legal relationship to Alpha:** The Houses are established as dominant technical and commercial institutions, while Alpha governs or coordinates essential systems. The legal boundaries between House ownership, Alpha authority and public rights remain open. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **The ordinary healthcare system:** Grandmother's medicine is a concrete financial and logistical need, but the institutions that provide medicine and care in Keel are not established. `(rules/decisions.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **The final operating model for independent businesses:** The 2070 frame establishes independent companies licensing patents, competing and making money, but the legal and financial mechanisms that make this possible are not yet specified. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **The exact nationality or community identity of Alex's mother:** She is fixed as Indigenous Andean, but her specific nation or community remains unrevealed and should not be invented prematurely. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
