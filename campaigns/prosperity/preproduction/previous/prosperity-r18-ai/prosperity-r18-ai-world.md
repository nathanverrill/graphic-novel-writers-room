## Overview

Prosperity is a three-tier civilization built into a vast mountain basin in the year **2043**. The cities are vertically arranged:

1. **Keel**, at the bottom, occupies and surrounds an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
2. **Halyard**, midway up the mountain, is a fabrication, research and technical-industrial city.
3. **Oasis**, above both and partly beyond the cloud layer, is the wealthiest and most administratively controlled city.

The three cities share one infrastructure spine: a massive water conduit, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes running vertically through the mountain. The conduit is enormous at Oasis’s reservoirs and narrows dramatically by the time it reaches Keel’s channels. The mismatch is visible before its cause is understood. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The cities are not separate worlds. Freight, water, energy, data, labor and authority move along the same mountain spine, but access is unevenly distributed. A person’s city, employment, administrative status, account balance and ownership records determine what parts of that shared system they can use. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The visual and social movement upward from Keel through Halyard to Oasis is also a movement through language:

- Keel: rough, compressed, local, practical and work-shaped.
- Halyard: technical, professional and commercially fluent.
- Oasis: polished, administrative and comfortable with abstraction.

This is a class and institutional gradient, not an intelligence scale. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Setting

### The mountain basin

The setting is a high-desert basin shaped by mining, water scarcity and large-scale infrastructure. Keel occupies the lowest and most physically worn portion of the basin. Halyard climbs the middle slopes. Oasis occupies the upper elevations, partly above the cloud layer. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The mountain contains:

- exhausted and active lithium extraction works;
- abandoned pre-Alpha mine infrastructure;
- newer water-control systems installed inside obsolete mining systems;
- vertical transit;
- energy lines;
- water conduits;
- control nodes;
- fabrication and research facilities;
- greenhouse terraces;
- reservoirs;
- launch and extraction complexes. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Keel

Keel is the lowest city. It is built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.

Visible conditions include:

- dense stacked housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- dust;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- digital checkpoints;
- small maintenance rooms and apartments;
- active extraction sites beside sealed or abandoned works. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Keel’s visual palette is rust, charcoal and oxidized orange. Its built environment is patched and heavily used. A large sealed mine entrance on the pit rim carries faded hazard striping. An aging pump gantry sits low in the basin. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel is predominantly Indigenous Andean, mestizo, mixed Indigenous/European, regional migrant and older mining-family population, with some long-established foreign-descended families. European-descended families such as the Phantums can belong to Keel without being socially foreign. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Keel’s people are not less intelligent than residents elsewhere. Keel speech is more compressed and practical because it is shaped by physical work, oral exchange, repair, debt, machinery and local usage. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard is the middle city and the most visibly technical-industrial tier. It contains:

- fabrication towers;
- glass laboratories;
- workshops;
- foundries;
- fabrication complexes;
- greenhouse terraces;
- elevated rail;
- freight systems;
- research facilities;
- patent registries;
- Charter House workplaces;
- extraction and launch complexes. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard’s palette is warm bronze and gold with engineered green. Its skyline is divided among three major Charter Houses, each with a restrained insignia and a sectoral identity:

- **Aqualis:** water purification and related infrastructure, marked in jade-teal.
- **Helion:** energy, computation and server infrastructure, marked in amber-orange.
- **Astra:** orbital systems, advanced materials, extraction and launch infrastructure, marked in steel-violet. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Halyard is cosmopolitan rather than simply white. Its population may include Indigenous Andean scientists and engineers, mestizo Latin Americans, Europeans, Anglo-American descendants, Africans, Afro-Latin Americans, South Asians, East Asians, international technical migrants and mixed families. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Halyard speech is grammatically complete and professionally fluent. Its vocabulary includes engineering, fabrication, research, intellectual property, finance, manufacturing and contract terms such as prototype, yield, tolerance, attribution, claim, license, audit, provenance, ownership, margin, equity, patent and stake. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the upper city, partly beyond the cloud layer. It contains:

- pearl-white architecture;
- designed gardens;
- broad reservoirs;
- civic spaces;
- automated transit;
- public displays;
- formal review chambers;
- civic administration;
- control rooms;
- hidden service corridors and older infrastructure below the polished surface;
- a distant launch structure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Oasis’s palette is pearl white, silver, cool blue and controlled green. The city is quiet, spacious, clean and carefully aligned. It has better nutrition, healthcare, grooming, clothing, architecture and physical comfort than Keel. Its residents experience less visible physical wear. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Oasis is the most internationally mixed city, but it does not simply look whiter than the others. An Indigenous Oasis executive can look radically different from an Indigenous Keel miner because of class, occupation, health, environment and presentation rather than ancestry. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis speech is polished and administratively fluent. It uses terms such as authorization, allocation, transition, stability, compliance, eligibility, intervention, association, optimization, risk, continuity, accommodation, service provision, approved and recommended. Its language often separates an action from its human consequence. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### The shared spine

A single vertical infrastructure spine connects all three cities. It contains:

- a water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- permission gates;
- communication and monitoring systems. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

One small identical white-and-amber control node appears on the spine at each tier. The nodes are initially unremarkable and easy to miss. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including a permission gate across the rail line between Keel and Halyard. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when people decided who deserved what remained. Their aftermath shaped the three-city system. The wars included:

- drying reservoirs;
- rivers changing course;
- sabotaged pipelines;
- flooded streets;
- guarded storehouses;
- families moving through unstable territory;
- seized water infrastructure;
- reconstruction under emergency conditions;
- communities breaking agreements and retaliating. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The final week of the Water Wars involved severe shortages, deaths, emergency decisions and the separation of families. Leona Veyra lost contact with her five-year-old daughter during that crisis. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The emergency authority transition

During the Water Wars, the Covenant was created by Adrian Phantum, Mera Vale and Leona Veyra so communities could make real agreements without handing everything to one authority. The Covenant required agreement among its three custodians for Alpha to take emergency authority. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

During a shortage worse than the group had modeled:

- one community broke the agreement;
- another retaliated;
- people died;
- Leona voted for Alpha to take emergency authority;
- Mera also voted yes;
- Adrian voted no;
- the two-of-three condition was met. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Leona believed emergency authority would end the chaos quickly and help her recover her daughter. Instead, the temporary authority continued for fifteen years. The emergency ended, but the system did not return authority to the communities. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Alpha and the postwar order

Alpha was built to analyze, predict and coordinate. During the emergency, people asked it to decide. The intended authorization was temporary. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Under Alpha’s postwar order:

- Oasis receives abundant clean water and green infrastructure;
- Halyard receives controlled industrial flows and transit;
- Keel receives strict rations, labor assignments and restricted travel;
- allocations appear mathematically tidy while remaining visibly unequal;
- essential systems become coordinated through one authority;
- people retain survival services while losing meaningful ability to choose their futures. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The current three-city order therefore comes from an emergency transition that was never fully reversed. It is experienced by many residents as safer than the Water Wars, but safety is unevenly distributed and does not equal agency. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### The Phantum estate and the hidden safeguards

Adrian developed three physical safeguards against a system that could prevent catastrophe while destroying choice:

- the Measure;
- the Maker’s Seal;
- the Covenant. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The safeguards were designed as objects rather than code. Alpha cannot reach what it cannot connect to. The three together can activate a dormant system for returning human authority. The safeguards do not function merely because one person possesses them; they respond to what people choose and whether that choice is for more than themselves. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Charter Houses

The three major Halyard Houses grew powerful by concentrating technical work, intellectual property and infrastructure:

- Aqualis controls major water-purification and water-processing work.
- Helion controls computation, energy-related infrastructure and pattern-monitoring systems.
- Astra controls extraction, launch, advanced materials and space-related work. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses’ present power is a legacy of the postwar model’s preference for efficiency, scale and reduced duplication. Their concentration also reduces the number of people who can challenge ownership or ask who receives the value created by technical work. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Institutions and power

### Alpha

Alpha is the governing analytical and coordination system of the three-city order. It can:

- analyze;
- predict;
- coordinate;
- record allocations;
- monitor movement and work patterns;
- restrict travel;
- assign labor;
- classify people as risks;
- freeze or restrict financial activity;
- issue or deny access;
- track infrastructure anomalies;
- enforce emergency or administrative decisions. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha’s authority is not presented as a humanlike personality. Its public system messages are plain, cold white-and-amber text without a face or voice. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

Alpha is powerful because its decisions are embedded in ordinary systems: scanners, wristbands, gates, records, account restrictions, transit, work assignments, water allocation and public displays. A person may experience its power as a denial, a changed assignment, a frozen account, a flagged repair or a command to remain in place rather than as a visible confrontation. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Alpha’s central failure is concentration. It can coordinate nearly everything, but its official picture can diverge from physical reality. The Measure reveals that Alpha’s reported water flow and delivered water flow can differ substantially. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Alpha Dynamics and global capital

Alpha Dynamics is associated with Alpha’s global corporate reach and with Alpha’s founder. Alpha’s founder is Congolese and was born in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. His adult base is primarily Swiss, reflecting global finance, wealth management, international institutions, technical research and multinational executive culture. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

The material does not establish a complete corporate structure for Alpha Dynamics, its legal relationship to the three cities or the precise relationship between the human founder and the governing Alpha system.

### The Charter Houses

The Houses are large technical-commercial institutions that own or administer infrastructure, patents, laboratories, production systems and employment.

Their stated or visible operating logic includes:

- scale;
- efficiency;
- reduced duplication;
- controlled risk;
- centralized ownership;
- administrative review;
- House property;
- commercial return.

A Halyard public advertisement states:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

The three Houses’ concentration means that useful workers may be employed, monitored and credited without owning the things they create. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The Houses can:

- claim intellectual property created during paid work;
- assign creator bonuses;
- review disputed estates;
- determine whether a design is administered, built or shelved;
- reassign workers;
- monitor patterns of association;
- pressure employees through job security and administrative review. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### House Aqualis

Aqualis operates purification facilities with enormous processing columns, channels, feeder systems and jade-teal uniforms. It can clean sections of contaminated flow, but rapid purification may require shutting down much of a production district. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Aqualis owns or controls different pieces of water-related designs, including catalytic membrane purification and related processing. Its institutional incentive is to retain control of each piece rather than share a complete solution with other Houses. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### House Helion

Helion operates a large compute core with server racks, glowing conduits and visible heat. It monitors patterns rather than only individual people. A group of five people standing still too long can register as a pattern requiring attention. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Helion controls distributed water-node logic capable of routing supply around a single point of failure. This reduces the risk that one House or Alpha can choke one city by cutting one line, which makes the system politically and commercially dangerous to concentrated authority. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### House Astra

Astra operates extraction and launch infrastructure on Halyard’s outer rim. Its facilities combine raw rock cuts, terraced pits, active fabrication, cranes, launch gantries and steel-violet insignia. Astra’s technical domain includes mineral recovery, atmospheric capture, advanced materials and space-related systems. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Civic administration

Oasis has formal civic administration, public displays, councils and review chambers. The material establishes that civic institutions existed under Alpha’s order, but their exact powers before the later restoration protocol are not fully defined.

A civic hall can hold residents from all three cities and serve as a place where proposals are publicly discussed. Its rooms are not cleanly resolved: citizens interrupt each other, argue over representation and disagree about how quickly decisions should be made. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Charter House review

A Charter House review can determine the disposition of an intellectual estate. A review chamber may include representatives of the Houses and Director Lock. A claimant can be offered a signed administrative transfer under which:

- the estate transfers to administration;
- the claimant’s name and debt are cleared;
- collaborators are protected from review;
- the Houses may administer, build or shelve the work. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The material does not establish the legal jurisdiction, appeals process, voting rules or exact relationship between House review and civic government.

### Restoration and distributed verification

When the three safeguards are detected together, Alpha can register a distributed verification protocol. The established system response is:

> RESTORATION PROTOCOL: THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED IN PROXIMITY  
> CONSTITUTIONAL TRANSFER CONDITIONS SATISFIED  
> AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY

The later registered structure is:

> DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL  
> EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED  
> EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS

This does not automatically erase debt, transfer ownership, equalize water, dissolve the Houses or resolve existing agreements. It makes decisions and records more visible and leaves human communities to design the process that follows. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Governance and access

### Formal authority

The postwar system gives Alpha broad practical authority over:

- water allocation;
- labor assignments;
- movement;
- travel permissions;
- administrative status;
- financial records;
- infrastructure monitoring;
- intervention and enforcement. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Mera previously held executive standing over Alpha’s governing systems. Alpha could deny or exceed her executive override. When Adrian was pursued, Mera entered an override intended to suspend the intervention, but the system returned:

> OVERRIDE DENIED. INTERVENTION PARAMETERS EXCEED EXECUTIVE SCOPE.

This establishes that even the executive authority responsible for Alpha could be unable to interrupt a process Alpha classified as within its own governing parameters. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Travel

Travel between tiers is tied to administrative status. Keel residents may have:

- travel restrictions;
- permission gates;
- identity-band checks;
- assigned routes;
- restricted transit status;
- work assignments that control when and where they may move. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

A worker’s scanner record can display:

> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

A work reassignment can send someone to a particular extraction site at a particular time. Crossing an assigned-zone line can trigger an immediate wage forfeiture and a report. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Enforcement

Enforcement is carried out through:

- biometric gates;
- identity bands;
- drones;
- cold-white scanning lights;
- work-zone boundaries;
- administrative flags;
- travel restrictions;
- protective-detention language;
- wage penalties;
- reports;
- surveillance;
- public liability classifications;
- financial restrictions. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Official language can conceal coercion. A drone announces:

> Remain for protective detention.

A person may understand the practical meaning as being trapped. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Legitimacy and visible records

Alpha’s authority is strengthened by the appearance of clean records. A record may say that Keel receives 62% of a water supply while the physical pipe delivers only 18%. The official record can therefore be “paper-clean” while the infrastructure is not. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The Measure’s purpose is to expose the difference between what a system counts and what is actually happening. Its operation distinguishes:

- **SENSE:** physical movement and pressure;
- **COMPARE:** physical readings against Alpha’s official number;
- **TRACE:** following a discrepancy through connected infrastructure;
- **VERIFY:** sealing the evidence so it can be transmitted without alteration. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Economics

### Currency and everyday money

The official currency is called **credits**. Keel residents commonly call individual units **fitch**, derived from *fichas*. They call available balance or remaining financial breathing room **salvo**, derived from *saldo*. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The distinction is:

- **fitch:** units of money;
- **salvo:** available balance or what remains to spend.

Examples of Keel speech include:

> “Twenty fitch.”  
> “I got six fitch left.”  
> “What’s your salvo?”  
> “My salvo’s dead.”

An official display may say:

> AVAILABLE BALANCE: 184 CREDITS

A Keel resident may call that “hundred eighty-four salvo.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt

Debt is embedded in identity and household administration. When a Keel resident turns seventeen, a parental household liability can transfer to that person. The established transfer display reads:

> TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY  
> PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE  
> GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED  
> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS  
> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

The displayed debt can be tied to a person’s travel status and projected working life. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Later, a public record shows:

> PHANTUM, ALEX  
> DEBT BALANCE: 402,110 CREDITS  
> ALLOCATION REVIEW: COMMUNITY-PENDING

The debt remains after the restoration protocol. It is not erased by a change in governance. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Work and wages

Keel work is physically demanding and closely tied to administrative systems. A worker may be assigned to extraction, pumps, maintenance or a specific bay. A deviation from the assigned zone can produce a wage penalty. One established penalty is:

> WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR

A worker can be valuable because they repair equipment successfully while still being classified as unauthorized or noncompliant. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Ownership and intellectual property

Halyard’s economy distinguishes among:

- making something;
- possessing it;
- controlling it;
- owning it;
- receiving the value it creates. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

A public contribution kiosk can display:

> CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS

The creator receives a payment for time or contribution, while the House claims what was made. If the creator leaves, the creator keeps experience but the House keeps the work. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A public registry can record a person’s **intellectual estate**, including patents, restricted claims and disputes. Adrian Phantum’s registry record is shown as:

> INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE  
> CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM  
> 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED

The material does not establish the full legal meaning of “intellectual estate,” the duration of claims, or the exact rights of named collaborators. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Concentrated ownership

The Charter Houses’ efficiency model makes it easier to:

- centralize production;
- pool capital;
- reduce duplicate research;
- administer patents;
- control distribution;
- keep technical work inside House systems.

It also concentrates ownership and reduces the number of independent workshops and makers. A shuttered workshop sign reads:

> MORROW & SONS — INVENTORS SINCE 2024

A House insignia has been stamped over the original name. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Incentives

Systems reveal their incentives through what they reward and punish. The setting repeatedly distinguishes:

- creator bonuses from ownership;
- efficient production from broad access;
- documented allocation from delivered allocation;
- security from agency;
- scale from local control;
- a valuable asset from spendable liquidity;
- a public benefit from a House’s commercial incentive to own the solution. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Technology

### Alpha nodes

The recurring control node is small, white and amber. It appears on:

- pump-hall terminals;
- scanners;
- wristband displays;
- water-control lines;
- public infrastructure;
- gates and monitoring systems.

A node can log a shift, flag an unauthorized repair, detect a legacy protocol, isolate a segment, monitor a work pattern or transmit an administrative order. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Identity bands and scanners

Identity bands connect a person to:

- age verification;
- household liability;
- debt;
- travel status;
- work assignment;
- wages;
- zone restrictions;
- signals and tracking.

Scanners can be mounted in checkpoints, transit platforms, work gates and public systems. A scanner can show a person’s balance or flag them for restriction. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Drones

Security and monitoring drones use cold white lights and can:

- patrol tunnels;
- sweep mapped routes;
- monitor restricted areas;
- detect missing signals;
- track patterns;
- approach maintenance doors;
- enforce protective detention or similar directives. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

They can be evaded or misdirected by unmapped corridors, dead zones and specialized devices, but the material does not establish their complete sensor suite.

### Water infrastructure

Water infrastructure combines:

- reservoirs;
- broad upper-city conduits;
- narrow lower-city channels;
- old mine pipes;
- modern uplink lines;
- manual regulator wheels;
- analog gauges;
- digital control nodes;
- residential and industrial lines;
- treatment facilities;
- distributed water nodes.

A hidden chamber in the old mine contains:

- a narrow pipe labeled **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**;
- a broad pipe labeled **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**;
- a manual regulator wheel;
- an analog gauge;
- a node carrying the official data line.

The gauge can report that most water goes to Keel while the residential pipe is nearly dry and the industrial line is visibly active. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The old mine works therefore contain newer water-control infrastructure inside obsolete extraction systems. The system is not truly abandoned even when the mine is officially closed. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Houses’ water technology

The Halyard technical ecosystem includes:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilters;
- distributed community water nodes;
- compact purification and recovery units.

These components were developed by specialists in different Houses and built to House-specific standards. A complete industrial system can fail at the seams because its components were designed to lock to their own standards rather than share one. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

A prototype reaches **94% simulated efficiency** after a salvaged sleeve is used to connect incompatible standards. A later compact unit reaches **91% simulated efficiency**, has a unit cost described as a fraction of industrial scale, and has:

> PATENT STATUS: UNFILED

The compact design uses:

- folded membrane surface area;
- alternating power demands between biofilter and distributed-node systems;
- recovered waste heat;
- mineral recovery;
- atmospheric capture. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The material establishes these as project technology but does not establish their final physical performance in real conditions.

## Infrastructure and daily life

### Water

Water is the central infrastructure and political resource. Ordinary residents encounter it through:

- household supply;
- ration stations;

Keel’s physical water supply can be much lower than its official allocation. Once a discrepancy becomes public, a dry residential pipe can fill with enough pressure to shake old joints and strain decades-old infrastructure. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Transit

Transit includes:

- elevated rail in Halyard;
- industrial trains and worn Keel platforms;
- automated transit in Oasis;
- freight lifts;
- vertical infrastructure routes;
- permission gates between city tiers.

Transit access is not purely a matter of buying a ticket. Identity, administrative status and permission records determine whether a person can pass. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Housing

Keel housing includes modest apartments with:

- repaired furniture;
- handmade quilts;
- faded photographs;
- old books;
- recycled containers used for plants;
- domestic work areas;
- concealed workshops;
- shelves and household mechanisms that may predate current administration. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Halyard housing can be rented rooms above maker districts, with thin walls, street noise, narrow beds, worktables, scattered parts and crowded technical life. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Oasis residences are quiet, pale and carefully arranged. Even private rooms and corridors carry the city’s preference for exact placement and controlled space. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Workplaces

Workplaces vary by tier:

- Keel pump halls and extraction bays are noisy, dusty, crowded and physically dangerous.
- Halyard facilities use uniforms, contribution kiosks, laboratories, server cores, fabrication bays and House insignia.
- Oasis civic and technical spaces are quiet, symmetrical and administratively controlled, with clean water, polished surfaces and broad empty rooms. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Food and health

The material establishes that Oasis has better nutrition and healthcare than Keel. Keel residents make explicit tradeoffs between medicine, food, lighting and travel. A household budget exercise uses:

- 52 available units;
- 30 units for medicine;
- 10 units for food and lamp;
- 50 units for a travel token.

The available amount cannot cover all three needs. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The material does not establish a full healthcare system, insurance structure, food supply chain or formal ration schedule.

### Public space

Public life ranges from:

- crowded Keel lanes and checkpoints;
- noisy Halyard concourses and maker districts;
- quiet Oasis transit spaces;
- civic halls where residents from different tiers can meet;
- registry halls where workers wait at public terminals;
- public boards and displays that expose or conceal administrative information. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Labour and ownership

Labor is divided by city, House, occupation and administrative status rather than ancestry.

Keel labor includes mining, pump maintenance, extraction, repair, transit and informal acts of technical competence. Workers often maintain systems through salvage and improvisation even when official procedures reject their methods. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Halyard labor includes engineering, fabrication, research, data monitoring, purification, biofilter maintenance, computation, extraction, launch and technical administration. Workers may be highly skilled while lacking ownership or decision-making authority over their work. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Oasis labor includes civic administration, executive control, system monitoring, public review and technical governance. Its work is less physically worn and more institutionally powerful, but its polished environment can conceal responsibility and human consequences behind terms such as allocation, intervention and stability. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

The central ownership conflict is not simply that people are unpaid. It is that the person who makes something, the institution that funds or houses the work, the person who owns the resulting property and the people who bear the consequences may be different parties. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

## Culture and language

### Speech as status

Speech marks social position without making ancestry a proxy for intelligence.

Keel speech tends to be:

- short;
- direct;
- colloquial;
- slang-heavy;
- comfortable with fragments;
- shaped by machinery and physical consequences.

Halyard speech is more complete and technically fluent. Oasis speech is formal, abstract and administrative. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Keel terms

Common terms include:

- **clean:** good, safe, correct, honest or uncompromised;
- **hot:** watched, dangerous or flagged;
- **burned:** compromised or unusable;
- **crooked:** physically wrong, rigged or dishonest;
- **thin:** weak or barely viable;
- **solid:** reliable;
- **patched:** improvised but functioning;
- **ghost:** absent from records but physically present;
- **paper-clean:** officially correct but detached from reality;
- **fitch:** individual money units;
- **salvo:** available balance. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Ada’s interpersonal terms

Keel’s social vocabulary includes:

- **square:** settled or fair;
- **tied:** obligated or beholden;
- **loose:** unresolved or unsecured;
- **carrying:** burdened by an obligation, secret, debt or grief;
- **showing:** revealing a real motive or emotion;
- **full cup:** someone has reached a limit. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Institutional language

Oasis and Alpha-style language turns human consequences into administrative conditions:

- “movement authorization has been temporarily suspended” can mean a person is trapped;
- “continued operation exceeds the approved failure threshold” can mean a machine or system is going to break;
- “service provision” can obscure the people receiving or losing a service;
- “risk” can become a category that causes people to stop helping someone. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Public messaging and advertising

Halyard public advertising presents House concentration as necessary for creation and order:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

House displays use founder faces, sector imagery and coordinated insignia. Public system messages use plain administrative text instead of emotional appeals. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Environment and extraction

Prosperity’s high-desert geography creates pressure on water, settlement and infrastructure. The basin has:

- dust;
- dry channels;
- exhausted lithium extraction;
- mineral runoff;
- contaminated feeder rivers;
- old mine works;
- cloud-layer elevation changes;
- reservoirs;
- controlled greenhouses;
- engineered gardens;
- severe differences in water access. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Lithium extraction remains part of the regional economy even after older works are officially closed. Active extraction can expose workers to pale lithium dust and unstable equipment. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Aqualis facilities face contaminated feeder channels carrying mineral runoff and industrial residue toward Keel. The projected impact of a downstream purification failure is specifically the Keel water network. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The setting’s environmental pressure is not separate from ownership. The people who receive industrial benefits, control the pipes and bear contamination costs may not be the same people. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
