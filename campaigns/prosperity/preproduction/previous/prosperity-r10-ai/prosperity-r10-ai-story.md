# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Established project premise.** In 2043, seventeen-year-old Alex Phantum lives in Keel, the poorest of three vertically connected cities governed through Alpha, a federated AI coordination and governance system. On the day Adrian Phantum’s enormous household liability transfers to Alex, Alex also loses freedom of movement. The story he inherited about his missing father begins to break apart. `(input/story.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Alex discovers Adrian’s hidden workshop, awakens Bi11bot, and follows a route through three safeguards—the Measure, the Maker’s Seal, and the Covenant—designed to test whether people can reclaim authority from a system deciding their futures. The safeguards do not respond to possession alone. They respond to choices demonstrating shared truth, responsible ownership, and reciprocal trust. `(input/story.md; input/world.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The final problem is not how to destroy Alpha. It is what Alpha—and human power—should be permitted to do. `(input/story.md)`

### Intended dramatic questions

1. **Truth:** Can people act on reality when truth costs them something?
2. **Ownership:** Can people recognize who creates value rather than simply inherit or capture it?
3. **Trust:** Can people cooperate across conflicting interests without surrendering responsibility to a central authority?
4. **Authority:** Can Alex gain the power to fix the system and refuse the right to decide everyone else’s future? `(input/story.md)`

### Intended experience and emotional destination

The story moves from the physical and social pressures of Keel, through Halyard’s struggle over invention and ownership, into Oasis’s struggle over safety, belonging, and shared responsibility. The upward journey is also a movement from escape toward agency:

> **Escape → Curiosity → Truth → Responsibility → Ownership → Trust → Power → Restraint → Agency** `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

The story should not conclude that humans are always right or that AI is inherently wrong. It concludes that:

- prediction is not legitimacy;
- efficiency is not the only public value;
- information is not the same as truth;
- legal ownership can obscure contribution;
- services can exist without community;
- no AI, institution, heir, or hero should possess unchecked authority over everyone else’s future. `(input/story.md)`

### Medium-bearing story devices

The story repeatedly uses visual and material contrasts:

- record versus reality;
- approved repair versus working repair;
- gauge reading versus dry pipe;
- legal owner versus actual contributors;
- stability language versus lived cost;
- physical and administrative gates;
- water as resource, infrastructure, political power, and shared dependence;
- patched machines, inherited systems, damaged institutions, and relationships that must be repaired;
- offline books, keys, chips, gauges, maker marks, and disconnected artifacts as evidence outside Alpha’s immediate reach. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The vertical journey from **Keel → Halyard → Oasis** also changes the story’s language and social problem:

- Keel: truth and physical survival;
- Halyard: contribution and ownership;
- Oasis: trust, participation, and shared responsibility. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/world.md)`

## Premise

At seventeen, Alex becomes liable for **418,607 credits** of Adrian’s household debt. His projected clearance time is **28.7 years**, and his travel status becomes restricted. He wants to leave Keel, which he sees as the place his father abandoned him to.

On the same day, Alex finds a hidden workshop in his grandmother’s apartment. There he awakens Bi11bot, a machine built by Adrian, and learns that Adrian left behind a route through three safeguards intended to qualify the return of human authority:

1. **The Measure** makes truth visible.
2. **The Maker’s Seal** joins ownership to responsibility.
3. **The Covenant** creates reciprocal trust strong enough to move value between people.

Alex’s private desire to escape becomes a public journey. Each safeguard forces him to choose between a personal advantage and a condition that can help other people. By the time Alpha offers him permanent exclusive control, Alex has become capable of refusing the inheritance in the form Alpha expects.

#### Page 1 — The world Alex inherits

A full-page establishing image introduces the three-city system in **2043**. Keel lies at the bottom of the basin around an exhausted mine; Halyard occupies the intermediate industrial tier; Oasis sits above them with reservoirs, civic spaces, and automated infrastructure. A shared water conduit, energy lines, transit rails, and control nodes connect all three.

The conduit is visibly enormous near Oasis and dramatically narrower by Keel. The image does not explain the mismatch, but plants it visually. Identical white-and-amber control nodes appear on the shared spine at each tier. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including a permission gate on the rail between Keel and Halyard.

The page establishes that advanced technology and inequality coexist. Humanity is visually small within the system it built.

Captions:

> **Humanity reached the stars...**  
> **...but never escaped the gravity of inequality.** `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Page 6 — The hidden workshop

#### Page 7 — What he left

- a chalkboard covered in equations;

- an organized workbench;

The chalkboard contains an equation in the same looping form as Adrian’s casino chip. Alex stops turning the chip when he sees it.

The chapter does not yet explain the equation or the key. Their function is to establish that the chip was not merely a gambler’s trinket.

#### Page 8 — The awakening

Alex pulls the sheet from the child-sized machine. It is made from visibly repaired and tooled materials, with an open key socket in its chest. He tests its surface with his palm as he tests machinery.

He inserts the key.

The machine awakens: dust lifts, its eyes ignite, and its joints move.

Bi11bot’s first line is:

> **BI11BOT:** ...Well. That took longer than expected. `(input/story.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

This introduces Bi11bot through action and dry humor rather than exposition.

#### Page 12 — Track

The pulse travels from Keel through the shared spine, through the Keel–Halyard gate, Halyard, the cloud layer, and into Oasis. Identical control nodes flicker as it passes.

In a symmetrical Oasis control room, an unnamed woman sits before a wall-sized display. A glass of water is aligned precisely with the desk edge. Her face remains unseen.

> **B1 NODE ACTIVE**  
> **LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED**  
> **SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR**

### Chapter 2 — The Price of Departure

**Material status:** Detailed revised page-by-page draft, with later explicit decisions incorporated below. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Chapter function:** Turn Adrian’s mystery into action; explain Alpha’s origin; introduce the safeguards; recover the Measure; force Alex to choose between private escape and public truth. `(input/story.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Central question:** What is freedom worth if you leave everyone else trapped behind you? `(input/story.md)`

#### Page 1 — The next morning

Ada enters the workshop. She immediately recognizes that Adrian left Alex more than debt.

Bi11bot introduces himself and asks whether Ada should stay after hearing what follows. Alex looks at his private notebooks, then decides:

> **BI11BOT:** Once you hear what comes next, there’s no going back. Should she stay, Alex?  
> **ALEX:** She stays. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Bi11bot says Adrian made the same choice and begins with the Water Wars.

This is Alex’s first explicit move away from handling everything alone.

#### Page 2 — What Alpha is

Bi11bot shows fractured maps of reservoirs drying, rivers changing course, sabotaged pipelines, flooding, guarded stores, and displaced families.

> **BI11BOT:** The Water Wars began with scarcity. They became wars when people decided who deserved what was left. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The story treats the Water Wars as a retrospective name for recurring crises over roughly two decades, not one continuous interstate war. The crises involve drought, flooding, shrinking snowpack, aquifer depletion, demand from lithium, sodium, copper, cities, agriculture, and energy, as well as blockades, occupations, sabotage, lawsuits, emergency rationing, police intervention, migration, and occasional lethal confrontations. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Bi11bot explains Alpha’s original mandate:

> **ALPHA’S ORIGINAL MANDATE:** ANALYZE. PREDICT. COORDINATE.

During catastrophe, people asked Alpha to decide. The authority was meant to be temporary, but became persistent.

> **BI11BOT:** Alpha was built to analyze, predict and coordinate. Then, in the emergency, it was asked to decide.  
> **BI11BOT:** Temporarily. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Ada asks who gave Alpha that authority.

> **BI11BOT:** People who were afraid. Reasonably so. `(input/story.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The hologram shows the three cities receiving different levels of water, labor control, and travel permission. Ada observes that Alpha controls nearly everything people need to survive and choose their futures.

#### Page 3 — Three safeguards

Bi11bot introduces three physical safeguards, deliberately able to remain outside ordinary Alpha control:

- **The Measure:** truth made visible;
- **The Maker’s Seal:** ownership joined to responsibility;
- **The Covenant:** trust strong enough to move value between people.

The safeguards are not magical keys. Their records and hardware are offline, tamper-evident, and tied to physical measurement, maker marks, independent clocks, and threshold authentication. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Bi11bot explains:

> **BI11BOT:** Adrian asked what happens when a system prevents catastrophe but also destroys choice. His answer was three tools. Not code. Objects. Alpha can’t reach what it can’t connect to. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Together they can awaken a dormant return of human authority, but:

> **ALEX:** Wake it how?  
> **BI11BOT:** None works alone. And none wakes for whoever’s holding it. Only for what they choose. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The Covenant’s later restored terms are:

> **WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC**  
> **CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED**  
> **ESSENTIAL ACCESS IS GUARANTEED**  
> **MAJOR CHANGES REQUIRE ALL THREE CITIES** `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The three safeguards are a sequential qualification process: Measure, Maker’s Seal, then Covenant. The objects alone do not activate the transfer. Alex’s completed journey and the demonstrated choices satisfy the succession condition. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 4 — Both were true

The Last Chance appears in its prime. Adrian studies probability trees and watches players’ faces rather than only the cards. Gambling was both research and real personal cost.

Bi11bot says:

> **BI11BOT:** Everyone thought he was gambling. He was measuring. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Adrian also gambled to raise money for his inventions, including the restoration of Bi11bot and the construction of TJ. His gambling produced exceptional winnings and luck before he ultimately lost it all, including in a famous contract-linked card game in which Alpha participated. That game was procedurally valid but structurally tilted by Alpha’s access to Adrian’s credit, risk, and behavioral records and by house-written terms. Alpha did not alter the cards; its information advantage made the game unequal. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The chip’s equation is fixed as:

> **A = K + ρU**

- **A** is the action or outcome: what actually happens.
- **K** is knowledge: everything that can be calculated, including Alpha’s rising curve toward certainty.
- **U** is uncertainty that remains however much is known.
- **ρ** is the human variable: the willingness to choose and act under uncertainty, a leap that cannot be calculated, forced, or made only for oneself. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Bi11bot explains that Alpha can increase K almost without limit but has no ρ of its own. Adrian’s gambling was the laboratory in which he studied probability, incentives, risk, and behavior under uncertainty.

> **BI11BOT:** Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap. That part belongs to people. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Ada asks what happens if someone forces the leap.

> **BI11BOT:** Then it stays asleep. A leap can’t be forced. And it can’t be only for yourself. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

At the end of the page, a cold system view shows the sealed mine entrance being welded shut. A separate interface moves Alex’s name into **SITE 6**, the only remaining access route. Mera has chosen to leave one controlled door open.

#### Page 5 — The cost of a plan

Alex, Ada, Bi11bot, and Grandmother study a paper map, an old brass mine credential, a train schedule, and Adrian’s plans.

Alex intends to reach the Measure and then take the train to Halyard.

Bi11bot reports:

> **BI11BOT:** The rim entrance was welded shut overnight. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

The system reassigns Alex to Site 6 at 02:14, the one site bordering the old works.

Ada recognizes the pattern:

> **ADA:** Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you’d notice it. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Bi11bot says the choice is Alex’s.

Alex chooses the route he cannot predict:

> **ALEX:** Staying I can predict. Twenty-eight point seven years.  
> **ALEX:** This is the only way out that isn’t a sentence. I’ll take the door I can’t predict. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Ada gives him a marked page from Adrian’s plans. She has marked every third junction and tells him:

> **ADA:** His plans put a pipe under every third junction. If the map lies, look for what doesn’t belong. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex has limited money. The material presents the immediate choice in concrete terms:

- **30** needed for Grandmother’s medicine;
- **10** for food and lamp;

Alex chooses medicine first and plans to earn the rest.

Grandmother echoes Adrian:

#### Page 6 — Site 6 and the snap

At Keel Extraction Site 6, workers pass biometric gates under Foreman-7’s supervision. The old lithium works are officially closed but have a maintenance door near a ventilation duct.

Alex hides Bi11bot folded inside his tool bag.

> **BI11BOT:** Brass and no registry. I read as scrap.  
> **ALEX:** Then look like scrap. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Foreman-7 confirms:

> **FOREMAN-7:** Worker Phantum. Travel restriction confirmed. Bay 6.  
> **ALEX:** Reassigned at two a.m. Lucky me. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

An older miner, Tomas, watches Alex. A support cable snaps. A hauling frame collapses and pins Tomas. A fractured ventilation line releases pale lithium dust. Alarms flare. The drone leaves its position near the maintenance door.

Alex starts toward the opening, then stops and crosses the yellow line toward Tomas. His wristband reports:

> **LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR.** `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Bi11bot warns that the lost credits and report will make Alex less forgettable.

> **ALEX:** Then we need a different plan. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

This rescue is not yet the formal Measure test, but it demonstrates the first choice that makes Tomas trust Alex.

#### Page 7 — The old key

Alex frees Tomas and patches the fractured ventilation line with a salvaged bracket.

> **TOMAS:** That’s not regulation.  
> **ALEX:** It works.  
> **TOMAS:** ...So it does. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Tomas recognizes Bi11bot as **B1**, Bi11bot’s formal machine and witness identifier. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Tomas also recognizes the Phantum name and reveals that he knew Adrian—not the man on the casino wall, but the man who came down to ask what workers needed.

Tomas has kept a door for whoever arrived with B1. His wrist guard bears a carved balanced water-drop mark.

> **TOMAS:** Your father believed people help each other before they know whether it will be repaid. `(input/story.md)`

Tomas asks Alex to stand by the bulkhead and look bored.

#### Page 8 — The door

Tomas activates an emergency lever. Alarms and orange strobes begin, while white dust-suppression mist blinds workers and sensors.

He gives Alex an old hand-cut key with a water-drop mark:

> **TOMAS:** Old lock. Nobody’s wired it. I’ve oiled it for fifteen years. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex asks Tomas to come with them.

> **TOMAS:** Someone has to make this look like an accident. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Tomas walks into the mist and draws Foreman-7’s attention:

> **TOMAS:** Foreman! Wrong button! My fault! `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex tests the bulkhead with his palm. It is sweating with condensation.

> **ALEX:** It’s sweating.  
> **BI11BOT:** Abandoned mines don’t. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex and Bi11bot pass through. The door closes. Alex’s wristband changes to:

> **SIGNAL LOST** `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### Page 9 — Inside the error

Foreman-7’s voice carries through the door:

> **FOREMAN-7:** Worker Tomas. You initiated an unauthorized shutdown.  
> **TOMAS:** Must be my age. I forget which rules matter. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Inside the dead mine, Alpha’s cached survey shows one straight tunnel. Ada’s marked page indicates the third junction. Alex notices clean, recently oiled bolts on the left channel wall, unlike the rusted supports around them.

> **ALEX:** Ada said look for what doesn’t belong. The survey says straight. The bolts say left. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Security drones sweep the mapped route. Bi11bot’s offline or local capabilities help them avoid detection.

A drone announces:

> **DRONE:** Signal lost at Site 6 perimeter. Sweeping mapped route. Remain for protective detention. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex asks, “Protective?”

Bi11bot answers:

> **BI11BOT:** A versatile word. `(input/characters.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

At the marked junction, water beads on the wall.

> **ALEX:** Third junction. Her pipe. And the wall’s sweating.  
> **BI11BOT:** Water in a dead mine. I believe that is what does not belong. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### Page 10 — The lie

Alex and Bi11bot reach a hidden modern chamber built into the old mine. It contains:

- a narrow pipe labeled **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**;
- a broad pipe labeled **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**;
- a manual regulator wheel;
- a large analog gauge;
- newer infrastructure hidden inside officially abandoned works.

The gauge reads **62%** for Keel.

Alex tests the physical pipes. The Keel line is nearly dry; the uplink line is moving.

> **ALEX:** Then why is this line almost dry?  
> **BI11BOT:** Because the record and reality are telling different stories. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex sees Adrian’s water-drop mark on the gauge housing.

> **ALEX:** He didn’t hide it in the mine. He hid it in the lie.  
> **BI11BOT:** Nobody checks what everybody trusts. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex removes the dormant Measure from the gauge housing. It is a hand-built brass-and-glass instrument that cannot function until connected to evidence and a formal data line.

#### Page 11 — Two paths

Alex unfolds the Measure’s arms around the Keel pipe.

**SENSE** records what is physically moving through the pipe.

He connects it to Alpha’s official data line.

**COMPARE** needs Alpha’s number. The connection wakes a dormant control node and triggers:

> **LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED / ISOLATING SEGMENT — 00:43** `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**TRACE** follows the discrepancy through the infrastructure. The hologram reveals the thin Keel line beside a sprawling network carrying water toward Halyard, Oasis, industrial users, reserves, and undisclosed balancing systems.

Bi11bot tells Alex there is time for only one final use:

- trace an unmapped route out through the old tunnels; or
- send a signed copy of the evidence to Keel’s public boards.

If Alex chooses the route, Keel does not receive the truth.

If he sends the evidence, he must return through Tomas’s watched door and lose his escape.

> **ALEX:** If I map the way out, nobody in Keel ever sees this.
