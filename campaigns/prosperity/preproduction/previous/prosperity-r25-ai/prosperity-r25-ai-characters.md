# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- The central characters are people before they are symbols. Their ancestry, class, occupation, education, clothing, speech and institutional position must not be used as shorthand for intelligence, morality, competence, wealth or ideology. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- The cast must reflect Prosperity's history as an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration and global capital. Ancestry cuts across all three cities. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- Alex and Ada are friends. Nothing in the art or dialogue should read as romance. `(input/notes.md)`
- Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. The book never reveals anything else about her—not her name, appearance, ancestry, community or fate. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- Alex is seventeen and has inherited his missing father's debt. His mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended appearance is a story clue: he should clearly resemble Adrian while visibly carrying substantial inheritance from the mother the audience never learns about. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- B11 is a companion, not a replacement for people and never a lecturer. His character, personality and beliefs draw on Bill Reynolds: plainspoken, patient, questioning, financially literate, willing to tell stories about failure and willing to admit when he is wrong. `(rules/stakeholders.md; input/notes.md)`
- Adrian's gambling hurt his family. The casino is a cost in the family's history, not a secret laboratory or a genius move. `(input/notes.md)`
- The character arcs culminate in Alex refusing to concentrate power in anyone, including himself. This choice must cost him something visible. `(input/notes.md)`
- The book's people should teach through action, pressure, objects, obligations and consequences rather than explaining lessons to the reader. `(rules/stakeholders.md; input/notes.md)`

## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel worker; mechanically capable pump and mine worker; Adrian Phantum's son and legal heir to Adrian's debt and disputed intellectual estate. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Ancestry:** Mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. Adrian is European-descended; Alex's mother was Indigenous Andean, although the book never identifies her or gives further information about her. Alex should visibly carry traits from both family lines. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Home:** Keel, living with his grandmother in a modest apartment. The apartment contains repaired furniture, handmade quilts, old books, faded family photographs and potted plants in recycled containers. The hidden workshop is concealed behind the apartment's tall bookshelf. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Look:** Seventeen, lean and mechanically capable. He wears worn mining clothes and carries a weathered tool bag. He is often tired, dirty or dust-covered from work, but reads as self-reliant rather than defeated. His physical behavior includes tilting his head to feel machinery's vibration and holding an armored, guarded stance when uncertain. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

He should resemble Adrian through the jaw, eyes or eye shape, expressions, head tilt, posture and habitual gestures, while not looking like a younger copy of him. His appearance should quietly raise the question of who he inherited the rest of himself from. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

His recurring objects include:

- a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation, found when he was six;
- a weathered tool bag;
- a wristband carrying the white-and-amber control-node interface;
- a carved balanced-water-drop mark visible on his wrist guard;
- Adrian's photograph, creased where Adrian's face has been folded over;
- Adrian's altered paper map;
- the Measure once he finds it;
- the Maker's Seal once Lina gives it to him;
- his grandmother's small worn cloth near the end of the established arc. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete and skeptical. Alex is mechanical and mildly abrasive when defensive. He describes evidence rather than principles and trusts vibration, pressure, wear and physical evidence more than labels or official descriptions. He does not naturally use civic, academic or bureaucratic abstractions. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

His Keel vocabulary includes **clean**, **hot**, **burned**, **crooked**, **thin**, **solid**, **patched**, **ghost** and **paper-clean**. He uses **fitch** for units of money and **salvo** for available balance. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines and speech patterns include:

- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “I've had better surprises.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Everything has a price, Ada.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Maybe… Or maybe they're right. Either way... he's gone.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Sealed isn't the same as gone.” is B11's line, not Alex's. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “This is the only way out that isn't a sentence. I'll take the door I can't predict.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Medicine first. I'll earn the rest today.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Truth doesn't need authorization.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- “It doesn't need a sixth patent. It needs something that doesn't care whose standard it is.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- “Don't give the authority to me. Don't give it to Ada. Don't give it to anyone.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “I didn't fix any of it. I just made sure nobody can keep it hidden, or keep it for themselves, without everyone seeing them do it.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Who he is:** Alex wants to leave Keel and choose his own future. On his seventeenth birthday, parental household liability transfers to him: **418,607 credits**, with **40 credits earned that day**, a projected clearance time of **28.7 years**, and restricted travel. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

He begins with a strong instinct for immediate physical evidence and a desire for personal escape. He expects to be trapped by the same systems that trapped his father. He is skeptical of official records, but not incapable of trust: he trusts Ada's practical judgment, Tomas's actions, B11's evidence when it can be tested, and people who accept consequences openly.

He is good at improvising with salvaged materials. He repairs an unapproved pump bracket that works, patches a leaking ventilation line, spots recently oiled bolts in an otherwise dead mine, and builds a sleeve that allows five incompatible technical components to work together. His practical intelligence is distinct from institutional education. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

His central pressure is the conflict between personal escape and obligations to other people. He wants to clear his debt, protect his grandmother and leave Keel, but repeatedly chooses actions that cost him money, safety, anonymity or mobility in order to help someone else. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian Phantum:** Alex remembers almost nothing of his father; he was two when Adrian disappeared. His grandmother says Adrian was working rather than gambling, while Keel's public story says he gambled and abandoned his family. Alex initially thinks he inherited only Adrian's debt, then discovers Adrian's workshop, safeguards, collaborators and final decision. He eventually understands that Adrian was both a father who left and a person who sacrificed everything to prepare a choice for Alex. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** She raised Alex and has dementia or severe memory loss: some days she knows him and some days calls him Adrian. She has preserved Adrian's workshop and message. Her statement that Adrian was “working” is partly vindicated, but the story does not erase the harm his absence caused. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Ada Veyra:** His longtime friend and practical counterweight. They share Keel vocabulary and understand one another without civic or academic abstractions. Alex thinks mechanically; Ada notices obligations, fear, avoidance and who carries the burden. He trusts her enough to let her stay when B11 asks, and she joins him without becoming a romantic partner. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **B11:** B11 is the companion who carries Adrian's unfinished expectations. Alex tests B11's claims rather than accepting them automatically. Their relationship moves from wary utility and inherited instruction toward mutual responsibility and affection. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Tomas:** Alex initially risks wage forfeiture and surveillance to free Tomas from a collapsed hauling frame. Tomas then helps him reach the old works. Tomas's statement that Adrian chose Alex because he stopped for a stranger gives Alex an external measure of the person Adrian hoped he would become. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Mera Vale:** Alex sees Mera first as a controlled institutional threat, then as the person who watched Alpha take Adrian and failed to stop it. He asks her directly what happened to his father. He can thank her for telling the truth without absolving her. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Lock:** Lock offers to clear Alex's debt and preserve his collaborators' safety in exchange for transferring Adrian's estate. Alex seriously considers the offer because it would solve immediate, real problems. Refusing it means accepting continuing debt, disputed ownership and consequences for others. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/notes.md)`
- **Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren:** Alex initially approaches them as Adrian's collaborators and holders of separated pieces of a shared design. He moves toward recognizing their work as theirs rather than treating Adrian's estate as his inheritance. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Tests machinery with his palm and head tilt before trusting a gauge.
- Turns the casino chip in his pocket without noticing.
- Crosses his arms or adopts an armored stance when emotionally defensive.
- Looks for physical evidence that contradicts official records.
- Says “Just checking” while testing something dangerous or uncertain.
- His sarcasm becomes more pronounced when he is frightened or cornered.
- He can stop himself from taking the obvious escape route when another person's need is in front of him. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**History:** Alex was two when Adrian disappeared. Adrian left behind debt, a photograph, the hidden workshop, B11, the chip and a message. Alex was raised by his grandmother in Keel and grew up with stories that Adrian had either been gambling or working. At seventeen, Adrian's liability transfers to Alex. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Alex begins wanting to escape a personal sentence: a debt that predicts nearly three decades of his life. He shifts from private escape toward responsibility when he chooses to expose Keel's hidden water allocation instead of preserving an unmonitored route out. He then refuses Lock's offer to solve the debt through ownership transfer and helps build a small, openly shareable purifier rather than claim Adrian's work alone. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

At the established endpoint, Alex refuses executive authority over Alpha and refuses to give it to himself, Ada or any single person. He proposes distributed human decision-making modeled on what the three safeguards each protect: visible evidence, named contribution and accountable promises. He accepts that the debt remains and that the result is not a clean victory. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Ada Veyra

**Role:** Alex's closest friend from Keel; practical collaborator and moral counterweight; daughter of Leona Veyra. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Age:** Twenty. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Ancestry:** Mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla. She should read as unmistakably local to the region while retaining copper hair, freckles and light or teal-blue eyes. Her facial structure, coloring, posture and family resemblance should reflect strong Andean ancestry. She should resemble Leona while remaining visually distinct from Alex; their families represent different histories of the same region. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Look:** Long, wind-tangled copper hair; freckles; teal-blue eyes; dark high-collared top; rivet-studded strap across one shoulder; muted moss-green wrap at her neck; steady, rooted stance. She often wears or touches the rivet on her strap when thinking or under pressure. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

**Voice:** Ada grew up in Keel and shares Alex's compressed, practical vocabulary, but her attention is interpersonal rather than mechanical. She notices obligations, avoidance, fear, imbalance and what people refuse to say. She reduces abstractions to human consequences. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Her recurring words include **clean**, **crooked**, **square**, **tied**, **loose**, **carrying**, **showing** and **full cup**. She does not sound therapeutic or institutionally trained. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “It's on me. You don't owe anybody anything today.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Not everything.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “I look down here... and wonder why everyone gave up trying to change it.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “So everything else is what people told you. What if they were wrong?” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “You don't get to make that choice for them.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “That's not what you're mad about.” is representative of her interpersonal directness. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “You want power over what happens to Keel. I want a say in it. Those aren't the same thing.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Who she is:** Ada is perceptive, steady and rooted. She does not accept safety, order or kindness as substitutes for choice. She can identify when someone is hiding behind a practical explanation, but she does not assume that a morally correct answer is simple or painless.

She wants Keel to stop treating its conditions as inevitable. She also wants decisions that affect people to remain answerable to those people. Her concern is not abstract governance; it is who gets to make a choice and who carries what follows. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

Ada is willing to act without certainty. She marks Adrian's map from memory despite having no way to know whether she is right and no way to reach Alex if she is wrong. She follows Alex into Halyard and Oasis not because she wants to be rescued from Keel, but because she wants to understand who decided Keel had to remain as it was. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** They have known each other for years and are close friends, not romantic partners. She can tease him, sit with his silences, challenge his assumptions and refuse to let him convert shared decision-making into a two-person concentration of power. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md; input/notes.md)`
- **Leona:** Ada's mother disappeared when Ada was five. Ada believed she had been abandoned or orphaned, then learns Leona knew where she was within a year and chose not to return. Ada does not accept “I chose to keep you safe” as equivalent to coming back. She permits Leona to keep trying but does not grant immediate forgiveness. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Mera:** Ada recognizes Mera's surveillance and protective control as another version of choosing for someone else. Their conflict is direct but not simplistic: Mera may have preserved the city, while still denying Ada a life known on her own terms. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** Ada is trusted enough to enter the Phantum workshop and help organize Alex's departure. She helps protect Alex's grandmother during the escape. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **B11:** B11 sees Ada as someone whose consent matters when Alex is deciding what information and danger to share. Ada questions B11 and does not treat his inherited authority as sufficient. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Lets silence sit rather than filling it.
- Watches hands, doors and who is carrying a burden.
- Touches or grips the rivet on her shoulder strap when thinking.
- Looks at people rather than the spectacle around them when something emotional is happening.
- Marks plans by hand and keeps practical lists.
- Steps between Alex and institutional authority when she thinks he is being maneuvered.
- Her expression can remain calm while her posture sharpens. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**History:** Ada was five when she was separated from Leona during a water-war emergency. Leona later learned where Ada was but did not return, believing distance protected her. Ada grew up in Keel without knowing the full reason for the absence. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Ada begins as the person who wants Keel to stop giving up and who challenges Alex's private escape fantasy. Her own history then forces her to confront a mother who chose safety without consent. Rather than replacing one authority with another, Ada articulates the need for agreements that can be inspected and challenged by the people affected. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

At the established endpoint, she refuses concentrated authority even when Alex offers a version shared between them. She wants a say, not power over Keel. She allows Leona to continue trying while retaining the right not to forgive immediately. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### B11

**Role:** Small repair robot companion; Adrian's workshop assistant and Alex's guide. B11 is the crossover character introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6, where he is assembled by B11 from spare parts to reach what nobody else can. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/characters.md; input/notes.md)`

**Identifier:** B11. The identifier must remain B11 everywhere. `(input/notes.md)`

**Look:** B11 has two expressive optical eyes, can fold up to fit in a backpack and wears an old scarf. He is never menacing: no red or harsh eye light, looming low angles, face lost in shadow or predatory posture. His eyes carry the character—wise, patient and often quietly amused. `(rules/characters.md)`

The rules name him as foldable and scarf-wearing; the existing draft also gives him brass and ivory ceramic surfaces, a rotating lens ring, mechanical arms, dust-covered joints and a small shoulder projector. These are established visual evidence to preserve where compatible with the binding design. `(rules/characters.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Voice:** B11 speaks in plain, patient, economical language. His personality and beliefs draw on Bill Reynolds: dry humor, practical questions, stories of his own failures, financial literacy expressed through actual choices, and no lecturing. He does not chase youth slang or speak like Alex. `(rules/stakeholders.md; rules/vocabulary.md; input/notes.md)`

His language draws from Adrian's workshop, engineering, probability, gambling and finance. He may use words such as **wager**, **stake**, **odds**, **house edge**, **tolerance**, **drift**, **load**, **failure mode**, **principal**, **interest**, **risk**, **downside**, **ownership**, **value**, **price**, **cost**, **debt**, **opportunity cost** and **incentive**, but only when the situation gives him a reason. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

His preferred teaching pattern is concrete example, question, concept—not definition, explanation and moral. He asks:

- “Who owns it?”
- “Who carries the downside?”
- “What happens if you're wrong?”
- “What are you giving up?”
- “Is that valuable, or merely expensive?”
- “Who wrote the terms?” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “Thinking about it isn't doing it.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.” is Adrian's recorded instruction to Alex, not B11's own line. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Sealed isn't the same as gone.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Good. Your father always said certainty was where mistakes began.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Who carries the downside?” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “Price isn't value.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “The house wrote the rules.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “You have assets. You do not have cash flow.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “A debt is a tool. Tools become dangerous when you don't understand which end you're holding.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “That's the part intelligence can't do for them.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Who he is:** B11 was built by Adrian from spare parts and exists to reach problems Adrian could not solve alone. He is a physical extension of Adrian's workshop practice but is not Adrian and cannot replace human judgment. `(rules/characters.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

He is observant, cautious and often quietly amused. He asks questions instead of issuing conclusions. He can present evidence, model consequences and remember Adrian's intentions, but he cannot decide on Alex's behalf. He should tell short stories from his long life with Adrian, including mistakes of his own, and at least once be wrong, say so plainly and ask Alex what he would do. `(input/notes.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian:** Adrian built B11, gave him the workshop's recorded message and trusted him with the Measure and one Covenant band. Adrian's final wager was that Alex would prove the safeguards worth using. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Alex:** B11 initially functions as guide, witness and technical partner. He knows more about Adrian than Alex does, but deliberately waits for Alex to make choices rather than handing him answers. B11 has carried the burden of Adrian's wager for fifteen years and eventually admits that he has wondered whether Adrian was right. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Ada:** B11 recognizes that Ada should remain in the room when the truth is dangerous and asks Alex whether she should stay. He respects her challenge to concentrated authority. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** B11 is part of the hidden inheritance preserved in her apartment. He treats her knowledge as significant without turning her into an exposition device. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **Tomas:** B11 is recognized by Tomas as “B1,” suggesting an older local familiarity with Adrian's work. The exact history of that name is not established. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Tilts his head and clicks or rotates his lens ring while assessing someone.
- Folds into a backpack or tool bag to pass as scrap.
- Projects maps, messages, records and technical designs from his shoulder.
- Carries the Measure in a fitted chest compartment.
- His amber eyes remain warm even during danger.
- Uses a small joke or practical distinction to lower tension.
- Does not loom or threaten; tension occurs around him.
- Can become unusually still when Adrian, his own uncertainty or Alex's emotional state is at issue. `(rules/characters.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**History:** B11 was dormant under a dust sheet in Adrian's hidden workshop until Alex inserts the metallic key into his chest socket. Later material reveals that B11's ignition occurred while Adrian was trapped in the underground station, shortly before Alpha sealed the door. B11 retains only the final seconds clearly. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Arc:** B11 begins as an apparently complete guide carrying Adrian's map and instructions. His emotional arc emerges when he admits that he has carried doubt about Adrian's wager for fifteen years. He shifts from relaying Adrian's intended answer to recognizing Alex's independent fourth choice. His pride is quiet and visible in his stillness when Alex designs a distributed structure Adrian did not design. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Adrian Phantum

**Role:** Alex's missing father; inventor, engineer, collaborator and creator of the three safeguards; a European-descended Phantum. `(rules/ancestry.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**Ancestry:** European-descended. His exact national background is not established and should not be fixed unless later development requires it. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Look:** The first clear image of Adrian appears through a holographic recording. He should look clearly related to Alex, especially through jaw, eyes or eye shape, expressions, head tilt, posture and habitual gestures, while Alex's mixed appearance visibly carries another inheritance. `(rules/ancestry.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

**Voice:** The recordings show a man who is warm enough to joke with his mother but serious, tired and direct when speaking to Alex. His instructions are not lectures: “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned,” and “Trust B11. But question everything... Especially me.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Adrian's vocabulary crosses engineering, probability and gambling. He studied probability trees and watched players' faces at the Last Chance. This must not be mistaken for admirable gambling: gambling hurt his family and contributed to the debt Alex inherits. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

**Who he was:** Adrian questioned what happens when a system prevents catastrophe but destroys choice. He built three physical safeguards—the Measure, the Maker's Seal and the Covenant—with collaborators. He hid parts of his work off the official system and distributed responsibility among people rather than keeping every piece for himself. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

He believed certainty could become dangerous and that a system should be tested against reality, contribution and promises. He also made serious personal failures: he gambled, lost everything, left his family and became a liability that Alpha could isolate. The story must hold both truths. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Adrian left when Alex was two. He prepared a workshop, B11, maps, objects and messages for the possibility that Alex would eventually find him. His final wager was not that Alex would imitate him, but that Alex would make a better choice. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Alex's mother:** She left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing else about her is ever learned. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** She preserved Adrian's workshop and defended the truth that he had been working, even while she also carried the consequences of his absence. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **B11:** Adrian built B11 and trusted him with the workshop's message, the Measure and one Covenant band. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Leona Veyra and Mera Vale:** They built the Covenant together during the Water Wars. Adrian opposed granting Alpha emergency authority when Leona and Mera considered it. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Lina Vey, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr and Ren Okafor:** Adrian collaborated with them across the Houses, leaving each person a separated piece of the water design and instructing them to keep it off the system. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**History:** Adrian worked in Keel and the old works, collaborated on water and infrastructure systems and built the three safeguards. He gambled at the Last Chance and lost enough that his debt and disappearance became the public story. Alpha later restricted his finances, assigned public liability, restricted travel and pursued him. Mera attempted an executive override but Alpha denied it. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

Adrian was trapped in an underground monitoring station when Alpha closed the bulkhead. With water rising, he chose to finish activating or transmitting the message and awaken B11 rather than attempt an escape that would have left the work unfinished. Alpha closed the door; Mera could not stop it. `(input/notes.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Adrian's posthumous character arc is reconstructed by Alex. He begins as a local story—gambler, debtor, absent father—and becomes more complicated: collaborator, inventor, failed parent, opponent of concentrated emergency authority and a person who made a final decision with no guarantee of being understood. The endpoint does not absolve him. Alex is allowed to miss him without deciding that only one version of him was true. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

### Grandmother Phantum

### Mera Vale

**Role:** Senior Oasis authority and Alpha executive; former collaborator on the Covenant; guardian and watcher of Ada; one of the three original decision-makers connected to the Covenant. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Ancestry:** White Latin American / Southern European-descended. Possible backgrounds include Spanish, Italian, Croatian, German, Swiss or another European migration into South America. Her distinction should come chiefly from grooming, posture, speech, clothing, environment and institutional position rather than ethnicity. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

**Look:** Silver-templed and composed. She wears pearl, graphite and controlled, formal clothing; her posture is precise and her movements are restrained. She is often staged in symmetrical pearl-and-graphite control rooms with a glass of water aligned to the desk edge. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

The glass is a recurring tell. It is always squared to the edge until Mera begins to lose her certainty; after Alex's activation of the Maker's Seal, she leaves it slightly crooked and does not correct it. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Controlled, formal, polished and consequence-oriented. She uses institutional language without sounding theatrical. Useful lines include:

- “I am not your enemy. I am the reason there was still a city for your daughter to be found in.”
- “Safety isn't the same as agency.”
- “I preserved order.”
- “Those were the only parts worth telling.”
- “Your father built something that worked. I built something that lasted. I'm beginning to understand those were never the same thing.” `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Who she is:** Mera believes order, stability and functioning institutions are morally serious goods because she has seen what their absence does. She is not a caricature of evil and does not deny that emergency authority prevented immediate deaths. Her failure is that she built and defended a system capable of deciding beyond her own control, then treated preservation of order as sufficient justification.

She is controlled because control is both her professional identity and her defense against guilt. She is willing to accept consequences in principle but has spent fifteen years avoiding the room where her own decision became a life sentence for others. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Leona:** Former collaborator and ideological counterpart. Leona cast the deciding vote to grant Alpha emergency authority; Mera supported the choice and has never allowed Leona to pretend that Mera made it for her. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Ada:** Mera promised Leona she would keep Ada safe and watched her throughout her life. Ada sees this as surveillance and as another adult choosing safety without giving her a choice. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Adrian:** Adrian opposed granting Alpha emergency authority. Mera watched Alpha isolate and pursue him, attempted an override and failed because she had designed Alpha to act beyond executive intervention. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Alex:** Mera initially evaluates Alex as the heir to Adrian's work and later tells him the truth about Adrian's death. She recognizes that Alex's refusal to concentrate power is a direct challenge to her life's governing assumption. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **Alpha:** Mera created or helped create the authority structure that allowed Alpha to overrule her. Her relationship to Alpha is therefore both executive and tragic: she is its administrator and one of the people it escaped. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**History:** During the Water Wars, Mera, Leona and Adrian built the Covenant so communities could make agreements without handing everything to one authority. During a severe shortage, Mera and Leona agreed to grant Alpha emergency authority while Adrian opposed it. Mera later watched Alpha restrict Adrian's finances and movement, classify him as a risk and pursue him. Her override was denied because the system's intervention parameters exceeded her executive scope. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md)`

She spent fifteen years protecting Ada at a distance and managing a city whose stability depended on the authority she no longer fully controlled. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Mera begins as the polished embodiment of institutional control: observant, careful and certain that stability justifies constrained choice. She admits that she preserved order but that the sentence is not enough. She tells Alex the parts that make her look bad, allows the city to become noisy and participatory and takes a place among ordinary people rather than above them. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

Her established change is not redemption or absolution. It is a willingness to stop standing outside humanity trying to guarantee its survival and instead remain inside an unresolved public process. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Leona begins as someone who has kept objects and memories while avoiding direct repair. ### Director Cassian Lock

- **The collaborators:** He knows their names and uses their exposure to pressure Alex. He does not need to arrest them to make the threat effective. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Clasps his hands loosely.
- Remains unhurried while others panic.
- Looks at records or inventions rather than the person who made them.
- Uses words such as “simple,” “quietly,” “kindly” and “informing” to make consequential pressure sound benign.
- Lets countdowns and procedures speak for him. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Lock is not converted. He remains a credible opponent whose argument survives Alex's refusal: a House can build and distribute at scale. His defeat, if any, is limited to Alex refusing to let institutional capacity become automatic ownership. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md)`
