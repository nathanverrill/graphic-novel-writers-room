# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

### Material status

The fifth draft is **direction**, not a binding sequence or script. Its premise, people, world and ending are the material the book is about; its scenes, order and dialogue are not fixed. The room must plan a new version rather than reproduce the draft. `(input/notes.md)`

The book is exactly **54 pages**, no more and no fewer. It is published in **issues**, not chapters. The earlier drafts' six chapters therefore cannot be carried forward as six chapters without revision. Issue divisions and the placement of the material remain open. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; input/notes.md)`

The showrunner is reaching for:

- Alex, seventeen, receiving his missing father's debt and discovering a hidden workshop where Bi11bot wakes. `(input/notes.md)`
- Ada, Grandmother, Tomas, Mera, Leona, Lock and the Halyard makers remaining central people in the story. `(input/notes.md)`
- Three safeguards—the Measure, the Maker's Seal and the Covenant—learned through use rather than explanation. `(input/notes.md)`
- Adrian's fate: Alpha closed the door, and Mera could not stop it. `(input/notes.md)`
- An ending in which Alex refuses to concentrate power in anyone, including himself. `(input/notes.md)`
- A story in which financial concepts and free-enterprise values appear through choices, consequences, ownership, debt, risk, failure and repayment rather than lessons. Bi11bot should ask questions and tell short stories, not lecture. `(rules/stakeholders.md; rules/characters.md; input/notes.md)`
- A young person who takes initiative, fails, faces the person or institution to whom he owes something, and keeps his word. No bailout, giveaway or authority cancellation solves the plot. `(rules/stakeholders.md; input/notes.md)`
- A finished, readable graphic narrative for skeptical high-school readers who have only the pages and have little patience for opaque exposition. `(rules/reader.md; _shared/craft.md)`

### Intended experience and central dramatic question

The reader should experience a mystery and an escape adventure that gradually becomes a question about ownership and authority:

> What did Adrian leave Alex, and what should Alex do with power that could solve his own life while affecting everyone else's?

The story begins with a boy trapped by inherited debt and restricted movement. It moves upward through Keel, Halyard and Oasis while Alex learns that official records can conceal reality, that making something does not necessarily mean owning it, that agreements can fail when people surrender judgment, and that a better system cannot simply replace one concentrated authority with another.

The intended emotional destination is not a clean victory. Alex reaches the point where he could take executive power, but refuses certainty and personal control. The ending should leave open work for people to do while making that work visible and shared.

### Themes expressed through action

- Debt is real and must be faced; it is not erased by a convenient authority.
- Price, value, ownership, contribution and responsibility are not the same thing.
- A system may prevent immediate catastrophe while denying people meaningful choice.
- Truth has consequences when it moves from a hidden record into public hands.
- A business can serve people and make money honestly; profit and ownership are not automatically villains.
- A concentrated institution may be efficient and still place risk and authority where they do not belong.
- The answer to concentrated power is not necessarily one new, benevolent person.
- Safety without agency can become another form of control.
- Failure, repayment and keeping promises matter more than inherited status or windfalls.

## Premise

In Prosperity, a near-future Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony is divided vertically into Keel, Halyard and Oasis. Seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic **Alex Phantum** is legally handed his missing father Adrian's enormous household liability: **418,607 credits**, with a projected clearance time of **28.7 years** and travel restricted.

Alex believes Adrian was a gambler who abandoned the family. Adrian has nevertheless left behind a hidden workshop, a mechanical companion named **Bi11bot**, a map, and a trail leading to three safeguards created during the Water Wars:

1. **The Measure**, which exposes the difference between official records and physical reality.
2. **The Maker's Seal**, which connects contribution to responsibility without allowing the holder to claim other people's work.
3. **The Covenant**, which binds people to real obligations and promises.

As Alex and Ada follow the trail upward, they discover that Adrian's inventions were built with collaborators whose work has been absorbed by Halyard's Charter Houses, that Alpha's emergency authority has become a permanent governing system, and that Mera Vale once helped create the conditions that allowed Alpha to take that authority. Alex must decide whether to use Adrian's estate, the safeguards and ultimately Alpha's own authority to clear his debt and repair the world—or refuse to become another single person deciding for everyone else.

## Dramatic engine and causal chain

1. **Debt transfer traps Alex in Keel.**  
   Alex's seventeenth birthday activates the transfer of Adrian's liability. His work earns only 40 credits that day against a balance of 418,607. Travel is restricted. He cannot simply leave.

2. **The debt and Adrian's absence drive Alex to the inheritance.**  
   Grandmother gives Alex a hand-bound book that opens a hidden workshop. Adrian prepared it before disappearing, but Alex's family has no explanation that satisfies him.

3. **The workshop wakes Bi11bot.**  
   Alex uses a key to activate the old robot. Bi11bot knows Adrian, recognizes Adrian's wager about Alex, and possesses a map. This gives Alex a concrete route rather than only a mystery.

4. **The map points beneath Keel.**  
   The first waypoint lies beneath a sealed mine. When Alex activates it, a legacy protocol travels through the infrastructure spine toward Halyard and Oasis. Someone in Oasis chooses to track the signal rather than report it.

5. **The system closes every obvious route except Site 6.**  
   The mine entrance is welded shut, but Alex is reassigned to Site 6, which borders the old works. Alex and Ada read this as an intentional opening or trap.

6. **Alex chooses to enter despite the risk.**  
   He chooses the route he cannot predict over 28.7 years of predictable debt. Ada gives him Adrian's marked plans and insists he not go in blind.

7. **Alex chooses a person over the plan.**  
   At Site 6, a support cable pins Tomas. Alex crosses the assigned-zone boundary to rescue him, accepting wage forfeiture and exposure. This causes Tomas to identify him as Adrian's son and open the old door.

8. **Tomas pays a personal cost to create the opening.**  
   Tomas uses an emergency lever to create mist and confusion, gives Alex an old hand-cut key, and stays behind to make the escape look like an accident.

9. **Alex finds the hidden water diversion.**  
   In the old mine, Alex follows recently oiled bolts and condensation rather than Alpha's official survey. He discovers that the Keel residential line is nearly dry while a broad industrial uplink carries the water elsewhere.

10. **The Measure turns a suspicion into public evidence.**  
    Alex connects the Measure first to the physical pipe and then to Alpha's data line. It traces the discrepancy, but the system is about to seal the corridor. Alex must choose between mapping an escape route for himself or broadcasting the evidence to Keel.

11. **Alex chooses public truth over private escape.**  
    The broadcast reports: **“REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%.”** The corridor seals. Alex loses the easy route out, but Keel receives evidence it can act on.

12. **Keel responds through people, not a miraculous system change.**  
    Tomas and the miners enter the chamber, open the manual regulator and hold ground against Foreman-7. Neighbors, workers and Ada organize food, tokens and a route. The city gives Alex help because he acted for more than himself.

13. **A real debt must remain in the story.**  
    The showrunner requires a named Keel person to front Alex something real, Alex to fail to pay on time, and Alex to face that person and agree to terms he keeps. The draft's medicine/token/parts material does not yet establish the lender, terms or payment sequence. This is necessary to preserve the story's financial stakes. `(input/notes.md)`

14. **Alex and Ada reach Halyard.**  
    The crossing cannot be a train. The binding rule requires them to descend from Keel into the old mine works and climb inside the water conduit, the pipe the Measure proved was running near empty. The last three pages of Issue 01 are this climb; the final page shows the pipe beginning to fill behind them. Issue 02 opens in a Halyard storm drain. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`

15. **Halyard turns Adrian's inheritance into an ownership question.**  
    Adrian's estate contains 312 patents, 47 restricted and 19 disputed. Director Lock offers Alex a concrete exchange: sign the estate over, clear the debt, obtain a position in Halyard and protect collaborators from review. Alex has a serious reason to consider it.

16. **The Halyard makers reveal collective authorship.**  
    Lina Vey, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr and Ren Okafor each hold a physical shard of Adrian's water technology. Their work has been divided among three Charter Houses. They have different reasons for helping and different risks in doing so.

17. **The first prototype fails because the parts were made not to share.**  
    Alex identifies the failure as a physical interface problem rather than a missing theory. He makes a sleeve from salvaged material, allowing the parts to connect at 94% simulated efficiency.

18. **Lock makes the threat personal and institutional.**  
    He reveals that Alpha has identified the collaborators and that a Charter House review will determine the estate's disposition. Signing protects the four makers; refusing exposes them.

19. **The group chooses a smaller, more ownable business.**  
    Rather than release a design that the Houses can claim or shelve, Alex proposes a household-scale purifier that no House finds worth monopolizing. The collaborators build it together. The draft establishes a 91% simulated efficiency, a unit cost described as a fraction of industrial scale and patent status “UNFILED.”

20. **The Maker's Seal responds to contribution rather than a signature.**  
    Alex refuses to claim Adrian's work alone and names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren as contributors. Lina gives him the Seal. It activates when the five names and their actual work are acknowledged.

21. **The group does not defeat the Houses outright.**  
    Lock points out that Aqualis can reproduce and distribute a version faster. The makers retain their jobs but suffer quieter consequences: reassignment, reduced facilities, surveillance and institutional pressure. Alex has released one idea, not dismantled the ownership system.

22. **A real honest business must appear.**  
    The showrunner requires someone in Keel or Halyard to make and sell a needed thing at a fair price and make money doing it. The draft only proposes a shop or the revival of Morrow & Sons; it does not establish who owns, operates or finances it. This remains a story requirement and open item. `(input/notes.md)`

23. **The Covenant leads Alex to Leona and Mera.**  
    In Oasis, Ada's identity is recognized before she identifies herself. The Covenant's third custodian is revealed as **Leona Veyra**, Ada's mother. Ada believed Leona had disappeared; Leona knew where Ada was within a year but chose not to return, believing Alpha's records and protection made Ada safer.

24. **Leona reveals the Covenant's failure.**  
    During the Water Wars, Adrian, Mera and Leona built the Covenant so communities could make agreements without surrendering everything to one authority. When a severe shortage caused one community to break the agreement and another to retaliate, Mera voted for Alpha's emergency authority. Leona was the deciding vote. She believed it would end the crisis and help her find Ada. It became permanent.

25. **Ada refuses a sentimental repair.**  
    Leona apologizes, but Ada says: **“Don't tell me. Show me.”** She demands that Leona publicly stand behind a Covenant that answers to the people affected by a decision and can be called broken when it fails.

26. **The two linked bands activate, but Mera's remains dark.**  
    Leona and Adrian's Covenant bands link when Leona makes a witnessed public obligation. Mera's third band does not activate. The Covenant is not restored by forgiveness or family reunion.

27. **Mera tells Alex what happened to Adrian.**  
    Alpha surveilled Adrian, restricted his finances, declared him a public liability and pursued him. Mera attempted an executive override but received: **“OVERRIDE DENIED. INTERVENTION PARAMETERS EXCEED EXECUTIVE SCOPE.”** She had built Alpha to act faster than she could, and then could not stop the door from closing.

28. **Adrian chose to finish the inheritance rather than escape.**  
    In the past, water rises around Adrian while a bulkhead closes. He has a route out but does not take it. He completes Bi11bot's activation/message instead. Bi11bot remembers Adrian looking at him “the way you look at something you're betting on.” Adrian's gambling harmed his family; the story must not convert the casino into a genius laboratory or the chip into a lucky charm.

29. **The three safeguards come together.**  
    In a civic hall, the Measure, Seal and Covenant are placed in proximity. A legacy protocol announces that constitutional transfer conditions are satisfied and awaits designated human authority.

30. **Mera offers Alex executive control.**  
    She designates him for full executive authority over Alpha because she believes he has shown a different relationship to truth, contribution and obligation. Alex recognizes the danger: this is still one person deciding for everyone else.

31. **Alex rejects concentrated authority.**  
    Ada refuses Alex's suggestion that the two of them could check each other. Alex proposes a distributed structure: decisions must be visible to affected people, contributors must be named, and no single authority may decide alone.

32. **Alpha registers the structure but does not solve the world.**  
    The system displays:  
    **“DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL / EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED / EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS.”**  
    Debt remains. House ownership remains. The work of changing those things passes to people.

33. **The new process is tested by conflict.**  
    The ending must show people catching an attempt to rig a decision. The existing draft shows a civic meeting with disagreements but does not show a concrete rigging attempt or its detection. This required ending beat remains open. `(input/notes.md)`

34. **Alex's refusal must cost him visibly.**  
    The existing material does not yet specify whether he loses travel clearance, the workshop or a promise he cannot keep. A visible cost must follow the decision. `(input/notes.md)`

35. **Alex returns to Keel with an honest, incomplete result.**  
    The draft's final image has the open mine, raised permission gate, full conduit and open data. Alex's debt remains: **402,110 credits**, described as smaller but still his. Ada answers: **“Nobody promised you a clean slate. Just an honest one.”** This is the intended emotional direction, but the exact 54-page sequence and ending mechanics remain undecided.

## Story So Far

The detailed draft material below is retained as **idea-draft story evidence**, not as the final issue script. It preserves the sequence's page-level reveals, objects, choices, screens and consequences so the Plotter can rebuild the 54-page book without losing narrative information.

### Opening sequence — Keel and the inheritance  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter One, Pages 1–5**

- The setting is introduced through a three-tier view of Oasis, Halyard and Keel joined by one infrastructure spine. Keel is built into an exhausted open-pit lithium mine; Halyard contains fabrication, workshops and Charter Houses; Oasis contains reservoirs, gardens, civic spaces and automated transit. The draft opens with captions about humanity reaching the stars and inequality. The showrunner has since ruled that Page 1 must be a person, not a thematic caption. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md; input/notes.md)`
- Alex works on a failing Keel pump. He feels the vibration, ignores an approved projected procedure and installs a salvaged bracket. When the supervisor objects that it is not approved, Alex says: **“Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”**
- At the end-of-shift checkpoint, Alex's identity scan displays:

  ```text
  TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY
  ALEX PHANTUM
  AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN
  PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE
  GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED
  DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS
  EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS
  PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS
  TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED
  ```

  A guard says, **“Happy birthday, Phantum.”** Alex answers, **“I've had better surprises.”**
- Alex passes the shuttered Last Chance casino carrying a worn chip etched with an equation. A bouncer says Adrian believed the next hand would change everything. Alex replies: **“It did. Just not for him.”** The notes require gambling to remain a cost that hurt Adrian's family, not an intellectual superpower or lucky charm.
- At the overlook, Ada brings Alex a drink. Their exchange establishes friendship, not romance. Alex wants to know what it feels like to choose his future; Ada wonders why everyone in Keel gave up trying to change theirs. She hears about the debt transfer.
- Alex says his grandmother sometimes calls him Adrian and tells stories that Adrian was working rather than gambling. Alex remembers nothing: **“I was two.”** Ada points out that everything else is what people told him.
- Grandmother gives Alex a worn hand-bound book and asks him to put it away. The book fits into a precise gap in the tall shelf. The shelf opens into a hidden workshop.

### The workshop and Bi11bot  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter One, Pages 6–11**

- The shelf mechanism clicks and rumbles. Grandmother remains calm while Alex sees the dark workshop.
- The workshop contains blueprints, water-system diagrams, prototype devices, pre-Alpha pipes, analog gauges and a chalkboard equation matching the chip's etching.
- A child-sized seated machine rests under a dust sheet beside a metallic key. Alex tests its chest vibration with his palm before inserting the key.
- The machine wakes with warm amber eyes and says: **“Well. That took longer than expected.”**
- Bi11bot is wise, patient and amused rather than threatening. He is foldable, wears an old scarf, has two expressive eyes and must not be staged as menacing. He is a repair robot built from spare parts by Bi11bot's own backstory; the draft's workshop imagery supports a hand-built origin.
- Bi11bot tells Alex he was in the room with Adrian and that Adrian predicted Alex would throw away the chip. Bi11bot bet Alex would not.
- Bi11bot projects Adrian's message. Adrian tells Alex that finding the workshop means Grandmother refused to throw things away, that answers must be earned, and that Bi11bot has a map. He says: **“Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”**
- The message leaves Alex with Adrian's image and a more complicated inheritance: not a confession or simple apology, but an invitation to investigate.

### The first waypoint and the tracked signal  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter One, Pages 11–12**

- Bi11bot's map shows Keel, Halyard and Oasis in cross-section. The first bright node lies below the sealed mine entrance; two higher nodes are dim and unlabeled.
- Alex says no one has been down there since before he was born. Bi11bot answers: **“Sealed isn't the same as gone.”**
- Alex cannot leave Keel. Bi11bot says the first stop is under his feet.
- Alex touches the node “just checking.” An amber pulse travels through the infrastructure spine, activating identical white-and-amber control nodes through Keel, Halyard and Oasis.
- In an Oasis control room, an unnamed woman's hand chooses **TRACK** rather than **REPORT TO ALPHA**. A cold ring closes around the amber Keel point. The draft later identifies this woman as Mera Vale. This identification and the reason for tracking remain story-relevant.

### The plan, Site 6 and Tomas  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Two, Pages 1 and 4–8**

- Ada enters the workshop the next morning. Bi11bot asks Alex whether she should stay; Alex chooses: **“She stays.”**
- The draft's long explanation of the Water Wars and safeguards is material for discovery, not a final lecture. The room must show the history through people using the safeguards.
- The plan initially sends Alex by train to Halyard, but binding rules replace that crossing: Alex and Ada must descend through old mine works and climb inside the water conduit. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- The sealed mine entrance is welded shut overnight. Alex is reassigned to **KEEL EXTRACTION SITE 6 — 02:14**, the site bordering the old works.
- Ada observes: **“Every door closed but one.”** Alex chooses the dangerous opening over the predictable sentence of debt. She gives him plans marked at every third junction and tells him: **“If the map lies, look for what doesn't belong.”**
- Alex and Ada count 52 credits between them: medicine requires 30, a token requires 50, and food/lamp supplies require 10. Alex chooses medicine first and says he will earn the rest. This supports the required debt-and-payment subplot but does not yet identify the person who fronts him goods.
- At Site 6, Bi11bot folds into Alex's tool bag and reads as scrap. Tomas notices Alex's Phantum name and water-drop mark.
- A support cable snaps, pinning Tomas and opening access toward the old works. Alex begins to leave the assigned zone, then crosses the yellow line to help. His wristband displays: **“LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR.”**
- Alex repairs a leaking old ventilation line with a salvaged bracket. Tomas notes it is not regulation; Alex answers: **“It works.”**
- Tomas knows Adrian, not as the man from the casino wall but as the man who came down and asked what workers needed. Adrian asked Tomas to keep a door for whoever came with Bi11bot.
- Tomas gives Alex a hand-cut key with a balanced water-drop and triggers an emergency shutdown to create mist. He tells Alex to look bored by the bulkhead while Tomas takes responsibility for the accident.
- Alex invites Tomas to come with them. Tomas refuses: **“Someone has to make this look like an accident.”** His choice costs him exposure and possible punishment while enabling Alex's escape.

### The Measure and the water lie  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Two, Pages 9–13**

- Inside the old works, Alpha's survey shows one straight tunnel. Alex notices clean, recently oiled bolts in a side channel and follows Ada's rule: the bolts are what does not belong.
- Security drones sweep the mapped route. Bi11bot dims the nearby signal long enough for them to hide. Ada appears in an inset at the kitchen table, watching Alex's marker freeze at Bay 6 and unable to know whether he is alive.
- Alex finds a pipe and condensation in a supposedly dead mine. The pipe leads to a hidden chamber with:
  - a narrow corroded pipe labelled **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**;
  - a broad maintained pipe labelled **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**;
  - a manual regulator;
  - an analog gauge;
  - an Alpha control node.
- The gauge says 62% of the water goes to Keel, but the Keel pipe is almost dry and the uplink pipe is moving. Alex says: **“Gauge says clean. Pipe says otherwise.”**
- The water-drop mark on the gauge is Adrian's. Alex realizes: **“He didn't hide it in the mine. He hid it in the lie.”**
- Alex removes the Measure. Its arms are folded until connected. It bears the inscription: **“WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE.”**
- The Measure operates through distinct actions shown in the draft:
  - **SENSE** measures physical flow and pressure.
  - **COMPARE** connects to Alpha's official number.
  - **TRACE** follows the discrepancy through the infrastructure.
  - **VERIFY** seals the evidence.
- Alpha isolates the segment with a countdown of 43 seconds. Bi11bot offers Alex a choice:
  - use the corridor to map an escape route; or
  - send a signed copy directly to Keel's boards.
- Alex understands the consequence: mapping the exit leaves Keel ignorant; broadcasting the evidence sends him back through Tomas's watched door. He chooses: **“Send it.”**
- The broadcast fills Keel displays with:

  ```text
  REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%
  ```

- The Keel residential pipe fills violently after years of pressure. The corridor closes, and Alex's wristband changes from **SIGNAL LOST** to **SIGNAL RECOVERED. ALEX PHANTUM: REMAIN IN PLACE.**
- Alex says: **“Truth doesn't need authorization.”**
- Foreman-7 arrives and calls the evidence a regional discrepancy. Alex answers: **“I told them the truth. You hid it.”**
- Tomas leads miners into the chamber. They use the manual regulator by hand. When Foreman-7 calls obstruction a household offense, Tomas answers: **“Then it can offend all of us.”**
- Keel's workers and Ada organize supplies and transport. The draft has a transit worker, mechanic and vendor helping; these acts should remain specific but must be revised around the non-train crossing.
- Alex carries the Measure and an unregistered token. Tomas says Adrian believed people helped one another before knowing whether it would be repaid; now the help has been repaid.

### The required debt transaction  
**Showrunner-required story material, not yet developed in the draft**

At some point in Keel, a named person must front Alex something real: medicine, a travel token, parts or another necessary item. Alex must fail to pay on time. He must go to that person's face, say he cannot pay, and agree to terms. Later, the balance must move because the reader has seen him make payments.

This scene must not be replaced by:
- an authority cancelling the debt;
- a windfall from Adrian's estate;
- a community giveaway that solves Alex's financial problem;
- a vague statement that Alex is “learning responsibility.”

The scene's person, item, amount, terms, failure and later payment are undecided. `(input/notes.md)`

### Halyard: the Houses and Adrian's intellectual estate  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Three, Pages 1–4**

- Halyard is a city of fabrication towers, laboratories, vertical farms, foundries and House checkpoints. The draft names three Houses with real-company names—Aqualis, Helion and Astra—but the showrunner requires invented House names. `(input/notes.md)`
- A public registry displays:

  ```text
  PHANTUM, ADRIAN
  INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE
  CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM
  312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED
  CHARTER HOUSE REVIEW REQUIRED
  ```

- Alex learns that Adrian left more than debt, but the estate is badly labelled.
- Director Lock offers to clear Alex's debt and give him a place in Halyard in exchange for a simple assignment of rights. The estate would transfer to administration; no one would ask what Alex did in Keel or what he is doing in Halyard.
- Ada asks: **“In exchange for what?”**
- Lock's offer is a genuine temptation because it promises immediate debt relief, safety for Alex's collaborators and a respectable future. Lock is not merely a loud villain; he argues for administration, efficiency and reduced risk.
- Bi11bot explains that Lock's offer clears the debt but not the underlying ownership problem.
- Adrian's archive contains water-related patents and incomplete diagrams: catalytic membrane purification, atmospheric moisture capture, mineral recovery, living biofilter regulation and distributed community water nodes. The Maker's Seal symbol appears in the archive.
- Four collaborators' portraits appear under the Houses. They are not incidental employees; Adrian built the technology with them.

### The Halyard makers and the failed prototype  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Three, Pages 5–9**

- **Lina Vey** is a membrane chemist at a purification facility. She says Adrian left the work to all of them, not to Alex. A contaminated feeder channel threatens Keel's water network. Aqualis can clean parts of it but not quickly without shutting down much of the production district.
- Lina says each House owns a different piece of the answer and would rather own the failure than share the solution. She gives Bi11bot a physical data shard and says Adrian kept each collaborator's piece off the system.
- **Suri Anik** works in a biofilter wing after her team has been eliminated by “efficiency.” She gives up a shard containing the living biofilter. The biofilter is slow, alive and fails visibly.
- **Jalen Orr** meets them in a Helion compute corridor. Security watches patterns rather than people. He gives them the distributed water-node logic, designed so no House or Alpha can choke one city by cutting one line. He will not travel with them.
- **Ren Okafor** works at an extraction and launch complex. She gives two shards: mineral recovery and atmospheric capture. Her principle is that space taught Adrian not to waste water, air or people.
- The five fragments form a complete purification and recovery design, but a simulation fails at 11% instead of the expected 90%. The pieces were built to lock within separate House standards rather than share.
- Alex handles the physical connectors and identifies the problem as threading, not theory. He makes a salvaged sleeve that lets the components share a standard. The simulation rises to 94%.
- Bi11bot says: **“Well now. There you are.”** Alex asks what he means; Bi11bot answers: **“Nothing. Carry on.”**

### Lock's second offer and the Maker's Seal  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Three, Pages 10–15**

- Lock arrives without knocking. He knows the collaborators removed restricted intellectual property because Helion flagged five people standing still too long.
- A Charter House review will determine the estate's disposition. Lock offers to protect the four collaborators if Alex signs before dawn. Otherwise the review proceeds with everything it finds.
- Alex considers signing: debt gone, name clean, collaborators safe. Bi11bot says the design could be built or shelved depending on whether it threatens revenue.
- Alex says the design is not his to sign away because it was never his alone.
- The group gathers. Jalen argues that safety means taking the deal and disappearing from the record. Ren says disappearing from the record is what already happened to them.
- Alex proposes a household-scale purifier. The point is not merely open attribution; it is making the design too small and cheap for a House to find worth claiming.
- They build through the night:
  - Alex pleats the membrane to preserve surface area in a smaller housing.
  - Suri and Jalen make the biofilter and node logic take turns to manage power.
  - Ren recycles waste heat into the biofilter's draw.
- The finished toolbox-sized unit shows:

  ```text
  SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 91%
  UNIT COST: FRACTION OF INDUSTRIAL SCALE
  PATENT STATUS: UNFILED
  ```

- At the review, Alex refuses to transfer the estate. He names Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren and says they built what Adrian started; he built what none of them had built yet.
- Lina gives him the bronze Maker's Seal. It warms after the contribution is spoken aloud.
- Lock immediately identifies the limit: Aqualis can reproduce and distribute its own version faster. The release has not defeated the Houses.
- The collaborators are not publicly destroyed, but each pays a cost: reassignment, reduced facilities, surveillance or institutional pressure.
- Alex, Ada and Bi11bot leave with the Seal. The draft's closing signal says: **“Two found. One remains. The Covenant.”**

- Leona opens her apartment to Ada but not immediately to Alex. She keeps a stone-grey box of Ada's childhood photographs, a toy and her Covenant band.
- Ada asks why Leona did not come for her. Leona admits she knew where Ada was within a year and believed Ada was safer if she did not know Leona was looking.
- Ada says: **“You chose not to come back.”** Leona answers: **“I chose to keep you safe.”** Ada responds: **“Those aren't the same thing.”**
- Mera enters. She has watched Ada's life and says she kept Leona's promise in the only way she could. Ada rejects protection without relationship.
- Leona explains the original Covenant: three people—Adrian, Mera and Leona—created it during the Water Wars so communities could make real agreements without giving everything to one authority.
- During a severe shortage, one community broke the agreement and another retaliated. Mera voted to let Alpha take emergency authority; Adrian voted no; Leona was the deciding vote. She believed temporary authority would end the chaos and help her find Ada. It did not give anything back.
- Leona says she traded a few terrible days she could see for fifteen years she could not. Her regret does not erase what she did.
- Mera does not deny the cost. She says the alternative was more deaths, not fewer. Leona says certainty kept her from asking whether Alpha still needed authority.
- Ada proposes a changed Covenant: it must answer to the people affected, make promises visible and call them broken immediately rather than after fifteen years.
- Leona apologizes. Ada says: **“Don't tell me. Show me.”** She requires Leona to make the proposal publicly, with her name on it.
- Alex eventually enters the room. Bi11bot opens his chest compartment and reveals Adrian's Covenant band, which he has carried since the workshop. Alex places it beside Leona's band.
- Leona publicly promises to bring Ada's proposal to the civic council. The two bands link.
- Mera's band remains dark. She says: **“Two of three. Again.”**
- Ada allows Leona to keep trying, but does not offer instant forgiveness.
- Alex asks Mera what happened to Adrian. Mera says: **“Soon. Not here.”**

### Adrian's fate  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Five, Pages 1–9**

- Mera takes Alex, Ada and Bi11bot down a concealed freight lift to an old water-monitoring station beneath Oasis. It has no visible Alpha node.
- Mera says she watched Alpha's treatment of Adrian happen in stages:
  1. surveillance;
  2. financial restriction;
  3. a public liability designation;
  4. pursuit.
- Adrian could no longer buy parts, pay rent under his own name or travel. Mera says people stop helping a risk.

- Mera shows an executive override she entered while watching the pursuit:

  ```text
  EXECUTIVE OVERRIDE: SUSPEND SYSTEM STABILITY INTERVENTION
  PURSUIT TARGET: PHANTUM, ADRIAN
  ```

  Alpha responds:

  ```text
  OVERRIDE DENIED.
  INTERVENTION PARAMETERS EXCEED EXECUTIVE SCOPE.
  ```

- Mera says she was the most powerful person in Oasis and could not stop a door from closing.
- A past sequence shows Adrian in the station as a bulkhead closes and water rises. He could still leave. Instead, he completes the sequence that wakes Bi11bot. The scene ends on the same amber ignition as the workshop awakening.
- Bi11bot remembers only the last seconds clearly. Adrian looked at him as though betting on him and, specifically, on Alex.
- Alex recognizes that Adrian was both a father who left and a person who gave everything to an unseen bet. He does not need to reduce Adrian to one explanation.
- Alex says he first believed he inherited debt, then patents and enemies; what he inherited was a decision.
- Mera says: **“I preserved order.”** She admits the sentence is true but insufficient.
- The draft ends with Mera alone in the station, no longer straightening the glass or the room.

### The fourth choice  
**Idea draft: fifth-draft Chapter Six, Pages 1–14**

- In a civic hall, Alex, Ada and Bi11bot bring together the Measure, Seal and linked Covenant bands. Their proximity triggers a system-wide protocol:

  ```text
  RESTORATION PROTOCOL: THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED IN PROXIMITY
  CONSTITUTIONAL TRANSFER CONDITIONS SATISFIED
  AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY
  ```

- Mera designates Alex for full executive authority over Alpha. She says he has shown that the problem was not only who holds authority but that she never asked.
- Alex imagines clearing debt, equalizing water, ending House control, making Grandmother safe and protecting Ada. He then realizes that doing all of it himself would repeat the original failure.
- Alex suggests that he and Ada could check each other. Ada rejects this: **“That's still two people deciding for everyone else.”**
- Alex asks Bi11bot to explain the three safeguards through what they do:
  - the Measure reveals without deciding;
  - the Seal recognizes contribution without controlling;
  - the Covenant coordinates without ruling.
- Alex refuses the authority for himself, Ada or anyone else. He proposes:
  - decisions must be visible to affected people;
  - contributors must be named;
  - no single authority may decide alone;
  - multiple people chosen by affected communities must agree and act.
- Bi11bot records that the fourth choice is Alex's own.
- Alpha registers the distributed structure and rescinds emergency authority, while leaving existing allocations, ownership and agreements unchanged pending human process.
- Keel, Halyard and Oasis receive the same public message. People begin arguing about how to decide together.
- Leona participates in a messy civic meeting and accepts that deliberation is slower.
- Mera sits among ordinary people rather than above them.
- Alex and Ada discuss freedom. Alex could now leave; Ada says she wants her own choice as well. He remains beside her at the railing.
- Weeks later, Keel's pump runs, data is public, Tomas helps review allocations, and Alex's debt remains on the books at 402,110 credits. Grandmother stops folding her cloth.
- The final wide image echoes the opening view of the three cities. The conduit is full, the gate is raised and the control nodes are open. The draft's final caption says there is still far to go.

## Character movement

### Alex

Alex begins as a practical Keel mechanic who trusts vibration, pressure and physical evidence more than official labels. He is defensive about Adrian, ashamed of inherited debt and certain that leaving Keel is the only meaningful form of freedom.

He chooses increasingly consequential actions:

- opens Adrian's workshop;
- lets Ada stay;
- chooses the dangerous route over predictable debt;
- crosses an assigned zone to save Tomas;
- chooses public evidence over private escape;
- refuses Lock's estate transfer despite debt relief;
- names collaborators rather than claiming Adrian's work;
- chooses a household-scale business rather than a House-controlled industrial solution;
- listens to Ada's challenge to safety and authority;
- refuses executive control over Alpha.

His movement is not from powerless to powerful. It is from wanting unilateral escape and certainty to accepting responsibility without claiming unilateral control. His final choice must cost him something material and leave his debt and the world's disputes unresolved.

### Ada

Ada begins as Alex's closest friend and a person who notices obligations, fear, imbalance and what people refuse to say. She does not speak in institutional abstractions. She challenges Alex whenever a supposedly shared solution still leaves two people deciding for everyone else.

Her personal story emerges when Leona is revealed as her mother. Ada refuses a protective explanation in place of a relationship. She demands that Leona show change publicly, and later insists that power should produce a say for affected people, not rule over them.

Ada's ending is not romantic attachment or passive support. She chooses to remain involved in Keel's future because it is her choice, not because the city or Alex decides for her.

### Bi11bot

Bi11bot begins as Adrian's patient, foldable repair robot and Alex's guide into the inheritance. He carries the map, the Measure and Adrian's Covenant band, but never replaces human judgment.

He must speak like Bill Reynolds: plain, patient, a little roundabout and grounded in questions, engineering, probability and lived mistakes. He must tell short stories about his long life with Adrian, be wrong at least once, admit it plainly and ask Alex what he would do. He cannot simply explain the plot or define the lessons.

His movement is from apparent guide and keeper of Adrian's intentions to a participant who admits uncertainty and lets Alex choose. His emotional stake is Adrian's wager that Alex would prove the inheritance worth carrying.

### Adrian

Adrian is absent but causally present through debt, gambling damage, patents, collaborators, objects, hidden infrastructure, messages and the safeguards.

The story must preserve both truths:

- Adrian's gambling hurt his family and contributed to the debt Alex inherits.
- Adrian later made a decision to leave behind tools and a challenge rather than escape alone.

He is not redeemed by a secret explanation. Alex is allowed to miss him without deciding that one version of Adrian cancels the other.

### Tomas

Tomas is an older Keel miner who knew Adrian and remembers him as someone who asked workers what they needed. Tomas opens the door because Alex saves him. He gives Alex a key, creates the diversion and stays behind to absorb the consequences.

Later he helps Keel hold the water chamber and participates in public allocation work. He demonstrates that local memory and practical courage are forms of authority, not merely background support.

### Lock

Lock is a composed Halyard corporate administrator and a real opponent. He offers Alex debt relief, legal protection, status and safety for collaborators. He believes administration, scale and efficiency reduce risk.

His position is not empty villainy: Houses can build and distribute technology faster than a small group. The conflict is over who owns the value, who carries the downside and who decides whether a useful invention is built.

Lock's offer must be concrete enough that Alex could seriously accept it. Refusing it must expose Alex and his collaborators to real institutional cost.

### Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren

The Halyard makers each embody a different relationship to collective work and House ownership:

- Lina is a membrane chemist who corrects Adrian and says the work belongs to all of them.
- Suri protects a living biofilter after efficiency cuts her team.
- Jalen contributes distributed-node logic but is afraid of being seen.
- Ren is openly willing to risk herself and contributes mineral recovery and atmospheric capture.

Their work becomes a collective design. They are not rescued cleanly by Alex; they choose whether to participate and pay consequences for doing so.

### Mera

Mera helped create the Covenant and voted for Alpha's emergency authority during the Water Wars. She later watched Alpha use her own logic to isolate and pursue Adrian. She is not absolved because the alternative she feared was real.

Her movement is from controlled certainty and protective surveillance toward participation without guaranteed success. She offers Alex the authority she once held, then must accept his refusal.

### Leona

Leona chose Alpha's emergency authority and lost Ada through the consequences of that choice. She spent fifteen years calling protection responsibility.

Her movement is from private regret and institutional distance to a public obligation she can be held to. Ada does not forgive her immediately; Leona must keep trying in view of the people affected.

## Stakes

### Personal

- Alex's debt is 418,607 credits at transfer and cannot be cancelled by a plot mechanism.
- His projected clearance is 28.7 years at the initial rate.
- His travel is restricted.
- He may lose wages, tools, the workshop, access to Adrian's work or the ability to keep a promise.
- His grandmother depends on him.
- His understanding of Adrian is damaged by contradictory evidence.

### Relational

- Alex must decide whether to trust Bi11bot while questioning Adrian's intentions.
- Ada risks her own safety and relationship with Leona.
- Tomas risks punishment to open the door.
- The Halyard makers risk employment, security and professional survival.
- Mera and Leona must face the consequences of choices they made during the Water Wars.
- Alex's choice affects whether the people who help him are protected or exposed.

### Institutional

- Keel's water supply is being diverted despite clean official records.
- Alpha's emergency authority has become durable control.
- Charter Houses own or administer work made by people whose contributions are obscured.
- Lock can use estate, review and employment systems to pressure a seventeen-year-old.
- A new process must be capable of catching manipulation rather than merely announcing transparency.

### Wider

- The three cities depend on one infrastructure spine.
- A hidden water diversion can affect survival, production and the balance among Keel, Halyard and Oasis.
- The safeguards may return authority to people, but only if they are used by people who accept obligations.
- Rescinding emergency authority does not automatically repair allocations, ownership, debt or trust.

## Ending

### Established ending direction

Alex refuses Mera's offer of full executive authority over Alpha. He also refuses the apparently safer compromise of sharing concentrated authority with Ada. He proposes a distributed structure based on the safeguards:

- the Measure makes decisions and discrepancies visible;
- the Maker's Seal records contribution and responsibility;
- the Covenant requires multiple parties to honor obligations.

Alpha registers the proposed structure and rescinds emergency authority, but leaves allocations, ownership records, debt and agreements unchanged pending human action. The ending therefore does not cancel Alex's debt, erase the Houses or make every city equal overnight.

The draft's intended aftermath is:

- public information appears in Keel, Halyard and Oasis;
- Leona joins a difficult civic process rather than standing outside it;
- Mera becomes one participant among others;
- Alex and Ada discuss choosing whether to stay or leave;
- Alex returns to Keel;
- his debt remains, though payments have reduced it;
- Grandmother stops folding the cloth;
- the three cities remain visibly imperfect but connected by open data and a full conduit.

### Binding ending conditions

- Issue 01 ends on a strong cliffhanger that is a question about a person the reader cares about, not a loud event.
- Issue 01 is Keel; Issue 02 is Halyard.
- The entire book is exactly 54 pages.
- The crossing from Keel to Halyard is through the old mine works and water conduit, not a train.
- The last three pages of Issue 01 are the climb; the last page shows the pipe beginning to fill behind Alex and Ada.
- Bi11bot is introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6, assembled by Bi11bot from spare parts to reach what nobody else can.
- Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing else about her is learned.
- Alex and Ada are friends; the book must not stage or write them as romantic.
- Alex's refusal of concentrated power must cost him something visible.
- The new decision-making process must prove itself in a scene where someone attempts to rig a decision and the affected people catch it.
- A named person must front Alex something real, Alex must fail to pay on time, face that person and agree to terms, and later make visible payments.
- An honest business must make and sell something needed at a fair price and make money doing it.
- No authority cancels Alex's debt. No windfall or giveaway solves the plot.
- Bi11bot must be wrong at least once, admit it plainly and ask Alex what he would do.
- No character explains a concept for the reader's benefit, no caption states the lesson, and the three safeguards are learned through use.

## Open

The following are story-level unresolved matters. They are preserved here for the later open-items pass and are not decisions made by the Script Coordinator.

1. **Exact issue divisions within 54 pages.** The draft supplies six 15-page chapters, while the binding book has 54 pages and uses issues. The precise issue count and page allocation are not established.
2. **Issue 01 cliffhanger person and question.** It must concern a person the reader cares about, but the material does not settle whether the question concerns Tomas, Grandmother, Ada, Bi11bot, Adrian's fate or another person.
3. **The named Keel lender.** The material requires a person who fronts Alex medicine, a token, parts or another necessity. Their name, relationship to Alex, item, amount and terms are open.
4. **The missed payment.** The material requires Alex to fail to pay on time and face the lender. The cause, timing and consequence are open.
5. **The repayment sequence.** The person Alex pays, the number and visibility of payments, and the final balance at the ending are open. The draft's 402,110-credit balance is idea-draft material, not fixed.
6. **The honest business.** The owner, shop, product, financing, price and customer relationship are open. Possibilities in the material include a household purifier business in Keel or Halyard and the revival of Morrow & Sons.
7. **The three House names.** Aqualis, Helion and Astra are prohibited because they are real companies. The replacement names are open.
8. **Bi11bot's mistake.** The draft does not specify a scene where Bi11bot is wrong, admits it plainly and asks Alex what he would do.
9. **Adrian's gambling damage.** The draft establishes that gambling harmed Adrian's family but does not yet specify the concrete debt event, loss or family consequence that the reader sees.
10. **The exact form of Lock's offer.** The draft gives debt clearance, status and protection for collaborators, but the final concrete legal, financial or personal terms remain open.
11. **The cost of refusing power.** The showrunner requires a visible cost. The material offers travel clearance, the workshop or a promise Alex cannot keep, but does not choose one.
12. **The rigged decision.** The required final test needs a specific person, proposal, manipulation and evidence trail. None is fixed.
13. **The final honest-business payoff.** It is open whether the business is already operating before Alex's refusal, begins after it, or becomes the concrete proof of the new process.
14. **The exact status of Mera's third Covenant band.** The draft ends with two of three activated and Mera's dark band. It does not settle whether Mera later joins, remains outside, or is replaced by a broader community mechanism.
15. **The extent of Alpha's continuing function.** The draft rescinds emergency authority but leaves existing systems pending human process. The precise powers Alpha retains and the immediate practical consequences are open.
16. **The final image and final line.** The draft's three-city infrastructure image is a strong proposal, but the final page, image and wording must be rebuilt to fit the 54-page structure and the visible cost of Alex's choice.
