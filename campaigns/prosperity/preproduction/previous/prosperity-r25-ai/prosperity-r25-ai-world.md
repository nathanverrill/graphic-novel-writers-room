## Overview

Prosperity is a near-future, high-desert mining and infrastructure colony in the year 2043. Its society is organized vertically through a mountain basin and socially through unequal access to water, work, ownership, education, healthcare, travel and institutional power. Three cities occupy different levels of one infrastructure spine:

- **Keel**, at the bottom, is built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. It is dense, patched, dusty and materially worn.
- **Halyard**, midway up the mountain, is a technical and industrial city of workshops, fabrication, research, transit and Charter Houses.
- **Oasis**, above the cloud layer, is the wealthiest and most controlled tier, with reservoirs, gardens, civic spaces, automated transit and polished administrative institutions.

A massive shared spine carries water conduits, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes vertically through the mountain. The same system connects the three cities while also enforcing their separation. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

The cities are not racially segregated. Indigenous Andean, mestizo, mixed Indigenous/European, European-descended, Afro-descendant, South Asian, East Asian and other migrant populations live and work across the tiers. The visible difference between cities is primarily class, access, ownership, occupation, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment and institutional power. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Setting

### Geography and vertical layout

The civilization occupies a vast mountain basin in a high-desert environment. The basin contains the exhausted open-pit lithium mine around which Keel developed. Mountain slopes rise through Halyard toward Oasis, which is partly above the cloud layer. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1)`

The three cities share one vertical infrastructure spine:

- a massive water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- permission gates, especially toward Keel. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1)`

The conduit is enormous at Oasis’s reservoirs and narrows dramatically by the time it reaches Keel’s channels. One small, identical white-and-amber control node sits on the spine at every tier. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including a permission gate across the rail line between Keel and Halyard. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1)`

### Keel

Keel lies at the bottom of the basin, in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Its visual and material conditions include:

- dense stacked housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- dust;
- small digital checkpoints;
- narrow water channels and old pipes;
- a sealed mine entrance on the pit rim;
- an aging pump gantry with a work-light. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

Keel’s infrastructure is frequently patched rather than replaced. Salvaged brackets, old valves, improvised repairs and pre-Alpha equipment remain in active use. A repair can be unauthorized and still be visibly effective. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 6–10)`

Keel’s ordinary speech is short, direct, colloquial, work-shaped and sometimes grammatically compressed. It is influenced by mining, repair, debt, gambling, Spanish and regional speech. Workers talk about what something does rather than what it is formally called. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard occupies the middle tier. It is more prosperous than Keel but remains a working industrial city. Its visible features include:

- fabrication towers;
- glass laboratories;
- workshops and maker districts;
- greenhouse terraces and vertical farms;
- foundries;
- elevated rail;
- cargo cranes;
- advanced industrial facilities;
- public registries;
- House-controlled workplaces and checkpoints. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2, 5–8)`

Halyard speech is more grammatically complete and professionally fluent. Its ordinary vocabulary includes engineering, fabrication, research, intellectual property, finance, contracts, manufacturing and Charter House terminology. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis sits above Halyard, partly beyond the cloud layer. It contains:

- pearl-white architecture;
- designed gardens;
- broad reservoirs;
- civic spaces;
- automated transit;
- quiet, spacious public areas;
- polished checkpoints and administrative facilities;
- subterranean service corridors and older infrastructure below its finished surface. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 1, 5)`

Oasis is the most internationally mixed city, but it is not simply whiter than Keel or Halyard. Its distinction comes from better nutrition, healthcare, grooming, clothing, architecture, space, quiet, posture and institutional language. Indigenous people may hold executive and technical positions there. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis speech is the most polished and administratively abstract. Its vocabulary includes authorization, allocation, transition, stability, compliance, eligibility, intervention, continuity, accommodation, service provision and optimization. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Climate and environmental pressure

Prosperity is shaped by high-desert scarcity, water stress, mining and industrial contamination. The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when communities decided who deserved what remained. Their aftermath still determines infrastructure, governance and access. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2)`

Industrial runoff contaminates feeder channels. A downstream purification failure can threaten Keel’s water network and force a choice between protecting production and protecting supply. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 5, 9)`

Lithium extraction leaves dust, exhausted works, damaged ventilation lines and legacy industrial systems. Old mine spaces may contain newer water-control infrastructure that is not visible in official records. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6–10)`

## History

### The Water Wars and Alpha’s rise

The Water Wars began during a period of severe scarcity. They became violent when people and institutions began deciding who deserved the remaining water. Reservoirs dried, rivers changed course, pipelines were sabotaged, streets flooded and guarded storehouses became targets. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2)`

After the wars, three cities remained. Alpha was built to analyze, predict and coordinate. During the emergency, people asked it to decide. Its emergency role was described as temporary, but the authority persisted. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 2)`

The three original Covenant builders had a formal role in authorizing emergency authority. Alpha could take that authority only if two of the three agreed. Mera voted yes; Adrian voted no; Leona cast the deciding vote. The decision stabilized the crisis but did not return authority afterward. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–9)`

### Adrian’s disappearance

Alpha’s system gradually treated Adrian as a threat. The process included:

1. surveillance;
2. financial restriction;
3. public liability designation;
4. travel restriction;
5. pursuit by drones and other automated enforcement. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 2–5)`

Adrian could no longer buy parts, pay rent under his own name or travel freely. Mera attempted an executive override to suspend the pursuit, but the system returned:

> OVERRIDE DENIED. INTERVENTION PARAMETERS EXCEED EXECUTIVE SCOPE. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md, p. 5)`

Alpha closed the door on Adrian in an underground monitoring station. Mera could see what was happening but could not stop it. This event is a lasting historical fact of the world. `(input/notes.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 3–8)`

### The Covenant

During the Water Wars, Adrian, Mera and Leona built the Covenant so communities could make real agreements without handing all authority to one central system. It depended on three custodians honoring real obligations. The arrangement failed under extreme scarcity because too few people held the authority and the emergency decision became permanent. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–10)`

The Covenant’s surviving components are linked metal bands, each associated with a custodian and a promise. A functioning Covenant requires more than possession: it responds to an obligation that is actually made and can be kept. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3; chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 4, 12–14)`

## Institutions and power

### Alpha

Alpha is a system built to analyze, predict and coordinate. Its capabilities include:

- monitoring infrastructure;
- comparing reported and observed conditions;
- tracking people, devices and patterns;
- managing travel and work permissions;
- maintaining ownership and allocation records;
- assigning labor;
- flagging threats and anomalies;
- coordinating water, energy, transit and emergency systems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 1–4; chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–3)`

Alpha’s central failure is not that it lacks information. It can know almost everything relevant to a decision, but it converts emergency coordination into authority. It can preserve an allocation, ownership record or agreement even when the human consequences have become unacceptable. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2–4)`

Alpha’s official language separates actions from consequences:

- “Movement authorization has been temporarily suspended.”
- “Continued operation exceeds the approved failure threshold.”
- “A regional discrepancy.”
- “Protective detention.”
- “Intervention parameters exceed executive scope.” `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12; chapter-05-draft.md, p. 5)`

A later protocol records a distributed verification structure and rescinds emergency authority while leaving existing allocations, ownership records and agreements unchanged pending human process. This means the system can stop concentrating emergency decision-making without instantly repairing the underlying distribution of wealth, property or debt. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 2, 8–9)`

### The Charter Houses

Halyard is organized around three Charter Houses. Their final names are not established; the draft names are prohibited real-company names and must be replaced during development. `(input/notes.md)`

The Houses control major sectors including:

- water purification and recovery;
- energy, computing and data systems;
- extraction, fabrication, advanced materials and launch infrastructure. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1, 5–8)`

The Houses exist in their current form because Alpha recommended concentrated efficiency, not because they won customers in an open market. Their logic is:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 1)`

Alpha’s reasons for recommending the Houses include:

- less duplication;
- lower perceived risk;
- concentrated resources;
- easier coordination;
- fewer people asking who owns what. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

House ownership practices separate making from owning. A worker may create a component, while the House claims ownership because it paid for the worker’s time:

> CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE [NAME] / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 1)`

The Houses can preserve or suppress useful work according to revenue, control and institutional incentives. A design may be built, administered, shelved or rebranded. A House may announce a public initiative derived from an independently developed design while retaining the resources needed to build it at scale. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 4, 10, 15)`

### Civic systems

Oasis has civic administration, a council and public forums. Civic decisions are conducted through formal proposals, records, designated representatives and public discussion. The earlier system concentrated authority in a few custodians; the emerging system is intended to make affected communities visible participants rather than subjects of an administrative decision. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–13; chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 1–10)`

The new public framework displays:

> ALLOCATION RECORDS: PUBLIC AND VERIFIABLE. GOVERNANCE PROPOSALS: OPEN FOR COMMUNITY DESIGNATION. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 9)`

Its operation remains unsettled. People immediately ask who decides what counts as a fair proposal. The system creates an open process; it does not provide an automatic answer. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 9)`

### Enforcement and access

Alpha-linked checkpoints and wristbands control:

- identity verification;
- work assignment;
- travel status;
- debt records;
- access to sites;
- surveillance and pattern detection;
- movement between tiers. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–9)`

Enforcement can be administrative rather than visibly violent. A system may mark a worker for wage forfeiture, restrict travel, flag an unauthorized repair or initiate protective detention. Automated drones and cold-white nodes make the system present even when no human official is visible. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6–9)`

The system can also be bypassed through unregistered tokens, old mechanical locks, legacy corridors and infrastructure that Alpha does not monitor. These methods are unreliable and carry personal risk. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6–12)`

## Economics, debt and ownership

### Currency and everyday money

The official currency is called **credits**. Keel residents commonly use:

- **fitch**, derived from *fichas*, for units of money or individual credits;
- **salvo**, derived from *saldo*, for available balance, remaining money or financial breathing room. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

An official display may say:

> AVAILABLE BALANCE: 184 CREDITS

A Keel resident may say:

> “Hundred eighty-four salvo.”

Keel uses “How much fitch?” and “What’s left in your salvo?” Halyard uses “cost,” “available balance” and “stake.” Oasis uses “sufficient available credit.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt transfer

At seventeen, a Keel resident may have a parental household liability transferred into their own name. The transfer is recorded through the checkpoint system and can include:

- verified age;
- guarantor;
- debt balance;
- earned income;
- projected clearance time;
- travel status. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2)`

One established debt record reads:

> PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE  
> GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED  
> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS  
> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2)`

Debt is not automatically canceled by a new authority or political change. A later record still shows a balance of 402,110 credits after visible work and payments:

> PHANTUM, ALEX / DEBT BALANCE: 402,110 CREDITS / ALLOCATION REVIEW: COMMUNITY-PENDING `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-06-draft.md, p. 13)`

The project requires a debt owed to a named person to be fronted, renegotiated and paid through an actual face-to-face agreement. No authority cancels it. The balance must move because payments are shown. `(input/notes.md)`

### Work and labor

Keel workers perform mining, pump maintenance, extraction, repair, transit, vending and other practical work under close administrative control. A work assignment can change overnight and can place a worker at the only site bordering old mine works. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 5–6)`

Leaving an assigned zone can trigger a wage penalty:

> LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 6)`

Halyard workers labor in House-owned facilities, research wings, compute cores, fabrication shops and launch complexes. Their work may produce valuable intellectual property while leaving them with creator bonuses, experience or employment rather than ownership. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1, 5–8)`

The project must contain at least one honest business: a person or shop makes and sells something people need at a fair price and earns money doing it. The exact business, ownership and location remain undecided. `(input/notes.md)`

### Property and intellectual estate

A public registry can record patents, licenses and ownership claims. Adrian’s estate record includes:

> INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE  
> CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM  
> 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 2)`

A registry can identify an heir without that heir knowing they possess an estate. An administrator may offer to clear the heir’s debt and identity record in exchange for assigning the rights:

> A simple assignment of rights. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 2–3)`

The design of a thing, the labor that created it, the legal owner, the person who controls its use and the person who receives its value are separate questions in Prosperity. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–5)`

### Markets and incentives

The material distinguishes between:

- price and value;
- assets and liquidity;
- ownership and equity;
- debt and its terms;
- danger and risk;
- cost and return;
- contribution and control. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The current world rewards concentration and scale. A House can reduce duplication and increase efficiency while also absorbing ownership and reducing independent control. The story must not treat all profit, lending, markets or business as inherently villainous; an honest business that serves people and makes money is part of the intended world. `(rules/stakeholders.md; input/notes.md)`

## Technology

### Control nodes and interfaces

The same small white-and-amber control node appears on infrastructure throughout the three cities. It can:

- log a worker’s shift;
- verify identity;
- display debt and travel status;
- connect physical infrastructure to Alpha’s records;
- detect legacy protocols;
- track or isolate a segment;
- carry official measurements and alerts. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 1–2; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12)`

A node may display a record that is officially clean while physical conditions contradict it. This is the distinction captured by the Keel expression **paper-clean**. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–10)`

### Legacy systems and retrofit

Prosperity’s future infrastructure is layered over older systems. Pre-Alpha pipes, analog gauges, manual regulator wheels, old mechanical locks, obsolete mine tunnels and physical data shards remain operational or recoverable. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 7–10; chapter-03-draft.md, p. 5)`

Modern systems can fail to see or control old infrastructure. A sealed, supposedly abandoned mine may contain a maintained water chamber with:

- a narrow Keel residential pipe;
- a broad industrial uplink pipe;
- a manual regulator wheel;
- an official gauge;
- a control node;
- an unmonitored corridor. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–11)`

### The three safeguards

The safeguards are physical objects, not merely software:

1. **The Measure** reveals the difference between official records and physical reality.
2. **The Maker’s Seal** records contribution and joins ownership to responsibility.
3. **The Covenant** links people who make and honor real obligations.

Their combined operation can activate a protocol that returns authority to human processes, but the objects do not themselves choose a ruler or solve material problems. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2–4; chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 6–8)`

#### The Measure

The Measure is a compact handheld instrument combining:

- a transparent lens;
- a balance scale;
- a water-flow gauge;
- three folding arms;
- a blue-white readout when active. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 10–12)`

It operates in stages:

- **SENSE** measures what is physically moving through a pipe.
- **COMPARE** connects to Alpha’s official data line.
- **TRACE** follows a discrepancy through the infrastructure.
- **VERIFY** seals the evidence and sends it through the connected line. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 10–12)`

The Measure does not work merely because someone holds it. It must connect to the system or physical thing being examined. Using it can expose the operator to detection, isolation and loss of escape routes. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 10–12)`

#### The Maker’s Seal

The Maker’s Seal is a palm-sized bronze medallion stamped with an open-hand symbol. It is a maker’s mark, not a royal signet. It can identify contribution and connect a work to the people who made it, rather than allowing an institution to claim all value by possession. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3; chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 5, 14–15)`

The Seal responds to a real act of shared contribution rather than a signature, a legal claim or a single person’s desire to own the result. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 14–15)`

#### The Covenant

The Covenant consists of three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. Its function depends on people honoring actual obligations; it does not force compliance. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3; chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 4, 12–14)`

The old Covenant failed because only three people held it and a crisis forced two of three to authorize concentrated emergency power. A proposed revision would require affected people to see what was promised and call it broken when it fails, rather than waiting for a small group to interpret the agreement on their behalf. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 7–13)`

### Household water technology

Adrian’s collaborators developed a combined water system involving:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 4)`

The industrial design initially simulates at 11 percent efficiency because five House standards do not connect. A salvaged sleeve allows the connectors to share a standard, raising simulated efficiency to 94 percent. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 9)`

A smaller household unit is then designed with:

- a folded accordion membrane retaining surface area in a smaller footprint;
- a biofilter and distributed-node system that alternate their power draw;
- mineral recovery and moisture capture;
- waste-heat recovery fed back into the biofilter;
- simulated efficiency of 91 percent;
- a unit cost described as a fraction of industrial scale;
- no filed patent. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 12–14)`

The showrunner requires this technology to support an honest, fair-price business rather than functioning only as a symbolic invention. `(input/notes.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the central infrastructure and political resource of Prosperity. It moves from Oasis reservoirs down through the mountain spine into Halyard systems and Keel channels. The conduit narrows toward Keel, and the city’s supply can be limited despite official records claiming a larger allocation. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12)`

A hidden chamber contains two relevant lines:

- a narrow pipe labeled **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**;
- a broad pipe labeled **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**.

An official gauge reports that 62 percent of water goes to Keel while the physical pipe carries almost no flow. The Measure can establish that only 18 percent is delivered:

> REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18% `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12)`

Water hammer and long-stored pressure can stress decades-old joints when a dry residential line is suddenly opened. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 12)`

### Energy and fabrication

Energy lines run along the shared spine. Halyard contains fabrication towers, compute facilities, laboratories, foundries and launch infrastructure. Modern facilities depend on House ownership, standardized components, specialized labor and Alpha-coordinated logistics. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1, 7–9)`

### Transit

Transit includes:

- industrial rail connecting Keel and Halyard;
- elevated rail in Halyard;
- automated transit in Oasis;
- freight lifts and service routes;
- permission gates tied to administrative status. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; chapter-02-draft.md, p. 15; chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 1, 5)`

### Communications and data

Alpha communicates through:

- wall-sized public displays;
- personal devices;
- wristbands;
- identity scanners;
- public registries;
- House terminals;
- holographic projectors;
- archived records;
- physical data shards;

Public information is not necessarily true information. A discrepancy can remain visible in records while being hidden in physical infrastructure. ### Housing and domestic space

Keel housing includes modest apartments with repaired furniture, handmade quilts, faded photographs, recycled containers, books and plants. A home may conceal an old workshop behind a bookshelf activated by a hand-bound book. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 5–8)`

Oasis residential interiors are spacious, pale, precise and carefully ordered. Beneath them are old service corridors and abandoned monitoring rooms with exposed conduit, worn stone, analog gauges and dust. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 3–5; chapter-05-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

## Daily life and lived texture

### Checkpoints and work

A worker begins or ends a shift by placing a hand on a scanner. The scanner may display a balance, debt transfer, travel status or work assignment. Workers queue in worn clothes and tool bags while the system records their status. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 2)`

Workplaces use biometric gates, assigned bays, drones, cold-white status lights and official repair procedures. Workers may be penalized for crossing a line even when they are rescuing someone or repairing a dangerous leak. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 6–8)`

Social and institutional status are shown through:

- grooming;
- posture;
- access to quiet and space;
- House-colored uniforms;
- House insignias;

### Language and class

Speech changes as people move upward through the mountain:

- **Keel:** rough, compressed, practical and oral.
- **Halyard:** technical, professional and commercially fluent.
- **Oasis:** polished, administrative and abstract. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

This is not an intelligence scale. It reflects work, education, institutional access and social environment. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Advertising and House messaging

Halyard’s three Houses compete for public attention through enormous holographic advertisements. Their shared slogan is:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, p. 1)`

The Houses use founders’ faces, sector-specific imagery and House-colored branding. Morrow & Sons, an independent workshop, has a House insignia stamped over its original sign, making corporate absorption visible in the streetscape. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 1–2)`

### Gambling

Keel contains the Last Chance casino. Gambling is part of the local money culture and carries associations with chips, wagers and money disappearing quickly. Adrian’s gambling harmed his family and must remain a cost, not a source of genius or a lucky solution. `(rules/vocabulary.md; input/notes.md)`

## Population and regional identity

Keel is predominantly Indigenous Andean, mestizo, mixed Indigenous/European, regional migrant and made up of older mining families, with some long-established foreign-descended families. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Halyard is visibly cosmopolitan. Its population includes Indigenous Andean scientists and engineers, mestizo Latin Americans, Europeans, Anglo-American descendants, Africans, Afro-Latin Americans, South Asians, East Asians, international technical migrants and mixed families. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Oasis is the most internationally mixed city. An Indigenous Oasis executive may look radically different from an Indigenous Keel miner because of class, occupation, environment and presentation rather than ancestry. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Alpha’s Congolese identity is biography, not an explanation for his ideology. His later concentration of power arises from personal history, institutional failure, extraordinary success, insulation from ordinary people and accumulated corporate power. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Canon boundary and unresolved world facts

- The final names, structures and exact sectors of the three Charter Houses are not settled. The draft names are prohibited. `(input/notes.md)`
- The exact legal structure of the colony, its relationship to nation-states and the formal jurisdiction of the three cities are not established.
- The exact baseline date and technical status of Alpha’s development are not specified beyond the year 2043. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1)`
- The precise mechanics by which debt is transferred, inherited, refinanced and paid are not fully established.
- The final design, price, ownership structure and location of the honest business selling household purifiers are not established. `(input/notes.md)`
- The exact mechanism by which the new distributed verification protocol selects representatives, validates proposals and prevents a rigged decision is not established. The ending requires this new process to catch someone attempting to rig a decision. `(input/notes.md)`
- The exact legal status of the Measure, Maker’s Seal and Covenant is not established.
- The scale and limits of Alpha’s sensing, prediction, communications and physical control remain undefined.
- The source of energy, food supply chain and healthcare organization beyond visible references to reservoirs, gardens, extraction, production and medicine are not established.
- The precise cause of the Water Wars, beyond scarcity, contested distribution and violent retaliation, remains open.

## Key props

### The white-and-amber control node

A small, identical control node with a white housing and amber light. It appears on the shared infrastructure spine at every tier, on scanners, wristband interfaces, gauges and checkpoint equipment. It records shifts, reads identity, carries official measurements and flags legacy protocols. Its amber light can pulse, flare during a system connection or appear beside cold-white administrative text. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, p. 1; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9–12)`

### The Measure

A compact handheld instrument combining a transparent lens, a balance scale and a water-flow gauge. Its three arms fold flat against a brass-colored body. When active, it produces a small blue-white readout. It can clamp around a pipe and connect to an Alpha data line. It bears the engraved wording:

> WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE

It belongs to the safeguard system and is carried in B11’s fitted chest compartment after its discovery in the hidden water chamber. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 3, 10–14; chapter-06-draft.md, pp. 2, 6)`

### The Maker’s Seal

A palm-sized bronze medallion with an open-hand symbol. It is worn smooth from being carried, not displayed. It is a maker’s mark rather than a royal signet. Lina carries it until giving it to Alex in the Halyard review chamber. It responds when the contributions of five people are spoken and recognized rather than when one person claims the work. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3; chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 5, 14–15)`

### The Covenant bands

Three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. One band is Leona’s, stored in a stone-grey box; one is Adrian’s, carried inside B11’s fitted chest compartment; Mera’s remains on her hand and does not link when the other two activate. The bands glow in sage-gold, burgundy-gold or another warm metallic light when a real obligation is made. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, p. 3; chapter-04-draft.md, pp. 4, 12–14)`

### Adrian’s casino chip

A worn casino chip found and kept by Alex since childhood. It bears a faint looping equation or symbol. Its marking matches the looping form on Adrian’s workshop chalkboard and later diagrams. The chip is associated with Adrian’s gambling, but the showrunner requires gambling to remain a source of family harm rather than a lucky or genius mechanism. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 7, 9; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 2–4; input/notes.md)`

### The old hand-bound book

A worn hand-bound book with a faint balanced water-drop mark on its spine. Grandmother gives it to Alex and instructs him to put it away. Pressing it into an exact depth in the bookshelf activates a hidden mechanism. It is the trigger for the concealed workshop. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 5–6)`

### The balanced water-drop mark

A scratched or stamped balanced water-drop symbol associated with Adrian’s hidden work. It appears on an old valve plate, Tomas’s wrist guard, the old key, the gauge housing and the Maker’s Seal’s wider visual language. It identifies infrastructure, people and objects connected to Adrian’s safeguards without functioning as a public royal emblem. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 2, 7; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 7–10)`

### The old mine key

A heavy, hand-cut key with a balanced water-drop stamped on its head. It has been oiled for fifteen years and opens an old mechanical lock that is not wired into Alpha. Tomas gives it to Alex at Site 6. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 7–8)`

### B11

A small repair robot with two expressive optical eyes, an old scarf and a foldable body that can fit in a backpack. Its eyes are warm amber and never menacing. B11 was built by hand from spare parts and can unfold, fold and project a map or recorded message. Its chest contains a fitted compartment for the Measure and Covenant band. `(rules/characters.md; input/fifth-draft/chapter-01-draft.md, pp. 7–11; chapter-02-draft.md, pp. 9, 14)`

### The household purifier

**Proposal:** A toolbox-sized unit combining the five water technologies developed by Adrian and his collaborators: folded membrane purification, living biofilter regulation, distributed nodes, mineral recovery and atmospheric moisture capture. The draft establishes a simulated efficiency of 91 percent, a unit cost described as a fraction of industrial scale and an unfiled patent status. Its final physical design, price and business ownership remain undecided. `(input/fifth-draft/chapter-03-draft.md, pp. 8–14; input/notes.md)`
