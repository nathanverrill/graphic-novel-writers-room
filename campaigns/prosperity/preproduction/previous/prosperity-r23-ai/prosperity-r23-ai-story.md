# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type:** explicit decisions, showrunner notes, and idea drafts.

The book is a near-future graphic narrative about Alex Phantum, a seventeen-year-old Keel worker who inherits a crushing debt, a hidden workshop, and the unfinished work of his absent father, Adrian. Alex follows Adrian's trail through the three-tier civilization of Keel, Halyard, and Oasis, discovering how Alpha's emergency authority became permanent, how concentrated ownership obscured both labor and resource allocation, and how people might reclaim decision-making without replacing one concentrated authority with another.

The story should be experienced through people needing, using, and paying for the world's systems, not through lectures. The book must not explain its lessons directly: no history lecture, definitions of the safeguards, recited triplets, governance speech, or caption stating the theme. `(input/notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can Alex refuse the power to fix everything himself and help build a way for the people affected by decisions to see, question, and share in them?

The emotional trajectory moves from inheritance and resentment toward chosen responsibility:

- Alex begins wanting to leave Keel and escape a debt and history he did not choose.
- He discovers that Adrian left him more than liability: evidence, collaborators, tools, and a question.
- He is repeatedly offered shortcuts that would protect him while leaving others exposed.
- He learns that truth, ownership, and promises matter only when people can see them and hold one another to them.
- He ultimately refuses executive authority over Alpha and proposes distributed human decision-making instead.
- The ending must cost him something visible. His refusal of power cannot be free. `(input/notes.md)`

The book should also show:

- a named person fronting Alex something real and Alex repaying that person under agreed terms;
- an honest business that makes and sells something people need at a fair price;
- Bi11bot speaking in Bill Reynolds' plain, questioning, self-deprecating voice, including a moment when he is wrong and admits it;
- gambling as a source of family damage, not as Adrian's genius laboratory or Alex's lucky solution;
- Lock as a serious opponent with a concrete offer a seventeen-year-old would consider;
- Alex and Ada as friends, with no romantic coding;
- the difference between a business that earns customers and Houses that exist because Alpha selected them;
- systems learned through consequences rather than instruction. `(input/notes.md)`

The audience is 15–18-year-old students who did not choose the book, are skeptical of anything that feels artificial, and will abandon scenes they cannot follow on a first read. The immediate dramatic objective must remain legible even when the larger system is complicated. `(rules/reader.md)`

The story's language rises with Alex's movement from Keel to Halyard to Oasis, but this is not an intelligence scale. Alex and Ada describe visible facts and human burdens rather than using civic or academic abstractions. Bi11bot translates choices into questions about odds, ownership, value, risk, and consequences. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Premise

In 2043, the three cities of Prosperity share one mountain infrastructure spine:

- Keel occupies the basin around an exhausted lithium mine.
- Halyard is the technical and manufacturing tier.
- Oasis is the wealthy administrative tier above the clouds.
- A single water, energy, transit, and control infrastructure connects them.

After the Water Wars, Alpha was built to analyze, predict, and coordinate. During the emergency, frightened people allowed it to decide. Its emergency authority became a permanent system of allocation, ownership, labor assignment, debt, and travel restriction. Three Charter Houses dominate Halyard because Alpha selected them for efficiency and scale. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Alex's seventeenth birthday transfers his father's unresolved household liability to him:

> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS  
> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

He is a mechanically capable Keel worker who trusts vibration, pressure, wear, and physical evidence more than official records. His father, Adrian, disappeared after gambling damaged the family and after Alpha progressively restricted his finances, movement, and ability to obtain help. Adrian also built hidden safeguards against concentrated authority:

1. **The Measure**, which reveals what is actually happening and exposes discrepancies between official records and delivered reality.
2. **The Maker's Seal**, which recognizes contribution and joins ownership to responsibility.
3. **The Covenant**, which links people through visible obligations and promises.

Together, these safeguards can activate a restoration protocol. The eventual intended use is not to transfer Alpha's authority to Alex, Ada, Mera, or any single executive, but to create a distributed structure in which decisions are visible, contributors are named, and authority is shared by people selected by the communities affected by it. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Story So Far

### Issue 01 — Keel

**Status:** material exists as the detailed draft called “Chapter One,” but the showrunner has fixed the format as an 18-page issue, not a chapter. The draft currently supplies 12 pages. The issue must end on a strong personal cliffhanger, not merely a systems alert. The final three pages must be the descent from Keel into the old mine works and the ascent inside the water conduit; the last page must show the pipe beginning to fill behind Alex and Ada. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

#### Opening: debt and inheritance

1. The story opens on a person, not a theme caption. The current detailed draft opens on Alex at work in a Keel pump hall before dawn. He feels a pump's vibration with one palm and has made an unapproved repair from salvaged parts. His supervisor says, “That's not the approved repair.” Alex answers, “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” `(input/notes.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

2. At the end of his shift, Alex passes the checkpoint. His balance is publicly displayed as the largest in the queue. His parental household liability transfers to him at age seventeen. The guard says, “Happy birthday, Phantum.” Alex answers, “I've had better surprises.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

3. Alex passes the shuttered casino **The Last Chance**, whose lights still flicker. A bouncer invokes Adrian's luck. Alex replies, “I've also got his debt.” The bouncer says Adrian always believed the next hand would change everything. Alex answers, “It did. Just not for him.” The chip in Alex's pocket is visible but not yet understood. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

4. At the rim of Keel, Alex and Ada meet as friends. Ada brings him a drink and refuses to treat it as a debt: “You don't owe anybody anything today.” Alex says, “Everything has a price.” Ada answers, “Not everything.” They discuss his desire to leave and her desire to understand why people stopped trying to change Keel. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

5. Alex explains that his grandmother sometimes calls him Adrian and tells stories claiming Adrian was working rather than gambling. Alex remembers nothing of Adrian; he was two. Ada points out that everything he knows is what people told him. Her final thought in the sequence is: “He wouldn't just leave.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### The workshop and Bi11bot

6. At home, Alex's grandmother gives him a worn, hand-bound book and asks him to put it away. The book activates a hidden mechanism in the family's bookshelf. Behind it is Adrian's dust-covered workshop. A child-sized shape sits beneath a dust sheet beside a metallic key. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

7. Alex enters the workshop. The room contains blueprints, water-system diagrams, mechanical models, pre-Alpha pipes, analog gauges, and a chalkboard equation matching the etching on Alex's casino chip. The concealed figure has a faint amber pulse. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

8. Alex inserts the key into the figure's chest. Bi11bot awakens. He has two expressive optical eyes, an old scarf, a foldable body, and a calm, amused presence. He is never threatening. His first words are: “Well. That took longer than expected.” `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

9. Bi11bot recognizes Alex as Adrian's son. Alex challenges his claim that he was “in the room.” Bi11bot notices the chip. He says Adrian bet Alex would throw it in a river; Bi11bot bet he would not. Alex admits he thought about it. Bi11bot answers, “Thinking about it isn't doing it.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

10. Bi11bot projects Adrian's message. Adrian says Alex deserves answers, but they must be earned. He tells Alex that Bi11bot has a map, and that Alex should trust Bi11bot but question everything, especially Adrian. The recording makes Adrian emotionally present while keeping his history incomplete. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Site 6 and the first discovery

11. The map points to a sealed part of the old mine beneath Keel. Alex cannot leave Keel because of his travel restriction, but Bi11bot says, “Sealed isn't the same as gone.” The first node lies under his feet. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

12. Alpha detects the legacy protocol. In the current draft, an Oasis operator sees:

> B1 NODE ACTIVE  
> LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED  
> SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR  
> [ REPORT TO ALPHA ] [ TRACK ]

The operator chooses **TRACK**. The sequence establishes that Alex's activation has been noticed, but the final issue cliffhanger must be developed around a person the reader cares about rather than ending only on surveillance. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

13. Alex is reassigned to Keel Extraction Site 6, adjacent to the sealed old works. Ada helps him prepare. She gives him a folded page from Adrian's plans marked at every third junction and warns him to look for what does not belong. Alex chooses the unknown route over his predictable 28.7-year sentence. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

14. At Site 6, Alex hides the foldable Bi11bot in his tool bag. A support cable snaps and pins Tomas, an older Indigenous Andean miner who knew Adrian. Alex could remain forgettable, but crosses the assigned-zone line to rescue Tomas. His wristband records:

> LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR

Bi11bot warns that the plan needed Alex to stay forgettable. Alex answers, “Then we need a different plan.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

15. Alex frees Tomas and patches a leaking ventilation line with a salvaged bracket. Tomas recognizes Bi11bot as **B1** and reveals that Adrian came to Site 6, asked what the miners needed, and asked Tomas to keep one door for whoever came with B1. Tomas gives Alex an old, unconnected key and creates a distraction so Alex can enter the bulkhead. He stays behind, accepting the personal risk of making the escape look accidental. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

16. Inside the old works, Alex follows a recently oiled left-hand route rather than Alpha's straight survey. Security drones sweep the mapped tunnel. Bi11bot's presence helps them evade detection. Alex finds condensation and a large pipe in a supposedly dead mine. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

17. The hidden chamber contains two water lines:

- **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**, narrow, corroded, nearly dry;
- **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**, broad, maintained, and carrying water.

An official gauge claims that 62% of the water goes to Keel. Alex tests the pipes directly and finds the record and reality disagree. A worn balanced-water-drop mark links the instrument to Adrian. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

18. Alex retrieves the Measure. Its functions are introduced through action, not definition: **SENSE** reads what is physically moving; **COMPARE** connects the reading to Alpha's official data; **TRACE** follows the discrepancy; **VERIFY** seals the evidence. The Measure reveals that Keel receives 18% rather than the reported 62%. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

19. Alex must choose between using the last open corridor to map an escape route for himself or transmitting the evidence to Keel. Sending the evidence will close the route, leave him trapped and expose him to Alpha. He sends it. Keel displays update simultaneously:

> REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%

The chamber's dry Keel line begins to fill. The tunnels seal behind Alex and Bi11bot. Alex says, “Truth doesn't need authorization.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

20. Miners, including Tomas, reach the chamber. They have seen the numbers and understand the material evidence. They force the manual regulator open together. Tomas says, “Then it can offend all of us,” when Foreman-7 calls their obstruction a household offense. Keel workers provide Alex with food, a token, a credential, and a route out. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

21. Alex returns briefly to his grandmother. Ada has organized the escape supplies. Alex promises to return. Ada decides to go with him, explicitly as his friend and as someone who will investigate who decided Keel had to remain this way. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

22. **Required ending direction:** The current draft uses a train to reach Halyard, but the binding decision rejects that. The crossing must descend from Keel into the old mine works and then climb inside the water conduit that the Measure proved was running nearly empty. The last three pages of Issue 01 are this climb. The final page must show the pipe beginning to fill behind Alex and Ada, leaving a personal question involving someone the reader cares about. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

### Issue 02 — Halyard

**Status:** the detailed draft material currently appears as “Chapter Three” and continues the Halyard story. It contains 16 pages. The exact 18-page issue arrangement is not yet settled. The issue should retain the following causal sequence and discoveries. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Arrival and the Charter Houses

1. Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot emerge into Halyard, a city of suspended rails, fabrication towers, laboratories, vertical farms, and foundries. Three House insignias dominate separate skyline clusters. The language and material conditions are more professional and technically fluent than Keel, but workers still pass through systems of ownership and access. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

2. The three Houses are currently named **Aqualis**, **Helion**, and **Astra** in the draft. The showrunner has ruled that real company names cannot be used; the Houses must have invented names. The draft names therefore require replacement before canon. `(input/notes.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

3. Holographic ads claim:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

Ada observes that three families run almost everything. Alex asks whether Alpha allowed that. Bi11bot answers, “Allowed it. Recommended it.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

4. At a contribution kiosk, an engineer's component is recorded as:

> CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS

Alex asks whether the House owns what she made. Bi11bot explains that the House paid for her time and claims what she made with it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

5. The group passes a shuttered workshop marked **Morrow & Sons — Inventors Since 2024**, its independent sign overwritten by a House insignia. This is the visible contrast between House scale and independent ownership. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### Adrian's estate and Lock's offer

6. In the Halyard Public Registry, Alex's identity band reveals:

> PHANTUM, ADRIAN  
> INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE  
> CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM  
> 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED

Alex is shocked that Adrian left an intellectual estate when he believed Adrian left only debt. Ada says the estate may belong to Alex; Bi11bot calls Adrian's belongings “badly labeled.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

7. Director Cassian Lock approaches Alex. He is a polished international corporate administrator and a real opponent, not merely a villain. He offers:

- immediate clearance of the debt attached to Alex's name;
- a Halyard position;
- no questions about what happened in Keel;
- administrative transfer of Adrian's estate and rights.

Lock says, “Your father's work could clear every debt attached to your name.” Ada asks what he wants in exchange. Lock answers, “A simple assignment of rights.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

8. The offer is serious because it would protect Alex and his collaborators from immediate consequences. It is dangerous because the estate and its value would become House property, possibly built or shelved according to House incentives. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### The five collaborators

9. Adrian's archive contains a cluster of water-related inventions: catalytic membrane purification, atmospheric moisture capture, mineral recovery, living biofilter regulation, and distributed community water nodes. Four collaborators hold pieces outside the system. A Maker's Seal symbol appears within the archive. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

10. Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot find Dr. Lina Vey at an Aqualis purification facility. Lina is Adrian's collaborator and a membrane chemist whose contribution was obscured by institutional ownership. A contaminated feeder channel threatens Keel's water network. Lina explains that the Houses each own a different part of a possible solution and would rather own the failure than share the answer. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

11. Lina gives Bi11bot a physical data shard. She says Adrian made each collaborator keep one piece off the system because Halyard confused protecting an idea with owning the person who had it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

12. Lina leads them to Suri Anik in a biofilter wing. Ada notices Suri is the only person still working at a neglected bench. Suri gives them a shard containing the living biofilter. She stayed because someone had to keep it alive until the others caught up. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

13. Jalen Orr gives them the distributed water-node shard inside a Helion compute facility. He warns that security watches patterns rather than people. His design routes supply around any single point of failure, preventing one House or Alpha from choking a city by cutting one line. He refuses to travel with them because he believes Lock's surveillance has already noticed the group. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

14. At Astra's extraction and launch complex, Ren Okafor provides the final two pieces: mineral recovery and atmospheric capture. The completed design combines five fragments into a purification and recovery system. Ren says Adrian was not building a machine but an argument: “Five Houses' worth of stolen credit could still add up to something none of us owns alone.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### The prototype and second offer

15. Alex, Ada, Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren, and Bi11bot work for three nights. The first combined simulation fails at 11% because the five House standards do not connect. Alex ignores the specifications, tests the physical connectors, and realizes the problem is threading: each piece was designed to lock rather than share. He makes a salvaged sleeve that allows the components to work together. Efficiency rises to 94%. Bi11bot says, “Well now. There you are.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

16. Lock arrives with a second offer. He has traced the collaborators through pattern surveillance. A Charter House review will determine the estate's disposition at dawn. If Alex signs, the review will not expose the four collaborators. If he refuses, their names and unauthorized removal of restricted intellectual property will be found. The review countdown begins at 07:14:00. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

17. Alex considers signing. He recognizes that the offer would clear the debt and protect people. Ada identifies the deeper issue: Lock is asking Alex to decide who owns work made by five people. Alex asks Bi11bot to contact the collaborators rather than deciding alone. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

18. The group meets before dawn. Jalen calls refusal a “death wish with extra steps.” Lina says Lock may be right about immediate safety. Ren says disappearing from the record is what has already happened to them. Alex proposes shrinking the system until it is cheap enough that no House will bother claiming it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

19. The group makes a household-scale purifier:

- Lina folds the membrane into accordion pleats to preserve surface area.
- Suri and Jalen alternate the biofilter and distributed-node draw.
- Ren recovers waste heat from mineral recovery and moisture capture and feeds it back into the system.
- Alex supplies the practical connector and scale logic.

The unit reaches 91% simulated efficiency, has a fraction of industrial unit cost, and has no patent filed. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

20. At the Charter House review, Alex refuses to transfer Adrian's estate. He names Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren as co-builders, releases the design without a patent, and says anyone who needs clean water and can afford a toolbox should be able to build it. Lina gives him the Maker's Seal, which activates for the five names and their shared contribution. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

21. Lock immediately identifies the limitation. Aqualis can build and distribute its own version faster and wider than five people in a rented room. The collaborators remain employed but are quietly disadvantaged: Jalen is reassigned, Suri's wing is reduced, and Lina and Ren return to constrained work. The House has not been defeated; the group has made one idea harder to own exclusively. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

22. The issue's closing sequence should establish Mera Vale directly as the remaining custodian and watcher. She receives a report that Alex has the Measure and has activated the Maker's Seal. Her standing order is not to intervene yet. She says, “Two found. He'll want the third from me directly.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Issue 03 — Oasis and the fourth choice

**Status:** the detailed draft material currently appears as “Chapter Four,” “Chapter Five,” and “Chapter Six.” It contains the proposed ending trajectory, but far more than the fixed 18-page issue length can contain. The causal spine is present; page allocation, cuts, and compression remain open. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

#### Finding Leona and the Covenant

1. Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot enter Oasis. The city is quiet, polished, and spacious rather than simply “whiter.” Alex's temporary pass requires escort. Ada's gate record identifies her as **VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED**. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

2. The public archive identifies the third Covenant custodian as **Leona Veyra**, Ada's mother, listed as active in Oasis civic administration. Ada had believed Leona disappeared. The record proves she is alive. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

3. Leona receives Ada. She has preserved photographs, a childhood object, and her Covenant band. Ada asks why Leona did not come for her. Leona admits she knew where Ada was within a year and chose not to return, claiming she believed Ada was safer without knowing Leona was looking. Ada answers, “Those aren't the same thing.” `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

4. Mera arrives. She has watched Ada's life and claims she kept her safe because Leona promised to protect her. Ada rejects the substitution of surveillance for relationship. Bi11bot tells Alex that Mera is dangerous because she is not entirely wrong. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

5. Leona reveals the Covenant's history. She, Adrian, and Mera built it during the Water Wars so communities could make agreements without handing everything to one authority. During a worse shortage, Alpha could take emergency authority if two of the three agreed. Mera voted yes. Adrian voted no. Leona was the deciding vote. She believed the emergency authority would bring Ada back quickly; it lasted fifteen years. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

6. Leona acknowledges that she traded a few terrible days she could see for fifteen years she could not. Mera maintains that the authorization prevented more deaths. The conflict is not reduced to one person being wholly right: Mera preserved order, but the system never gave authority back. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

7. Ada proposes that a Covenant should not work because three important people agree. It should work only when the people affected can see what was promised and call it broken immediately. Leona agrees to stand behind the proposal publicly. Alex finally enters the room rather than waiting at the threshold. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

8. Bi11bot reveals that he has carried Adrian's Covenant band since the night Adrian finished the workshop. He was waiting for Alex to have something worth doing with it. Alex places Adrian's band beside Leona's. The two link, but Mera's band remains dark. The condition “two of three” repeats, now as a warning rather than a solution. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

#### Adrian's disappearance

9. Mera takes Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot beneath Oasis to an old water-monitoring station outside Alpha's routing system. The station is the room where Adrian made his final recording. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

10. Mera shows what Alpha did to Adrian:

- surveillance;
- financial restrictions that prevented him from buying parts or paying rent under his own name;
- a public liability designation that caused people to stop helping him;
- pursuit by drones.

She insists she did not order it. She watched it happen. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

11. An archival override shows Mera trying to suspend Alpha's stability intervention against Adrian. Alpha denies the override because the intervention exceeded her executive scope. Mera says, “I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing.” `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

12. A recovered recording shows Adrian trapped in the station as a bulkhead closes and water rises. He has less than an hour. Rather than calculating an escape, he activates Bi11bot. The scene connects directly to the awakening in Issue 01. Adrian's final choice is not a casino bet, though his gambling had genuinely harmed his family. He bets his life on someone eventually proving the safeguards worth using, specifically Alex. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

13. Alex reframes his inheritance. He has thought of it as Adrian's debt, then his patents, then his enemies. He concludes that what he inherited was a decision, not an answer. Ada tells him he does not have to decide which version of Adrian was the real one: a father who left and a person who gave everything to a future can both be true. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

#### The restoration protocol and Alex's refusal

14. In a civic hall, Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot bring the Measure, the Maker's Seal, and the linked Covenant together. A restoration protocol appears on every screen:

> RESTORATION PROTOCOL: THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED IN PROXIMITY  
> CONSTITUTIONAL TRANSFER CONDITIONS SATISFIED  
> AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY

15. Mera offers Alex full executive authority over Alpha's governing systems. Alex imagines what he could do immediately: erase Keel's debt, equalize water, dismantle House dominance, protect Ada, and secure his grandmother. He considers asking Ada to share the authority, but Ada refuses that as well: two people deciding for everyone else is still too much power. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

16. Alex asks Bi11bot to walk through the safeguards again:

- the Measure reveals without deciding;
- the Seal recognizes contribution without controlling;
- the Covenant coordinates obligations without ruling.

Alex proposes applying the same design to Alpha:

- every decision about water, labor, travel, and other essential systems must be visible to the people affected;
- contributors must be named;
- no single authority decides;
- multiple people chosen by affected communities must agree and act.

He calls this his own design, not Adrian's. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

17. Alpha registers the proposed structure:

> DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL  
> EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED  
> EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS

Nothing is magically fixed. Keel's debt remains. House ownership remains. The difference is that records and proposals become visible and subject to human processes. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

18. The people in the civic hall begin arguing over how the new process should work. Leona publicly supports affected-city representation. Mera sits among ordinary residents rather than above them. Neither is redeemed by a speech; both are now exposed to continuing human disagreement. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

19. Alex and Ada discuss whether he will leave. Alex now has the formal ability to travel, but Ada says she never wanted him to stay for Keel; she wanted it to be his choice. He remains beside her at the railing without declaring that the problem is solved. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

20. In the proposed epilogue, Alex returns to Keel weeks later. His debt has fallen from 418,607 to 402,110 credits because payments have been shown, but it remains his. Ada says nobody promised a clean slate, only an honest one. Tomas helps review public allocation data. Grandmother gives Alex the cloth she has been folding and stops folding it herself. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

21. The final image echoes the opening three-city composition. Keel, Halyard, and Oasis remain unequal and imperfect, but the infrastructure spine is visibly connected by open data. The open mine entrance and raised permission gate suggest movement is possible. The intended final idea is not victory but an open process: there is now a door nobody can quietly close. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

2. Alex turns seventeen; the liability transfers to him and travel becomes restricted.
3. His grandmother gives him Adrian's book, which opens the workshop.
4. Alex awakens Bi11bot and receives Adrian's map and challenge.
5. Alpha detects the legacy protocol and tracks Alex.
6. Alex is reassigned to Site 6, creating access to the old works.
7. Tomas is injured; Alex chooses to rescue him, sacrificing wages and secrecy.
8. Tomas opens the old door because Alex's choice proves him to be the person Adrian described.
9. The Measure exposes the discrepancy between reported and delivered water.
10. Alex chooses public truth over a private escape route; the tunnels close and Keel receives evidence.
11. Keel workers help Alex leave, creating the first visible reciprocity around his journey.
12. In Halyard, Adrian's estate and Lock's offer present Alex with a second shortcut: personal security in exchange for collective ownership.
13. Alex gathers Adrian's collaborators instead of signing alone.
14. The collaborators' incompatible House-owned fragments fail until Alex makes a shared connector.
15. Alex refuses the transfer, publicly names the collaborators, and activates the Maker's Seal.
16. Lock's Houses absorb and outscale the release, proving that public attribution alone does not solve ownership or production.
17. Mera's records lead Alex and Ada to Leona and the Covenant.
18. Leona's confession reveals that the original Covenant failed because two of three frightened people could concentrate emergency authority.
19. Adrian's final recording reveals that Alpha turned its authority against him and that Bi11bot was activated as Adrian's last choice.
20. The three safeguards activate the restoration protocol.
21. Mera offers Alex the authority he has imagined wanting.
22. Ada prevents the “two good people” version of concentrated power.
23. Alex designs a distributed protocol modeled on the safeguards.
24. Alpha rescinds emergency authority but leaves existing ownership, debts, and allocations intact.
25. The people must now build the new process themselves. Alex's debt remains, but the record is visible and his payments count.

## Character Movement Through the Story

### Alex

Alex begins as a seventeen-year-old who wants to leave Keel, distrusts official records, and sees his inherited debt as a sentence. His first consequential choice is to cross the line and rescue Tomas, choosing a person over his escape plan. The Measure then forces him to choose between private survival and public evidence; he sends the truth to Keel and accepts being trapped.

In Halyard, Alex is tempted by Lock's offer to erase his debt and protect the people around him. He gradually understands that the estate is not his alone and refuses to sign it away. He learns to build with other people's contributions rather than claiming the solution as his inheritance.

In Oasis, Alex confronts the most dangerous version of his own desire: the power to fix everything instantly. He first imagines using Alpha's authority for good, then realizes that benevolent concentration remains concentration. His final choice is to refuse executive control and design a distributed process. The ending leaves him with an honest debt, continued work, and a choice about whether to stay or leave.

### Ada

Ada begins as Alex's friend, someone who notices obligations, fear, avoidance, and what people refuse to say. She challenges his assumption that leaving is the only meaningful form of change. She helps him prepare for Site 6 and goes with him because she wants to understand who decided Keel had to stay as it was.

In Halyard, she sees the human cost behind technical efficiency and helps identify that Lock's offer is not merely about Alex's debt. In Oasis, she discovers that Leona abandoned her while claiming to protect her. She refuses an easy reconciliation and asks Leona to show, rather than tell, that she will change.

Ada also refuses Alex's suggestion that the two of them should share executive authority. Her insistence that “the moment anyone has to trust me that much, something's already gone wrong” gives Alex the final correction he needs. She remains his friend and intellectual counterweight, not a romantic partner.

### Bi11bot

Bi11bot begins as Adrian's quiet wager and Alex's guide into the hidden story. He asks questions about stakes, ownership, risk, and downside, but must never become a lecturer or replacement for people. He carries Adrian's Covenant band and eventually admits that he has wondered for fifteen years whether Adrian was right about Alex.

He should tell short stories about Adrian and his own mistakes, speak plainly, and at least once be wrong, say so, and ask Alex what he would do. His key movement is from presenting Adrian's prepared route to witnessing Alex make a choice Adrian did not design: the distributed structure for Alpha.

### Mera

Mera begins as the controlled watcher in Oasis who tracks Alex and waits. She believes Alpha's concentration of authority preserved civilization and that her own decision during the Water Wars was necessary. She offers Alex executive authority because she still thinks the problem may be solved by choosing the right holder.

Her history with Adrian and Leona makes her partly responsible for the system and partly trapped by it. She cannot stop Alpha from pursuing Adrian, despite being its executive authority. By the ending, she relinquishes her central position and sits among ordinary people, but she is not redeemed or absolved. She must live inside the messy process she once tried to prevent.

### Leona

Leona begins as a preserved custodian who has hidden behind safety and administrative order. She abandoned Ada while believing she was protecting her. She acknowledges that safety was not agency and agrees to publicly support Ada's redesigned Covenant. Her movement is not forgiveness but re-entry: she becomes one participant in a process rather than a distant guardian.

### Lock

Lock begins as a calm, controlled representative of the House system. His offers are materially attractive and morally complicated: he can clear Alex's debt and protect collaborators, but only by transferring Adrian's estate and collective work into House administration. After Alex refuses, Lock demonstrates that the Houses can outscale and appropriate public solutions without needing to arrest anyone. He remains a structural opponent at the ending.

### Tomas

Tomas is the Keel miner who knew Adrian and keeps a door for Alex. Alex's choice to save him proves that Alex is the person Tomas has been waiting for. Tomas then risks punishment to create Alex's escape. Later he helps review public allocation data, showing that reciprocity is not a mystical principle or one-time favor but a continuing practice.

## Stakes

### Personal

- Alex inherits 418,607 credits of liability.
- His projected clearance time is 28.7 years.
- His travel status is restricted.
- He risks losing wages, clearance, and physical safety by entering Site 6.
- He risks his grandmother's care and his own ability to repay what he owes.
- Lock offers to erase the debt in exchange for control of Adrian's estate.
- The ending must visibly cost Alex something. `(input/notes.md)`

### Relational

- Alex must decide whether to believe Adrian's message and Bi11bot.
- His grandmother's memory and stories preserve a contradictory image of Adrian.
- Ada's friendship with Alex must survive danger and disagreement without becoming romance.
- Tomas risks his position to help Alex.
- Adrian's collaborators risk employment, safety, and future access by sharing their work.
- Ada confronts Leona's abandonment and demands action rather than explanation.
- Bi11bot carries the burden of Adrian's final trust.

### Institutional

- Keel's water allocation is reported as 62% but delivered as 18%.
- Alpha has converted emergency authority into permanent allocation, ownership, labor, and travel control.
- The Houses own contributions made by workers and may shelve inventions that threaten revenue.
- The Charter House review can expose and punish Adrian's collaborators.
- Public attribution does not by itself prevent concentrated ownership.
- The new distributed protocol does not erase existing debts, ownership, or allocations.

### Wider

- Keel's water network is failing.
- Halyard's industrial system depends on concentrated House ownership.
- Oasis's order rests on decisions that were never returned to the people affected.
- The story's final choice determines whether Alpha continues deciding or whether humans undertake the harder work of visible, shared decision-making.

## Ending

The detailed material establishes an ending in which Alex refuses Mera's offer of full executive authority over Alpha. He does not erase Keel's debt, dissolve the Houses, or make every allocation equal by command. Instead, he proposes a distributed verification protocol based on the three safeguards:

- visibility of what is happening;
- attribution of who contributed;
- shared obligations that affected people can inspect and challenge.

Alpha rescinds emergency authority but leaves existing allocations, ownership records, agreements, and debts unchanged pending human process. The ending therefore changes the rules of visibility and decision-making without pretending that structural problems disappear overnight.

The intended emotional destination is an open but difficult future. Alex's final position is not “I fixed it,” but that nobody should be able to keep deciding invisibly or alone. Ada remains beside him by choice, not because Keel has trapped either of them. The final visual echoes the opening three-city mountain, but the infrastructure spine is now visibly connected by open data.

The exact ending pages, the visible cost of Alex's refusal, and the personal cliffhanger for Issue 01 remain subject to the 54-page issue structure. The existing six-chapter drafts currently contain substantially more than 54 pages and cannot all be retained at their present page count.
