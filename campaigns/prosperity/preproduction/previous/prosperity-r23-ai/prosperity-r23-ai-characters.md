# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- The cast must reflect Prosperity as an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration and global capital. Ancestry must cut across class, access, education, occupation, dress, health and institutional power. Ethnicity must never function as shorthand for intelligence, morality, competence, poverty, wealth, political position, or support for Alpha. **FIXED** `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- Alex and Adrian must visibly read as father and son while Alex also visibly carries substantial ancestry from his unrevealed Indigenous Andean mother. That appearance is a story clue, not a subject for explanatory dialogue. **FIXED** `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. The book never reveals anything about her beyond that fact. **FIXED** `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- Alex and Ada are friends. Nothing in the art or dialogue should read as romance. **FIXED** `(input/notes.md)`
- Bi11bot is never a threatening or menacing figure, and he is never a butler, lecturer or replacement for human relationships. His personality, questions, stories, humour and beliefs come from Bill Reynolds, filtered through Adrian's workshop, engineering, gambling and financial literacy. **FIXED** `(rules/characters.md, rules/stakeholders.md, rules/vocabulary.md)`
- TJ is introduced early in Issue 01 at Site 6, where Bi11bot assembled him from spare parts to reach places nobody else can. **FIXED** `(rules/showrunner-rules.md, rules/characters.md)`
- Dialogue should reveal the vertical movement from Keel's compressed, practical language to Halyard's technical and commercial fluency to Oasis's polished institutional speech. This is a class and institutional gradient, not an intelligence scale. **FIXED** `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- The story should show a person making and keeping a real obligation: Alex fronts something from a named person, fails to pay on time, faces that person directly, agrees to terms, and visibly reduces the balance through payments. No authority cancels the debt. **FIXED** `(input/notes.md)`
- The story should include an honest business that makes and sells something people need at a fair price. It should contrast with Charter Houses that exist because Alpha selected them rather than because they won customers. **FIXED** `(input/notes.md)`
- Lock must be a real opponent with a concrete offer a seventeen-year-old would seriously consider and a genuine case for the Houses. Refusing him must cost Alex. **FIXED** `(input/notes.md)`
- Adrian's gambling hurt his family. The casino is not a laboratory, lucky charm or genius move. **FIXED** `(input/notes.md)`
- The ending must make Alex's refusal of concentrated power cost him something visible, and a new way of deciding must be demonstrated rather than explained. **FIXED** `(input/notes.md)`
- The book is published in issues, not chapters. The supplied drafts are evidence and are not the final book. **FIXED** `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## Characters

### Alex Phantum

**Role:** Seventeen-year-old Keel worker; mechanically capable pump and mine worker; Adrian Phantum's son; inheritor of Adrian's debt and disputed intellectual estate; eventual bearer of the Measure, the Maker's Seal and Adrian's Covenant band. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md, drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Look:** Alex is lean, mechanically capable and visibly work-worn. He wears worn mining clothes and carries a weathered tool bag. His hands are scarred and work-rough. He has a habitual head tilt when listening to vibration or testing machinery. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. His father, Adrian, is European-descended; his mother should be understood as Indigenous Andean, probably from a community native to the fictional basin or surrounding region, but the exact community is not established. Alex should visibly carry traits from both family lines, look clearly related to Adrian, and not simply look like a younger version of him. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

His visual inheritance is intentionally a question: the audience should believe he is Adrian's son while also seeing that a substantial part of his appearance comes from someone else. Nothing in the book explains this immediately. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

Recurring visual anchors include:

- a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation, found when Alex was six and repeatedly turned in his pocket;
- Adrian's creased photograph, later smoothed flat;
- a carved or stamped balanced water-drop mark appearing on his wrist guard and on objects associated with Adrian;
- a weathered tool bag;
- the wristband that records his debt, travel restriction, wage deductions and later public status;
- the Measure, eventually stored in Bi11bot's fitted brass chest compartment;
- a plain unregistered token used to leave Keel;
- the worn cloth from his grandmother's apartment, which he eventually accepts and puts away rather than keeping in his hand. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md, drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Alex speaks in short, dry, concrete lines. He is skeptical, mechanical and mildly abrasive when defensive. He describes evidence rather than principles and trusts vibration, pressure, wear, physical evidence and what people actually do more than labels or official descriptions. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

His Keel vocabulary includes **clean**, **hot**, **burned**, **crooked**, **thin**, **solid**, **patched**, **ghost**, **paper-clean**, **fitch** and **salvo**. He talks about what something does rather than its formal designation. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

He does not naturally use civic, academic or bureaucratic language such as “governance,” “legitimacy,” “stakeholder,” “systemic,” “institutional” or “public accountability.” If such language appears, it should be something he is mocking, repeating or reading rather than his normal vocabulary. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who they are:** Alex wants to leave Keel and choose his own future. At the beginning, his personal freedom and escape are more legible to him than collective responsibility. He distrusts official records because he sees the difference between “paper-clean” and what machinery, pipes and people actually show. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, rules/vocabulary.md)`

He is self-reliant but not indifferent. His defining pressure test is whether he will preserve his own plan or stop for another person. At Site 6, he abandons the plan of remaining forgettable and crosses an assigned-zone line to rescue Tomas, accepting a wage loss and an adverse report. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Alex is not naturally a speechmaker. He learns through physical evidence and consequential choices. He tests the Measure by feeling pipes and fittings as he tests pumps; he recognizes that the water records are false because the Keel pipe is nearly dry while the industrial line is moving; and he solves the collaborators' failed prototype by handling the connectors rather than merely reading the specifications. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

He is capable of being tempted by power. When offered authority over Alpha, he imagines clearing Keel's debts, equalizing water, protecting his grandmother and Ada, and removing the Houses' control. He recognizes that doing it himself would make him another concentrated authority. Ada's refusal to let “you and me” become the answer forces him to see that distributing power is different from sharing it with one trusted person. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian Phantum:** Alex's absent father. Alex was two when Adrian disappeared and initially knows him through family stories, debt, the casino's reputation, a photograph and inherited objects. Alex begins by treating Adrian primarily as the source of a crushing liability and possibly as a gambler who abandoned his family. He later learns that Adrian built with collaborators, left behind safeguards and made a final decision that protected the possibility of Alex's future. Alex does not have to decide that Adrian was only a good father or only a gambler; he can miss a father who left and recognize the value of what Adrian chose. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-03-draft.md, drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Alex's mother:** She left Adrian after he lost everything. The book never reveals anything else about her. **FIXED.** `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **Grandmother Phantum:** Alex's caregiver and home anchor. Her memory sometimes fails; she calls him Adrian, tells stories about Adrian and gives Alex access to the hidden workshop. She has preserved Adrian's book, photographs and workshop for years. Alex is gentle and patient with her even when her stories unsettle him. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- **Ada Veyra:** Alex's friend and closest human companion. Their dynamic is affectionate, teasing, practical and emotionally direct, never romantic. Ada notices his obligations and evasions; Alex notices physical evidence and machinery. She brings him coffee, challenges his assumptions, marks Adrian's map from memory and organizes Keel's support network. She is willing to correct him when his solution enlarges rather than reduces concentrated power. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-06-draft.md, input/notes.md)`
- **Bi11bot:** Mentor, witness and inherited connection to Adrian, but not a substitute for people. Alex initially questions him and eventually trusts him enough to ask him to contact the four Halyard collaborators. Their relationship changes when Bi11bot admits uncertainty about whether Adrian was right and when Alex physically rests a hand on his shoulder. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Tomas:** A Keel miner who knew Adrian. Alex earns Tomas's help not by inheriting it but by stopping to rescue him. Tomas tells Alex that Adrian asked him to keep a door for whoever came with B1. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Director Cassian Lock:** Alex's institutional opponent. Lock offers to clear Alex's debt, restore his name and give him a place in Halyard in exchange for assigning Adrian's estate rights to administration. Alex takes the offer seriously because it would help him and protect his collaborators, but rejects it because the work was made with others and is not his alone to transfer. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- **Mera Vale:** Mera offers Alex executive authority over Alpha because she sees what he did in Halyard and believes he may avoid her failure. Alex identifies that her offer repeats the old mistake by placing the decision in one person's hands. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **Ada's family:** Alex respects Ada's need to confront Leona without intruding. He waits outside when Ada enters Leona's apartment, then later crosses the threshold when Ada says the next part is his to be present for. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Alex tests objects with his palm and head tilt. He turns the casino chip without noticing. When defensive, he folds his arms, tightens his jaw and leans into an armored stance. He often goes silent rather than explain an emotion. He reaches toward people and objects, then stops himself when he recognizes that the choice is theirs. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

He prioritizes immediate needs concretely: when his available 52 credits cannot cover both his grandmother's medicine and the token required for travel, he chooses medicine first and intends to earn the rest. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**History:** Alex was two when Adrian disappeared. His grandmother raised him in Keel. He found Adrian's casino chip at age six. At seventeen, parental household liability transferred to him: the recorded debt was 418,607 credits, with 28.7 years projected to clear it and travel status restricted. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Alex works around patched infrastructure and is willing to use an unauthorized repair when it works. He is assigned to Keel Extraction Site 6, where he encounters Tomas, the old mine works, the concealed water diversion and the Measure. He publishes evidence that Keel receives 18% of the water officially recorded as 62%, sacrificing the unmapped route out. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

In Halyard, Alex discovers that Adrian's estate contains 312 patents, including 47 restricted and 19 disputed, and that Adrian's work was built with Lina Vey, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr and Ren Okafor. He helps turn their industrial design into a household-scale purifier rather than signing the estate to a House. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Alex's actions repeatedly establish that his identity is made through choices rather than inherited labels:

- He repairs a pump with a salvaged bracket and refuses to pretend that an approved repair matters more than a working one. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- He enters Adrian's workshop despite fear, wakes Bi11bot and accepts the map's first route. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- He chooses medicine for his grandmother before the token needed for escape. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- He crosses the assigned-zone line to save Tomas, accepting a 40-credit wage forfeiture. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- He sends the Measure's evidence to Keel rather than using its last trace to secure a private escape. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- He refuses Lock's offer despite its immediate relief and the threat to his collaborators. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- He credits four collaborators publicly and rejects sole ownership of the household purifier. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- He refuses Mera's offer of full executive authority over Alpha and proposes distributed verification, visible attribution and multiple community-designated decision-makers instead. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- He leaves his debt on the books rather than accepting a false clean slate. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Alex begins as a technically competent seventeen-year-old focused on escape from Keel, convinced that a predictable personal sentence is the central problem. He becomes someone who accepts obligations that are not strictly his own: first by facing Tomas and keeping a debt or promise honestly, then by giving up a private route to expose Keel's water diversion, then by refusing to transfer collaborative work to a House. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

His established endpoint is not triumph or personal freedom. He refuses concentrated authority even when it could solve visible problems quickly, accepts that Keel's debt and the Houses' ownership remain, and chooses an open process in which affected people can see and contest decisions. He has moved from “I want out” toward “people should get a say,” without becoming a civic lecturer or abandoning his desire to leave. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Ada Veyra

**Role:** Alex's friend; Keel-raised organizer and practical partner in the journey; daughter of Leona Veyra; connected to the Covenant's history. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Look:** Ada is twenty in the supplied draft. She has long, wind-tangled copper hair, freckles and light teal-blue eyes. Her features should reflect strong Andean ancestry as well as mixed European/criolla ancestry; she should read as unmistakably local to the region while retaining the established copper hair, freckles and eye color. She should visibly resemble Leona without looking ethnically interchangeable with Alex. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, rules/ancestry.md)`

Her clothing includes a dark high-collared top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her stance is steady and rooted. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Recurring visual anchors include the rivet on her strap, which she touches under stress or while thinking, Adrian's folded map marked with ink ticks, a travel pack and later the Covenant bands. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Voice:** Ada shares Keel's compressed, colloquial vocabulary but is less mechanical than Alex. She notices obligations, avoidance, fear, imbalance, hidden motives and what people refuse to say. She speaks plainly and can be emotionally perceptive without sounding therapeutic or academic. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Her vocabulary includes **clean**, **crooked**, **square**, **tied**, **loose**, **carrying**, **showing** and **full cup**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “Not everything.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “I look down here—and wonder why everyone gave up trying to change it.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Nobody stays either. Not really.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “What if they were wrong?” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Then you don't go in blind.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “You're not leaving Keel behind. You're finding out who decided it had to stay this way.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “That’s not what you're mad about.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “That's still two people deciding for everyone else.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “Not because I don't trust myself. Because the moment anyone has to trust me that much, something's already gone wrong with how we built it.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “I want a say in it. Those aren't the same thing.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

She does not naturally use civic or institutional abstractions. She describes what people owe, hide, choose and carry. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who she is:** Ada is attentive to the human cost of decisions. She believes that a person should not make a choice for others merely because they are trusted or competent. She is more willing than Alex to name the interpersonal burden underneath a practical dispute.

She wants Keel to stop being treated as a place that must remain unchanged. Her first stated wish is not simply to escape: she looks down at Keel and wonders why everyone gave up trying to change it. She is also unwilling to treat leaving as the same thing as helping. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Ada is not naïvely anti-institutional. She recognizes when people act under pressure and accepts that Leona's choices may have prevented worse immediate harm, but she refuses to confuse safety with agency or explanation with repair. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Alex:** Friend, confidante and corrective. She teases him, brings him coffee, reads his silences and helps him plan without taking over his choices. She supports his movement toward responsibility but challenges his instinct to turn responsibility into personal authority. Their friendship is not romantic. `(input/notes.md, drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **Leona Veyra:** Mother and source of a fifteen-year wound. Ada believed Leona had disappeared, then discovers that Leona knew where she was and chose not to return. Ada does not accept “I chose to keep you safe” as an answer to “You chose not to come back.” She permits Leona to keep trying but does not offer immediate forgiveness. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Mera Vale:** Ada sees Mera's protection as another form of control. She can recognize that Mera helped preserve a city while still rejecting the claim that this makes Mera right about Ada's life. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **Alex's grandmother:** Ada is comfortable in the Phantum household and helps organize Alex's departure while respecting the grandmother's place in it. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **Bi11bot:** She questions him, notices when he is holding back information and accepts his practical help without treating him as an authority above scrutiny. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Ada touches or presses the rivet on her shoulder strap when thinking or unsettled. She watches people's hands and faces, not only their words. She lets silences sit. She steps between Alex and a threat when necessary, but also deliberately stays outside Leona's apartment until Ada herself chooses to invite Alex into the conversation. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

She can organize under pressure. During Alex's departure from Keel, she checks the map, food and credential, removes vulnerable notebooks, arranges a packed bag and coordinates people willing to open a temporary route. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

**History:** Ada grew up in Keel and shares its vocabulary and practical knowledge. Her mother, Leona, disappeared from her life when Ada was five during the Water Wars. Ada believed she had been abandoned or orphaned until the Oasis records revealed that Leona was alive and had known where she was within a year. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

Ada's family history connects her to the Covenant. Leona was one of its three original builders, alongside Adrian and Mera, and carried one linked band. Ada has inherited both the family wound and the unresolved question of whether an agreement can protect people without concentrating authority in a few trusted hands. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Ada brings Alex coffee, challenges his escape fantasy, helps decode Adrian's route and marks the map from memory. She notices that the only open route into the old works is probably intentional. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

She recognizes the human significance of the Halyard collaborators and the danger of Lock's offer. She asks whether the estate belongs to Alex alone and supports his decision to credit Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

With Leona, Ada refuses a symbolic apology and says, “Don't tell me. Show me.” She proposes a Covenant that answers to the people affected by it, makes promises visible and allows those people to call an agreement broken immediately. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

When Alex considers accepting Alpha's authority, Ada refuses to become his co-ruler. She insists that two trusted people deciding for everyone else is still the same structural mistake. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Arc:** Ada begins as someone who believes Keel has given up and who wants to understand why. She becomes the clearest advocate for agency without concentrated power. Her personal arc is not simple reconciliation with Leona; it is the establishment of terms under which Leona may continue trying.

Her established endpoint is choosing for herself whether to stay or leave. She does not ask Alex to stay in Keel, and she does not accept anyone else choosing her future in the name of safety. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Bi11bot

**Role:** Small workshop robot built by Adrian; Alex's inherited guide, witness and companion. The identifier is always **B11** in the book. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md, input/notes.md)`

**Look:** B11 has two expressive optical eyes. He can fold up and fit in a backpack. He wears an old scarf. His eyes carry the character: wise, patient and often quietly amused. He is never drawn as menacing: no red or harsh eye light, looming low angles, face lost in shadow or predatory posture. When scenes are tense, tension is around him rather than in him. `(rules/characters.md)`

He is small enough to hide in Alex's tool bag and can unfold from a compact form. His shoulder projector creates soft-blue holograms. His chest contains a fitted brass compartment for the Measure and, earlier, Adrian's linked Covenant band. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**Voice:** B11 speaks in a dry, economical, patient and precise voice with warmth underneath. He asks a question before offering an interpretation and tells short stories from his long life with Adrian. His tone is plain, a little roundabout and Western, with jokes mostly at his own expense. He is not a lecturer, but he can introduce financial concepts when the immediate choice makes them necessary. `(input/notes.md, rules/stakeholders.md, rules/vocabulary.md)`

His vocabulary draws from engineering, probability, gambling and financial literacy:

- “Within tolerance.”
- “Long odds.”
- “Different failure mode.”
- “Noise isn't always error.”
- “Who carries the downside?”
- “Price isn't value.”
- “That's the ante.”
- “Who owns it?”
- “What are you giving up?”
- “Valuable isn't the same as spendable.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

He teaches through **concrete example → question → concept**, not definition followed by moral. He asks who owns something, who gets paid, who carries the risk, what happens if someone is wrong and what the downside is. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Useful established lines include:

- “Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “Thinking about it isn't doing it. Now, shall we say hello to your father?” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

- “Your father always said certainty was where mistakes began.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
- “The tunnels are gone. That was the price.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Lock's offer clears the debt. It doesn't clear anything else.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`
- “You are trying to save what has already been spent.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “A debt is a tool. Tools become dangerous when you don't understand which end you're holding.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- “I don't know yet. I know it isn't nothing.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- “Which is why I'm not choosing.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- “Your father built three objects that each refuse to decide alone.” `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

**Who he is:** B11's personality, character and beliefs are Bill Reynolds's: patient, questioning, financially literate, willing to admit mistakes, interested in responsibility and consequences, and hostile to lecturing. He is a mentor but not an answer machine. **FIXED.** `(rules/stakeholders.md, input/notes.md)`

He should tell stories of his own mistakes with Adrian and, at least once, be wrong, say so plainly and ask Alex what he would do. `(input/notes.md)`

B11 understands the distinctions between price and value, debt and liability, ownership and control, risk and danger, and assets and liquidity. He uses those distinctions to sharpen a character's choice rather than to deliver a lesson. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Adrian:** Adrian built B11 and entrusted him with the workshop, the map, the Covenant band and the wager that Alex would become capable of choosing. B11 was present for Adrian's last moments but was not fully awake; he clearly remembers only the final seconds. He has carried the emotional burden of wondering for fifteen years whether Adrian was right about Alex. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`
- **Alex:** B11 is Alex's guide but deliberately does not choose for him. He presents the consequences of both routes through the Measure, refuses to make the decision, and later witnesses Alex's “fourth choice.” His trust in Alex grows from guarded observation to visible pride. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **Ada:** B11 recognizes her as someone Alex trusts and asks Alex whether she should stay before disclosing the next stage of Adrian's plan. He respects her judgment and gives her space during the meeting with Leona. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **TJ:** B11 built TJ from spare parts to solve problems B11 could not solve alone. `(rules/characters.md)`
- **Mera:** B11 has carried Adrian's history of Alpha and understands that Mera's authority and guilt are connected to Adrian's disappearance. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

**Habits and tells:** B11's head tilts and lens ring clicks when he is focusing or deciding how much to say. He tracks people with his eyes. He can fold into a tool bag and present himself as scrap when entering Site 6. In emotionally significant moments he becomes unusually still rather than theatrical. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, drafts/chapter-02-draft.md, drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

He occasionally uses a dry joke to reduce pressure, but he does not turn danger into a performance. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md, rules/characters.md)`

**History:** Adrian built B11 in the hidden workshop and prepared him to guide Alex to the first waypoint. B11's own final memory of Adrian is the workshop flooding, the bulkhead sealing and Adrian choosing to finish the transmission and wake B11 rather than escape. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

B11 has carried Adrian's Covenant band for fifteen years, waiting until Alex had something worth doing with it. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

**In existing drafts:** B11 wakes after Alex inserts the key into his chest and immediately identifies that Alex kept the chip.
