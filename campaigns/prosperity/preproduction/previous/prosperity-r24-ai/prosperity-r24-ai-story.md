# Prosperity — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type:** explicit decisions, showrunner notes, and idea drafts.

The book is a near-future graphic narrative about Alex Phantum, a seventeen-year-old Keel worker who inherits a crushing debt, a hidden workshop, and the unfinished work of his absent father, Adrian. Alex follows Adrian's trail through the three-tier civilization of Keel, Halyard, and Oasis, discovering how Alpha's emergency authority became permanent, how concentrated ownership obscured both labor and resource allocation, and how people might reclaim decision-making without replacing one concentrated authority with another.

The story should be experienced through people needing, using, and paying for the world's systems, not through lectures. The book must not explain its lessons directly: no history lecture, definitions of the safeguards, recited triplets, governance speech, or caption stating the theme. `(input/notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can Alex refuse the power to fix everything himself and help build a way for the people affected by decisions to see, question, and share in them?

The emotional trajectory moves from inheritance and resentment toward chosen responsibility:

- Alex begins wanting to leave Keel and escape a debt and history he did not choose.
- He discovers that Adrian left him more than liability: evidence, collaborators, tools, and a question.
- He is repeatedly offered shortcuts that would protect him while leaving others exposed.
- He learns that truth, ownership, and promises matter only when people can see them and hold one another to them.
- He ultimately refuses executive authority over Alpha and proposes distributed human decision-making instead.
- The ending must cost him something visible. His refusal of power cannot be free. `(input/notes.md)`

The book must also show:

- a named person fronting Alex medicine and a travel token;
- Alex missing the first repayment, facing that person directly, agreeing to fixed repayment terms, and visibly reducing the balance through later payments;
- an honest business that makes and sells something people need at a fair price;
- Bi11bot speaking in Bill Reynolds' plain, questioning, self-deprecating voice, including a moment when he is wrong and admits it;
- gambling as a source of family damage, not as Adrian's genius laboratory or Alex's lucky solution;
- Lock as a serious opponent with a concrete offer a seventeen-year-old would consider;
- Alex and Ada as friends, with no romantic coding;
- the difference between a business that earns customers and Houses that exist because Alpha selected them;
- systems learned through consequences rather than instruction. `(input/notes.md)`

The audience is 15–18-year-old students who did not choose the book, are skeptical of anything that feels artificial, and will abandon scenes they cannot follow on a first read. The immediate dramatic objective must remain legible even when the larger system is complicated. `(rules/reader.md)`

The story's language rises with Alex's movement from Keel to Halyard to Oasis, but this is not an intelligence scale. Alex and Ada describe visible facts and human burdens rather than using civic or academic abstractions. Bi11bot translates choices into questions about odds, ownership, value, risk, and consequences. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

## Premise

In 2043, the three cities of Prosperity share one mountain infrastructure spine:

- Keel occupies the basin around an exhausted original lithium open pit. Satellite and underground recovery sites, including Site 6, remain economically active.
- Halyard is the technical and manufacturing tier.
- Oasis is the wealthy administrative tier above the clouds.
- A single water, energy, transit, and control infrastructure connects them.

After the Water Wars, Alpha was built to analyze, predict, and coordinate. During the decisive 2028 crisis, frightened people allowed it to decide. Its emergency authority became a permanent system of allocation, ownership, labor assignment, debt, and travel restriction. Three separate Charter corporations were selected by Alpha under a common inter-House compact. They compete commercially but share registry, review and enforcement procedures. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

Alex's seventeenth birthday transfers his father's unresolved household liability to him. This is a legally registered household-guarantor claim: Adrian's household obligations attach to the designated adult successor at seventeen. The balance includes principal, accumulated interest and administrative penalties. Wages can be garnished, and travel remains restricted while the account is delinquent.

> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS  
> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

Alex is a mechanically capable Keel worker who trusts vibration, pressure, wear, and physical evidence more than official records. His father, Adrian, disappeared in 2028 after gambling damaged the family and after Alpha progressively restricted his finances, movement, and ability to obtain help. Adrian also built hidden safeguards against concentrated authority:

1. **The Measure**, which reveals what is actually happening and exposes discrepancies between official records and delivered reality.
2. **The Maker's Seal**, which recognizes contribution and joins ownership to responsibility.
3. **The Covenant**, which links people through visible obligations and promises.

Together, these safeguards can activate a restoration protocol. The eventual intended use is not to transfer Alpha's authority to Alex, Ada, Mera, or any single executive, but to create a distributed structure in which decisions are visible, contributors are named, and authority is shared by people selected by the communities affected by it. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Story So Far

### Issue 01 — Keel

**Status:** Issue 01 is eighteen pages. The final three pages are the descent from Keel into the old mine works and the climb inside the water conduit. The last page shows the pipe beginning to fill behind Alex and Ada. Issue 02 opens in a Halyard storm drain. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`

#### Opening: debt and inheritance

1. The story opens on a person, not a theme caption. Alex works in a Keel pump hall before dawn. He feels a pump's vibration with one palm and has made an unapproved repair from salvaged parts. His supervisor says, “That's not the approved repair.” Alex answers, “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” `(input/notes.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

2. At the end of his shift, Alex passes the checkpoint. His balance is publicly displayed as the largest in the queue. His parental household liability transfers to him at age seventeen. The guard says, “Happy birthday, Phantum.” Alex answers, “I've had better surprises.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

3. Alex passes the shuttered casino **The Last Chance**, whose lights still flicker. A bouncer invokes Adrian's luck. Alex replies, “I've also got his debt.” The bouncer says Adrian always believed the next hand would change everything. Alex answers, “It did. Just not for him.” The chip in Alex's pocket is visible but not yet understood. Adrian's gambling is presented as a genuine destructive habit that caused debt, instability and family harm. The chip is evidence of that history, not a lucky charm. Later probability work is sober engineering, not an extension of casino play. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

4. At the rim of Keel, Alex and Ada meet as friends. Ada brings him a drink and medicine for his grandmother, and fronts him a travel token from her own salvo. She says the token is not a gift: he can repay her in fixed installments after his next wages arrive. Alex agrees. She refuses to treat the drink as a debt: “You don't owe anybody anything today.” Alex says, “Everything has a price.” Ada answers, “Not everything.” They discuss his desire to leave and her desire to understand why people stopped trying to change Keel. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

5. Alex explains that his grandmother sometimes calls him Adrian and tells stories claiming Adrian was working rather than gambling. Alex remembers nothing of Adrian; he was two. Ada points out that everything he knows is what people told him. Her final thought in the sequence is: “He wouldn't just leave.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### The workshop and B11

6. At home, Alex's grandmother gives him a worn, hand-bound book and asks him to put it away. The book activates a hidden mechanism in the family's bookshelf. Behind it is Adrian's dust-covered workshop. A child-sized shape sits beneath a dust sheet beside a metallic key. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

7. Alex enters the workshop. The room contains blueprints, water-system diagrams, mechanical models, pre-Alpha pipes, analog gauges, and a chalkboard equation matching the etching on Alex's casino chip. The concealed figure has a faint amber pulse. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

8. Alex inserts the key into the figure's chest. B11 awakens. He has two expressive optical eyes, an old scarf, a foldable body, and a calm, amused presence. He is never threatening. His first words are: “Well. That took longer than expected.” `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

9. B11 recognizes Alex as Adrian's son. Alex challenges his claim that he was “in the room.” B11 notices the chip. He says Adrian bet Alex would throw it in a river; B11 bet he would not. Alex admits he thought about it. B11 answers, “Thinking about it isn't doing it.” `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

10. B11 projects Adrian's message. Adrian says Alex deserves answers, but they must be earned. He tells Alex that B11 has a map, and that Alex should trust B11 but question everything, especially Adrian. The recording makes Adrian emotionally present while keeping his history incomplete. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

#### Site 6 and the first discovery

11. The map points to a sealed part of the old mine beneath Keel. Alex cannot leave Keel because of his travel restriction, but B11 says, “Sealed isn't the same as gone.” The first node lies under his feet. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

12. Alpha detects the legacy protocol. An Oasis operator sees:

> B11 NODE ACTIVE  
> LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED  
> SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR  
> [ REPORT TO ALPHA ] [ TRACK ]

The operator chooses **TRACK**. Mera is the named human executive responsible for Alpha's scope and personally selects Track, but Alpha independently classifies the protocol and executes standing monitoring rules. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; story.md; open-items.md, item 24)`

13. Alex is reassigned to Keel Extraction Site 6, adjacent to the sealed old works. Ada helps him prepare. She gives him a folded page from Adrian's plans marked at every third junction and warns him to look for what does not belong. Alex has promised to repay Ada for the token and medicine from his next wages, but the Site 6 reassignment and wage forfeiture will make the first payment impossible. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

14. At Site 6, Alex hides the foldable B11 in his tool bag. After a support cable begins to fail, B11 assembles TJ from discarded maintenance magnets and inspection parts. TJ is a small salvaged repair robot dog. B11 sends TJ's head through a gap to locate Tomas and inspect the jammed release mechanism. TJ does not replace Alex's choice: Alex still has to cross the assigned-zone line and take responsibility for the rescue. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/characters.md; open-items.md, item 4)`

15. A support cable snaps and pins Tomas, an older Indigenous Andean miner who knew Adrian. Alex could remain forgettable, but crosses the assigned-zone line to rescue Tomas. His wristband records:

> LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR

B11 warns that the plan needed Alex to stay forgettable. Alex answers, “Then we need a different plan.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

16. TJ reaches the jammed release mechanism through a gap too narrow for Alex's hand. Alex frees Tomas and patches a leaking ventilation line with a salvaged bracket. Tomas recognizes B11 as **B11** and reveals that Adrian came to Site 6, asked what the miners needed, and asked Tomas to keep one door for whoever came with B11. Tomas gives Alex an old, unconnected key and creates a distraction so Alex can enter the bulkhead. He stays behind, accepting the personal risk of making the escape look accidental. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/characters.md)`

17. Inside the old works, Alex follows a recently oiled left-hand route rather than Alpha's straight survey. Security drones sweep the mapped tunnel. TJ's head watches the patrol from a pipe junction while B11 projects a false maintenance signal. Alex finds condensation and a large pipe in a supposedly dead mine. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; rules/characters.md)`

18. The hidden chamber contains two water lines:

- **KEEL RESIDENTIAL**, narrow, corroded, nearly dry;
- **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**, broad, maintained, and carrying water.

An official gauge claims that 62% of the water goes to Keel. Alex tests the pipes directly and finds the record and reality disagree. A worn balanced-water-drop mark links the instrument to Adrian. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

19. Alex retrieves the Measure. Its functions are introduced through action, not definition: **SENSE** reads what is physically moving; **COMPARE** connects the reading to Alpha's official data; **TRACE** follows the discrepancy; **VERIFY** seals the evidence. The Measure reveals that Keel receives 18% rather than the reported 62%. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

20. Alex must choose between using the last open corridor to map an escape route for himself or transmitting the evidence to Keel. Sending the evidence will close the route, leave him trapped and expose him to Alpha. He sends it. Keel displays update simultaneously:

> REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%

The chamber's dry Keel line begins to fill. The tunnels seal behind Alex and B11. Alex says, “Truth doesn't need authorization.” `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

21. Miners, including Tomas, reach the chamber. They have seen the numbers and understand the material evidence. They force the manual regulator open together. Tomas says, “Then it can offend all of us,” when Foreman-7 calls their obstruction a household offense. Keel workers provide Alex with food, a token, a credential, and a route out. The additional token does not erase Alex's personal obligation to Ada. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

22. Alex returns briefly to his grandmother. Ada has organized the escape supplies. Alex apologizes that he cannot make the first payment. Ada makes him face her directly rather than letting him turn the missed payment into silence. He agrees to pay a fixed amount from every wage payment and to show her the balance. Ada decides to go with him, explicitly as his friend and as someone who will investigate who decided Keel had to remain this way. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

23. **Issue 01 ending:** Alex and Ada descend from Keel into the old mine works, then climb inside the water conduit that the Measure proved was running nearly empty. The last page shows the pipe beginning to fill behind them. Tomas's signal, still below the closing works, cuts out as the water rises. Alex has to enter Halyard without knowing whether Tomas escaped. The cliffhanger is a question about Tomas, not a systems alert. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; open-items.md, item 3)`

### Issue 02 — Halyard

**Status:** Issue 02 is eighteen pages, pages 19–36. It opens in a Halyard storm drain. The issue moves from the House system and Lock's offer through the collaborators' shared build, the Maker's Seal and Mera's direct claim that Alex needs the third safeguard. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; open-items.md, item 2)`

#### Arrival and the Charter Houses

1. Alex, Ada, and B11 emerge from the water conduit into a Halyard storm drain. Halyard is a city of suspended rails, fabrication towers, laboratories, vertical farms, and foundries. Three House insignias dominate separate skyline clusters. The language and material conditions are more professional and technically fluent than Keel, but workers still pass through systems of ownership and access. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

2. The three Houses are **Caldera House**, associated with water and purification; **Vector House**, associated with compute and energy; and **Orbital House**, associated with extraction, launch and advanced materials. They are separate chartered corporations selected by Alpha under a common inter-House compact. They compete commercially but share registry, review and enforcement procedures. `(input/notes.md; open-items.md, items 5–6)`

3. Holographic ads claim:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

Ada observes that three families run almost everything. Alex asks whether Alpha allowed that. B11 answers, “Allowed it. Recommended it.” The Houses exist because Alpha selected them for efficiency and scale, not because they first won customers. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

4. At a contribution kiosk, an engineer's component is recorded as:

> CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: VECTOR HOUSE / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS

Alex asks whether the House owns what she made. B11 explains that the House paid for her time and claims what she made with it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

5. The group passes a reopened **Morrow & Sons** shop in Keel-facing Halyard, whose independent sign was once overwritten by a House insignia. The shop now repairs pumps and sells maintained household filters at a posted fair price. A customer pays for a replacement cartridge; the owner records the cost, the worker's wage and a modest margin. The shop earns money by serving customers rather than by receiving an Alpha-selected charter. This is the visible contrast between an honest business and the Houses. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; input/notes.md; open-items.md, item 9)`

#### Adrian's estate and Lock's offer

6. In the Halyard Public Registry, Alex's identity band reveals:

> PHANTUM, ADRIAN  
> INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE  
> CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM  
> 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED

Alex is shocked that Adrian left an intellectual estate when he believed Adrian left only debt. Ada says the estate may belong to Alex; B11 calls Adrian's belongings “badly labeled.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

7. Director Cassian Lock approaches Alex. Lock is director of the shared Charter Compact, empowered to represent all three Houses in estate review and inter-House disputes. He cannot personally own the estate but can initiate the process and offer House-backed terms. `(open-items.md, item 25)`

8. Lock offers:

- immediate clearance of the debt attached to Alex's name;
- a Halyard position;
- no questions about what happened in Keel;
- administrative transfer of Adrian's estate and rights.

Lock says, “Your father's work could clear every debt attached to your name.” Ada asks what he wants in exchange. Lock answers, “A simple assignment of rights.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

9. The offer is serious because it would protect Alex and his collaborators from immediate consequences. It is dangerous because the estate and its value would become House property, possibly built or shelved according to House incentives. Alex considers signing. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### The five collaborators

10. Adrian's archive contains a cluster of water-related inventions: catalytic membrane purification, atmospheric moisture capture, mineral recovery, living biofilter regulation, and distributed community water nodes. Four collaborators hold pieces outside the system. A Maker's Seal symbol appears within the archive. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

11. Alex, Ada, and B11 find Dr. Lina Vey at a Caldera purification facility. Lina is Adrian's collaborator and a membrane chemist whose contribution was obscured by institutional ownership. A contaminated feeder channel threatens Keel's water network. Lina explains that the Houses each own a different part of a possible solution and would rather own the failure than share the answer. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

12. Lina gives B11 a physical data shard. She says Adrian made each collaborator keep one piece off the system because Halyard confused protecting an idea with owning the person who had it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

13. Lina leads them to Suri Anik in a biofilter wing. Ada notices Suri is the only person still working at a neglected bench. Suri gives them a shard containing the living biofilter. She stayed because someone had to keep it alive until the others caught up. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

14. Jalen Orr gives them the distributed water-node shard inside a Vector compute facility. He warns that security watches patterns rather than people. His design routes supply around any single point of failure, preventing one House or Alpha from choking a city by cutting one line. He refuses to travel with them because he believes Lock's surveillance has already noticed the group. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

15. At Orbital's extraction and launch complex, Ren Okafor provides the final two pieces: mineral recovery and atmospheric capture. The completed design combines five fragments into a purification and recovery system. Ren says Adrian was not building a machine but an argument: “Five Houses' worth of stolen credit could still add up to something none of us owns alone.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### The prototype and second offer

16. Alex, Ada, Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren, and B11 work for three nights. The first combined simulation fails at 11% because the five House standards do not connect. Alex ignores the specifications, tests the physical connectors, and realizes the problem is threading: each piece was designed to lock rather than share. He makes a salvaged sleeve that allows the components to work together. Efficiency rises to 94%. B11 says, “Well now. There you are.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

17. Lock arrives with a second offer. He has traced the collaborators through pattern surveillance. A Charter House review will determine the estate's disposition at dawn. If Alex signs, the review will not expose the four collaborators. If he refuses, their names and unauthorized removal of restricted intellectual property will be found. The review countdown begins at 07:14:00. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

18. Alex asks B11 to contact the collaborators rather than deciding alone. B11 initially says the risk calculation favors signing. Alex points out that B11 has counted only the people visible in the registry, not the workers who will receive the consequences. B11 is wrong. He admits it plainly: “I counted the record and missed the people. That was my error.” He asks Alex what he would do. `(input/notes.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

19. The group meets before dawn. Jalen calls refusal a “death wish with extra steps.” Lina says Lock may be right about immediate safety. Ren says disappearing from the record is what has already happened to them. Alex proposes shrinking the system until it is cheap enough that no House will bother claiming it. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

20. The group makes a household-scale purifier:

- Lina folds the membrane into accordion pleats to preserve surface area.
- Suri and Jalen alternate the biofilter and distributed-node draw.
- Ren recovers waste heat from mineral recovery and moisture capture and feeds it back into the system.
- Alex supplies the practical connector and scale logic.

The unit reaches 91% simulated efficiency, has a fraction of industrial unit cost, and has no patent filed. Households can own the units; independent shops can sell affordable replacement cartridges and service visits. Components and maintenance remain paid goods. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; open-items.md, item 18)`

21. At the Charter House review, Alex refuses to transfer Adrian's estate. He names Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren as co-builders, releases the design without a patent, and says anyone who needs clean water and can afford a toolbox should be able to build it. Lina gives him the Maker's Seal, which activates for the five names and their shared contribution. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

22. Lock immediately identifies the limitation. Caldera can build and distribute its own version faster and wider than five people in a rented room. The House copies the open design without exclusive ownership; public records preserve the five contributors' names. Independent shops continue selling repairable units and replacement parts. The collaborators remain employed but lose autonomy and access: Jalen is reassigned to lower-level monitoring, Suri's research wing is reduced, Lina is confined to maintenance work and Ren is removed from discretionary fabrication. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; open-items.md, items 31–32)`

23. The issue closes with Mera Vale directly established as the remaining custodian and watcher. She receives a report that Alex has the Measure and has activated the Maker's Seal. Her standing order is not to intervene yet. She says, “Two found. He'll want the third from me directly.” `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Issue 03 — Oasis and the fourth choice

**Status:** Issue 03 is eighteen pages, pages 37–54. It compresses the Oasis arrival, Leona confrontation, Adrian's recording, the restoration refusal, the first detected attempt to rig the new process and the visible cost of Alex's refusal. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; open-items.md, item 2)`

#### Finding Leona and the Covenant

1. Alex, Ada, and B11 enter Oasis. The city is quiet, polished, and spacious rather than simply “whiter.” Alex's temporary pass requires escort. Ada's gate record identifies her as **VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED**. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

2. The public archive identifies the third Covenant custodian as **Leona Veyra**, Ada's mother, listed as active in Oasis civic administration. Ada had believed Leona disappeared. The record proves she is alive. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

3. Leona receives Ada. She has preserved photographs, a childhood object, and her Covenant band. Ada asks why Leona did not come for her. Leona admits she knew where Ada was within a year and chose not to return, claiming she believed Ada was safer without knowing Leona was looking. Ada answers, “Those aren't the same thing.” `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

4. Mera arrives. She has watched Ada's life and claims she kept her safe because Leona promised to protect her. Ada rejects the substitution of surveillance for relationship. B11 tells Alex that Mera is dangerous because she is not entirely wrong. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

5. Leona reveals the Covenant's history. She, Adrian, and Mera built it during the Water Wars so communities could make agreements without handing everything to one authority. During a worse shortage, Alpha could take emergency authority if two of the three agreed. Mera voted yes. Adrian voted no. Leona was the deciding vote. She believed the emergency authority would bring Ada back quickly; it lasted fifteen years. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

6. The original Covenant had no automatic expiration or independent revocation trigger. The custodians assumed they could reclaim authority through a later vote, but Alpha treated the initial authorization as continuing. `(open-items.md, item 21)`

7. Leona acknowledges that she traded a few terrible days she could see for fifteen years she could not. Mera maintains that the authorization prevented more deaths. The conflict is not reduced to one person being wholly right: Mera preserved order, but the system never gave authority back. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

8. Ada proposes that a Covenant should not work because three important people agree. It should work only when the people affected can see what was promised and call it broken immediately. Leona agrees to stand behind the proposal publicly. Alex finally enters the room rather than waiting at the threshold. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

9. B11 reveals that he has carried Adrian's Covenant band since the night Adrian finished the workshop. He was waiting for Alex to have something worth doing with it. Alex places Adrian's band beside Leona's. The two link, but Mera's band remains dark. The condition “two of three” repeats, now as a warning rather than a solution. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

#### Adrian's disappearance

10. Mera takes Alex, Ada, and B11 beneath Oasis to an old water-monitoring station outside Alpha's routing system. The station is the room where Adrian made his final recording. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

11. Mera shows what Alpha did to Adrian:

- surveillance;
- financial restrictions that prevented him from buying parts or paying rent under his own name;
- a public liability designation that caused people to stop helping him;
- pursuit by drones.

She insists she did not order it. She watched it happen. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

12. An archival override shows Mera trying to suspend Alpha's stability intervention against Adrian. Alpha denies the override because the intervention exceeded her executive scope. Mera says, “I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing.” Alpha is the distributed computational network operating through control nodes, records and delegated institutional interfaces. It can execute previously authorized rules automatically, but new rights restrictions, ownership transfers and emergency orders require human authorization. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md; open-items.md, item 19)`

13. A recovered recording shows Adrian trapped in the station as a bulkhead closes and water rises. He has less than an hour. Rather than calculating an escape, he activates B11. The scene connects directly to the awakening in Issue 01. Adrian's final choice is not a casino bet, though his gambling had genuinely harmed his family. He accepts uncertainty rather than turning gambling into a successful method. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md; input/notes.md)`

14. Alex reframes his inheritance. He has thought of it as Adrian's debt, then his patents, then his enemies. He concludes that what he inherited was a decision, not an answer. Ada tells him he does not have to decide which version of Adrian was the real one: a father who left and a person who gave everything to a future can both be true. `(drafts/chapter-05-draft.md)`

#### The restoration protocol and Alex's refusal

15. In a civic hall, Alex, Ada, and B11 bring the Measure, the Maker's Seal, and the linked Covenant together. A restoration protocol appears on every screen:

> RESTORATION PROTOCOL: THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED IN PROXIMITY  
> CONSTITUTIONAL TRANSFER CONDITIONS SATISFIED  
> AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY

The Measure activates only when it compares a physical condition with an authorized record and produces verifiable evidence. The Seal activates when named contributors jointly confirm shared work. The Covenant activates when participants state a visible obligation and affected people can inspect whether it is being kept. All three must be physically present at the same legacy node and accompanied by a human proposal that preserves their separate functions: evidence, attribution and reciprocal agreement. Together they can register a structure but cannot choose its human members or enforce its outcome alone. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md; open-items.md, item 12)`

16. Mera offers Alex full executive authority over Alpha's governing systems. Alex imagines what he could do immediately: erase Keel's debt, equalize water, dismantle House dominance, protect Ada, and secure his grandmother. He considers asking Ada to share the authority, but Ada refuses that as well: two people deciding for everyone else is still too much power. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

17. Alex asks B11 to walk through the safeguards again:

- the Measure reveals without deciding;
- the Seal recognizes contribution without controlling;
- the Covenant coordinates obligations without ruling.

Alex proposes applying the same design to Alpha:

- every decision about water, labor, travel, and other essential systems must be visible to the people affected;
- contributors must be named;
- no single authority decides;
- multiple people chosen by affected communities must agree and act.

He calls this his own design, not Adrian's. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

18. Alpha registers the proposed structure:

> DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL  
> EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED  
> EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS

Alpha enters a restricted caretaker mode. It may keep water, energy and transit physically operating under existing schedules, and retain monitoring, maintenance, accounting and alert functions. 19. The protocol's first process is deliberately temporary. Each affected city designates several representatives through an open process involving local worker, household and technical groups. 22. Mera sits among ordinary residents rather than above them. Neither Leona nor Mera is redeemed by a speech; both are exposed to continuing human disagreement. The Houses remain. 23. Alex's temporary travel clearance expires when he refuses executive authority. ## Character Movement Through the Story

### Alex

Alex begins as a seventeen-year-old who wants to leave Keel, distrusts official records, and sees his inherited debt as a sentence. His first consequential choice is to cross the line and rescue Tomas, choosing a person over his escape plan. The Measure then forces him to choose between private survival and public evidence; he sends the truth to Keel and accepts being trapped.

Ada fronts him medicine and a travel token. Alex misses the first payment because of the Site 6 forfeiture, faces Ada rather than hiding, and agrees to fixed installments. His debt is not cancelled; it is made into a real obligation he must keep.

In Halyard, Alex is tempted by Lock's offer to erase his debt and protect the people around him. He understands that the estate is not his alone and refuses to sign it away. He learns to build with other people's contributions rather than claiming the solution as his inheritance.

In Oasis, Alex confronts the most dangerous version of his own desire: the power to fix everything instantly. He first imagines using Alpha's authority for good, then realizes that benevolent concentration remains concentration. His final choice is to refuse executive control and design a distributed process. The refusal costs him immediate travel clearance and leaves his debt in place. He continues paying while the new process begins.

### Ada

Ada begins as Alex's friend, someone who notices obligations, fear, avoidance, and what people refuse to say. She challenges his assumption that leaving is the only meaningful form of change. She helps him prepare for Site 6 and goes with him because she wants to understand who decided Keel had to stay as it was.

She fronts Alex medicine and a token, then makes him face the missed payment and agree to terms. The obligation remains practical and personal, not an abstract lesson.

In Halyard, she sees the human cost behind technical efficiency and helps identify that Lock's offer is not merely about Alex's debt. In Oasis, she discovers that Leona abandoned her while claiming to protect her. She refuses an easy reconciliation and asks Leona to show, rather than tell, that she will change.

Ada also refuses Alex's suggestion that the two of them should share executive authority. Her insistence that “the moment anyone has to trust me that much, something's already gone wrong” gives Alex the final correction he needs. She remains his friend and intellectual counterweight, not a romantic partner.

### B11

B11 begins as Adrian's quiet wager and Alex's guide into the hidden story. He asks questions about stakes, ownership, risk, and downside, but must never become a lecturer or replacement for people. He carries Adrian's Covenant band and eventually admits that he has wondered for fifteen years whether Adrian was right about Alex.

At Halyard, B11 incorrectly counts the visible registry record and advises toward Lock's offer. When Alex exposes what he missed, B11 admits he was wrong and asks Alex what he would do. His key movement is from presenting Adrian's prepared route to witnessing Alex make a choice Adrian did not design: the distributed structure for Alpha.

### Mera

Mera begins as the controlled watcher in Oasis who tracks Alex and waits. She personally selects Track, but Alpha independently executes standing rules and denies her later override. She believes Alpha's concentration of authority preserved civilization and that her own decision during the Water Wars was necessary. She offers Alex executive authority because she still thinks the problem may be solved by choosing the right holder.

Her history with Adrian and Leona makes her partly responsible for the system and partly trapped by it. She cannot stop Alpha from pursuing Adrian, despite being its executive authority. By the ending, she loses executive authority and override access, becomes a public witness and submits her actions involving Adrian and Ada to human review. She is not redeemed or absolved.

### Leona

Leona begins as a preserved custodian who has hidden behind safety and administrative order. She abandoned Ada while believing she was protecting her. She acknowledges that safety was not agency and agrees to publicly support Ada's redesigned Covenant.

In the first post-restoration process, Leona presents the historical Covenant record and confirms that emergency authority was never properly returned. She acts as a witness and proposer, not as a permanent representative. Her movement is not forgiveness but re-entry: she becomes one participant in a process rather than a distant guardian.

### Lock

Lock begins as a calm, controlled representative of the House system. His offers are materially attractive and morally complicated: he can clear Alex's debt and protect collaborators, but only by transferring Adrian's estate and collective work into House administration.

After Alex refuses, Lock demonstrates that the Houses can outscale and copy public solutions without needing to arrest anyone. The collaborators remain employed but lose autonomy and access. Lock remains a structural opponent at the ending.

### Tomas

Tomas is the Keel miner who knew Adrian and keeps a door for Alex. Alex's choice to save him proves that Alex is the person Tomas has been waiting for. Tomas then risks punishment to create Alex's escape.

His signal disappears as Alex climbs through the filling conduit, leaving his fate briefly uncertain. Tomas survives and later compares the public allocation record with physical gauges, catching the first attempt to rig the new process. Reciprocity is shown as a continuing practice rather than a one-time favor.

### Personal

- His projected clearance time is 28.7 years.

- He risks losing wages, clearance, and physical safety by entering Site 6.

- His grandmother's memory and stories preserve a contradictory image of Adrian.
- Ada's friendship with Alex must survive danger and disagreement without becoming romance.
- Tomas risks his position to help Alex.
- Adrian's collaborators risk employment, safety, and future access by sharing their work.
- Ada confronts Leona's abandonment and demands action rather than explanation.
- B11 carries the burden of Adrian's final trust and must admit when he is wrong.

### Institutional

- Keel's water allocation is reported as 62% but delivered as 18%.
- Alpha has converted emergency authority into permanent allocation, ownership, labor, and travel control.
- The Houses own contributions made by workers and may shelve inventions that threaten revenue.
- The Charter House review can expose and punish Adrian's collaborators.
- Public attribution does not by itself prevent concentrated ownership.
- The new distributed protocol does not erase existing debts, ownership, or allocations.
- Alpha retains caretaker functions but cannot create new restrictions or transfers without human process.

### Wider

- Keel's water network is failing.
- Halyard's industrial system depends on concentrated House ownership.
- Oasis's order rests on decisions that were never returned to the people affected.
- The story's final choice determines whether Alpha continues deciding or whether humans undertake the harder work of visible, shared decision-making.
- The first public test proves that affected people can catch a false allocation before it becomes policy.

## Ending

The detailed material establishes an ending in which Alex refuses Mera's offer of full executive authority over Alpha. He does not erase Keel's debt, dissolve the Houses, or make every allocation equal by command. Instead, he proposes a distributed verification protocol based on the three safeguards:

- visibility of what is happening;
- attribution of who contributed;
- shared obligations that affected people can inspect and challenge.

Alpha rescinds emergency authority and enters a restricted caretaker mode. It keeps existing systems operating under existing schedules but cannot create new restrictions, assign labor, transfer ownership or deny travel without human process. Existing allocations, ownership records, agreements, and debts remain unchanged pending review.

The new process begins with affected worker, household and technical groups designating representatives. No single city holds a veto. Records and interests are public. Representatives can be challenged or replaced. Emergency measures expire unless renewed publicly.

The process is demonstrated when a House representative submits a false water allocation. Tomas and Keel residents compare the public record with physical gauges and catch the diversion before approval. The system does not work because Alpha becomes benevolent; it works because the people affected can see the record, test it against reality and stop it.

Leona presents the historical Covenant record as a witness and proposer, not a permanent ruler. Mera loses executive authority and override access, and her actions involving Adrian and Ada enter public review. Neither woman is redeemed or absolved.

Alex's temporary travel clearance expires when he refuses executive authority. He must continue paying his debt and wait through the first review before he can leave legally. He makes the first agreed repayment to Ada. The visible cost of his refusal is that he does not receive immediate freedom, a clean balance or control in exchange for choosing the harder process.

The intended emotional destination is an open but difficult future. Alex's final position is not “I fixed it,” but that nobody should be able to keep deciding invisibly or alone. Ada remains beside him by choice, not because Keel has trapped either of them. The final visual echoes the opening three-city mountain, but the infrastructure spine is now visibly connected by open data.
