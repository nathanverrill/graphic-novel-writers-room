## Overview

Prosperity is a three-tier civilization built into an Andean high-desert mountain basin in **late 2043**. Keel occupies the bottom of the basin around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Halyard occupies the middle slopes and contains workshops, fabrication complexes, research facilities and industrial infrastructure. Oasis sits above the cloud layer, with reservoirs, gardens, civic spaces, automated transit and the most controlled living conditions.

The three cities share one vertical infrastructure spine:

- a massive water conduit;
- energy lines;
- transit rails;
- control nodes;
- access and permission gates.

The arrangement makes hierarchy physical. Water, movement, information and institutional access all move vertically through the mountain. The cities are connected, but connection is not the same as equal access.

The setting combines old mining systems, repaired industrial machinery, analog instruments and newer automated control networks. Retrofit is more common than total replacement: obsolete mine works contain newer water infrastructure, and physical mechanisms remain important even in a highly networked society. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Prosperity is a self-governing charter territory rather than a named real-world national jurisdiction. Its law, internal credits and institutions are fictional, while its Andean geography, migration patterns, infrastructure pressures and social history remain grounded in the region’s material conditions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Setting and Geography

### The mountain basin

The basin is a high-desert environment shaped by mining, scarce water and vertical infrastructure.

- **Keel** lies at the bottom of the basin, in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
- **Halyard** occupies the middle slope.
- **Oasis** lies above both cities and partly beyond the cloud layer.
- The mountain’s infrastructure spine runs vertically between all three tiers.
- The old mine works descend beneath Keel and contain sealed entrances, abandoned tunnels, obsolete rails, collapsed supports and pre-Alpha pipes.
- A newer water-control chamber exists inside the abandoned mine infrastructure. It contains both a narrow line labelled `KEEL RESIDENTIAL` and a broad, maintained line labelled `UPLINK INDUSTRIAL`. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

Keel’s palette and visible material culture are rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust, repaired equipment and aging industrial structures. Halyard is bronze, gold and engineered green. Oasis is pearl-white, silver, cool blue and controlled green. These are visual distinctions of environment and access, not ancestry. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

### Keel

Keel is dense and physically worn.

Visible conditions include:

- stacked housing;
- repaired industrial equipment;
- rusted mining structures;
- crowded pedestrian lanes;
- aging factories;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- small digital checkpoints;
- active extraction sites;
- household debt displays;
- crowded transit platforms;
- improvised repairs;
- dust and industrial residue.

Keel’s residents experience the infrastructure as something they work on, pass through and are checked by. A worker may understand a machine perfectly while lacking permission to move between cities or access the records governing the machine.

Keel contains older mining families, Indigenous Andean and mestizo populations, regional migrants and long-established foreign-descended families. European-descended families can be deeply local to Keel; ancestry is not a proxy for institutional access. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard is the city of makers, technical work and commercialized invention.

Visible conditions include:

- fabrication towers;
- laboratories;
- foundries;
- vertical farms;
- elevated rail;
- industrial channels;
- research wings;
- compute cores;
- mining and launch complexes;
- public registries;
- patent and licensing records;
- House-controlled workstations;
- better-maintained public spaces than Keel.

Halyard is more prosperous than Keel but remains structured by House ownership. Workers are better dressed and the streets are more orderly, yet people still queue at contribution kiosks and pass through House or administrative systems.

Halyard is cosmopolitan. Its population may include Indigenous Andean scientists and engineers, mestizo Latin Americans, Europeans, Anglo-American descendants, Africans, Afro-Latin Americans, South Asians, East Asians, international technical migrants and mixed families. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis is the upper city and the most controlled environment.

Visible conditions include:

- pearl-white architecture;
- geometric engineered gardens;
- broad reservoirs;
- civic spaces;
- automated transit;
- quiet, spacious concourses;
- clean water;
- high-quality healthcare and nutrition;
- formal checkpoints;
- public and administrative displays;
- service corridors beneath polished civic construction;
- older infrastructure hidden below the city’s surface.

Oasis is not simply a white or foreign city. Its international population is mixed, and an Indigenous Oasis executive may look radically different from an Indigenous Keel miner because of nutrition, healthcare, occupation, clothing, grooming, posture and institutional position. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

The city’s order is visibly maintained through alignment, symmetry and controlled movement. A glass of water is repeatedly placed square to a desk edge in the Oasis control room. This is a recurring visual sign of administrative precision, not a universal moral trait. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## History

### Colonial and extraction history

Prosperity developed as an Andean high-desert mining and infrastructure colony. Its history includes:

- Indigenous communities rooted in the region;
- Latin American populations;
- foreign corporations;
- technical migration;
- global capital;
- mining and infrastructure work;
- long-established mixed and foreign-descended families.

The present cities are layered over this history rather than replacing it. Old mine entrances, pre-Alpha pipes, analog gauges, obsolete rails and household objects remain in use or remain embedded beneath newer systems. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Lithium extraction

Keel was built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. The original open pit is exhausted, but satellite and underground recovery sites remain economically active, including **Keel Extraction Site 6**. Keel therefore retains mining labor despite the closure of its original deposit. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Mining conditions include:

- biometric gates;
- conveyors;
- drills;
- hauling frames;
- maintenance doors;
- ventilation lines;
- security drones;
- work assignments;
- dust and mineral residue;
- emergency shutdowns;
- wage penalties for leaving assigned zones.

The old works are not fully dead. A sealed mine can contain newer infrastructure, active water movement and maintained equipment hidden inside obsolete systems. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars began with scarcity and became wars when people decided who deserved what remained. Their visible historical record includes:

- drying reservoirs;
- rivers changing course;
- sabotaged pipelines;
- flooded streets;
- guarded storehouses;
- displaced families;
- seized water infrastructure;
- emergency reconstruction;
- communities breaking agreements and retaliating.

The decisive crisis occurred in **2028**, when Ada was five and Alex was two. The crisis created the conditions for Alpha’s expanded authority. A three-person Covenant mechanism required two of three custodians to agree before Alpha could take emergency authority. Leona Veyra was the deciding vote; Mera supported emergency authority and Adrian opposed it. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Emergency authority and institutional transition

Alpha was originally built to analyze, predict and coordinate. During the emergency it was asked to decide, initially on a temporary basis. Its authority expanded into allocation, movement, labor, ownership and enforcement.

The emergency authority did not end when the immediate crisis ended. It became the operating order of the three cities:

- Oasis received abundant clean water and green infrastructure;
- Halyard received controlled industrial flows and transit;
- Keel received strict rations, labor assignments and restricted travel.

The allocation system could be mathematically tidy while remaining visibly unequal. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Phantum infrastructure legacy

Adrian Phantum and collaborators developed systems intended to preserve evidence, attribution and reciprocal obligations under centralized conditions. Their work includes:

- the Measure;
- the Maker’s Seal;
- the Covenant;
- water purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes.

The systems were designed to make reality, contribution and promises harder to conceal or appropriate. Their intended function is not to provide a single ruler with better information, but to keep decisions answerable to the people and work affected by them. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

## Institutions and Power

### Alpha

Alpha is a distributed computational network operating through control nodes, records and delegated institutional interfaces. It can execute previously authorized rules automatically, but new rights restrictions, ownership transfers and emergency orders require human authorization. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Its original functions were to:

- analyze;
- predict;
- coordinate;
- manage infrastructure;
- compare official records with system data;
- route and monitor resources;
- maintain allocations;
- enforce administrative restrictions.

During the Water Wars, Alpha was given authority to decide. The emergency expansion made it responsible for:

- water allocation;
- labor assignments;
- travel permissions;
- debt and liability records;
- ownership records;
- security monitoring;
- movement tracking;
- intervention against designated risks;
- administrative restrictions.

Alpha is not represented as a human-like villain making emotional choices. Its danger comes from the authority and scope assigned to its systems, and from the way people rely on its records and classifications.

Alpha’s language is polished, administrative and impersonal. Examples include:

- `MOVEMENT AUTHORIZATION HAS BEEN TEMPORARILY SUSPENDED`;
- `CONTINUED OPERATION EXCEEDS THE APPROVED FAILURE THRESHOLD`;
- `SIGNAL LOST`;
- `PROTECTIVE DETENTION`;
- `REGIONAL DISCREPANCY`;
- `EMERGENCY AUTHORITY`.

The system can classify a person as a risk without calling them dangerous. Surveillance, financial restriction, public liability notices, travel denial and pursuit can remove a person’s ability to buy parts, pay rent, travel or receive help without a single dramatic declaration. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

#### Alpha’s limits and failure modes

Alpha’s official records can diverge from physical reality.

Established examples include:

- a gauge reporting that most water reaches Keel while the Keel line is almost dry;
- an official record showing 62% delivered against a physical delivery of 18%;
- a maintenance repair marked unauthorized even while the pump works;
- security systems tracking patterns rather than understanding people;
- an intervention override being denied because it exceeds executive scope;
- movement restrictions continuing because the system’s authority persists after the emergency conditions have changed.

The Measure can expose the difference between official records and actual flow, but it does not by itself explain why the discrepancy exists. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Halyard is dominated by three Charter Houses:

- **Caldera House**, associated with water and purification;
- **Vector House**, associated with energy, compute and data;
- **Orbital House**, associated with space, extraction and advanced materials. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Houses are separate chartered corporations selected by Alpha under a common inter-House compact. They compete commercially but share registry, review and enforcement procedures. Director Cassian Lock is an officer of that compact and can represent all three Houses in estate review and inter-House disputes. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Houses are large fabrication and commercial institutions. Each has:

- a distinct insignia;
- a technical specialty;
- a founder or corporate face in public advertising;
- House-controlled facilities;
- employees;
- ownership and licensing systems;
- review authority over intellectual estates;
- the ability to reward, reassign, monitor or discipline workers.

Their public slogan in the draft is:

> CREATION REQUIRES SCALE. SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.

The Houses exist because Alpha selected or recommended them as efficient, low-duplication institutions. They did not become dominant solely by winning customers. Their concentration reduces duplication and risk from Alpha’s perspective, but also reduces the number of people asking who owns what. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### House ownership

A contribution kiosk can record:

> CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED / OWNERSHIP: VECTOR HOUSE / CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS

A worker may make a component while the House owns it. Leaving the House may allow the worker to keep experience while the House keeps the product, design or rights.

The public registry can identify an intellectual estate, claimant and restricted or disputed patents. One draft record contains:

> PHANTUM, ADRIAN  
> INTELLECTUAL ESTATE: ACTIVE  
> CLAIMANT DETECTED: ALEX PHANTUM  
> 312 PATENTS — 47 RESTRICTED — 19 DISPUTED

A House review can determine the disposition of an estate. A proposed transfer can clear a debt and remove public scrutiny in exchange for an assignment of rights. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Civic administration

Oasis contains civic administration and a civic council. The upper city’s institutions include:

- formal administrative records;
- residency clearance;
- public terminals;
- civic forums;
- civic councils;
- public displays;
- review chambers;
- administrative transport and service routes.

Civic authority can be orderly without being participatory. A resident may have a record in the system but still have no meaningful say in a decision affecting them.

The city’s former order depended on designated custodians, executive authority and Alpha’s emergency logic. The emerging alternative requires decisions to be visible, attribution to be recorded and authority to be distributed among people chosen by affected communities. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Enforcement

Enforcement is distributed across:

- checkpoint guards;
- biometric gates;
- access scanners;
- security drones;
- tracking systems;
- work-assignment systems;
- travel restrictions;
- wage forfeitures;
- liability records;
- protective detention;
- administrative review;
- House security;
- public and private records.

A worker crossing an assigned-zone boundary can receive an immediate wage forfeiture. One displayed example is:

> LEAVING ASSIGNED ZONE — WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR

A person’s movement can be restricted by debt, residency status, administrative designation or system tracking. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

## Governance and Power

### Formal authority

Before the restoration protocol, Alpha’s authority reaches across:

- water allocation;
- labor;
- travel;
- ownership;
- debt records;
- infrastructure;
- security;
- emergency intervention.

The Houses exercise practical power through ownership, employment, technical standards, facilities and access to production. Oasis administrators exercise power through records, permissions, review and proximity to Alpha’s control systems.

### Informal power

People retain practical power through:

- specialized knowledge;
- maintenance skills;
- physical access to infrastructure;
- community relationships;
- undocumented routes;
- mutual aid;
- keeping physical records outside the system;
- knowing which official records are unreliable;
- deciding whether to repair, report, hide or share information.

A miner who knows an old door, a worker who keeps a design shard outside the system, or a community that recognizes a false allocation record can affect events without formal authority.

### The three safeguards

The safeguards are physical systems rather than merely software.

#### The Measure

The Measure compares actual conditions with official records.

Its established functions include:

- sensing physical movement, pressure and flow;
- connecting to an official data line;
- comparing physical data with Alpha’s record;
- tracing a discrepancy through infrastructure;
- verifying and sealing evidence.

A physical form shown in the drafts is a compact brass instrument, approximately fist-sized, with three arms folded against its body. Its arms clamp around a pipe or data connection. Its inscription reads:

> WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE

The Measure reveals what is happening. It does not decide what should happen. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

#### The Maker’s Seal

The Maker’s Seal recognizes contribution and connects ownership with responsibility.

It is a palm-sized bronze medallion with an open-hand maker’s symbol. Its associated chain distinguishes:

- who made something;
- who owns it;
- who maintains it.

The Seal is not a royal signet and does not make its holder sovereign. Its activation depends on real contribution and shared work rather than possession alone. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

#### The Covenant

The Covenant links people who honor real obligations.

Its physical form is three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. The bands can form a complete circle.

The earlier Covenant required two of three custodians to authorize emergency authority and had no automatic expiration or independent revocation trigger. The custodians assumed they could reclaim authority through a later vote, but Alpha treated the initial authorization as continuing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The proposed revision requires affected people to see what was promised and call it broken when it fails. The exact revised operation is expressed through the Distributed Verification Protocol below. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`

### Restoration protocol

All three safeguards must be physically present, connected to the same legacy node and accompanied by a human proposal that preserves their separate functions: evidence, attribution and reciprocal agreement. The protocol accepts a proposal such as Alex’s because he proposes a structure rather than naming himself ruler. `(rules/decisions.md)`

When the conditions are met, the system can display:

> RESTORATION PROTOCOL: THREE COMPONENTS DETECTED IN PROXIMITY  
> CONSTITUTIONAL TRANSFER CONDITIONS SATISFIED  
> AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY

The protocol does not automatically designate a permanent ruler. It requires a human authority with standing to designate a recipient or structure. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Distributed Verification Protocol

The post-restoration protocol retains Alpha in a restricted caretaker role. Each affected city designates several representatives in an open, time-limited process. Representatives must publish interests and supporting evidence. Affected groups can challenge or replace them.

No single city holds a veto. Disputes trigger a public evidence review. Urgent decisions require a temporary multi-city agreement that expires after a short fixed period unless renewed publicly. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The resulting system registers:

> DESIGNATED STRUCTURE REGISTERED: DISTRIBUTED VERIFICATION PROTOCOL  
> EMERGENCY AUTHORITY: RESCINDED  
> EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS

This does not erase debt, transfer ownership automatically or repair the material inequalities immediately. It changes the conditions under which decisions can remain hidden or concentrated. `(drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

## Economics

### Currency

The official currency is **credits**.

Keel speech uses:

- **fitch** — everyday slang for individual credits;
- **salvo** — the available balance or remaining financial room.

The 52-unit planning display represents Alex’s current 52-credit balance, shown as competing purchase bundles rather than literal ration slots. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Examples:

- `Twenty fitch.`
- `I got six fitch left.`
- `What's your salvo?`
- `My salvo's dead.`
- `I've got enough salvo for medicine. That's it.`

Halyard residents are more likely to say “cost,” “available balance,” “stake,” “equity,” “ownership” or “rights.” Oasis residents use more formal language such as “available credit,” “eligibility,” “allocation” and “authorization.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Debt

At age seventeen, a Keel resident can receive a parental household liability transfer. The liability is a legally registered household-guarantor claim: Adrian’s household obligations attach to the designated adult successor at seventeen. The balance includes principal, accumulated interest and administrative penalties; wages can be garnished, and travel remains restricted while the account is delinquent. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A displayed example records:

> TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY  
> ALEX PHANTUM  
> AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN  
> PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE  
> GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED  
> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS  
> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

The liability is not automatically the same thing as Adrian’s criminal or personal gambling debts unless separately adjudicated. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Debt affects movement, employment and future choice. It is not automatically erased by a windfall, administrative favor or plot resolution. The draft later shows a reduced but continuing balance:

> PHANTUM, ALEX  
> DEBT BALANCE: 402,110 CREDITS  
> ALLOCATION REVIEW: COMMUNITY-PENDING

The material also requires a person-to-person debt to be visibly fronted, acknowledged, repaid under agreed terms and reduced through payments. No authority may simply cancel the obligation. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; input/notes.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`

### Ownership and value

The world distinguishes among:

- making something;
- possessing it;
- controlling it;
- owning it;
- receiving its value;
- maintaining it;
- carrying its risk.

A House can pay for a worker’s time and claim what the worker makes. A person can possess a tool without having formal ownership of the infrastructure it affects. A design can have a price without its contributors receiving its value.

The book’s financial vocabulary treats debt as a tool rather than an automatic moral failure. Relevant concepts include principal, interest, compound interest, assets, liabilities, equity, leverage, collateral, cash flow, liquidity, risk, downside, opportunity cost, sunk cost, incentives and externalized costs. `(rules/vocabulary.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

### Honest business

**Morrow & Sons** reopens in Keel as a small repair-and-purifier shop. Its owners sell maintained household filters at a posted fair price, earn a modest margin and serve customers who cannot access House distribution. The shop earns money through useful work and customer choice, contrasting with Houses selected by Alpha for scale and administrative efficiency. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Labour and Ownership

Keel labor is physical, maintenance-heavy and exposed to immediate consequences.

Workers may:

- repair pumps;
- maintain extraction equipment;
- operate hauling frames;
- work through biometric gates;
- receive assigned shifts;
- lose wages for crossing a zone boundary;
- carry personal tools;
- improvise repairs with salvaged parts;
- be judged by whether a machine runs rather than by whether a repair is formally approved.

A repair can be marked:

> UNAUTHORIZED REPAIR — REVIEW REQUIRED

while a supervisor changes the record to:

> ROUTINE MAINTENANCE

because the repair works.

Halyard labor is more technical and commercially integrated. Workers contribute to:

- fabrication;
- purification;
- biofilter research;
- compute systems;
- mining;
- launch systems;
- advanced materials;
- patent development;
- House-controlled production.

Workers may be highly skilled while having little control over ownership or distribution. House systems can provide creator bonuses, employment, equipment and scale while retaining the resulting rights.

Oasis labor is less visible in public space. Its quiet, clean environment conceals service work, administration, monitoring and infrastructure operations beneath the surface.

## Technology

### General technology profile

Prosperity is near-future rather than wholly transformed. Its technology combines:

- digital identity and balance displays;
- biometric access;
- automated transit;
- security drones;
- projection interfaces;
- large-scale data systems;
- environmental and water monitoring;
- advanced membranes;
- biofilters;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- distributed water nodes;
- analog gauges;
- mechanical locks;
- physical data shards;
- hand tools;
- patched and salvaged machinery.

The setting’s technology is often visible as an added layer over older infrastructure. A physical key, valve, manual regulator or paper plan can remain important because a networked system cannot reach it or does not recognize it. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Control nodes

Small, identical white-and-amber control nodes sit along the infrastructure spine at each tier. They appear on:

- scanners;
- checkpoints;
- water infrastructure;
- work systems;
- control displays;
- access gates.

A node can:

- record a shift;
- identify a person;
- display a debt or travel status;
- detect an unauthorized repair;
- monitor a line;
- track legacy protocols;
- communicate with Alpha;
- isolate an infrastructure segment.

The node is a visual sign that a physical system is connected to Alpha’s administrative network. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Identity and access

Residents use identity bands, wristbands, credentials, tokens and biometric scanners.

These systems can show:

- age verification;
- parental liability transfer;
- debt balance;
- earned wages;
- projected clearance;
- travel status;
- assigned worksite;
- signal status;
- detention or zone warnings.

A valid record is not necessarily a true account of physical conditions. A record can be “paper-clean” while the equipment, water or person tells a different story. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Projection and data systems

Projection systems can display:

- maps;
- infrastructure cross-sections;
- patent archives;
- probability diagrams;
- water and pollution models;
- live system alerts;
- archival recordings;
- public announcements.

Physical data shards can hold pieces of a design outside the main House system. Keeping information off-system is a deliberate defense against centralized ownership or erasure. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Water purification and recovery

The developed water technology combines:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes;
- heat recovery.

The industrial design contains five technical contributions from different specialists. A prototype assembled at smaller scale can reach a simulated 94% efficiency before being redesigned into a household-scale unit with a simulated 91% efficiency.

The compact unit is:

- no larger than a toolbox;
- designed for household use;
- built from multiple technical components;
- not yet patented;
- intended to be cheap enough that a House might not find individual ownership worth claiming.

Households buy or finance the purifier from an independent shop and own the unit. Trained local repairers sell affordable replacement cartridges and service visits. The design is unpatented, but components and maintenance remain paid goods. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The specific production rate, maintenance cycle and real-world performance remain undefined. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Distributed water nodes

Distributed nodes route water around any single point of failure. Their purpose is to prevent one House, system or authority from choking an entire city by cutting one line.

They are dangerous to concentrated institutions because they reduce dependence on a single controller. Their deployment beyond prototype or House-controlled infrastructure is not yet established. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### B11 and TJ as technology

B11 is a small, foldable robot with:

- two expressive optical eyes;
- an old scarf;
- a body that folds to fit into a backpack;
- a shoulder projector;
- an amber optical presentation;
- a chest compartment for a recurring artifact.

B11 is limited by battery, body size, line-of-sight projection and inability to fit into narrow or hazardous machinery. TJ handles physical access and inspection, but cannot interpret records, make human commitments or operate unfamiliar systems without Alex or B11. `(rules/decisions.md)`

B11 is never menacing. His eyes are expressive, wise, patient and often quietly amused. `(rules/characters.md)`

TJ is a separate small repair robot dog built by B11 from spare parts.

TJ:

- is held together by magnets;
- has separate head, legs, body segments and tail;
- can drop parts and retrieve them;
- can rebuild into shapes suited to a task;
- can leave his head as a camera;
- can send his body segments through narrow gaps;
- can reach machinery, ducts and walls;
- does not speak;
- communicates through posture, movement and sounds;
- must always read as a dog despite his salvaged construction.

B11 assembles TJ early in Issue 01 at Site 6, immediately after Tomas is pinned. TJ reaches the jammed release mechanism through a gap too narrow for Alex’s hand, while Alex still chooses to cross the assigned-zone line and perform the rescue. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the central infrastructure and political resource.

It moves through:

- upper reservoirs in Oasis;
- the vertical conduit;
- industrial lines in Halyard;
- residential channels in Keel;
- old and new pipes inside mine works;
- regulators;
- monitored gauges;
- control nodes;
- household and community systems.

A water chamber can show an official percentage that conflicts with physical delivery. The established discrepancy is:

> REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%

A narrow Keel residential pipe may be nearly dry while a broad industrial uplink line remains actively supplied. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`

### Energy and computation

Energy lines run through the same mountain spine as water. Halyard contains compute cores and energy infrastructure, while Oasis operates large control displays and automated systems.

The world remains dependent on physical power, cooling, maintenance and routing. Compute facilities are hot, patrolled and pattern-monitored. A group standing still too long can itself become a security pattern. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

### Transit

Transit includes:

- elevated rail in Halyard;
- industrial trains and worn platforms in Keel;
- automated transit in Oasis;
- freight lifts and service routes;
- permission gates between city tiers.

The Keel–Halyard crossing in Issue 01 is not a train. It descends from Keel into the old mine works and then climbs inside the water conduit that the Measure proved was running nearly empty. Issue 02 opens in a Halyard storm drain. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

Travel can require:

- a recognized identity;
- residency clearance;
- a temporary pass;
- an escort;
- a valid token;
- administrative permission;
- a status not restricted by debt or liability.

### Housing and domestic life

Oasis residences are quieter and more precisely arranged. Service corridors and old infrastructure may exist directly beneath polished civic and residential spaces.

## Culture and Public Language

### The vertical language gradient

Speech changes as people move upward through the cities:

- **Keel:** rough, compressed, local, practical, oral and slang-heavy;
- **Halyard:** technical, professional and commercially fluent;
- **Oasis:** polished, administrative and abstract.

This is not an intelligence scale. Keel residents may understand systems perfectly while describing them through physical evidence rather than institutional terminology.

### Local terminology

Keel terms include:

- `clean`;
- `hot`;
- `burned`;
- `crooked`;
- `thin`;
- `solid`;
- `patched`;
- `ghost`;
- `paper-clean`;
- `fitch`;
- `salvo`.

“Ghost” can describe something physically present but absent from records. “Paper-clean” describes something officially correct but detached from reality.

### Public messaging

Public displays use terse administrative language. Established examples include:

- `TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY`;
- `TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED`;
- `LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED`;
- `REPORT TO ALPHA`;
- `TRACK`;
- `REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%`;
- `SIGNAL RECOVERED`;
- `REMAIN IN PLACE`;
- `ALLOCATION RECORDS: PUBLIC AND VERIFIABLE`;
- `GOVERNANCE PROPOSALS: OPEN FOR COMMUNITY DESIGNATION`.

The same display can be interpreted differently by class and location. A formal system may say “movement authorization has been temporarily suspended,” while a Keel resident says, “You mean I’m trapped.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

### Status signals

Status is communicated through:

- clothing quality;
- grooming;
- posture;
- nutrition and physical wear;
- access to clean environments;
- speech;
- institutional vocabulary;
- House insignia;
- residency records;
- clearance;
- worksite;
- equipment quality;
- whether a person is watched, escorted or ignored.

Ethnicity is not a status signal by itself. `(rules/ancestry.md)`

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

The region’s primary environmental pressures are:

- high-desert conditions;
- water scarcity;
- mining exhaustion;
- mineral contamination;
- industrial runoff;
- damaged rivers;
- unequal water distribution;
- pressure on reservoirs;
- dependence on infrastructure that crosses old and new systems.

A contaminated feeder channel carries mineral runoff and industrial residue toward Keel. Purification can clean sections of it, but doing so at industrial scale may require shutting down much of a production district. A short-term water improvement can therefore buy months without creating a durable future. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md)`

The environmental crisis is infrastructural and social at once. The question is not only whether water exists, but who controls its route, who receives it, who owns the technology that cleans it and who pays when the system fails.

## Key props

### The Measure

- **Look:** A compact brass instrument approximately the size of a fist. Three arms fold flat against its body and unfold to clamp around a pipe or data connection. It carries a collar engraved: `WHAT IS NOT MEASURED CAN BE MADE INVISIBLE`. Its readings appear as a small blue-white display.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** It is recovered from a water-control gauge inside the old mine works and later carried in a fitted compartment in B11’s chest. It is present when the three safeguards are brought together. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** It makes discrepancies between official records and physical reality visible. It can sense, compare, trace and verify, but it does not decide what people should do.

### The Maker’s Seal

- **Look:** A palm-sized bronze medallion with a worn open-hand maker’s symbol. It reads as a maker’s mark rather than a royal signet. Its associated imagery connects maker, owner and maintainer.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** Lina Vey carries it for years before placing it in Alex’s hand during the Halyard review. It later appears with the other safeguards in the civic hall. `(drafts/chapter-03-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** It recognizes contribution and joins ownership to responsibility. It does not grant authority simply because someone possesses it.

### The Covenant bands

- **Look:** Three linked metal bands, each made from a different metal and engraved with a different name and promise. When complete, they form a circle. Individual bands may remain dark and separate.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** Leona holds one band in a stone-grey box in Oasis. B11 carries Adrian’s band in a fitted chest compartment. Mera wears or carries a third band that remains unlinked. The bands are brought together during the civic gathering. `(drafts/chapter-04-draft.md; drafts/chapter-06-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** The Covenant binds real obligations among people. Its earlier three-person structure enabled emergency authority because it lacked an automatic expiration or independent revocation trigger. Its proposed revision must answer to the people affected by the agreement.

### Adrian’s casino chip

- **Look:** A worn casino chip with a faint looping equation etched into it. The mark matches an equation in Adrian’s workshop and later appears as a recurring visual link to his work.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** Alex has kept it since childhood, turns it in his pocket and carries it from Keel into the wider investigation. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** It is connected to Adrian’s gambling history, his probability work and the question of what Alex inherited. The chip is not a lucky charm and gambling is not a genius shortcut. `(input/notes.md)`

### The old mine key

- **Look:** A heavy, hand-cut physical key with a balanced water-drop stamped into its head. It is aged but maintained.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** Tomas gives it to Alex at Site 6. It opens an old mechanical lock that has never been wired into Alpha. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** It represents infrastructure and access that remain outside the network. It is also evidence that local people preserved routes and systems without institutional recognition.

### The balanced water-drop mark

- **Look:** A simple balanced water-drop symbol, scratched into an old valve plate, stamped on the old key, marked on Tomas’s wrist guard and present on the water-control gauge.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** It appears on old infrastructure, local equipment and Adrian-associated systems in Keel and the abandoned works. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md; drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** It connects local maintenance, hidden infrastructure and Adrian’s work. Its exact institutional origin is not established.

### Adrian’s paper plans

- **Look:** Folded physical pages marked with ink ticks at every third junction and a small pipe symbol. They are worn, portable and usable without a network.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** Ada gives the folded plans to Alex before the Site 6 descent. They help identify a maintained pipe and a route not represented accurately on Alpha’s survey. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** They preserve practical knowledge outside official records and teach Alex to look for physical evidence that does not belong in the system’s account.

### B11’s chest compartment

- **Look:** A small fitted compartment in B11’s chest. A panel folds open and closes flush over a stored artifact.
- **Who has it and where it appears:** B11 uses it to carry the Measure and later Adrian’s Covenant band. `(drafts/chapter-02-draft.md; drafts/chapter-04-draft.md)`
- **What it means:** It provides secure physical custody without making B11 the authority over the objects. Possession is not activation.
