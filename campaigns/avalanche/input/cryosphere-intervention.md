# Keeping ice where it is

What Alpha Dynamics is selling in EVOKE AVALANCHE. Every item labeled with the vocabulary in
`hard-sf-rules.md`. The book's license log is in `rules/bible.md` and **Avalanche spends no new
large license** — the engineering here must stay T, EG or S.

## The framing

Glacial geoengineering is a real and seriously argued field, split between people who think it
buys time and people who think it is a dangerous distraction (**T**). There are published
proposals, cost estimates, and a substantial scientific literature arguing against (**T**).

**This is the book's gift.** Alpha does not need invented technology. He needs money, ships,
steel and permission — and the objection to him is not that his plan is fake.

## The menu, from most grounded to least

### Seabed curtains / buttressing (the book's main line)

- The idea: a flexible barrier anchored to the seabed across a glacier's approach, to block warm
  deep water from reaching the grounding line where it does the melting (**T** that this is
  proposed, costed and actively argued; the engineering itself is **EG/S**).
- Aimed at places like Thwaites, where **warm Circumpolar Deep Water reaching the grounding line
  drives the melt** (**T**).
- Cost estimates are in the tens of billions, on the order of a large infrastructure programme
  (**T** as published estimates).
- Objections are serious and specific: Southern Ocean construction is brutal, the ecological
  effects of altering deep water flow are unknown, and it treats a symptom (**T**).

**For a small bay rather than Thwaites, at a colony's scale, this is the most plausible thing
Alpha could actually build.** Scale it down: a barrier across the mouth of a bay to keep warm
water out and let fast ice form and hold. Make the ships, the seasons and the steel real.

### Anchoring or thickening fast ice

- Fast ice often forms behind grounded icebergs that pin it in place (**T**). Artificial
  anchoring points are a straight-line extension (**EG**).
- Pumping seawater onto ice to thicken it has been proposed and trialled in the Arctic for sea
  ice (**T** that it is trialled; **S** at any meaningful scale — the pumping energy and the
  area involved are the problem).

### Basal water removal

- Proposals to drain or freeze the meltwater film that lets a glacier slide, by drilling and
  pumping (**T** that it is proposed; **S** in practice).

### Albedo modification on snow and ice

- Scattering reflective material on ice to reduce melt has been trialled at small scale, mostly
  on mountain glaciers and with mixed results (**T**).
- At ice-sheet scale it fails on logistics and does ecological harm (**T** as the standard
  objection).
- Note the honest irony available here: **dust-on-snow in Colorado is the same physics running
  the wrong way** (**T**). Tomas can say this in one line.

### Marine cloud brightening

- Spraying sea-salt aerosol to brighten low cloud and reflect sunlight. Field trials have run
  over the Great Barrier Reef (**T**). Regional deployment is **S**.

### Stratospheric aerosol injection

- The big lever: reflective aerosol in the stratosphere to cool globally (**T** as a proposal
  with a large literature). Deployment is **S**, and its governance problem is unsolved (**T**).

**Keep this offstage.** If Alpha Dynamics is doing planetary-scale solar geoengineering, the
book becomes about that instead. Mention it as something Alpha is interested in; do not let it
on the page.

## The governance fight, which is the real story

- Antarctica is run under the Antarctic Treaty System, with the Environmental Protocol
  prohibiting mineral resource activity and requiring environmental assessment (**T**).
- There is **no settled mechanism for authorising large-scale geoengineering** there, and it is
  unclear who would even decide (**T**).
- Decisions are consensus-based among Consultative Parties, which is slow (**T**).
- A private consortium acting faster than the treaty system can respond is **EG**, and it is
  exactly Alpha's move — not illegal so much as arriving before the law has an opinion.

**This is where the book's conflict actually lives.** Not "is the machine safe" but "who
agreed to this, and what happens when they want to stop".

## What the arrangement looks like

Grounded, and far more frightening than a machine:

- A programme with a season-by-season schedule, ships, and a crew (**T**-shaped).
- A monitoring contract: the sensors, the data and the models are Alpha Dynamics' (**EG**).
- Renewal terms. The ice holds while the programme runs (**EG**).
- The scientific record becomes an operational dependency — the count is now a performance
  metric (**EG**, and this is the book's real horror).

## Cut list

Anything here enters only as a logged License:

- Technology that reverses ocean warming.
- Anything that refreezes an ice sheet, or works in one season.
- A machine small enough to be carried by the cast.
- Nanotechnology, engineered organisms, weather control.
- Any intervention with no maintenance cost — **the maintenance cost is the plot**.
