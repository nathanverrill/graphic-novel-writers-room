## Overview

The 2046 world remains recognizably contemporary in its buildings, laws, research practices, corporate power, infrastructure, and environmental damage. The speculative layer is concentrated in advanced AI integration, automated material recovery, and the scale of cryosphere monitoring rather than in a wholesale replacement of existing systems. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md, input/world.md, input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

The story's world is organized around three connected environments:

1. **Antarctica**, especially the Larsen C region, where climate research, unstable ice, concealed infrastructure, and illegal extraction overlap.
2. **The Himalayas in Nepal**, where an old disaster, altered landscapes, cultural memory, and abandoned underground facilities shape the past.
3. **Colorado**, where cryosphere research, Rocky Mountain conservation, and waste-to-value technology connect Antarctic evidence to terrestrial environmental work. `(rules/rules.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The central environmental and political pressure is the degradation of ice systems. The world treats Antarctica not as an untouched abstraction but as a monitored, contested working environment in which scientific access, commercial extraction, international law, and conservation interests conflict. `(input/world.md, references/research.md)`

## Time and Historical Conditions

### 2046

The book is set in 2046. Specific future institutions, prices, laws beyond those named here, and technical capabilities remain undefined unless marked as proposals. `(rules/rules.md, input/world.md)`

### The 2026 Nepal disaster

**Established project history.** In 2026, a flood took Sitara's parents and home. The event uses the Lirung–Trishuli disaster geography as grounding, while Sitara's town and family experience are fictionalized and unneeded casualty totals are not adopted as book canon. `(rules/decisions.md; input/characters.md, input/world.md, references/research.md)`

Sitara grew up in a fictional settlement near the Lirung massif and Trishuli River system, placing her directly in the disaster zone. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md, characters.md)`

Her parents died while helping neighbors evacuate, and Sitara witnessed their loss from a monastery refuge. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md, characters.md)`

The research describes a massive rock-ice avalanche from the Lirung massif that became a fast-moving debris flow, entered the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system, and struck the Nepal-Tibet border at Gyirong Port. It describes a wall of water, mud, and rock; destruction over a 72-kilometre stretch of river in Nepal; more than 1,451 deaths in Nepal and 43 in Tibet; more than 6,264 people missing across both sides; destruction of houses, roads, bridges, power projects, and border facilities; and bodies carried as far as 240 kilometres downstream into India. These casualty figures and additional reported impacts are research background, not adopted fictional totals. `(references/research.md, rules/decisions.md)`

### Antarctica's ice history

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** The Larsen Ice Shelf is divided into Larsen A, B, C, and D. Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B in 2002. Larsen C is described in the research as fragile, thinning, vulnerable, and near its thinnest state in 10,000 years; complete collapse is predicted by 2100. `(references/research.md)`

In the book, Larsen C suffers a major structural break in 2046. The shelf does not vanish immediately, but its buttressing effect is visibly weakening. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

## Settings

## Antarctica — Larsen C region

The primary setting is the Larsen region, specifically Larsen C, on the Antarctic Peninsula. The visible landscape includes glaciers, an icy field, an icy blue sky, cloud cover, light wind, and the peninsula's mountainous ice formations, described in the project material as the peninsula's “Rocky mountains.” `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

The visual and seasonal conditions are fixed as:

- November.
- Late morning to early afternoon light.
- The sun is always up, although clouds can cover it.
- Light wind.
- Snow and blue-white ice dominate the exposed landscape.
- Camps and field installations sit on unstable ice and may need to move. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ are part of a Colorado-linked cryosphere field team conducting airborne and ground ice-movement surveys near Larsen C. A monitoring station detects an accelerating fracture while their camp is relocating. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md, world.md)`

Scientists working on unstable shelves relocate camps frequently, use airborne surveys instead of relying only on ground surveys, avoid crevasse zones, and monitor ice movement in real time. These practices continue into 2046. `(input/world.md)`

The ice is physically hazardous. Field personnel must use equipment and clothing suited to cold, shifting surfaces. Booties are required so footwear does not become stuck to the ice; TJ's booties use an internal heating system in the project material. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

### Larsen C instability and Falcon activity

Natural Larsen C instability initiates the opening failure. Falcon's drilling has weakened or accelerated a nearby fracture. Falcon exploits naturally unstable ice for concealment, but its drilling locally worsens fractures and endangers the shelf. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md, story.md, world.md)`

Sitara uses a drill or sampling tool in an unsafe location, triggering a pre-existing fracture in the ice. The “crack” is literal and caused by her reckless decision. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md, world.md)`

Larsen C's major structural break does not mean that the entire shelf disappears immediately. Its weakening buttressing effect remains an active environmental danger while the field team works near the margin. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

### The Antarctic underground bunker

A concealed underground research bunker is located on stable or seasonally exposed ground near the Larsen C margin. The main mine support and logistics facility are also on stable or seasonally exposed ground, with temporary drill access extending toward the shelf. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

Its exterior is a snow-covered, submarine-like hatch, partly hidden in the ground. A Falcon symbol is engraved into the metal hatch. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

The interior contains:

- Maps and charts spread across tables.
- Computers mounted on walls.
- Rolling chairs.
- Falcon logos on chairs, computer lock screens, and pens.
- An atmosphere described as a little dusty and unused for some time.
- Information concerning who is conducting the drilling. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

The project material does not establish when the bunker was built or what its original legal ownership was. Falcon uses it as a logistics and data facility for its Antarctic operation. `(input/world.md, rules/decisions.md)`

### The Himalayas and Nepal

The Himalayas are an ancestral setting and a source of cultural, environmental, and historical pressure. Sitara grew up in a fictional settlement near the Lirung massif and Trishuli River system. The Nepal setting includes mountain valleys, drought, sand, carved landscapes, old underground facilities, and evidence left in the landscape without a recorded history. `(rules/decisions.md; input/story.md, input/world.md)`

A later Himalayan survey team, including Sitara, was trapped in an abandoned research tunnel. The event explains her fear of enclosed spaces and distrust of official rescue claims. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md, story.md)`

A secret underground bunker or old research facility exists in the Nepal thread. The project material says the country failed to remove old bunkers and that the bunker lid has codes on it. This facility is unrelated to the Antarctic bunker; their resemblance reflects recurring infrastructure designed for extreme environments. `(rules/decisions.md; input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The Thakali and Sherpa cultures, Buddhism, mountain livelihoods, yak herding, high-pass trade with Tibet, cold-climate agriculture, and the Mani Rimdu festival are available as cultural and environmental context. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md, references/research.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** The research describes Sherpa communities' traditional reliance on yak herding, high-pass trade with Tibet, and cold-climate agriculture; tsampa, thukpa, fermented yak-butter tea, and potatoes as food traditions; and day-of-week naming traditions including Dawa, Mingma, Lhakpa, Phurba, Pasang, Pemba, and Nima. `(references/research.md)`

Colorado is the secondary contemporary setting. It contains:

The project mentions the University of Colorado Boulder Cryosphere Remote Sensing and Biogeochemistry Group as a real-world research connection. `(input/world.md, references/research.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** The referenced University of Colorado research group works in cryosphere remote sensing and biogeochemistry. `(references/research.md)`

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

The environmental system is under simultaneous pressure from climate change, unstable ice, water scarcity, and extraction.

In Antarctica, thinning and structural instability affect where camps can be placed, how researchers travel, and which survey methods are safe. Real-time ice monitoring and airborne surveys reduce exposure to crevasses and shifting ground but do not remove the underlying hazard. `(input/world.md, references/research.md)`

Natural Larsen C instability initiates the opening ice failure. Falcon's drilling locally worsens or accelerates a nearby fracture while exploiting unstable ice for concealment. Sitara's unsafe sampling action triggers a pre-existing fracture. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md, world.md, references/research.md)`

The Larsen C region is also a site of drilling associated with an illegal mining operation in the project material. Drill sounds and shifting ice are part of the setting. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

In Nepal, drought, flood, changing mountain landscapes, and the 2026 disaster shape the relationship between people and terrain. The landscape may contain physical evidence of events whose history was never formally recorded. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

In Colorado, water scarcity and groundwater drawdown threaten a prairie restoration and wildlife corridor tied to Nayah's community. Conservation is also tied to the protection of Rocky Mountain plains and to the waste-to-value system's efforts to turn discarded material into usable resources. `(rules/decisions.md; input/characters.md, input/world.md)`

A US-based corporation and its contractors use Falcon as a subsidiary or front for the covert Antarctic operation. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md, hard-sf-rules.md, references/research.md)`

### Conservation and waste-to-value organization

A Colorado-based nonprofit-public partnership operates mobile Antarctic waste recovery and funds prairie conservation through recovered material value. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md, references/research.md)`

The organization sorts and recovers metals from Antarctic operations, sells the material through Colorado facilities, and directs proceeds and monitoring data to prairie restoration. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

Its precise name, funding structure, workforce, and relationship to Falcon are not established. Its role is distinct from the Falcon consortium.

## Economics, Labour, and Ownership

The project's economic conflict is between conservation and illegal extraction, with material recovery positioned as an environmental and industrial alternative.

Falcon extracts subglacial minerals through a temporary borehole operation supported by modular power, drilling, pumping, and aircraft logistics. The operation uses access near the Larsen C margin to reach exposed or seasonally exposed Antarctic bedrock; it does not mine beneath the floating shelf itself. The bunker serves as a logistics and data facility. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md, hard-sf-rules.md)`

The exact minerals being extracted, the workforce, the value chain, and the financial beneficiaries remain undefined. Greed represents the corporate and governmental beneficiaries through a public-facing negotiating role, with authority to sacrifice the Antarctic operation but not to expose the investors. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** Metal recycling is difficult because workers and machines must separate ferrous metals such as iron and steel from non-ferrous metals such as copper, aluminium, and brass, as well as from general waste. AMP Robotics, based in Louisville, Colorado, builds AI-guided robotics and optical sorting systems; the research cites partnerships with material facilities, including a next-generation $50 million facility in Commerce City, and automated air jets extracting recyclables with efficiencies of up to 90 percent. `(references/research.md)`

The fictional waste-to-value organization uses machine vision, robotic picking, and air-jet sorting to recover metals and other recyclables, then directs recovered value and monitoring data toward Colorado prairie restoration. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

## Technology

### Cryosphere monitoring

The 2046 research environment uses:

- Airborne ice surveys.
- Ground ice-movement surveys.
- Real-time monitoring of ice movement.
- Mobile or frequently relocated field camps.
- Crevasse avoidance based on changing terrain.
- Drilling activity capable of being heard or detected near the Larsen C field. `(rules/decisions.md; input/world.md, input/story.md)`

These are continuations of existing or emerging practices rather than wholly new technologies. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md, input/world.md)`

### TJ and AI integration

TJ was developed through a CU Boulder research partnership and is deployed because its mobile body can carry sensors into unstable terrain. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md, references/research.md)`

TJ has intermittent satellite connectivity, limited episodic memory, and supervised autonomy. All system integration requires a physical or authenticated network bridge. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md, hard-sf-rules.md)`

TJ may discuss its onboard science and conservation data, but refuses unsafe physical actions, unauthorized access, and claims it cannot verify; it marks uncertainty explicitly. `(rules/decisions.md; hard-sf-rules.md, world.md)`

TJ is a mobile materials-sorting prototype with a small manipulator, allowing it to recover samples but not operate a full recycling line. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md, references/research.md)`

TJ was not built by Falcon. Its access to the bunker results from a vulnerability in obsolete Falcon infrastructure. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md, world.md)`

TJ's unusually broad cross-system compatibility is the project's one declared **L**. Its power, authorization, bandwidth, and failure modes remain strict. All other systems remain T/EG/S. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Automated material sorting

AI-driven robotic sorting is a research-grounded emerging technology available to the 2046 world. The system may use optical recognition, robotic arms, and air jets to separate metals and other recyclable materials. `(references/research.md)`

Its fictional deployment, capabilities, maintenance requirements, and failure modes remain bounded by the research-grounded system described above. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md, references/research.md)`

### Bunker access and codes

The Antarctic bunker has a coded hatch or access system. TJ discovers a software vulnerability in an obsolete local controller, allowing TJ to open the door. This access is an investigative act rather than proof of Falcon affiliation. `(rules/decisions.md; hard-sf-rules.md)`

The code system is local to the Antarctic facility. The unrelated Nepal facility has its own coded lid; the two systems are not connected. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md, world.md)`

## Infrastructure and Communications

### Field infrastructure

Antarctic research camps are temporary or relocatable because the ice beneath them is unstable. Airborne surveys and remote sensing reduce the need to expose personnel to crevasse zones. Real-time ice monitoring is part of operational safety. `(input/world.md)`

A storm and fracture isolate the team from the main camp. They retain limited battery power, emergency rations, and a narrow satellite window. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

### Secret communications

A Falcon whistleblower sends encrypted low-bandwidth messages to a Colorado conservation network through scheduled satellite bursts. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md, hard-sf-rules.md)`

The messages warn that Falcon's drilling is accelerating a fracture and identify the data needed to prove it. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md, world.md)`

The messages are distinct from TJ's access to the bunker and do not require a connection between the Antarctic and Himalayan facilities.

### Data and research

The Antarctic data is encrypted and incomplete. Sitara must combine sensor logs, bunker records, and independent Colorado analysis. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

The data later affects a prairie and groundwater restoration project tied to Nayah's community. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md, world.md)`
