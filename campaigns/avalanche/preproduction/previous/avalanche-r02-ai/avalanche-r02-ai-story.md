# Avalanche — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

### Intended experience

A hard-science-fiction graphic novel set in 2046, beginning in Antarctica and connecting three environments: Antarctica, the Himalayas, and Colorado. The story should surprise the reader with “cool science,” while allowing the audience to discover an interesting environment rather than being told about it directly. `(rules/rules.md; input/story.md; input/minecraft.md)`

The story is intended to combine:

- Antarctic ice research and glacier instability;
- Sitara’s Himalayan family history and the disaster that drove her to Colorado;
- conservation work connected between Antarctica and Colorado;
- an AI character able to integrate into systems;
- a secret message originating in Antarctica;
- a mystery involving an underground bunker and the Falcon organization;
- a graphic-novel structure built around visual discovery. `(input/story.md; input/world.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

The story must follow the project’s hard-science-fiction rules. Any invented technology, institution, law, weather event, body, or other world element must carry a **T**, **EG**, **S**, **L**, or **Cut** label. The story is governed by the 80/15/5 mix: most of the world remains recognizable, emerging technology is ordinary by 2046, and only a very small number of elements are genuinely new. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Central dramatic question

The story asks:

> What is happening beneath the Antarctic ice, who is sending the secret messages, and can Sitara and Nayah uncover the truth before Falcon’s activity threatens the ice, conservation work, and the people connected to it?

The investigation concerns a Falcon whistleblower’s warnings that drilling is accelerating a fracture, and the data needed to prove it. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

### Theme and emotional direction

The material reaches toward a story about:

- the relationship between climate science and conservation;
- the consequences of exploiting fragile environments;
- the connection between Sitara’s Himalayan loss and the Antarctic crisis;
- the need to uncover truth despite institutional secrecy;
- the tension between ambition and responsibility;
- the possibility that scientific data can connect distant places and communities. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md; input/world.md)`

Sitara begins as passionate, ambitious, reckless, and desperate to uncover the truth. She learns that pursuing truth without protecting people reproduces the violence she is investigating. She accepts collaboration and refuses a dangerous solo proof-gathering act. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

Nayah begins protective, distrustful, spiritually grounded, and committed to their homeland. They learn to distinguish justified caution from protective control and choose to trust Sitara with a consequential decision. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

TJ’s meaningful choice is to refuse an unauthorized system integration that could save the mission but compromise human safety and evidence integrity. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

### Intended visual experience

The opening begins with tension and shows the consequences of Antarctic instability before supplying all the context:

1. open on the glacier failure;
2. cut to Sitara’s 2026 flood memory;
3. return to Larsen C with a location caption;
4. show drilling and shifting ice;
5. let TJ detect the buried hatch;
6. end Chapter One on TJ opening the Falcon-marked door. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

The project favors environmental discovery, visual evidence, recurring motifs, and images that carry information without requiring explanation. One migratory bird image is retained as a visual bridge between the three settings, with no coded or supernatural meaning. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

## Premise

In 2046, Sitara Lhakpa and her Colorado friend Nayah are members of a Colorado-linked cryosphere field team conducting airborne and ground ice-movement surveys near Larsen C. A monitoring station detects an accelerating fracture while their camp is relocating. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; world.md)`

Natural Larsen C instability initiates the opening failure, while Falcon’s drilling has weakened or accelerated a nearby fracture. Falcon exploits naturally unstable ice for concealment, but its drilling locally worsens fractures and endangers the shelf. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; world.md)`

Larsen C suffers a major structural break in 2046. The shelf does not vanish immediately, but its buttressing effect is visibly weakening. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

During the field operation, Sitara uses a drill or sampling tool in an unsafe location, triggering a pre-existing fracture in the ice. The “crack” is literal and caused by her reckless decision. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

TJ, an AI robot dog developed through a CU Boulder research partnership and deployed because its mobile body can carry sensors into unstable terrain, detects a buried bunker. TJ discovers a software vulnerability in an obsolete local controller and opens the Falcon-marked door. This access is an investigative act, not proof of Falcon affiliation: TJ was not built by Falcon, and its access results from a vulnerability in obsolete Falcon infrastructure. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

Inside, Sitara, Nayah, and TJ find evidence of Falcon’s covert extraction operation. Falcon is a consortium operating through shell companies, contractors, and research partnerships rather than one legal entity. A US-based corporation and its contractors conduct the operation covertly, using Falcon as a subsidiary or front. Falcon extracts subglacial minerals through a temporary borehole operation supported by modular power, drilling, pumping, and aircraft logistics. Its main mine support and bunker are on stable or seasonally exposed ground near the Larsen C margin, with temporary drill access extending toward the shelf. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; world.md)`

Secret messages from Antarctica are sent by a Falcon whistleblower to a Colorado conservation network through scheduled satellite bursts. The messages warn that Falcon’s drilling is accelerating a fracture and identify the data needed to prove it. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

Sitara and Nayah must preserve the evidence while enduring an Antarctic fracture, a storm, limited battery power, emergency rations, and a narrow satellite window. Sitara eventually returns to Colorado and combines encrypted, incomplete sensor logs, bunker records, and independent Colorado analysis to prove that Falcon accelerated the fracture and falsified its environmental monitoring. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

The evidence affects a prairie and groundwater restoration project tied to Nayah’s community. The waste-to-value organization sorts and recovers metals from Antarctic operations, sells the material through Colorado facilities, and directs proceeds and monitoring data to prairie restoration. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

## Story So Far

The story is organized from the settled premise, chapter outline, and story fragments. The events below establish the current narrative sequence and causal structure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The story is set in 2046. Antarctica is the current setting, with Larsen C as the primary location. The Himalayas function as Sitara’s ancestral home and backstory, while Colorado is the place where Sitara studies cryosphere remote sensing at the University of Colorado Boulder, works as a research technician on field data, and later analyzes Antarctic data. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/rules.md; world.md)`

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ form a Colorado-linked cryosphere field team conducting airborne and ground ice-movement surveys near Larsen C. Their monitoring station detects an accelerating fracture while their camp is relocating. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; world.md)`

### The 2026 disaster and Sitara’s history

Sitara’s home was in a fictional settlement near the Lirung massif and Trishuli River system. In 2026, a rock-ice avalanche and resulting flood struck the region. Her parents died while helping neighbors evacuate. Sitara survived from a monastery refuge, where she witnessed the loss of her family and home. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; characters.md)`

The event uses the research geography but does not require the book to repeat the research casualty figures. The disaster materially shapes Sitara’s present choices: she treats environmental systems as living systems, refuses to sacrifice people for evidence, and carries the fear that official rescue claims may fail. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

Later, a Himalayan survey team including Sitara was trapped in an abandoned research tunnel. That event explains her fear of enclosed spaces and distrust of official rescue claims. The Himalayan facility is unrelated to the Antarctic bunker; their resemblance reflects recurring infrastructure designed for extreme environments. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md; world.md)`

Sitara’s Thakali family taught her stewardship through mountain livelihood. She treats the ice as a living system and refuses to sacrifice people for evidence. Under pressure, she becomes quieter, rolls her mala beads, and records unanswered questions rather than conclusions in a field notebook. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

### Antarctic fieldwork and emergency

The team’s mission is to conduct airborne and ground ice-movement surveys near Larsen C. A monitoring station detects an accelerating fracture while their camp is relocating. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; world.md)`

A storm and the fracture isolate the team from the main camp. They retain limited battery power, emergency rations, and a narrow satellite window. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

The field systems remain grounded in existing and emerging practice: airborne surveys, real-time monitoring, relocatable camps, crevasse avoidance, modular equipment, and constrained satellite communications. TJ’s unusually broad cross-system compatibility is the project’s one declared **L**. Its power, authorization, bandwidth, and failure modes remain strict. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md; world.md)`

### The sound beneath the ice

Drilling sounds occur while the ice shifts. The team initially experiences the sound as an environmental threat and an unexplained human presence. Falcon’s drilling operation uses modular power, drilling, pumping, and aircraft logistics to extract subglacial minerals from exposed or seasonally exposed bedrock near the Larsen C margin. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md; world.md)`

Falcon uses the unstable region for concealment. Its drilling does not create the whole Larsen C crisis, but it locally worsens fractures and endangers the shelf. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

### Discovery of the bunker

The fracture and field investigation lead Sitara, Nayah, and TJ to the concealed bunker. The hatch is snow-covered and partly hidden in the ground. A Falcon symbol is engraved into the metal. `(input/story.md; world.md)`

The bunker serves as a logistics and data facility for Falcon’s operation. It is located on stable or seasonally exposed ground near the Larsen C margin rather than beneath the floating shelf itself. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

TJ detects and opens the door by exploiting a vulnerability in an obsolete local controller. All system integration requires a physical or authenticated network bridge, and TJ may refuse unauthorized access or unsafe action; in this case, the local vulnerability is exposed through a physical interface and TJ’s licensed compatibility. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

### Inside the Falcon bunker

Inside are maps and charts spread across tables, wall-mounted computers, rolling chairs, and Falcon logos on chairs, computer lock screens, and pens. Dust suggests that the facility has not been used recently. `(input/world.md; input/art-direction.md)`

The bunker contains authenticated drilling records and evidence concerning who is conducting the drilling. The records establish that Falcon operates through shell companies, contractors, and research partnerships, and that a US-based corporation and its contractors are conducting the covert operation through Falcon as a subsidiary or front. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

The facility changes the investigation from an environmental anomaly into an organized extraction and secrecy story. The Falcon operation violates the Madrid Protocol’s ban on mining in Antarctica. `(rules/rules.md; rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

### Secret messages from Antarctica

A Falcon whistleblower sends encrypted, low-bandwidth messages to a Colorado conservation network through scheduled satellite bursts. The messages warn that Falcon’s drilling is accelerating a fracture and identify the data needed to prove it. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

The first secret message appears during the Chapter Two investigation. It directs attention toward the authenticated drilling records and the independent Colorado analysis required to establish causation. It is evidence and warning, not a supernatural or automated communication. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

The messages deepen the investigation by showing that someone inside or adjacent to Falcon understands the environmental danger and is trying to expose it. They also create a time pressure: the team must preserve the data before Falcon erases the facility’s records and before the fracture isolates them completely. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

### Sitara and Nayah under pressure

Sitara’s reckless pursuit of evidence conflicts with Nayah’s protectiveness and institutional mistrust. Nayah distrusts institutions because prior conservation promises excluded their community. They protect Sitara because Sitara repeatedly treats evidence as more important than people. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

Nayah uses silence as resistance, physically places themself between Sitara and danger, and carries a water sample kit tied to their homeland’s conservation work. Their homeland faces water scarcity and groundwater drawdown threatening a prairie restoration and wildlife corridor. Nayah seeks enforceable water protections and community control over monitoring data. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

When the investigation becomes dangerous, Sitara wants to enter the bunker during a fracture event. Nayah refuses until the team secures a retreat route. Once a retreat route exists, Nayah agrees to a timed entry with TJ. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

The decision is the first meaningful change in their relationship. Nayah’s caution becomes a condition for action rather than a veto, while Sitara begins to understand that evidence is not worth abandoning the people who must carry it out. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

### TJ’s role in the investigation

TJ is an AI robot dog developed through a CU Boulder research partnership and deployed because its mobile body can carry sensors into unstable terrain. TJ is a mobile materials-sorting prototype with a small manipulator. It can recover samples but cannot operate a full recycling line. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

TJ has intermittent satellite connectivity, limited episodic memory, and supervised autonomy. All system integration requires a physical or authenticated network bridge. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

TJ may discuss onboard science and conservation data, but refuses unsafe physical actions, unauthorized access, and claims it cannot verify. It marks uncertainty explicitly. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

TJ’s broad cross-system compatibility is the project’s one **L**. Its power, authorization, bandwidth, and failure modes remain strict. TJ can exploit the obsolete Falcon controller only through a physical interface and only because the vulnerability is locally available; TJ cannot generally bypass encryption or connect to arbitrary systems without a bridge. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md; world.md)`

During the later investigation, TJ refuses an unauthorized system integration that might save the mission but would compromise human safety and evidence integrity. This is TJ’s meaningful choice: helping the team does not mean obeying every technically possible instruction. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

### The Colorado connection

Sitara studies cryosphere remote sensing at the University of Colorado Boulder and works as a research technician on field data. The Antarctic fieldwork is connected to the university and to a Colorado-based nonprofit-public partnership that operates mobile Antarctic waste recovery. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

The waste-to-value organization sorts and recovers metals from Antarctic operations, sells the material through Colorado facilities, and directs proceeds and monitoring data to prairie restoration. The conservation outcome is tied to Nayah’s community, where water scarcity and groundwater drawdown threaten a prairie restoration and wildlife corridor. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

The Antarctic data is encrypted and incomplete. Sitara must combine sensor logs, bunker records, and independent Colorado analysis. The resulting proof is not a single unchallengeable file; it is a corroborated record whose custody and comparison make Falcon’s falsified environmental monitoring visible. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

## Character Movement

### Sitara

Sitara begins as a young Thakali woman carrying the loss of her parents and home from the 2026 disaster. She studies cryosphere remote sensing at CU Boulder and works as a research technician. Her ambition drives her into unsafe action in Antarctica, where she triggers a pre-existing fracture while pursuing evidence. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

Her movement is:

1. loss in the Himalayas;
2. relocation to Colorado;
3. cryosphere study and research work;
4. Antarctic fieldwork;
5. reckless creation of the crack;
6. discovery of Falcon’s hidden operation;
7. pressure to gather proof alone;
8. acceptance of collaboration and refusal of a dangerous solo act;
9. return to Colorado;
10. analysis that turns incomplete evidence into protection for living communities. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

### Nayah

Nayah belongs to a fictional Colorado Plains nation and is connected to a homeland threatened by water scarcity and groundwater drawdown. They seek enforceable water protections and community control over monitoring data. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

They distrust institutions because prior conservation promises excluded their community. They protect Sitara because Sitara repeatedly treats evidence as more important than people. In Antarctica, Nayah’s caution initially blocks Sitara’s dangerous plan, then becomes a condition for a timed entry once a retreat route is secured. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

Nayah’s endpoint is to distinguish justified caution from protective control and trust Sitara with a consequential decision. In Colorado, they begin a community monitoring broadcast, turning the Antarctic evidence toward local conservation authority. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

### TJ

TJ is a CU Boulder research-partnership prototype whose mobile body carries sensors into unstable terrain. TJ has intermittent satellite connectivity, limited episodic memory, supervised autonomy, and strict requirements for physical or authenticated network bridges. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

TJ can recover samples with a small manipulator but cannot operate a full recycling line. TJ educates the team about conservation and cryosphere science, marks uncertainty, and refuses unsafe physical actions, unauthorized access, and unverifiable claims. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

TJ opens the bunker through a local vulnerability in obsolete Falcon infrastructure. Later, TJ refuses an unauthorized integration that could save the mission but compromise human safety and evidence integrity. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

### Greed

Greed is a consortium of corporate and governmental actors represented by one public-facing negotiator. Greed intermediates for the real beneficiaries and can sacrifice the Antarctic operation but cannot expose the investors. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

Greed’s endpoint is immediate escape from arrest but loss of operational control after the Colorado analysis exposes Falcon’s drilling and falsified monitoring. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; characters.md)`

### Falcon

Falcon is a consortium operating through shell companies, contractors, and research partnerships. Its covert operation uses a temporary borehole system, modular power, drilling, pumping, and aircraft logistics to extract minerals from bedrock near the Larsen C margin. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

Falcon’s operational control is lost when the evidence is preserved and analyzed in Colorado. The wider beneficiaries remain protected from immediate exposure through Greed’s escape and the consortium’s distributed structure. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

## Ending

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ preserve the evidence while evacuating the Antarctic operation. Falcon’s operation is exposed through the Colorado analysis. Greed escapes immediate arrest but loses operational control. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

Sitara proves that Falcon’s drilling accelerated a fracture and that the company falsified its environmental monitoring. The result triggers an emergency conservation and legal response. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

The evidence affects the prairie and groundwater restoration project tied to Nayah’s community. Nayah begins a community monitoring broadcast, asserting community control over the data that will guide protection of the homeland. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

The final emotional image is Sitara placing her mala beads beside the authenticated data archive while Nayah begins the broadcast. A single migratory bird image bridges the settings without coded or supernatural meaning. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; world.md)`

The final unresolved story question is:

> Can evidence become protection?

The answer is not guaranteed by exposure alone. The evidence must be held, interpreted, and placed under the control of the people it is meant to protect. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; characters.md; world.md)
