## Overview

The 2046 world remains recognizably contemporary in its buildings, laws, research practices, corporate power, infrastructure, and environmental damage. The speculative layer is concentrated in advanced AI integration, automated material recovery, and the scale of cryosphere monitoring rather than in a wholesale replacement of existing systems. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md, input/world.md, input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

The story's world is organized around three connected environments:

1. **Antarctica**, especially the Larsen C region, where climate research, unstable ice, concealed infrastructure, and illegal extraction overlap.
2. **The Himalayas in Nepal**, where an old disaster, altered landscapes, cultural memory, and abandoned underground facilities shape the past.
3. **Colorado**, where cryosphere research, Rocky Mountain conservation, and waste-to-value technology connect Antarctic evidence to terrestrial environmental work. `(rules/rules.md, input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The central environmental and political pressure is the degradation of ice systems. The world treats Antarctica not as an untouched abstraction but as a monitored, contested working environment in which scientific access, commercial extraction, international law, and conservation interests conflict. `(input/world.md, references/research.md)`

## Time and Historical Conditions

### 2046

The book is set in 2046. Specific future institutions, prices, laws beyond those named here, and technical capabilities remain undefined unless marked as proposals. `(rules/rules.md, input/world.md)`

### The 2026 Nepal disaster

**Established project history.** In 2026, a flood took Sitara's parents and home. The event is connected to the Lirung massif rock-ice avalanche described in the research material. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md, references/research.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** The research describes a massive rock-ice avalanche from the Lirung massif that became a fast-moving debris flow, entered the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system, and struck the Nepal-Tibet border at Gyirong Port. It describes a wall of water, mud, and rock; destruction over a 72-kilometre stretch of river in Nepal; more than 1,451 deaths in Nepal and 43 in Tibet; more than 6,264 people missing across both sides; destruction of houses, roads, bridges, power projects, and border facilities; and bodies carried as far as 240 kilometres downstream into India. `(references/research.md)`

**Open canon boundary.** The material connects the project disaster to this research account but does not explicitly settle whether every reported research statistic and location is part of the fictional book's history. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md, references/research.md)`

### Antarctica's ice history

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** The Larsen Ice Shelf is divided into Larsen A, B, C, and D. Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B in 2002. Larsen C is described in the research as fragile, thinning, vulnerable, and near its thinnest state in 10,000 years; complete collapse is predicted by 2100. `(references/research.md)`

**Research-informed proposal.** A major Larsen C structural failure or total collapse around 2046 could remove the floating shelf's buttressing effect, allowing land-based glaciers behind it to accelerate by up to five times and ultimately add several millimetres to global sea-level rise. This gives the Antarctic setting an immediate global consequence while remaining grounded in the cited ice-shelf mechanism. `(references/research.md)`

## Settings

## Antarctica — Larsen C region

The primary setting is the Larsen region, specifically Larsen C, on the Antarctic Peninsula. The visible landscape includes glaciers, an icy field, an icy blue sky, cloud cover, light wind, and the peninsula's mountainous ice formations, described in the project material as the peninsula's “Rocky mountains.” `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

The visual and seasonal conditions are fixed as:

- November.
- Late morning to early afternoon light.
- The sun is always up, although clouds can cover it.
- Light wind.
- Snow and blue-white ice dominate the exposed landscape.
- Camps and field installations sit on unstable ice and may need to move. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

Scientists working on unstable shelves relocate camps frequently, use airborne surveys instead of relying only on ground surveys, avoid crevasse zones, and monitor ice movement in real time. These practices continue into 2046. `(input/world.md)`

The ice is physically hazardous. Field personnel must use equipment and clothing suited to cold, shifting surfaces. Booties are required so footwear does not become stuck to the ice; TJ's booties use an internal heating system in the project material. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

### The Antarctic underground bunker

A concealed underground research bunker is located near Larsen C. Its exterior is a snow-covered, submarine-like hatch, partly hidden in the ground. A Falcon symbol is engraved into the metal hatch. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

The interior contains:

- Maps and charts spread across tables.
- Computers mounted on walls.
- Rolling chairs.
- Falcon logos on chairs, computer lock screens, and pens.
- An atmosphere described as a little dusty and unused for some time.
- Information concerning who is conducting the drilling. `(input/world.md, input/art-direction.md)`

The project material does not establish when the bunker was built, who legally owns it, what the Falcon organization is formally called, or whether it was originally a research, military, commercial, or mixed-use facility. `(input/world.md, input/characters.md)`

### The Himalayas and Nepal

The Himalayas are an ancestral setting and a source of cultural, environmental, and historical pressure. The Nepal setting includes mountain valleys, drought, sand, carved landscapes, old underground facilities, and evidence left in the landscape without a recorded history. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

A secret underground bunker or old research facility exists in the Nepal thread. The project material says the country failed to remove old bunkers and that the bunker lid has codes on it. The material does not establish whether this facility is connected to the Antarctic bunker, the Falcon organization, or the secret messages. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

The Thakali and Sherpa cultures, Buddhism, mountain livelihoods, yak herding, high-pass trade with Tibet, cold-climate agriculture, and the Mani Rimdu festival are available as cultural and environmental context. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md, references/research.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** The research describes Sherpa communities' traditional reliance on yak herding, high-pass trade with Tibet, and cold-climate agriculture; tsampa, thukpa, fermented yak-butter tea, and potatoes as food traditions; and day-of-week naming traditions including Dawa, Mingma, Lhakpa, Phurba, Pasang, Pemba, and Nima. `(references/research.md)`

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

The environmental system is under simultaneous pressure from climate change, unstable ice, water scarcity, and extraction.

In Antarctica, thinning and structural instability affect where camps can be placed, how researchers travel, and which survey methods are safe. Real-time ice monitoring and airborne surveys reduce exposure to crevasses and shifting ground but do not remove the underlying hazard. `(input/world.md, references/research.md)`

The Larsen C region is also a site of drilling associated with an illegal mining operation in the project material. Drill sounds and shifting ice are part of the setting. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

In Nepal, drought, flood, changing mountain landscapes, and the 2026 disaster shape the relationship between people and terrain. The landscape may contain physical evidence of events whose history was never formally recorded. `(input/story.md, input/world.md)`

In Colorado, conservation is tied to the protection of Rocky Mountain plains and to the waste-to-value system's efforts to turn discarded material into usable resources. `(input/characters.md, input/world.md)`

## Economics, Labour, and Ownership

The project's economic conflict is between conservation and illegal extraction, with material recovery positioned as an environmental and industrial alternative. The exact commodities being mined, the value chain, the workforce, and the financial beneficiaries are not established. `(input/world.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not automatically book canon.** Metal recycling is difficult because workers and machines must separate ferrous metals such as iron and steel from non-ferrous metals such as copper, aluminium, and brass, as well as from general waste. AMP Robotics, based in Louisville, Colorado, builds AI-guided robotics and optical sorting systems; the research cites partnerships with material facilities, including a next-generation $50 million facility in Commerce City, and automated air jets extracting recyclables with efficiencies of up to 90 percent. `(references/research.md)`

**Research-informed proposal.** The fictional waste-to-value organization could use machine vision, robotic picking, and air-jet sorting to recover metals and other recyclables, then direct a portion of the recovered value or service capacity toward Colorado conservation. This would extend the real-world Colorado model without requiring a new physics-breaking technology. `(references/research.md)`

The material does not establish whether workers remain on the sorting lines, whether the organization is nonprofit or commercial, who owns recovered material, or how Antarctic waste enters the same system. `(input/world.md, references/research.md)`
