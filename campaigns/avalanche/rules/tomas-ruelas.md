# Tomas Ruelas

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **In one line** | The forecaster who reads what is buried, calls it right, and watches it happen anyway. |
| **Age** | 47 |
| **Wants** | To keep the road open and nobody under the snow. |
| **Needs** | To stop measuring his worth by whether the mountain does what he said it would. |
| **Wound** | He closed the pass late once, years ago, and two people went up before the gate came down. |
| **Tell** | Digs. Given any snow and any pause, he is on his knees with a saw making a pit wall nobody asked for. |
| **Voice** | Plain, physical, funny in a flat way. Talks about the snowpack like a person with a history — "it's been holding a grudge since November." |
| **Palette** | Faded blue shell, sun-bleached orange pack, black beard going grey at the chin, raccoon goggle-tan. |

## Who he is

Tomas forecasts for the highway and the backcountry out of a small office in a mountain town.
The job is unglamorous and mostly about weather stations, phone calls, and telling people things
they do not want to hear. He is very good at it and everybody local knows him, which he treats
as a liability rather than a pleasure.

He is the book's translator. What Wren knows about ice as a curve on a screen, Tomas knows with
his hands, every winter, at a scale a reader can hold. When he explains a persistent weak layer
— a rotten sugary layer buried metres down that does not heal and waits — he is also explaining
an ice shelf, and the book never has to say so.

## The wound, precisely

Years ago he had the data to close a pass and took an extra forty minutes to be sure. Two
people drove up in those forty minutes. They survived — this matters, he does not get the clean
tragedy — and one of them still walks with a cane and waves at him at the grocery store.

So he over-closes now. He is known for it, mocked for it a little, and it is the reason the
county respects him and the reason his own crew finds him hard.

## What the avalanche does to him

He calls it. He raises the danger rating, he closes the road, he tells the study crew to pull
the instruments, and he is **right about everything**. It goes anyway, bigger than the record
allows, into terrain with no slide history anyone alive has seen. It takes the road, the power
line and the data link. He is at the edge of it — caught, carried a short distance, not buried,
walks out with a broken wrist and a cracked rib.

**The point is not survival. The point is that being right did nothing.** He gets what Wren has
been living with for twenty years, in ninety seconds, physically.

Afterwards he is not traumatised in the usual way. He is *unmoored*: the mountain did something
outside its own record, which means the record is no longer the tool it was. His whole trade is
pattern-matching against a past that is being retired.

> "I've been reading this range for twenty years. It just used a word I don't have."

## His arc

1. **Certainty.** He knows this snowpack. He is the one person in the book not frightened.
2. **The call.** He does everything correctly and in time.
3. **The slide.** It exceeds him. Nobody dies, which denies him even the clarity of grief.
4. **The doubt.** He stops issuing forecasts for three weeks. Not guilt — he cannot see the
   ground to stand on.
5. **The return.** He goes back with worse tools and less confidence, and keeps working. He
   starts writing down what he does not know, in a separate column.
6. **He is the one who refuses Alpha.** Not from principle at first, from trade instinct: a
   system that tells you the slope is safe takes away the habit of checking. He can name exactly
   what a community loses when it stops having to read its own snow, because he has watched
   tourists lose it.

Tomas is the book's answer to the principle. Wren argues her way to agency; Tomas already lives
there, and can say why in one sentence about a pit wall.

## How he talks

- Concrete nouns, short sentences, present tense about the mountain.
- Gives the snowpack a personality and is aware he is doing it.
- Deflects thanks: "Road's closed, that's all."
- Explains with his hands and an object — a glove, a coffee lid, a stack of anything.

### Lines that sound like him

> "There's a layer down there from November that never got the memo it's spring."

> "I didn't save anybody. I closed a road and it ran through the road."

> "You want the mountain managed. I'm telling you the guy who manages it stops teaching you to
> look at it."

## Relationships

**Wren** — his late wife's sister. They talk shop because the shop is the safest ground. He is
the only person she doesn't perform competence for, and vice versa.

**Priya** — met over the pika plots, which his crew helped instrument. He likes her because she
counts things and says the number.

**Dane** — Dane offers him a sensor network that would make his forecasts far better, and he
wants it badly, and that wanting is the most honest argument the book makes for Alpha.

## Drawn

Big through the shoulders, slightly stooped, moves carefully on flat ground like someone whose
knees are done. Beard, deep goggle-tan line across the face — pale where the goggles sit, dark
everywhere else — and that tan is his visual signature, present in every panel including
indoors.

The recurring image is **Tomas on his knees in a pit**, shoulder-deep in a wall of cut snow,
one bare hand against the layers. Use it early so the avalanche panels pay it off.
