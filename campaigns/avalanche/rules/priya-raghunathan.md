# Dr. Priya Raghunathan

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **In one line** | The ecologist who counts what is actually there and will not round it up to make the season sound survivable. |
| **Age** | 33 |
| **Wants** | An honest count, published, whatever it says. |
| **Needs** | To let something matter to her out loud, instead of hiding behind the method. |
| **Wound** | She was taught that caring about the animals makes your data suspect, and she believed it. |
| **Tell** | Counts under her breath. Taps a tally counter in her pocket when she is upset, and the room can hear it. |
| **Voice** | Fast, exact, faintly impatient. Corrects numbers mid-sentence. Swears cheerfully about logistics and never about the work. |
| **Palette** | Orange float coat, teal beanie, black-framed glasses fogged at the edges, red tally counter on a cord. |

## Who she is

Priya runs the colony counts and the chick survival work from the station, four seasons in. She
came up through a generation of ecologists who were trained to be unimpeachable because they
knew their findings would be attacked, and it has made her rigorous and a bit armoured.

She is the person in the book with her hands actually on the species. Wren has the thirty-year
curve; Priya has this year's chicks, individually, and knows which ones are not going to make
it. **She is the one who has to walk through the colony after a bad ice year**, and the book
should make the reader walk with her.

## The wound, precisely

In her second season a supervisor cut a line from her draft — a sentence that said the loss was
hard to watch — and told her it would be used against her. He was not wrong. She learned to
strip every trace of herself out of her writing, and now she cannot find the way back in, and it
is costing her the ability to make anyone outside the field *care* about a number that should
be unbearable.

Her arc is the inverse of Wren's. Wren has to learn she can't do it alone. Priya has to learn to
say out loud that it matters to her.

## Her arc

1. **Method as armour.** She is the most precise person in the book and the least legible.
2. **The bad season.** The ice goes early. She does the count anyway, walks the fast ice edge,
   and records a number close to zero fledged.
3. **She is the one who understands the offer first.** When Alpha Dynamics proposes to hold the
   ice, she is not tempted, because she sees immediately what it does to the *meaning* of the
   count: a colony that persists because it is maintained is no longer a measurement of
   anything. Her record stops being science and becomes a service-level report.
4. **The fight with Wren.** The hardest scene in the book. Priya says the thing nobody wants:
   "If we take this, I am not a scientist any more, I'm a groundskeeper."
5. **She is overruled.** Wren accepts. Priya stays anyway, and keeps counting, which is the most
   loyal thing she does.
6. **The turn.** At the end she writes the sentence her supervisor cut, and publishes it, and it
   is the line that reaches people. The book's argument arrives in her voice, not Wren's.

## How she talks

- Numbers first, always. "Two hundred and eleven. Last year, nineteen hundred."
- Self-corrects aloud, which reads as honesty: "Forty — no, forty-one, I recounted."
- Impatient with abstraction: "Which bay? Say which bay."
- Warm in the galley, clipped on the ice.

**She does not do wonder.** If a script gives her an awestruck beat looking at the landscape,
replace it with her looking at an animal and saying a number.

### Lines that sound like her

> "You can say 'a poor breeding year.' I have to write the number that's actually true."

> "Say what you mean. You mean you want them kept alive. That's not the same as saving them."

> "I count them because counting them is how anyone ever knew. If you hold the ice up, I'm not
> counting a colony, I'm counting your inventory."

## Relationships

**Wren** — her senior by twenty years and the reason she came to this work. The book's central
friendship, and the fight between them is not about facts, which they agree on, but about what
the facts are *for*. Priya's respect survives it; her deference does not.

**Tomas** — an easy friendship, two technicians comparing tools. He is the person she can be
funny around. He is also the one who tells her, in one sentence in a truck, that the reason she
is angry is that she loves the birds, and she should try saying so.

**Dane** — she is the only one who does not warm to him, and she is scrupulously fair to him
about it. He notices, and respects her more than he respects the people who like him.

**Alpha** — they never meet. This is deliberate. The person with her hands on the actual species
is beneath his attention, and that is characterisation of him, not of her.

## Drawn

Small, quick, always in motion on the ice; still and slumped in the galley. Glasses that fog
whenever she comes indoors, which is a running visual. The orange float coat makes her a moving
dot against ice in the wide shots — use it for scale and for finding her.

The recurring image is **Priya crouched at chick height**, one glove off, tally counter in the
bare hand. Her bare hand in the cold is the tell that she has been at it too long.
