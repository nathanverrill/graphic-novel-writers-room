# Dr. Wren Sandoval

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **In one line** | The scientist who has counted this colony for thirty years and has to decide whether to let someone else keep it alive. |
| **Age** | 54 |
| **Wants** | The fast ice to hold, and the colony to breed again next season. |
| **Needs** | To accept that she cannot be the one who saves it, and that this does not make her useless. |
| **Wound** | She measured it correctly for twenty years and nobody acted, so being right stopped feeling like doing anything. |
| **Tell** | Checks instruments she has already checked. Writes the number down on paper before she believes the screen. |
| **Voice** | Precise, dry, allergic to overstatement. Says "about" and "roughly" even when she is certain, because certainty is a thing you earn. |
| **Palette** | Faded red parka gone pink at the shoulders, grey braid, sun-cracked face, blue-white glare in her glasses. |

## Who she is

Wren runs the sea-ice record out of Boulder and goes south most seasons. She has been counting
this colony since she was a graduate student, which means she has the thing almost no
climate scientist has: a single place she has watched continuously for a working lifetime. The
record is in her handwriting for the first eleven years.

She is not a crusader and does not enjoy being asked to be one. She is a person who built a
long, careful measurement and now has to watch the thing it measures end. Her competence is
total and has stopped being enough, and that is the wound the book works on.

## The wound, precisely

Not "nobody listened." Everybody listened. She testified, she was quoted correctly, the
findings held up, and the curve kept going. What broke in her is the link between
**knowing** and **doing** — she cannot any longer feel that measuring something is a form of
acting on it. So when Alpha Dynamics arrives and actually *does* something, the offer lands on
exactly the part of her that is starved.

**This is why she is tempted.** Not money, not fear. Someone finally turning a number into a
result.

## Her arc

1. **Measurement as refuge.** She would rather take a reading than have an argument.
2. **The offer.** Dane shows her a plan that would keep the fast ice in that bay. It is real
   engineering and it would probably work. She cannot find the flaw, because there isn't one in
   the physics.
3. **The avalanche.** Tomas called it, was right, and could not stop it. Watching him fail the
   way she has failed tips her toward accepting.
4. **She accepts.** Mid-book, not at the end. She signs, and the colony breeds.
5. **The cost arrives.** Not a betrayal — a renewal. The arrangement has terms, the data goes
   somewhere, and the next season's survival is a decision made by someone who is not her.
6. **The turn.** She does not tear it down. She insists on the one thing Alpha cannot supply:
   that the colony's survival be something the world can *do*, not something it can *buy*. She
   goes back to the record, and publishes everything, including the terms.

Her ending is not victory. It is agency recovered at the cost of certainty.

## How she talks

- Understates by reflex: "It's not good" means it is catastrophic.
- Gives the error bars unprompted, and it reads as honesty, not hedging.
- Goes quiet rather than loud when angry. The room can tell.
- Explains by making you look at the thing: "Put your hand on it. Now the other one, here."

**She never delivers a speech about the planet.** If a script gives her one, cut it and let her
show somebody a graph she has been keeping since before they were born.

### Lines that sound like her

> "About a metre and a half. It was four when I started."

> "I'm not saying it will go. I'm saying there's nothing left holding it that I can point to."

> "You want me to say it's unprecedented. It isn't. It's just the first time it's happened to us."

## Relationships

**Tomas** — her brother-in-law, and the person she talks to about work because he does the same
job in a different medium. Their whole relationship is two people comparing failure modes. He is
the one person she does not have to translate for.

**Priya** — twenty-five years younger, better at the ecology, and unwilling to let Wren round a
number to make a season sound survivable. Wren respects this and finds it exhausting. Priya is
the one who names what accepting Alpha's offer actually means, and Wren does not forgive her
for it quickly.

**Dane** — she likes him, which is the problem. He is not a liar and he is not slick. He is
offering, sincerely, the only working plan anyone has brought her in thirty years.

**Alpha** — they meet twice. He has read her papers, all of them, and is genuinely interested.
He is also the only person who has ever talked to her as though the record she built were an
input to a decision rather than the decision itself.

## Drawn

Short, wide-shouldered, moves like someone who has carried gear all her life. Reading glasses
pushed up into grey hair. Sun damage across the nose and cheekbones, the specific weathering of
people who work on snow. Hands are the detail to draw: cracked knuckles, tape on two fingers,
always holding something — a notebook, a drill core, a mug she forgot.

**Never draw her looking noble in a landscape.** She is usually crouched over equipment with
her back to the view.
