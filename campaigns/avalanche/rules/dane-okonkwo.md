# Dane Okonkwo

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **In one line** | Alpha Dynamics' field lead, offering the only plan anyone has actually brought, and meaning every word of it. |
| **Age** | 41 |
| **Wants** | The ice to stay, the programme to run, and the people who know most about it to be on the team. |
| **Needs** | To notice that he has stopped being able to imagine the answer being no. |
| **Wound** | He watched a good project die in committee while the thing it was meant to protect was destroyed, and he decided never again. |
| **Tell** | Does the arithmetic out loud, in front of you, and hands you the paper. |
| **Voice** | Warm, direct, unhurried. Concedes points instantly and genuinely. Asks real questions and waits for the answer. |
| **Palette** | Black Alpha Dynamics softshell with no visible branding, steel-rimmed glasses, close-cropped grey at the temples, immaculate boots that are actually used. |

## Who he is

Dane runs cryosphere intervention for Alpha Dynamics in the field. He is an engineer by
training, a project lead by trade, and the most competent logistician any of these scientists
have ever worked with. When he says a ship will be there in March, the ship is there in March,
which after thirty years of grant cycles is close to a religious experience for Wren.

**He is not a company man in the cynical sense.** He believes the work is good and he is largely
correct. The book's difficulty is that he is offering something real, for decent reasons, and
the reason to refuse is not on the spreadsheet.

## Why he is sincere

He does not think of himself as buying anyone. He thinks of himself as *finally getting the
resources to the people who know what to do with them*, which is the thing every scientist in
this book has wanted their whole career. When Priya accuses him of turning a colony into
inventory, he is genuinely hurt, and his answer is honest: the alternative on the table is the
colony ending, and nobody has brought him a third option.

**He should win at least one argument outright.** Somewhere in the book he asks a question
nobody can answer: *if you refuse this, what specifically happens to the birds?*

## His blind spot, precisely

He has confused **delivering an outcome** with **serving the people who wanted it**. He cannot
conceive of a reason to decline that is not either squeamishness or ego, because inside Alpha
Dynamics such a reason has never been allowed to be legible. The word "dependency" reaches him
as a slur against work he is proud of.

This is Alpha's error arriving through a decent person, which is how it actually spreads —
alpha.md is clear that Alpha has followers and that they are not fools.

## His arc

1. **The arrival.** He shows up with a plan, a budget and a schedule, and no condescension.
2. **The offer.** He is straightforwardly persuasive and does not oversell. He volunteers the
   risks.
3. **The delivery.** The intervention works. He is right, publicly, and does not gloat.
4. **The terms.** He presents the renewal as a formality, because to him it is one. This is the
   scene where the reader sees it and he does not.
5. **The question.** Priya's phrase comes back to him in a quiet moment and does not leave.
6. **The end.** He does not defect and does not repent. He does one small thing: he makes the
   monitoring data open, which he has the authority to do and nobody asked for. It is not enough
   and he knows it. **Do not redeem him further than this.**

## How he talks

- Plain and specific. Numbers with sources, and he says when he is unsure.
- Concedes fully rather than partially: "That's right. I don't have an answer to that."
- Never uses corporate abstraction — no "solutions", no "partners", no "journey".
- Asks about the other person's work, and listens, and remembers it next time.

### Lines that sound like him

> "Two ships, March and September, and a crew of forty. I can show you the whole cost."

> "You're right that it doesn't fix the ocean. It keeps the ice in that bay. That's all I said
> it does."

> "I've watched the careful version. The careful version is how we got here."

> "If you say no, tell me what happens to the colony. Not in principle. In March."

## Relationships

**Wren** — genuine professional regard, which becomes genuine friendship, which is the most
dangerous thing in the book. He has read her whole record and is the first person in years to
treat it as decisive rather than decorative.

**Priya** — she does not like him and he knows why, and he respects her for saying it to his
face. He is more careful around her than around anyone.

**Tomas** — they get on immediately, two field people. Dane offers him the sensor network, and
means it as a gift, and cannot understand the refusal at all.

**Alpha** — he has met him four times, and each one is the reason he works here. He is not a
sycophant; he is a man who was given resources by someone who took his work seriously, and has
never examined the price of that.

## Drawn

Tall, easy in his body, the only person in the book whose gear fits properly. The visual note
is **absence of branding** — Alpha Dynamics is present everywhere in this book as quality, never
as a logo. His equipment is better than everyone's and unmarked.

The recurring image is **Dane with a tablet turned outward**, showing someone the figures rather
than reading them off. He always turns the screen around. That gesture is his whole character:
here, look, I'm not hiding it — and it is completely sincere, and it is still a sales technique.
