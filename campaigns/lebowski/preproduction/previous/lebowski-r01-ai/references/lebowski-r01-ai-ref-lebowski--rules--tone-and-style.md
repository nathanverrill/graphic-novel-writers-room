this book is a parody-homage to The Big Lebowski, set in Hanumangarh, Rajasthan — a
personal, non-commercial experiment

the film style is the Coen Brothers: wide symmetrical establishing shots, dry close-ups
held a beat too long, naturalistic dusty-gold light, violence of weather, one saturated
dream sequence; the tone is deadpan, never winking at the reader

the comic layout style is old-school: rectangular grid tiers with bordered panels and
visible gutters, classic caption boxes top-left, flat retro color with a halftone-dot
feel, bilingual signage drawn into the world

film quotes are short famous lines only ("Far out, man", "The Dude abides", "Am I
wrong?"), recontextualized; all other dialogue is original

the bowling alley is called GODARAZ — never translated, never respelled; the z is always
oversized on the sign

the parrot Duda speaks both Hindi and English and quotes the film at the worst possible
moments; the parrot is a prominent figure, not a background gag

the canonical scene: The Dude drinks a white Russian made with fresh buffalo milk and
says "Far out, man" — this must happen on the page

the opening mirrors the film's opening: a runaway cotton bale for the tumbleweed, Baba
Tau's narration for the Stranger, and The Dude in his bathrobe shopping at a street
market stall (not a grocery store), paying for buffalo milk by post-dated cheque for ₹69

stray dogs and cats are involved throughout: Moti's pack is the town's surveillance
network and the cat Billi saves the day exactly once

Hanumangarh is drawn real: the Junction/Town split, Bhatner fort, the Ghaggar, the mandi
economy (cotton, mustard, kinnow), canal-fed green, scheduled power cuts

The Big Lebowski's characters appear in bit parts only; the new characters carry the story
