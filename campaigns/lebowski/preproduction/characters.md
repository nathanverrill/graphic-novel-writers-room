# lebowski — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- This is a parody-homage to *The Big Lebowski* in which the new Hanumangarh characters carry the story; the film's characters appear only in bit parts. `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`
- Character behavior is deadpan and naturalistic rather than winking. The comedy comes from held reactions, misplaced arguments, bureaucratic absurdity, sudden weather, and characters treating strange references with complete confidence. `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- Animals are active characters, not background decoration: Duda is prominent and bilingual; Moti's pack functions as the town's surveillance network; Billi saves the day exactly once. `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`
- The central character dynamic concerns a small-town dream, family money, development pressure, bureaucracy, and the Dude's accidental influence. The Dude teaches nobody intentionally, but people learn from his calm. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- Film quotations remain short and sparing. The characters' other dialogue is original. `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`

## Characters

### Gopal “Guddu” Godara

**Role:** Early-thirties builder and owner of GODARAZ, Hanumangarh Junction. He is the principal dreamer behind the town's first bowling alley. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Look:** Wiry, with an oiled side-part. He wears a windcheater over a kurta and is always carrying a folder of permits that never has the right permit in it. `(input/characters.md)`

**Voice:** Earnest and fast-talking. He has “catchphrase energy” and repeatedly describes his ambitions as **“International standard, saab.”** `(input/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Guddu is incapable of a small dream. He has converted a family-disused cotton warehouse beside the mandi into a four-lane bowling alley because he believes Hanumangarh deserves an “international” third place of its own. He is earnest rather than ironic, and his confidence is larger than his practical control of permits, machinery and money. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Bua-ji:** His aunt and financial backer. Bua-ji has made him a formal family loan covering the conversion costs, with no ordinary interest but a fixed deadline. The warehouse is collateral. Her money makes GODARAZ possible, while her deadline and willingness to sell the warehouse make her the most immediate threat to his dream. After Saand's sabotage is exposed, she refuses to call the loan and defends GODARAZ. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Pinty:** His electrician and technical collaborator. Guddu depends on Pinty's ability to keep the alley's imported machinery alive through jugaad. `(input/characters.md)`
- **The Dude:** Guddu recruits him as a “technical consultant” because he is American and, in Guddu's reasoning, must know bowling. The Dude's calm becomes an accidental lesson to Guddu and the others. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Thekedaar Saand:** A direct opponent over the warehouse. Saand wants to replace Guddu's bowling alley with a marriage hall. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Inspector Chaudhary:** Guddu's adversary in the permit process. Chaudhary's thoroughness and deliberate dry stamp pad frustrate Guddu's urgency. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Carries the wrong permit folder everywhere.
- Explains GODARAZ's international status with the oversized z in its name: “because the z makes it international.” `(input/story-beats.md)`
- Argues intensely about practical and ceremonial details while larger problems wait, including the alley being unpowered. `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- Attempts to build and open the alley on an extremely compressed timetable.
- By the ending, he delegates practical decisions, accepts help without correcting every contribution, and allows the town's improvised solutions to remain visible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** He comes from a kinnow-orchard family in Hanumangarh Junction. His parents are deceased or absent from the business. He failed one engineering degree with honors and later saw *The Big Lebowski* at an engineering-hostel screening, after which he became fixated on building a bowling alley. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Arc:** Guddu begins as an overextended dreamer dependent on family money and a belief that imported equipment and the right branding can make GODARAZ “international.” Under pressure from Bua-ji, Saand, the permits, the unreliable pin-setter and the town's practical obstacles, he becomes part of a collective defense of the alley.

Guddu learns that “international standard” is not imported equipment or branding but a place that other people can claim as their own. He begins delegating practical decisions, accepts help without correcting every contribution, and lets the town's improvised solutions remain visible. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

### Bua-ji

**Role:** Guddu's aunt, financier of GODARAZ, and owner or ruler of a mustard-trading house operated from a courtyard office. She is the senior surviving manager of the family assets through the mustard trade. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** In her courtyard office she is surrounded by ledgers, a landline and sacks of mustard seed used as guest seating. No further physical description is established. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**Voice:** Commanding and economical. She slams the landline for emphasis. Her manner is that of a terrifying ledger-keeper who treats money, deadlines and accountability as material facts rather than emotional subjects. `(input/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Bua-ji is practical, financially powerful and initially positioned as the obstacle to Guddu's dream. She has made a formal family loan covering GODARAZ's conversion costs, with no ordinary interest but a fixed deadline. The warehouse is collateral. She gives GODARAZ a one-month deadline to open before Diwali or she will sell the warehouse to Saand.

Guddu's parents are deceased or absent from the business, and Bua-ji became the senior surviving manager of the family assets through the mustard trade. She funds him because she recognizes the same stubborn ambition in him that kept the family business alive. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Beneath the financial pressure, she secretly keeps a GODARAZ flyer folded in her cash box, revealing a private investment in the dream she publicly disciplines. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Guddu:** Aunt, lender, family authority and apparent antagonist at the beginning of the alley's struggle. Her loan has no ordinary interest but carries a fixed deadline, and the warehouse is collateral. She controls the money and therefore the deadline, but her hidden flyer suggests affection or belief she does not openly express. After Saand's sabotage is exposed, she refuses to call the loan. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`
- **Saand:** Initially connected to him through the possible sale of the warehouse; later becomes his opponent when she switches sides. She publicly withdraws the sale and refuses his bid. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **GODARAZ:** Her practical liability becomes something she fiercely defends. `(input/characters.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Slams the landline for emphasis.
- Keeps a folded GODARAZ flyer hidden in her cash box.
- Maintains terrifyingly precise ledgers.
- Uses sacks of mustard seed as seating in the courtyard office. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**History:** She runs the family's mustard-trading interests from the courtyard office and supplies the money behind Guddu's project. The former cotton-press warehouse is inherited family property held through her mustard-trading household. Title descended through the family line, and the other claimants have either died or surrendered their interests, giving her authority to sell it. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`

**Arc:** Bua-ji begins as GODARAZ's banker and apparent antagonist, insisting on the one-month opening deadline. She secretly cares about the alley, then openly switches sides against Saand with “the full force of the mustard lobby,” becoming its fiercest defender. Her decision to defend GODARAZ includes using the mustard-trading house's commercial account and local influence to secure a new power contractor before opening. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

### Pinty

**Role:** GODARAZ electrician, pin-machine whisperer and specialist in improvised repair. `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** Wears a tool belt made from a cricket-pad strap. Sunglasses are pushed up at all times. `(input/characters.md)`

**Voice:** Speaks mostly in spark-and-solder metaphors. His technical confidence is expressed through the language of electrical faults, wiring and repair. `(input/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Pinty is a maestro of jugaad: practical, inventive and willing to keep an imported system alive with whatever the town has available. He fixes the Mandarin pin-setter with a bicycle chain and faith. The machine's original drive belt is cracked and slips; Pinty's bicycle chain replaces the drive linkage well enough to reset the pins. The machine remains noisy, slow and vulnerable to power fluctuations. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`

His work is the technical counterpart to Guddu's oversized dream. `(input/characters.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Guddu:** Technical collaborator and employee or partner in the alley's construction and operation. `(input/characters.md)`
- **The Dude:** His work makes the Dude's unhurried participation possible, including the test night and final tournament. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Walter:** His improvised solutions become subject to Walter's rigid league-rule scrutiny when power cuts affect pin adjudication. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Uses a cricket-pad strap as a tool belt.
- Keeps his sunglasses pushed up.
- Consults the Mandarin pin-setter manual like an oracle, even though it is consulted wrongly by the group. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- Wires the alley to a tractor during a power cut and later finds a way to project the tournament at Bhatner fort during another outage. `(input/story-beats.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**History:** He has the hands-on skill to maintain a Mandarin-labelled machine that arrives without a usable manual. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Arc:** No complete personal arc is established. His continuing function is to turn breakdowns into workable, visually specific improvisations and to keep the alley operating against infrastructure and weather.

### Thekedaar Saand

**Role:** The contractor and GODARAZ's principal property rival. He wants the cotton warehouse for a marriage hall. `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** Flanked by two polite goons who keep apologizing. His office has a desk “you could land a plane on.” He is associated with a half-built fountain shaped like himself. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**Voice:** Delivers speeches about self-made men with total confidence. His manner carries the energy of the film's wealthy Big Lebowski figure, but he is a new Hanumangarh character rather than the same person. `(input/characters.md)`, `(references/big-lebowski.md)`

**Who they are:** Saand is a contractor whose vision of civic prestige is a marriage hall with a fountain shaped like himself. He represents money, development rhetoric and property power pressing against Guddu's smaller, stranger community space. He is confident enough to have polite goons apologize while carrying out intimidation or sabotage. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Guddu:** Rival over the warehouse and the future use of the site. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Bua-ji:** A possible buyer of the warehouse until she changes sides. He ultimately loses the sale when she withdraws it and publicly refuses his bid. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Jesus Quintana:** Jesus is the touring ten-pin hustler from Jaipur whom Saand hires for the opening-night tournament. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Brandt:** Brandt manages Saand's luxury hotel and laughs nervously at everything Saand says. `(input/characters.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Gives speeches about being self-made.
- Uses a desk of absurd scale.
- Keeps a half-built self-shaped fountain at the marriage-hall site.
- Operates through two apologetic goons.
- Sabotages GODARAZ by causing the power contractor to quit, spreading a rumor that the lanes are haunted and stealing the dhurrie. His goons offer the contractor a larger job and imply that GODARAZ's unpaid account or unsafe wiring will make the contractor responsible for any failure. The contractor removes his temporary service equipment, leaving GODARAZ with its internal wiring but no dependable external supply. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** He is established as a contractor seeking the disused cotton warehouse for a marriage hall. No earlier personal history is supplied. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Arc:** His pressure escalates from a property bid to sabotage and a public tournament challenge. After the dhurrie and sabotage are exposed, Bua-ji withdraws the sale and publicly refuses his bid. The marriage-hall project loses its claim to the warehouse. Saand remains free and loudly insists that the defeat is temporary. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

### Inspector Chaudhary

**Role:** Municipal permits inspector. `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** His defining visual prop is a stamp pad that is dry on purpose. He works behind a municipal counter where the queue-management system is one bench and seniority. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**Voice:** Thorough, formal and unhurried. He has never once been in a hurry. His bureaucratic logic is delivered as if it were a natural or cosmic law. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**Who they are:** Chaudhary is not corrupt; he is worse, because he follows procedure with deliberate completeness. He embodies bureaucracy as mythology. One permit requires a photograph of the applicant “with the premises, both smiling.” `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Guddu:** Applicant and frustrated petitioner. Guddu needs his permits quickly; Chaudhary's process refuses urgency.
- **GODARAZ:** An application to be processed rather than a dream to be encouraged or opposed. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Keeps the stamp pad dry on purpose.
- Makes people wait according to a one-bench seniority system.
- Treats the exact requirements of permits as self-evident, including the smiling premises photograph.
- Functions as a recurring deadpan closer through the dry stamp pad and his refusal to hurry. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**History:** No personal history is established beyond his municipal role and his consistent devotion to thorough procedure.

**Arc:** No personal arc is established. His fixedness is the joke and the pressure: he remains an immovable bureaucratic force while the others improvise around him. GODARAZ receives municipal approval only after Guddu supplies the smiling-premises photograph, waits through the seniority queue, pays the ordinary fees and brings a replacement stamp pad. Chaudhary stamps the papers without changing his expression. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Baba Tau

**Role:** Chai-stall elder at the mandi gate and narrator. `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** Associated with the chai stall at the mandi gate. No further physical description is established. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**Voice:** Performs the Stranger's drawl in Haryanvi-tinged English. He claims to have seen every film ever made **“before it released.”** `(input/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Baba Tau presents himself as an omniscient local elder whose authority comes from observation, confidence and impossible cultural reach. He narrates the story from the social edge of the mandi, giving local life the formal weight of a Western or a film myth. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **The town:** His chai stall is a point of observation at the mandi gate.
- **GODARAZ and Guddu:** He frames their story and returns to close the narration. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Bhatner fort and the town's past:** He cites Akbar's chroniclers to settle a bowling dispute, using historical authority for an absurdly immediate argument. `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Narrates from the chai stall at dawn and dusk.
- Claims to have seen every film before release.
- Uses references to Bhatner fort and Akbar's chroniclers to settle matters that have nothing to do with history. `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**History:** No personal history is established beyond his long-standing presence as the mandi-gate elder and his claim to universal film knowledge.

**Arc:** He bookends the story rather than undergoing a stated transformation. His narrative authority remains deliberately disproportionate to the events he describes.

### Duda

**Role:** Rose-ringed parakeet, GODARAZ mascot, prominent bilingual heckler, star witness and closer of scenes. `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** A rose-ringed parakeet who perches on the lane-3 ball return. Lane 3 is slightly crowned and is treated as Duda's lane. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**Voice:** Speaks both Hindi and English. He quotes *The Big Lebowski* at the worst possible moments. His established repertoire includes:

- **“The Dude abides”**
- **“Dude ji abide karte hain”**
- **“Far out, man”**
- **“Yeh galeecha, man”** about the dhurrie. `(input/characters.md)`

He is not limited to background squawks: his bilingual timing interrupts, reframes and sometimes exposes the people around him. `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Duda is an active observer with better information than the people in the alley. He overhears events, repeats phrases until they mutate, and delivers film quotations precisely when they are least helpful. He is both mascot and social nuisance, with enough useful knowledge to become a witness. `(input/humor-beats.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

During the dhurrie theft, Duda witnesses the theft from the lane-3 ball return and repeats the goons' distinctive phrase. He does not hear the full sabotage plan. His testimony corroborates the dogs' physical evidence without solving the case alone. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **GODARAZ:** Mascot and recurring presence, especially around lane 3.
- **Billi:** They occupy separate ball returns: Duda uses lane 3's return while Billi claims the one Duda does not use. `(input/characters.md)`
- **Moti and the pack:** Part of the alley's animal information network, though their methods differ: the dogs track by scent and movement while Duda overhears and speaks. `(input/story-beats.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- **The Dude:** Repeats and translates the Dude's lines, including “Far out, man” and “The Dude abides.” `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Perches on the lane-3 ball return.
- Quotes the film bilingually at moments of maximum inconvenience.
- Repeats phrases such as “International standard, saab,” the dhurrie line and “Far out, man” until they return in altered form. `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- Testifies about witnessing the dhurrie theft and repeating the goons' phrase. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`
- Gets the final word: **“Dude ji abide karte hain.”** `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**History:** He lived for five years above a video parlour before becoming associated with GODARAZ. `(input/characters.md)`

**Arc:** Duda's role develops from prominent mascot and heckler into a corroborating witness and scene-closing voice. He gets the final word: **“Dude ji abide karte hain.”** `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

### Moti

**Role:** Leader of the five stray dogs that adopt GODARAZ as territory. The pack forms the town's surveillance network. `(input/characters.md)`, `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`

**Look:** Moti has one ear up and an incorruptible nose. The other four dogs are not individually described. The pack is repeatedly associated with sleeping in exact circles of shade. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

**Voice:** No speech is established. Moti communicates through territorial behavior, scent and observation.

**Who they are:** Moti and the pack know more than the humans do. Their territory is limited to the area around GODARAZ and the routes between the alley and Saand's marriage-hall site. Moti identifies the scent; the other four dogs mark and guard the trail until Guddu and Duda notice their behavior. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Their incorruptible nose gives them a form of intelligence that contrasts with human arguments and bureaucratic confusion. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **GODARAZ:** The pack adopts the alley as its territory.
- **Billi and Duda:** They form the animal ensemble that repeatedly sees, hears or understands what the humans miss. `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- **Saand:** The pack identifies and trails the stolen dhurrie to his marriage-hall site. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Operates as a five-dog surveillance network.
- Moti keeps one ear up.
- Moti follows scent with an incorruptible nose.
- The pack sleeps in exact circles of shade.
- The dogs always know who is responsible before the humans do. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/godaraz-and-town.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**History:** They are stray dogs who adopt GODARAZ as territory. No individual histories are established.

**Arc:** The pack shifts from recurring territorial presence to active investigators whose tracking helps recover the stolen dhurrie. Their established function is to make animal knowledge materially useful to the human story.

### Billi

**Role:** Stray cat and silent judge of GODARAZ. `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** Billi sits on the ball return Duda does not use. No further physical description is established. `(input/characters.md)`

**Voice:** No speech is established. Her communication is stillness, refusal and judgment.

**Who they are:** Billi moves for no one and accepts no thanks. She is the animal embodiment of indifference: she sees what happens, judges it, and does not volunteer assistance except once. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/humor-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Duda:** Claims the ball return Duda does not use, creating a fixed visual opposition between the parrot's noisy commentary and the cat's silence.
- **GODARAZ:** Occupies the alley as though it belongs to her.
- **The human characters:** Judges them without participating in their arguments except at the one necessary intervention. `(input/characters.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Sits on the ball return.
- Moves for no one.
- Appears as a held deadpan reaction at the end of scenes.
- During the recovery of the dhurrie, refuses to move from beneath a cement-bag stack. When the stack is shifted, her movement reveals a second stolen object or physical evidence confirming Saand's involvement. She saves the investigation rather than physically rescuing a person, and accepts no thanks. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`

**History:** No history is established.

**Arc:** Billi remains an otherwise immobile, judgmental presence. Her one consequential intervention occurs during the recovery of the dhurrie and provides the evidence that confirms Saand's involvement. She saves the investigation exactly once. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Dude

**Role:** Older American bowler, accidental technical consultant to GODARAZ and the person whose arrival activates the homage structure. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Look:** He remains visually identical to the film's Dude: bathrobe, sunglasses and chappals bought at the Hanumangarh bazaar. He shops in the bathrobe at the mandi-side street stall. He bowls the final frame in the bathrobe. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Voice:** Unhurried, kind and laconic. He calls things “man.” His required line after tasting the buffalo-milk white Russian is **“Far out, man.”** `(references/big-lebowski.md)`, `(rules/tone-and-style.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Who they are:** The Dude is serene, unemployed or otherwise without a stated conventional occupation, and perpetually available to be acted upon by other people's schemes. He was booked onto the wrong spiritual tour, intended to end in Rishikesh, but stays in Hanumangarh because the fresh-buffalo-milk white Russians are that good. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

After Guddu invites him to stay, The Dude sleeps in a spare office or storage room at GODARAZ. He remains there from the first test night through the opening, making his continuing presence part of the alley's accidental community. `(rules/decisions.md)`

He does not set out to teach anyone anything. His calm, passivity and ability to remain unhurried become useful to people who are overwhelmed by permits, money, machinery, weather and rivalry. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Guddu:** Recruited as a “technical consultant” because he is American and therefore presumed to know bowling. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **Bua-ji and the GODARAZ group:** His presence makes him part of the alley's community without his deliberately choosing that role.
- **Duda:** Duda repeats his phrases and translates his film identity into bilingual local commentary.
- **Walter:** Walter treats the test-night power-cut problem as a matter of league rules while the Dude remains unbothered. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- **The town:** He is an outsider who stays, making his foreignness a source of mistaken expertise rather than authority.

**Habits and tells:**

- Wears a bathrobe and bazaar-bought chappals in public.
- Shops at the street market, sniffs the buffalo-milk ladle and pays for one pouch of milk with a post-dated cheque for **₹69**. `(input/story-beats.md)`
- Mixes a white Russian with fresh buffalo milk.
- Gives a single nod as a deadpan scene closer. `(input/humor-beats.md)`
- Bowls the final frame in his bathrobe.
- Remains calm during a power cut and other escalating disruptions. `(input/story-beats.md)`

In the final tournament, Jesus leaves one pin standing after an earlier power interruption creates a disputed frame. The Dude calmly rolls the final ball, knocks down the remaining pin and wins the tournament by one point. The victory depends equally on Pinty's technical recovery and Walter's ruling. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** He is the older version of the film character. A travel agent mistakenly booked him onto a spiritual tour intended for Rishikesh; he reached Hanumangarh instead and chose to remain. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Arc:** The Dude begins as a transient outsider who has arrived by mistake and stays for the drinks. He becomes, without intending to, part of Guddu's project and the alley's community. His endpoint is not a conventional transformation: he remains himself, bowls the final frame in his bathrobe and abides. `(input/story-beats.md)`

### Walter Sobchak

**Role:** Technical bowling-rules authority and intermittent adviser to GODARAZ. He is not the representative of a formal pre-existing league; the opening-night tournament is created specifically for the event. Saand publicizes it as a challenge between the two competing futures for the warehouse. Walter's authority is limited to resolving technical bowling disputes, not granting institutional recognition. `(rules/decisions.md)`, `(input/characters.md)`

**Look:** When he arrives in person for the tournament, he carries a laminated rulebook. No further appearance is established. `(input/characters.md)`

**Voice:** Volcanic, rule-bound and argumentative. His established line is **“Am I wrong?”** `(input/characters.md)`

**Who they are:** Walter treats league rules as scripture. He is more concerned with formal adjudication than with the practical fact that the alley is unpowered, creating the wrong-argument comedy beat. He has strong opinions about power cuts and whether a pin toppled during one counts. `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **GODARAZ:** Acts as an external rules authority whose technical rulings matter during the opening-night tournament.
- **Guddu and Pinty:** Argues with them about pin adjudication while their immediate technical problem remains unresolved.
- **The Dude:** His rule intensity contrasts with the Dude's unbothered calm. `(input/story-beats.md)`

**Habits and tells:**

- Appears mostly by video call.
- Carries a laminated rulebook when he attends the tournament in person.
- Treats a toppled pin during a power cut as a matter for formal argument.
- Repeatedly asks, **“Am I wrong?”** `(input/characters.md)`, `(input/story-beats.md)`

**History:** No history beyond his role as a bowling-rules authority is established.

**Arc:** No personal arc is established. His function is to bring rigid external rules into collision with GODARAZ's improvised local reality. His ruling validates the disputed frame that leaves Jesus with one standing pin, enabling the Dude's one-point final-frame victory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Donny
