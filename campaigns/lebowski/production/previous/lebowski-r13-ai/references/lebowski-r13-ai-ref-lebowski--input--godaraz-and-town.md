# The world — Hanumangarh, and Godaraz

## The town

Hanumangarh, northern Rajasthan, on the Ghaggar river — a two-part town: **Hanumangarh
Junction** (the railway side, where Guddu's family lives) and **Hanumangarh Town** (the old
side, under the walls of **Bhatner fort**, one of India's oldest forts — brick-built,
roughly 1,700 years old, mentioned by Akbar's chroniclers). The economy is the mandi:
cotton, mustard, wheat, gram, and the kinnow citrus the district is proud of. Canal
irrigation keeps it greener than the Rajasthan of postcards; winters are cold, summers are
a furnace, and a dust storm can eat an afternoon without warning.

Texture to use: mandi loudspeakers announcing auction lots; tractor-trolleys stacked with
cotton; the 5am train horn from the Junction; kinnow pyramids at fruit stalls; wedding
bands rehearsing in the wrong key; power cuts that everyone schedules around; stray dogs
asleep in exact circles of shade; a camel cart double-parked next to a new SUV.

## Godaraz

A disused cotton-press warehouse beside the mandi, re-skinned as a four-lane bowling
alley. Tin roof, whitewash over old cotton-bale stencils, festoon lights borrowed from the
wedding-supply shop. The sign says **GODARAZ** in English and Hindi, the z painted twice as
large as any other letter. Inside: four lanes of imported pine (one slightly crowned — lane
3, the parrot's lane), a Mandarin-labelled pin-setting machine kept alive by Pinty's
jugaad, a counter serving chai, kachori and — for one customer — white Russians made with
fresh buffalo milk. Maude's folk-art mural, "The Ascent of the Pin," covers the back wall.
The hand-woven **dhurrie** from the bazaar lies at the entrance; it really ties the alley
together.

House rules are posted and bilingual. Rule 1: THE PARROT IS NOT AN UMPIRE. Rule 2: the
parrot is sometimes an umpire.

## Recurring places

- **The market stall** — the mandi-side sabzi and dairy stall where The Dude shops in his
  bathrobe; fresh buffalo milk from a steel urn, measured by ladle; the stallkeeper takes
  his post-dated ₹69 cheque with professional sorrow.
- **Bua-ji's courtyard office** — ledgers, a slammed landline, sacks of mustard seed used
  as guest seating.
- **The municipal office** — Inspector Chaudhary's counter; a queue management system made
  of one bench and seniority.
- **Baba Tau's chai stall** — the mandi gate; the narration happens here, dawn and dusk.
- **Saand's marriage-hall site** — scaffolding, a half-built self-shaped fountain, the
  stolen dhurrie rolled up behind the cement bags.
- **Bhatner fort walls** — where the town watches the tournament's final frames projected
  on whitewash during the power cut, because Pinty had one more idea.
