# lebowski — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type: explicit showrunner decision and pitch.** This is a parody-homage to *The Big Lebowski*, transplanted to Hanumangarh, Rajasthan, for a personal, non-commercial experiment. The film's recognizable energy is retained, but the story is original and the new Hanumangarh characters carry it. The film's characters appear only in bit parts. `(rules/tone-and-style.md; input/pitch.md; input/story-beats.md)`

The intended experience is deadpan, weathered and affectionate: wide, symmetrical town images; dry reactions held too long; dusty-gold natural light; abrupt weather; bureaucratic absurdity; overlapping arguments about the wrong thing; and one saturated dream sequence. The comic should feel like an old-school, bordered-panel story with visible gutters and classic captions, while the humor remains straight-faced rather than winking at the reader. `(rules/tone-and-style.md; input/humor-beats.md)`

The central dramatic question is whether Gopal “Guddu” Godara can open and keep his bowling alley, GODARAZ, as a third place for Hanumangarh before family money and Thekedaar Saand's development scheme take the building away. The Dude's accidental presence, the animals' information network, and the pressure of permits, power cuts and sabotage determine whether the alley becomes real. `(input/story-beats.md; input/godaraz-and-town.md)`

The story is about “a small town getting a third place of its own; the comedy of development, bureaucracy and family money; and an unemployed man's gift for being exactly calm enough.” The Dude teaches nobody anything on purpose, and everybody something by accident. `(input/story-beats.md)`

The emotional destination is the survival and public opening of GODARAZ. Guddu learns that “international standard” is not imported equipment or branding but a place that other people can claim as their own. He begins delegating practical decisions, accepts help without correcting every contribution, and lets the town's improvised solutions remain visible. `(rules/decisions.md; input/story-beats.md)`

Bua-ji, initially the money behind the project and effectively its antagonist, becomes its fiercest defender. The Dude remains essentially himself—passive, kind and unhurried—but his ability to abide becomes useful to the people who are doing the work around him. `(input/characters.md; input/story-beats.md)`

## Premise

Gopal “Guddu” Godara, an earnest, fast-talking failed engineering student from Hanumangarh Junction, is converting his family's disused cotton-press warehouse beside the mandi into the town's first bowling alley: **GODARAZ**. He calls it “international” because the final **z** makes the name feel international.

The warehouse is inherited family property held through Bua-ji's mustard-trading household. Bua-ji has made Guddu a formal family loan covering the conversion costs, with no ordinary interest but a fixed pre-Diwali deadline. The warehouse is collateral. If Guddu does not open, she can sell it to Thekedaar Saand, who wants a marriage hall with a fountain shaped like himself.

The Dude arrives in Hanumangarh through a travel error, stays for buffalo-milk White Russians and sleeps at GODARAZ. Guddu recruits him as a technical consultant because he is American and therefore presumed to know bowling. The Dude's calm is an accidental influence, not a lesson he intends to teach.

GODARAZ must survive permits, a cracked pin-setter belt, scheduled power cuts, Saand's sabotage, a haunted-lane rumor and the theft of its entrance dhurrie. Moti's five-dog pack tracks the stolen dhurrie, Duda corroborates the theft in bilingual testimony and Billi reveals confirming evidence exactly once. Bua-ji then reverses position, withdraws the sale and defends the alley. After a mural unveiling, dust storm and disputed tournament frame, The Dude wins the opening-night contest by one point.

## Structure

### Act I — The dream acquires a deadline  
**Pages 1–7**

Hanumangarh and GODARAZ are established as a place trying to become something new without ceasing to be itself. Guddu's dream is made urgent by Bua-ji's deadline and Saand's bid. The Dude arrives, drinks, joins the alley and becomes part of its accidental community. The first test proves the alley can function, but only provisionally.

**Act I turn — Page 7:** Saand's pressure becomes sabotage. The power contractor removes his temporary equipment, leaving GODARAZ with no dependable external supply.

### Act II — The place is attacked, and the town investigates  
**Pages 8–16**

Permits and machinery remain survivable because Guddu and Pinty can improvise. Saand's sabotage changes the problem from “can Guddu finish?” to “is someone actively preventing the alley from opening?” The dhurrie is stolen, the haunted rumor spreads and GODARAZ's public credibility weakens. The animals convert observation into evidence.

**Midpoint — Page 11:** Moti's pack finds the stolen dhurrie at Saand's marriage-hall site. The threat is no longer an abstract property dispute.

**Act II turn — Page 16:** Billi's evidence and Duda's testimony confirm Saand's involvement. Bua-ji publicly withdraws the sale and refuses to call the loan. She secures a new power contractor, turning family capital against Saand.

### Act III — The public place claims itself  
**Pages 17–22**

The mural makes GODARAZ publicly legible as more than Guddu's private project. The alley opens under Diwali lights despite restored-but-imperfect power. Saand answers with spectacle, Jesus and a public challenge. Weather interrupts; the community returns. The Dude's final roll completes the contest, but the victory belongs equally to Guddu's delegation, Pinty's repair, Walter's ruling, Bua-ji's defense and the town's presence.

**Climax — Page 20:** The Dude knocks down Jesus's final standing pin and wins by one point.

**Closure — Page 22:** Baba Tau observes that the alley remains crooked, doubtful and improvised, but open. Duda gets the final word.

## Page plot

### Page 1
A wide dawn view follows a runaway cotton bale from Hanumangarh Junction across the railway tracks, past sleeping dogs and canal-fed green. **End beat:** the bale rolls toward Bhatner's brick walls.

### Page 2
The bale passes beneath Bhatner fort and reaches the mandi-side warehouse, stopping against the shutter marked by old cotton-bale stencils. Baba Tau's caption introduces “a man for his time and place.” **End beat:** the shutter lifts just enough to reveal the oversized **z** of GODARAZ inside the unfinished building.

### Page 3
At a mandi street stall, The Dude—bathrobe, sunglasses and chappals—sniffs buffalo milk, pays for one pouch with a post-dated **₹69** cheque and watches a television promise development. Guddu recruits him as an American bowling expert. **End beat:** Guddu points toward GODARAZ while the Dude looks toward the wrong road.

### Page 4
In Bua-ji's courtyard office, she gives Guddu one month before Diwali. The warehouse is collateral; Saand waits with his absurd desk, apologetic goons and self-shaped fountain plan. Guddu insists, “International standard, saab.” A glimpse of the folded GODARAZ flyer sits in Bua-ji's cash box. **Page-turn reveal:** the deadline is not a threat in theory—Bua-ji has already placed Saand's sale papers beside the ledger.

### Page 5
Guddu lays the bazaar dhurrie at the entrance. A dog immediately pees on it while Moti's pack sleeps in exact circles of shade. Inside, Guddu and Pinty face Chaudhary's municipal requirements: smiling photograph, seniority queue and dry stamp pad. **End beat:** the Mandarin pin-setter jams with one pin hanging crookedly.

### Page 6
Guddu and Pinty return to the municipal counter with the smiling-premises photograph, ordinary fees and a replacement stamp pad. Chaudhary stamps without changing expression. Back at GODARAZ, Pinty replaces the cracked drive linkage with a bicycle chain. Duda claims lane 3's ball return; Billi occupies the other. **End beat:** the machine resets the pins, but its chain screams.

### Page 7
The first test night gathers a few curious bowlers. The scheduled power cut kills the alley mid-frame. Pinty connects a tractor; the lanes glow in uneven temporary light. The Dude makes a buffalo-milk White Russian and says, **“Far out, man.”** Walter appears on video, arguing about whether a pin toppled during the outage counts. **Page-turn reveal:** outside, Saand's goons speak privately to the power contractor beside his service equipment.

### Page 8
Saand's goons offer the contractor a larger job and imply that GODARAZ's unpaid account or unsafe wiring will make him liable. He removes the temporary equipment. Inside, Guddu finds the empty service point and tries to keep the workers moving. **End beat:** the alley's festoon lights go dark one row at a time.

### Page 9
The haunted-lane rumor spreads through the mandi. The Mandarin manual is held open like scripture while people interpret its unreadable diagrams. Duda repeats “haunted” in Hindi and English. Guddu argues that a machine can be broken without being supernatural. **End beat:** the entrance is shown without its dhurrie.

### Page 10
The theft is reconstructed in visual fragments: apologetic goons lifting the dhurrie; Duda watching from the lane-3 return; the goons repeating their distinctive phrase. Duda reports only what he heard, not the entire sabotage. Guddu discovers the power contractor's departure and the theft occurred in the same pressure campaign. **Page-turn reveal:** Moti's nose reaches the empty entrance and turns toward the road to Saand's site.

### Page 11
Moti leads the five-dog pack through the mandi routes. The other dogs mark and guard the trail while Guddu, Duda and The Dude follow. Cotton, kinnow, mustard sacks and traffic interrupt the path without stopping it. **End beat:** the dogs halt before Saand's half-built self-shaped fountain.

### Page 12
At the marriage-hall site, the stolen dhurrie is found rolled behind cement bags. Saand's goons apologize too quickly. Saand calls it an unfortunate coincidence and gestures toward his fountain. **End beat:** Billi is visible beneath the cement-bag stack, refusing to move.

### Page 13
Workers begin recovering the dhurrie. Billi does not move for anyone. The Dude waits; Guddu impatiently asks for a lever; Bua-ji watches the site and says nothing. **End beat:** the cement stack shifts, and Billi slips out carrying no object—but something remains revealed in the dust.

### Page 14
The revealed item is a distinctive piece of the power contractor's removed temporary equipment, bearing the same service markings as the equipment taken from GODARAZ. Billi returns to the ball return without accepting thanks. Duda identifies the goons' phrase and repeats it in Hindi and English. **Page-turn reveal:** Saand's confident face loses its composure as Bua-ji lifts the evidence.

### Page 15
At GODARAZ, Saand denies ordering anything. Duda's testimony corroborates the dogs' trail and Billi's evidence without pretending to know the full plan. Guddu begins to argue, then stops and lets Duda finish. Bua-ji opens her ledger and compares Saand's offer with the sabotage evidence. **End beat:** she tears the sale tab from the ledger.

### Page 16
Bua-ji publicly withdraws the sale, refuses to call Guddu's loan and turns the mustard-trading house's commercial force against Saand. She secures a new service contractor. Saand remains free, insisting the defeat is temporary. Guddu tries to correct the contractor's plan, then lets Pinty and Bua-ji decide. **Page-turn reveal:** a truck arrives carrying the covered mural.

### Page 17
Maude's mural, **“The Ascent of the Pin,”** is unveiled across GODARAZ's back wall: a rising pin as local saint and civic monument, surrounded by kinnow, mustard flowers, fort brick and tiny bowlers. The recovered dhurrie returns to the entrance. Guddu leaves the repaired chain and mismatched wiring visible. **End beat:** Diwali lights ignite outside as the crowd gathers.

### Page 18
Opening night begins. Saand presents Jesus Quintana, head to toe in purple, rolling his ball from a velvet cricket bag. The first four panels are the fixed dust-storm interruption: the crowd and lights disappear into dust, everyone shelters, and GODARAZ becomes a dark rectangle in the storm. **Page-turn reveal:** the storm has cleared the same night, and people are already returning.

### Page 19
The crowd resumes the tournament. The restored service holds until a power fluctuation interrupts a frame. Jesus is left with one standing pin. Walter, carrying his laminated rulebook, rules that the frame remains valid; Pinty gets the noisy chain-driven machine moving again. **End beat:** the single pin stands under the oversized **z**.

### Page 20
The Dude steps onto lane 3 in his bathrobe and chappals. Duda declares that the parrot is not an umpire; the contradictory rule board hangs behind him. The Dude rolls without hurry. The ball takes the crown of lane 3 and knocks down the last pin. **Climax:** GODARAZ wins by one point.

### Page 21
The town occupies GODARAZ: chai, kachori, children, workers, the dogs' shade circles, Billi on her return and Duda on lane 3. Bua-ji watches Guddu accept help without revising every detail. Saand leaves beneath his own fountain, still insisting the defeat is temporary. The Dude settles into the spare office as though nothing unusual occurred. **End beat:** Guddu looks at the crooked lane and says, quietly, “International standard.”

### Page 22
At dawn or dusk, Baba Tau returns to the mandi gate. His captions note the dry stamp, crooked lane, doubtful machine and parrot who is not an umpire—but the alley has opened. The final panel holds on Duda cocking his head above the chai stall: **“Dude ji abide karte hain.”**

## Reader vs. character knowledge

### Reader knows before the characters

- The opening image establishes the warehouse as a former cotton-press site before Guddu explains the project.
- Bua-ji's folded flyer shows that her deadline is not indifference, even while Guddu reads it as opposition.
- The reader sees Saand's goons approach the power contractor before Guddu knows the outage is sabotage.
- The reader sees Duda witness the dhurrie theft, while Guddu initially has only the absence of the object.
- The reader sees Billi beneath the cement bags before the humans understand why she refuses to move.
- The reader knows the dust storm clears the same night when the page turn reveals the returning crowd.
- The reader understands that the final victory depends on Pinty's repair and Walter's ruling, not solely on The Dude.

### Characters know before the reader

- Bua-ji knows the exact financial terms and sale authority before Guddu understands how final the deadline is.
- The Dude knows the buffalo-milk White Russian works before the rest of the alley accepts it as a serious drink.
- Moti's pack knows the dhurrie's route before any human notices the trail.
- Duda knows the goons' distinctive phrase but not the whole sabotage plan; this limitation must remain clear.
- Saand knows whether he commissioned the sabotage before the evidence is assembled.
- Chaudhary knows the permit process is proceeding normally while Guddu experiences it as an absurd emergency.
- Walter knows his technical ruling is narrow; the characters around him are tempted to treat it as larger institutional authority.

## Causal spine

1. Guddu's oversized dream requires Bua-ji's money.
2. Bua-ji's money creates the pre-Diwali deadline and makes Saand's offer credible.
3. The Dude's accidental arrival supplies companionship, a consultant-shaped body and the buffalo-milk drink.
4. Permits and the machine slow Guddu but are survivable through patience and repair.
5. The first test proves the alley can function while exposing dependence on temporary power.
6. Saand exploits that vulnerability, then attacks the alley's reputation and identity through rumor and theft.
7. Duda's witness and Moti's tracking create a lead; Billi's one intervention supplies confirmation.
8. The evidence changes Bua-ji's choice, which changes both ownership pressure and the power problem.
9. The mural publicly marks the changed ownership before the final test.
10. The tournament converts the property dispute into a public contest.
11. Weather and power interrupt the contest, but the community resumes it.
12. The Dude's roll is possible because the collective has made the alley usable.
13. GODARAZ opens imperfectly, which proves the theme rather than weakening it.

## Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

### Proposal — Billi's confirming evidence

Billi reveals a recognizable piece of the temporary power contractor's removed equipment beneath the cement bags, bearing the same service markings as the equipment taken from GODARAZ. This is the current plotting proposal because it confirms both the theft and the power sabotage without making Billi explain the case.

**Decision needed:** Showrunner approval of this specific object. The brief permits another object or physical evidence.

### Proposal — Final fort projection

The tournament remains centered at GODARAZ. The fort projection is not required for the one-point victory and is therefore omitted from the page plot to protect the final-frame intimacy and the 22-page budget.

**Decision needed:** Whether to restore Pinty's tournament projection onto Bhatner's whitewashed fort walls. If restored, it should replace or share Page 19 without displacing the disputed frame.

### Proposal — Ordinary customers

The page plot uses the town as a visible crowd at opening and as users of GODARAZ on Page 21, without adding named customers or a separate women's bowling group.

**Decision needed:** Whether the showrunner wants a recurring ordinary customer or women's bowling group made explicit. This would make the third-place theme more legible but would require a defined visual or page-space commitment.

### Proposal — Opening-night public framing

Saand's challenge is public through his tournament presentation and the assembled crowd, but no new formal league or civic institution is introduced. Walter remains a technical rules authority only.

**Canon protection:** Do not add a formal league, municipal contest body or punishment mechanism without approval.

## Stakes

- Guddu may lose his dream, family reputation and access to the warehouse.
- Bua-ji may lose control of inherited family property or allow a family project to fail.
- Hanumangarh may gain a locally rooted third place or receive another conventional event venue.
- GODARAZ must become both legally permitted and physically usable.
- Saand's marriage-hall plan depends on Guddu's failure; his defeat must remain commercial rather than punitive.
- The alley's reputation is threatened by the haunted rumor and missing dhurrie.
- The final tournament must prove public ownership without turning the story into a sports story.
- The emotional stake is whether Guddu can stop treating the alley as a private extension of his ambition and let other people make it theirs.
