# First Reader Report: Project Lebowski Auditions

Read cold, page by page, in order Version A then Version B.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, Panel 4:** The way the cotton bale finishes its journey at a specific physical destination—the whitewashed warehouse with the Godaraz sign underneath—creates an immediate sense of place and momentum. 
- **Page 2, Panel 4:** The reveal of the oversized English **z** on **GODARAZ** under the half-lifted shutter. It’s a strange, striking image that instantly anchors the surreal tone.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, Panels 2 and 3:** I skimmed slightly over the intermediate stops of the bale (the kinnow stall, the camel cart) because they felt a bit episodic rather than driving a narrative thread.

### I was confused
- **Page 3, Panel 1:** The introduction of the post-dated ₹69 cheque made me pause—is the ₹69 a joke about amount, or is currency conversion being played with? It’s minor, but it took a second to register the specific absurdity.

### I felt something
- **Page 2, Panel 2 & 3:** A sense of whimsical, mythic scale when Baba Tau starts dropping lines like "In Hanumangarh, every story begins with a thing that has already been moving." It immediately sets a specific folk-tale-meets-slacker atmosphere.

### I predicted
- **Page 3, Panel 4:** I predicted that Guddu is going to drag the Dude into the bowling alley whether the Dude wants to go or not, purely based on the classic mismatched-buddy setup.

---

## Version B

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, Panel 4:** The big reveal of the under-construction interior through the partially lifted shutter, paired with Baba Tau's dry caption, hits with great visual weight.
- **Page 3, Panel 3 & 4:** The rapid-fire dialogue between Guddu and the Dude ("I was headed to Haridwar, man" / "Haridwar can wait") is punchy and immediately sets up their dynamic.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, Panels 1 through 4:** Because Version B uses captions for Baba Tau right from Page 1 over a continuous sequence, I found myself reading the captions and half-skimming the visual descriptions of the rolling bale, treating it as montage rather than lived action.

### I was confused
- **Page 1, Panel 1:** The caption says "Every story needs a man for its time and place," which is a direct riff on the classic Lebowski intro, but dropping it over a stray cotton bale before any character is on screen felt a bit untethered.

### I felt something
- **Page 3, Panel 2:** The Dude's line "Everything's post-dated if you think about it long enough" made me smile—it captures the exact philosophical laziness of the character translated into a new setting.

### I predicted
- **Page 2, Panel 3 & 4:** When the bale hits the shutter, I predicted the shutter is about to lift to show whatever bizarre project is inside.

---

## Comparison

### Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
**Version A.** The moment that decided it was **Page 2, Panel 4**. Version A lets the environment and the action speak first, introducing Baba Tau *at his chai stall* on Page 2 watching the bale arrive, rather than turning him into an omniscient voiceover narrator on Page 1. Version A grounds the reader in the physical space of Hanumangarh before layering on the voiceover myth-making, making the strange arrival of the bowling alley feel earned and grounded.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **Version A:** A dusty, sunlit fable that casually drops a bowling alley into Rajasthan and lets the characters wander into it at their own lazy pace.
- **Version B:** A clever, self-aware adaptation that leans heavily into meta-commentary right from the first caption, feeling a bit more constructed and less like an organic world.
