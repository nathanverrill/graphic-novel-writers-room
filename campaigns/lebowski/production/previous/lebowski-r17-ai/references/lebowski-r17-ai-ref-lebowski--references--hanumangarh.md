# Hanumangarh — research notes (gathered from the web, 2026-10-01)

Real-world grounding for the setting. Facts below are from public sources; links at the end.

## The town

- City and municipal council in northern Rajasthan, on the banks of the **Ghaggar river**,
  roughly 400 km from Delhi. Two distinct halves: **Hanumangarh Junction** (grown around
  the railway junction) and **Hanumangarh Town** (the older settlement by the fort).
- Winters genuinely cold, summers extreme; canal irrigation (waters of the Ravi, Sutlej
  and Beas system) makes the district far greener than stereotype Rajasthan.

## Bhatner fort

- Considered one of the oldest forts in India: built ~1,700 years ago, attributed to
  Bhupat, son of Jaisalmer's King Bhatti. Brick-built, covering about 52 bighas, shaped
  as a parallelogram with a dozen bastions a side.
- Significant enough that Akbar's chronicle, the Ain-i-Akbari, mentions it. The fort
  stands over the Ghaggar.

## Economy and daily life

- Agriculture-first district: roughly 64% of workers are cultivators or agricultural
  labourers. The town's rhythm is the **mandi** (agricultural market yard).
- Krishi Upaj Mandi Samitis operate at Hanumangarh and nearby towns (Sangaria, Pilibanga,
  Nohar, Rawatsar, Bhadra, Goluwala); the traded commodities are paddy, **cotton**, wheat,
  **mustard**, gram and other pulses, bajra, barley, sesame.
- Fruit: **kinnow** (a mandarin hybrid) is the district's signature crop, with mausambi
  and date palm also grown. Kinnow pyramids at fruit stalls are an honest local image.
- Market towns in the district absorb migrant workers as vendors, cart pullers, dhaba
  workers, mandi labourers and small entrepreneurs — a believable labor pool for building
  and running Godaraz.

## Texture the pages can trust

Mandi loudspeakers calling auction lots; tractor-trolleys of cotton; buffalo dairies
selling fresh milk by the ladle from steel urns; scheduled power cuts; stray dogs and
cats around the market; the dawn train horn from the Junction side; the fort's brick
walls as the town's constant backdrop.
