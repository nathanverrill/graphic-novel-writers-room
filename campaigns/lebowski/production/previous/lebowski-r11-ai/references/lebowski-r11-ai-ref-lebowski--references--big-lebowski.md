# The Big Lebowski — reference notes for the parody

What the homage leans on (facts about the 1998 Coen Brothers film, described — not
transcribed). Personal, non-commercial parody experiment.

## Shape of the film

- A shaggy mistaken-identity noir: an unemployed, serene bowler (Jeffrey "The Dude"
  Lebowski) is confused with a millionaire of the same name; a soiled rug — "it really
  tied the room together" — sets off a kidnapping plot he never fully understands.
- Narrated by The Stranger, a cowboy voice with no plot function and total authority,
  who opens on a tumbleweed drifting into Los Angeles and closes with "The Dude abides."
- The opening follows the tumbleweed, then finds The Dude in a bathrobe at Ralphs
  supermarket, sniffing a carton and paying for 69-cent half-and-half by post-dated
  personal cheque while a president speaks on the store TV.

## The character energies the bit parts must keep

- **The Dude**: unhurried, kind, perpetually robed; drinks White Russians; everything is
  "man". Catch: "Far out, man", "The Dude abides."
- **Walter**: volcanic rule-lawyer and loyal friend; league rules as scripture; "Am I
  wrong?"; over-elaborate plans that detonate.
- **Donny**: sweet, permanently interrupted.
- **Maude**: imperious avant-garde artist; commissions and pronouncements from on high.
- **Jesus Quintana**: flamboyant rival bowler in purple; all swagger, own ball.
- **Brandt**: the rich man's nervous, laughing aide.
- **The Big Lebowski** (the millionaire): speeches about achievement, a giant desk, money
  that turns out to be complicated — the energy given here to Thekedaar Saand.

## Comic grammar worth copying

Deadpan wide shots; arguments that miss the point at full volume; a dream/fantasy musical
number staged around bowling; recurring objects (the rug, the ball) carrying the plot;
weathered narration bookending the story; the hero's passivity as the engine - things
happen to him and he abides.
