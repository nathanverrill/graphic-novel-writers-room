# The story — Godaraz, or: The Dude Abides in Hanumangarh

A parody-homage to The Big Lebowski, transplanted whole into Hanumangarh, Rajasthan.
Original story; the film's characters appear only in bit parts. Personal, non-commercial
experiment.

## The premise

Gopal "Guddu" Godara, a kinnow-orchard dreamer from Hanumangarh Junction, saw The Big
Lebowski once at an engineering-hostel screening and never recovered. He is converting his
family's disused cotton warehouse beside the mandi into the town's first bowling alley:
**GODARAZ** ("Godara's", with a z, "because the z makes it international"). The Dude
himself — older now, unchanged — washes up in town, victim of a travel agent who booked him
on a "spiritual tour" that was supposed to end in Rishikesh. He stays. The buffalo-milk
white Russians are that good.

## The opening — mirror of the film's opening

A runaway **cotton bale** rolls like a tumbleweed at dawn: across the rail tracks at
Hanumangarh Junction, past the sleeping dogs, under the walls of Bhatner fort, coming to
rest against the shutter of the half-built Godaraz. A narrator — **the chai-stall elder
Baba Tau**, doing the Stranger's drawl in Haryanvi-tinged English — introduces "a man for
his time and place." Cut to: **The Dude, in his bathrobe and chappals, shopping at a street
market stall** (not a grocery store — a mandi-side sabzi and dairy stall). He sniffs the
buffalo-milk ladle, pays for one pouch of milk by **post-dated cheque for ₹69**, and the
stallkeeper's TV plays a politician promising development.

## The beats

1. **Hook.** The cotton-bale opening; the Dude at the market; Guddu recruits him at the
   dairy stall as "technical consultant, saab — you are American, you must know bowling."
2. **Plot turn 1.** Bua-ji (Guddu's mustard-trade aunt, the money) gives Godaraz one month
   to open before Diwali or she sells to **Thekedaar Saand**, a contractor who wants the
   warehouse for a marriage hall. A hand-woven **dhurrie** bought at the bazaar "really
   ties the alley together." A stray dog pees on it. This cannot stand, man.
3. **Pinch 1.** Permits. Inspector Chaudhary and the ballet of stamps. The pin-setting
   machine arrives with no manual, in Mandarin. Pinty the electrician performs jugaad.
   The stray dogs move in; the cat Billi claims the ball return and judges everyone.
4. **Midpoint.** First test night: a power cut mid-frame. Pinty wires the alley to a tractor.
   The Dude, unbothered, mixes a white Russian with fresh buffalo milk, tastes it, and says
   **"Far out, man."** The parrot **Duda** repeats it in two languages. League rules arrive
   by video call from Walter, who has opinions about whether a toppled pin during a power
   cut counts. "Am I wrong?"
5. **Pinch 2.** Saand sabotages: the alley's power contractor quits, a rumor spreads that
   the lanes are haunted (the Mandarin manual is cited as evidence), and the dhurrie is
   stolen. The dogs know who did it.
6. **Plot turn 2.** The animals break the case: Moti's pack trails the dhurrie to Saand's
   marriage-hall site; Duda the parrot, who overheard everything, testifies — in Hindi and
   English, quoting the film at the worst possible moments. Bua-ji, who secretly loves the
   alley, switches sides with the full force of the mustard lobby.
7. **Resolution.** Opening-night tournament under Diwali lights: Godaraz vs. Saand's hired
   hustler (a touring ten-pin shark from Jaipur in purple). The Dude bowls the final frame
   in his bathrobe. The dhurrie is back. Baba Tau closes the narration from his stall;
   Duda gets the last word: **"Dude ji abide karte hain."**

## What it is about, under the jokes

A small town getting a third place of its own; the comedy of development, bureaucracy and
family money; and an unemployed man's gift for being exactly calm enough. The Dude teaches
nobody anything on purpose, and everybody something by accident.
