**Style (the same on every page):**

Draw Hanumangarh as a working agricultural market town, not an abstract desert: railway junction, mandi, cotton and mustard trade, kinnow, canal-fed green, Ghaggar geography, Bhatner’s brick walls, tin roofs, dust and scheduled power failure.

Use wide symmetrical establishing views, dusty-gold natural light, dry close-ups held one beat too long, and weather that behaves like an ordinary civic inconvenience with physical force. Keep faces and reactions naturalistic. Let environment replace exposition.

GODARAZ is a converted cotton-press warehouse: whitewashed industrial walls with old cotton-bale stencils showing through, a tin roof, borrowed festoon lights, four imported-pine lanes, a visibly crowned lane 3, and a large bilingual sign whose English **z** is twice the size of every other letter. The interior accumulates local repair and public ownership rather than becoming sleek.

The single saturated dream sequence is a Bollywood-scale bowling vision: marigold-covered pins, rolled-silk lanes, Guddu conducting a wedding band, and giant Bua-ji sending a ledger down lane 3. It is the only major departure from the book’s dusty-gold, flat-retro palette.

The mural **“The Ascent of the Pin”** is a three-panel folk-art/Mughal-miniature image: a rising bowling pin rendered like a local saint or civic monument, surrounded by kinnow, mustard flowers, fort brick and tiny bowling figures. It fills the back wall without obscuring the working alley.
