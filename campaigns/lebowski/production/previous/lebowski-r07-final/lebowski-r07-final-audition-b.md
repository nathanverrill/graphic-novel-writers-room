# Page 1 (right) — Hanumangarh wakes, and something is already moving before anyone decides to start it.

**Panel 1.** Wide dawn establishing shot. Hanumangarh Junction railway yard, mist and gold light. A loaded handcart tips at the edge of a platform and a single cotton bale breaks free, rolling off onto the siding tracks. A train silhouette sits distant and still.

CAPTION (BABA TAU): Every story needs a man for its time and place.
CAPTION (BABA TAU): This one starts with a bale of cotton. It already has better direction.
SFX: KHRRRRRR

**Panel 2.** The bale crosses the railway tracks at ground level. Five stray dogs sleep in a loose circle nearby, one ear lifting at the sound, otherwise undisturbed.

SFX: thunk-thunk

**Panel 3.** The bale rolls on past canal-fed green — mustard flower yellow, a kinnow orchard row — with a tractor-trolley stacked with raw cotton crossing behind it on a parallel lane.

CAPTION (BABA TAU): Hanumangarh. Not the desert you were promised. Greener, and more complicated.

**Panel 4.** The bale approaches the brick silhouette of Bhatner fort, dust rising gold in low sun, the wall dwarfing it completely.

CAPTION (BABA TAU): Seventeen hundred years old, that wall. It has seen worse mornings than this.

PAGE CHECK: Establishes Junction/tracks/fields/fort geography through one continuous journey rather than one fixed location — a deliberate exception to the single-location guideline, required by the brief's opening beat. # Page 2 (left) — Baba Tau claims the story, and the bale finds somewhere to stop.

**Panel 1.** Street level at the mandi gate. BABA TAU — wiry, elderly, wool cap, dusty shawl — sits at his chai stall pouring a glass without looking at it, watching the bale roll past.

BABA TAU: That bale is going somewhere. I am not.
SFX: glug

**Panel 2.** The bale continues along the mandi-side street, Bhatner's wall looming behind it, heading toward a shuttered warehouse front — old whitewash peeling to reveal something beneath.

CAPTION (BABA TAU): Some men build empires. Some men build bowling alleys. Hanumangarh does not always know the difference.

**Panel 3.** The bale strikes the warehouse's closed metal shutter and stops dead. Cotton-bale stencils, faded but legible, show through the whitewashed brick beside it.

SFX: KLANG

**Panel 4.** Big reveal panel. The shutter lifts a foot off the ground, pushed from inside by an unseen hand. Through the gap: a dim, dusty, under-construction interior, and on the back wall, lit by one bare hanging bulb, the bilingual GODARAZ sign — its English **z** twice the size of every other letter.

CAPTION (BABA TAU): They are calling it international standard.

PAGE CHECK: Baba Tau's first physical appearance; narrative-authority device now has a face and a stall. Reveal (the oversized z) lands on this even page per house rule. Continuity flag: cotton-bale stencils under whitewash established here, referenced later as the building's former identity. Page-turn function: who is inside, and what is this place — pulls into the market scene.

# Page 3 (right) — An American arrives in the wrong story, and a man with a folder recruits him.

**Panel 1.** Wide shot, mandi street stall — sabzi crates, a steel buffalo-milk urn, a small television propped on a shelf playing a suited politician mid-promise. THE DUDE stands at the counter in bathrobe, sunglasses and chappals, sniffing a pouch of buffalo milk with total seriousness.

TV (background, small balloon): …and I promise you, this district will see development like never—
THE DUDE: Smells about right.

**Panel 2.** Close on the STALLKEEPER accepting a cheque from the Dude — handwritten, post-dated, **₹69** clearly visible.

STALLKEEPER: Post-dated, sahib?
THE DUDE: Everything's post-dated if you think about it long enough.

**Panel 3.** GUDDU hurries into frame, permit folder clutched to his chest, already mid-sentence, looking the Dude up and down with sudden inspiration.

GUDDU: Sir — American?
THE DUDE: Depends who's asking.
GUDDU: I am asking. I need a bowling consultant. International standard, saab.

**Panel 4.** Guddu points off-panel with total confidence, already walking. The Dude remains facing the opposite direction, milk pouch in hand, unhurried.

GUDDU: This way. Very short walk to a very large opportunity.
THE DUDE: I was headed to Haridwar, man.
GUDDU: Haridwar can wait. Hanumangarh cannot.

PAGE CHECK: Single location (mandi stall) throughout, per guideline. Named-character cap respected (Guddu + Dude = 2; stallkeeper unnamed/generic, no sheet required). Voices established in four exchanges: Guddu fast and self-justifying, the Dude laconic and unbothered. Canon beats delivered: ₹69 post-dated cheque, buffalo milk, Dude's travel error, Guddu's recruitment logic. Page-turn function: Guddu is already walking toward GODARAZ — the Dude hasn't agreed to anything yet, which pulls forward into the deadline/Bua-ji pressure on page 4.
