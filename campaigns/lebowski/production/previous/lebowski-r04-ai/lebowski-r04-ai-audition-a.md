## Page 1 (right) — A runaway cotton bale carries the reader from Hanumangarh’s railway life toward the unfinished place Guddu is trying to build.

**Panel 1.** Wide dawn establishing view of Hanumangarh Junction. A cotton bale has broken loose from a tractor-trolley and rolls across the railway tracks. Beyond it: the first green fields, low buildings, sleeping stray dogs and the pale line of Bhatner’s brick walls. No people need to be recognizable.

SFX: **KRRR—THUMP**

**Panel 2.** The bale rolls past a kinnow stall being opened for the day. A steel ladle hangs above a buffalo-milk urn. The stallkeeper watches the bale pass without moving quickly enough to stop it.

SFX: **THUMP**

**Panel 3.** The bale crosses a dusty road beneath Bhatner fort. A camel cart and a new SUV sit nose-to-nose, both waiting for the other to move. The bale passes between them.

SFX: **KRRR**

**Panel 4.** Low, symmetrical view: the bale rolls toward a whitewashed cotton-press warehouse beside the mandi. Old cotton-bale stencils show through the paint. The tin roof and an unfinished bilingual sign are visible above the shutter.

SFX: **THUMP**

PAGE CHECK: The story moves from railway and agricultural town to the warehouse. The bale is now aimed at the GODARAZ shutter. Page-turn function: reveal what is behind the shutter.

## Page 2 (left) — The cotton bale stops at the warehouse, and Baba Tau names the story before the project is fully visible.

**Panel 1.** The cotton bale bumps against the closed warehouse shutter. Dust settles around it. Above the shutter, only the lower edge of the unfinished sign is visible. The old cotton-bale markings remain beneath the whitewash.

SFX: **TOK**

**Panel 2.** At the mandi gate nearby, Baba Tau sits at his chai stall with a chipped glass of tea. He is a wiry elderly man of short height with a long weathered face, deep-set brown eyes, a white mustache and sparse white hair beneath a faded wool cap. He wears a dusty shawl over a kurta-pajama. The warehouse sits in the background, small but centered.

CAPTION: In Hanumangarh, every story begins with a thing that has already been moving.

**Panel 3.** Baba Tau looks toward the warehouse. A railway horn hangs in the distance; the mandi is beginning to stir. His expression is certain, as if he has seen this exact story before.

CAPTION: This one concerns a man for his time and place.

**Panel 4.** The shutter lifts only a narrow amount. Inside the dark warehouse, the oversized English **z** of **GODARAZ** appears first—twice the height of every other letter. Through the gap: the silhouettes of four bowling lanes and hanging festoon lights.

SFX: **KRRRNNNK**

PAGE CHECK: Baba Tau frames the tale, and the shutter reveals the first impossible glimpse of GODARAZ. The oversized **z** is unmistakable. Page-turn function: move inside and introduce the outsider.

## Page 3 (right) — At a street stall, The Dude buys buffalo milk on credit and is invited into Guddu’s unfinished dream.

**Panel 1.** Mandi-side street stall, morning. A steel urn of buffalo milk, kinnow pyramids and vegetables fill the foreground. The Dude stands at the stall in a worn bathrobe, sunglasses and bazaar-bought chappals, holding a single pouch of buffalo milk. A small television behind the stallkeeper shows a politician promising development. The stallkeeper studies the post-dated **₹69** cheque with professional sorrow.

STALLKEEPER: Post-dated?

THE DUDE: It’s, like, a very near date.

**Panel 2.** Close on the cheque beside the milk pouch and the stallkeeper’s hand refusing to take it.

STALLKEEPER: The buffalo is not post-dated.

THE DUDE: Far out.

**Panel 3.** Guddu enters the edge of the stall, permit folder clutched against his chest. He is in his early thirties, wiry, of average height, with an earnest narrow face, alert brown eyes and an oiled side-part. He wears a windcheater over a kurta, practical trousers and worn shoes. He looks from the cheque to The Dude, already deciding something.

GUDDU: You are American?

THE DUDE: Among other things.

**Panel 4.** Guddu points decisively off-panel toward GODARAZ. The Dude looks the opposite way, toward the railway road. Behind them, the television shows the words **NEW DEVELOPMENT** above a smiling politician.

GUDDU: Bowling. International standard, saab.

THE DUDE: I was headed to Jaipur.

PAGE CHECK: The Dude has a local drink, a reason to stay temporarily and an accidental invitation. Guddu mistakes nationality for bowling expertise. Exit hook: Guddu must redirect him toward GODARAZ.
