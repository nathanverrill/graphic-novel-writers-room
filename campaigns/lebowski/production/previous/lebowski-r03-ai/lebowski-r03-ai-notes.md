# Verdict

**Needs a pass.** The script now restores the fort projection and makes the tournament score legible, but it contains one direct canon contradiction and several production-level violations of the showrunner’s drawability rules. The sabotage chain and Bua-ji’s deadline logic also remain under-explained, so the emotional reversal is not yet fully earned on the page.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. The Dude gives the wrong intended destination

- **Location:** Page 3, Panel 4.
- **Observation:** The Dude says, “I was headed to Jaipur.”
- **Problem:** Canon fixes the intended spiritual-tour destination as **Rishikesh**. Jaipur is also already the home base of Jesus Quintana, creating avoidable geographic and continuity confusion.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Replace the destination with Rishikesh. Preserve the travel-error joke without adding a new explanation.
- **Role:** Writer

### Major

#### 2. Saand’s responsibility for the power sabotage is still not visually demonstrated

- **Location:** Pages 7–8 and Pages 13–16.
- **Observation:** Page 7 shows the goons speaking to the contractor; Page 8 shows the contractor loading equipment; Page 13 reveals a matching equipment fragment under Saand’s cement bags.
- **Problem:** The reader can infer a connection, but never sees the goons or Saand take custody of the removed equipment or place it at the marriage-hall site. The fragment proves that equipment travelled, not who ordered the transfer. Bua-ji’s reversal still risks resting on circumstantial coincidence.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Add one unmistakable visual link without adding a new location: on Page 8, show a goon loading or directing the contractor’s removed equipment toward Saand’s site, or show the same marked equipment already visible among Saand’s cement bags. Keep Duda’s knowledge limited to the dhurrie theft.
- **Role:** Writer

#### 3. Bua-ji’s deadline status is unclear when she reverses the sale

- **Location:** Page 4, Panel 2; Pages 15–16; Page 18.
- **Observation:** Bua-ji gives Guddu one month before Diwali, but the script never establishes whether Page 16 occurs before or after the deadline. Page 16 says the warehouse is not for sale, while Page 18 presents opening night without clarifying the contractual timing.
- **Problem:** “Sabotage is not failure” has no precise contractual meaning unless the reader knows whether Guddu missed the deadline and Bua-ji is granting an exception, or whether the deadline has not yet arrived.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Add one concise temporal anchor in Page 4 or Page 15. For example, establish that the opening deadline is still ahead but the sabotage threatens to make it impossible, or establish that the deadline has technically passed and Bua-ji explicitly waives the failure because it was engineered.
- **Role:** Writer, Director

#### 4. Page 15 exceeds the named-character limit

- **Location:** Page 15, Panel 1 and overall page staging.
- **Observation:** The page includes Saand, Guddu, Duda and Bua-ji in the same scene, with all four functioning as named characters.
- **Problem:** The showrunner’s drawability rule allows at most three named characters in one panel. The current page also asks the image model to stage multiple humans, a parrot, the dhurrie, the equipment fragment, the office and the cash box. This is a high-drift page at the exact moment when evidence and emotional judgment must be clear.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Keep no more than three named characters in any panel. Move Guddu outside the office doorway or remove him from the scene after he stops arguing; let Bua-ji, Saand and Duda carry the judgment. Keep the evidence as a clear tabletop or foreground object.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 5. Page 17 is not a clean splash despite being assigned a major visual reveal

- **Location:** Page 17.
- **Observation:** Panel 1 is labeled “Full-page splash,” but Panels 2 and 3 are added as narrow inset panels on the same page.
- **Problem:** The showrunner’s rule says a big moment gets a splash: one panel, the whole page. The mural unveiling is explicitly a major public transformation and must not be diluted by inset staging. The current format also risks shrinking the mural and making the image model prioritize secondary details.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Make Page 17 one full-page panel containing the mural, working lanes, visible chain repair, mismatched wiring, recovered dhurrie, Moti and Duda. Move Guddu’s reaction and the animals’ positions into that single composition, or defer them to Page 18.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 6. The fort projection is restored but competes with the disputed-frame action

- **Location:** Page 19, Panel 3.
- **Observation:** Pinty repairs the pin-setter while the tournament image projects onto Bhatner’s fort wall beyond the open doors.
- **Problem:** This panel contains the machine repair, Pinty, the crowd, the open doors, the fort projection and the tournament context. The fixed projection now exists, but its inclusion risks making the central causal handoff unreadable: Walter’s ruling, Pinty’s repair and Jesus’s standing pin must all be immediately clear.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Keep the projection as a small environmental rectangle or reflected image, not a second action plane. Make the pinsetter linkage and the standing pin the dominant visual information. If the fort cannot read at this scale, use it as a narrow background band rather than a second focal point.
- **Role:** Layout Agent

#### 7. The power systems are distinguished in dialogue but not fully in the images

- **Location:** Pages 7–8.
- **Observation:** Page 7 shows Pinty connecting a tractor to “the temporary power service equipment.” Page 8 shows the contractor loading “the temporary service equipment,” while the tractor remains useless.
- **Problem:** The reader may still read the tractor setup and contractor equipment as the same thing, making Saand’s sabotage and the tractor’s limitation unclear.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Give the systems distinct visual identities: show the tractor cable as a crude emergency jumper and the contractor’s temporary service equipment as a separate meter/generator/service box. Page 8 should show the contractor removing the latter while the tractor remains visibly insufficient for ordinary operation.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 8. The buffalo-milk White Russian is not clearly shown being made and consumed

- **Location:** Page 3, Panels 1–2; Page 7, Panel 2.
- **Observation:** Page 3 establishes the milk purchase. Page 7 shows The Dude mixing ingredients and saying “Far out, man,” but the panel description does not explicitly show the finished drink or him tasting it.
- **Problem:** The canonical scene is not merely the purchase or ingredient list; the Dude stays because of the buffalo-milk White Russians. The current staging may read as an interrupted preparation rather than the completed localized drink.
- **Smallest repair direction:** In Page 7, show the finished buffalo-milk White Russian visibly in The Dude’s hand and his mouth just after tasting it. Keep the line “Far out, man.” The drink should be visually distinct from the milk pouch.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent

#### 9. The score handoff is clearer but still needs one visual confirmation of the final point

- **Location:** Page 19, Panel 4; Page 20, Panels 1 and 4.
- **Observation:** Page 19 shows “GODARAZ 48 — SAAND’S TEAM 48” and says the standing pin is the final point available. Page 20 shows the Dude knocking it down.
- **Problem:** The arithmetic is now inferable, but the page turn does not explicitly confirm that the final pin changes the score to a one-point victory. The fixed outcome should not depend on the reader calculating it from prose.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Add a compact scoreboard or reaction in Page 20, Panel 4 showing **49–48**, without giving the scoreboard more visual weight than the pin impact.
- **Role:** Writer, Letterer

### Minor

#### 10. The Page 7 location change risks violating the one-location-per-page rule

- **Location:** Page 7, Panels 1–4.
- **Observation:** Panels 1–3 occur inside/at GODARAZ; Panel 4 moves outside to the contractor and goons.
- **Problem:** The page’s turn is effective, but the page must introduce the exterior service point clearly enough that the image model does not merge the outside conversation into the interior test. This is especially important because the power equipment later becomes evidence.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Treat the exterior as the immediate threshold of GODARAZ, not a separate distant location. Keep the service equipment, warehouse wall and oversized sign visible in Panel 4.
- **Role:** Layout Agent

#### 11. Page 3’s market panel carries too many environmental anchors

- **Location:** Page 3, Panel 1.
- **Observation:** The panel includes buffalo milk, kinnow pyramids, vegetables, The Dude, the cheque, the stallkeeper and a television.
- **Problem:** No named-character limit is breached, but the canonical cheque, milk and travel-error information compete with several background objects. The image model may lose the ₹69 amount or the street-stall identity.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Make the cheque, milk pouch and street-stall transaction the dominant foreground; keep kinnow and the television as secondary, legible background anchors.
- **Role:** Layout Agent

#### 12. The Page 18 crowd should remain non-recognizable and visually simple

- **Location:** Page 18, all panels.
- **Observation:** The script correctly calls the crowd indistinct, but the page asks the model to show Diwali lights, Jesus, the velvet cricket bag, dust, sheltering bodies, the mural and the returning crowd.
- **Problem:** The fixed four-panel storm beat may drift into a crowded spectacle, weakening the intended ordinary-civic-inconvenience tone.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Keep only Jesus as a recognizable named figure. Render everyone else as simplified silhouettes, hands, shawls and partial faces. Preserve the oversized **z** as the storm’s recurring orientation anchor.
- **Role:** Layout Agent

#### 13. The permit-folder continuity remains visually ambiguous

- **Location:** Page 6, Panel 1; Page 14, Panel 1.
- **Observation:** Guddu’s folder is established as never containing the right permit, but Page 6 shows him presenting the required materials and Page 14 has Bua-ji compare the equipment number with “a carbon copy in his permit folder.”
- **Problem:** The folder risks becoming a convenient container for whatever document the scene needs, contradicting its established visual joke.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Show the correct service record as a loose paper or separate contractor document, not as the “right permit” inside Guddu’s folder. The folder may still contain the wrong permit and miscellaneous copies.
- **Role:** Writer, Layout Agent

### Optional

#### 14. Keep the closing geography deliberately separated

- **Location:** Page 22, Panels 1–4.
- **Observation:** Panels 1–3 establish Baba Tau at the mandi gate with GODARAZ in the distance; Panel 4 cuts to Duda at the lane-3 ball return.
- **Risk:** If Panel 4 is drawn as though Duda is physically at Baba Tau’s stall, the closing frame will collapse two locations and weaken the bookend.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Preserve the cut: Baba Tau narrates from the stall, then the final panel returns clearly inside GODARAZ to Duda.
- **Role:** Layout Agent
