# Verdict

**Needs a pass.** The four development files share a strong central premise and most major beats, but the story currently contradicts a fixed infrastructure payoff and leaves the sabotage evidence causally under-supported. Resolve those items before the brief passes to writing; the remaining issues are chiefly decision labels, setup/payoff clarity and production-risk control.

## Findings by severity

### Blocker

#### 1. The fort-projection payoff is fixed in the world but explicitly omitted from the story

- **Location:** `world.md`, “Bhatner fort walls” and “Technology and Infrastructure”; `brief.md` Canon; `story.md`, “Proposal — Final fort projection” and Pages 18–20.
- **Observation:** `world.md` and the brief establish that Pinty later projects tournament images onto Bhatner’s whitewashed fort walls during a power cut. `story.md` says the projection is omitted from the page plot and treats its restoration as a proposal.
- **Problem:** The story and world do not agree about a fixed event. This also weakens the established causal chain: Pinty’s improvised infrastructure is meant to solve the tournament’s power vulnerability, but the current plot only has him restart the machine.
- **Smallest repair direction:** The Director must either restore the fort projection to canon and the Plotter must place it in the tournament sequence, or explicitly remove the projection from `world.md` and the brief before writing. Do not leave the current “fixed but omitted” state.
- **Role:** Director, Plotter

### Major

#### 2. The sabotage evidence does not yet show how Saand is connected to the removed power equipment

- **Location:** `story.md`, Pages 8, 12–16; “Proposal — Billi’s confirming evidence”; `world.md`, Saand’s contractor economy and Saand’s marriage-hall site.
- **Observation:** The contractor removes GODARAZ’s temporary service equipment after Saand’s goons pressure him. Billi later reveals a piece of that equipment beneath Saand’s cement bags, but the current sequence does not state or show how the equipment travelled from GODARAZ to the marriage-hall site.
- **Problem:** The evidence may prove that equipment was moved, but not that Saand’s goons ordered or performed the sabotage. The required reversal—Bua-ji publicly defending GODARAZ—therefore risks feeling based on coincidence rather than demonstrated bad faith.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Add one clear visual or behavioral link: for example, show the goons taking custody of the removed equipment, or establish a distinctive service marking and show Saand’s site using or hiding it. Keep Duda’s knowledge limited to the dhurrie theft; he must not accidentally become a witness to the full sabotage plan.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer

#### 3. Bua-ji’s deadline and reversal need a precise timing decision

- **Location:** `story.md`, Pages 4, 15–16; Story Map, Act II turn; `characters.md`, Bua-ji’s causality.
- **Observation:** Bua-ji gives Guddu one month to open before Diwali and threatens sale if he misses it. The Act II turn says the deadline has “technically failed,” but she then refuses to call the loan and withdraws the sale after the sabotage is exposed.
- **Problem:** The text does not establish whether the deadline has actually passed, whether sabotage makes the failure excusable, or whether the opening is still before the deadline. Without that distinction, Bua-ji’s reversal can read as arbitrary rather than as a deliberate financial and moral judgment.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Decide and state one timing rule in the plot: either the deadline has not yet arrived and Bua-ji acts preemptively, or it has arrived and her line “Sabotage is not failure” explicitly makes the contractual exception. Seed that rule when she sets the loan terms.
- **Role:** Plotter, Director

#### 4. The first-test power problem and the later contractor sabotage overlap ambiguously

- **Location:** `story.md`, Pages 7–9; `world.md`, Power.
- **Observation:** On Page 7, Pinty wires GODARAZ to a tractor after a scheduled power cut. On Page 8, Saand’s goons pressure “the power contractor,” who removes temporary service equipment, leaving no dependable external supply.
- **Problem:** It is unclear whether the tractor setup is the contractor’s temporary equipment, a separate emergency workaround, or a service arrangement that still exists after the first test. The reader may not understand what exactly Saand has taken away or why the tractor solution cannot simply recur.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Distinguish the systems visually and causally: identify the contractor’s temporary service equipment on Page 7, then show what is removed on Page 8; separately establish that the tractor is only a one-night emergency and cannot support ordinary operation.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer

#### 5. The story’s stated animal-information chain is not fully staged in the beat language

- **Location:** `story.md`, Pages 10–15; Causal spine; `brief.md` Canon.
- **Observation:** Moti finds the route, Duda corroborates the theft, and Billi reveals confirming evidence. The intended division of labor is clear in the files, but the page plot moves rapidly from Duda’s observation to Billi’s evidence without specifying what each contribution proves.
- **Problem:** Because all three animals are investigative agents, the reader could experience their roles as redundant or conclude that any one of them solves the case. That would violate the fixed distinction that Duda corroborates and Billi saves the investigation exactly once.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Define the evidentiary sequence in the plot: Moti establishes location; Duda establishes who handled the dhurrie and repeats the distinctive phrase; Billi reveals the separate physical link to the power sabotage. Have Guddu or Bua-ji articulate the distinction only once, through action or a concise line.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer

#### 6. The Dude’s final victory needs a clearer bowling-state handoff

- **Location:** `story.md`, Pages 18–20; Facts, “Walter and the tournament.”
- **Observation:** Jesus leaves one pin standing after a disputed power-interrupted frame; Walter rules on it; Pinty restarts the machine; The Dude then knocks down the final pin and wins by one point.
- **Problem:** The current sequence does not specify the score state or why knocking down one pin produces a one-point tournament victory. The reader can understand the physical action but not necessarily the competition’s arithmetic.
- **Smallest repair direction:** Establish the score or deficit before the disputed frame and repeat the essential number at the handoff to Page 20. Keep Walter’s ruling narrow and avoid turning him into the tournament’s institutional authority.
- **Role:** Plotter, Writer
