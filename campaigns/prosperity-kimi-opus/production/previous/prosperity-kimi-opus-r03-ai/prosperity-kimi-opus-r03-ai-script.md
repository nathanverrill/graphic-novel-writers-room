# ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

## Page 1 (right) — The three cities establish a world built upward, while access and agency narrow toward Keel.

**Panel 1 — FULL-PAGE SPLASH.** A distant elevated view of the fictional Andean high-desert basin in 2043. The three cities occupy one vertical mountain system.

At the bottom: **KEEL**, wind-scoured and crowded around an exhausted mine. Rusted gantries, stacked housing, patched pumps, mineral dust, narrow service lanes, workers at small access gates.

Midway up: **HALYARD**, bright and productive. Fabrication towers, laboratories, greenhouse terraces, freight corridors, engineered greenery. Three House insignias on distant towers: water, energy, mobility.

Above them, near the salar edge: **OASIS**, spacious and pale, with reservoirs, controlled gardens, clean civic architecture, frictionless automated transit.

A shared water-and-power spine climbs between the cities — broad and carefully maintained near Oasis, narrower and visibly strained toward Keel. Identical white-and-amber Alpha control nodes punctuate the infrastructure.

No principal characters are identifiable at this scale. The human achievement is immense. The human figures are small.

CAPTION: 2043.

CAPTION: OASIS.

CAPTION: HALYARD.

CAPTION: KEEL.

PAGE CHECK: The reader understands the three-city hierarchy and their physical interdependence without explanation. Page-turn function: descend from the system to one person working inside it.

## Page 2 (left) — Alex proves his physical judgment, then receives the debt and restriction that make escape impossible.

**Panel 1 — WIDE.** DAWN, end of the night shift, inside a Keel pump hall. Alex Phantum, seventeen, lean and wiry in his patched charcoal work jacket, has one palm pressed to an aging pump housing, head tilted, reading the vibration. A salvaged bracket holds the casing where the approved part should be. A maintenance instruction glows beside him, ignored. The pump runs steadily. A SUPERVISOR watches from the control terminal.

SUPERVISOR: That isn't the approved repair.

ALEX: Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.

**Panel 2 — MEDIUM.** Alex pulls his hand away. The supervisor checks the official gauge — its needle trembles in the red — then looks at the patched bracket. Under Alex's hand, the pump's vibration has stayed level the whole time. The supervisor's jaw works; he jerks his head toward the exit.

SUPERVISOR: Keep moving, Phantum.

**Panel 3 — WIDE.** At the end-of-shift checkpoint, Alex places his hand on the scanner. Other workers queue behind him, anonymous, tired. Above the scanner, his balance display expands, impossibly large.

The white-and-amber interface reads:

**TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY**
**ALEX PHANTUM — AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN**
**PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE**
**GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED**
**DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS**
**EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**
**PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS**
**TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**

A GUARD studies the display, then Alex.

SFX: BEEP

GUARD: Happy birthday, Phantum.

ALEX: I've had better surprises.

**Panel 4 — TALL MEDIUM.** Morning street. Alex passes a shuttered casino, THE LAST CHANCE, its faded lights still flickering, a peeling poster of a man's face half-torn from the wall. Half-hidden in Alex's pocket, his scarred hand turns a worn dark-ivory casino chip, its tarnished brass rim and a shallow etched equation visible. He doesn't notice he's doing it.

BOUNCER: Care to see if luck runs in the family?

ALEX: I've also got his debt.

PAGE CHECK: Alex's competence cannot protect him from a system that converts family history into liability. The guarantor line plants the missing father; the chip and casino make him a question. Page-turn function: the person who wants him to look back at Keel.

## Page 3 (right) — Alex and Ada reveal the choice beneath their friendship: escape Keel, or repair it.

**Panel 1 — WIDE.** Later that afternoon, at the railing on the pit rim, worn smooth where they always sit. Warm gold light; Keel in shadow below, Oasis catching the sun above. Alex sits on the railing, shoulders down, turning the chip between two fingers. Ada Veyra — twenty, copper hair tied back, freckles, teal-blue eyes, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder — approaches with two battered metal mugs.

ADA: Thought you might need this.

ALEX: The birthday special? Not sure I can afford it.

**Panel 2 — MEDIUM TWO-SHOT.** Ada hands him the mug and sits beside him. Their shoulders nearly touch, familiar, unselfconscious. Steam rises in the rust-gold light.

ADA: It's on me. You don't owe anybody anything today.

ALEX: Everything has a price, Ada.

ADA: Not everything.

**Panel 3 — MEDIUM.** Over their shoulders, the view splits: Alex's eyeline climbs toward Oasis; Ada's drops toward Keel's pump lines and crowded housing.

ALEX: I keep thinking about waking up somewhere I get to choose what happens next.

ADA: I keep thinking about why everybody gave up choosing it down here.

ALEX: Because nobody leaves.

ADA: Nobody stays, either. Not really.

**Panel 4 — WIDE, QUIET.** The two remain on the railing, divided by its diagonal structure. Sunlight reaches Alex's side; Ada sits partly in Keel's long shadow. She watches him, not the view, her thumb resting on the brass rivet of her strap.

ALEX: I don't want to spend my whole life paying for what he did.

ADA: Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving.

Alex looks at her. The chip stops turning in his hand.

PAGE CHECK: Ada is named, placed, and established as the counterforce to Alex's escape plan. Page-turn function: home, Grandmother, and the hidden book.
