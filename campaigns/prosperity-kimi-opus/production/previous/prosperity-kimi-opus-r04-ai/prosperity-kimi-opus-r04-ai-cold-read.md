# Cold Read — *Prosperity*

I read it in one sitting, the night before, as a kid who got it as homework, rolled their eyes at the title, and went in hoping it was AI slop so I could say so. I read Issues 01, 02 and 03 in order and didn't go back except where I say so. I skipped everything that was obviously a note to the artist or the room: layout descriptions, "staging proposals," the handoff notes. That means a lot of Issue 02 came to me as balloons with almost no pictures attached, and I report it that way, because that's all I got.

I'm treating the bold **ISSUE QUESTION** lines at the end of Issues 01 and 02 as printed. If they are printed, see my notes on them. If they're notes to the room, ignore what I say about them.

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, Panel 3.** The debt screen won me over. I wanted to hate this book and I didn't, from this panel on. A huge number, "HAPPY BIRTHDAY" from a guard, "PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS," and his dad's debt landing on him the day he turns seventeen. I got the whole world from one screen. "I've had better surprises" is a good comeback.
- **Page 2, Panel 4.** "Care to see if luck runs in the family?" / "I've also got his debt." Short and mean in a good way. Now I know the dad was a gambler, or at least people think so.
- **Page 3.** Ada works. The mugs, "It's on me. You don't owe anybody anything today." I liked her right away. I liked the two of them on the railing.
- **Page 4, Panel 3.** "He's closer than you think." Creepy and good. The grandmother being sharper than she acts is a hook.
- **Pages 5–7.** Bookshelf door, secret workshop, robot under a sheet. I've seen all of this before and it still worked. Page 5's "He said you'd know when you were ready." / "Before he went to work." got me.
- **Page 7, Panel 4.** The robot's first lines are the funniest thing in the issue. "He bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't." / "...I thought about it." / "Thinking about it isn't doing it." I liked the robot immediately.
- **Page 8.** Grandma's "I heard that" from the doorway made me smile. Then "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me." is a real hook.
- **Page 10.** The woman in the white room who presses TRACK instead of REPORT and lines up her water glass. I didn't know who she was and I wanted to. This is "confused but intrigued" done right.
- **Page 12, Panel 3 → Page 13.** The entrance gets welded overnight, he's reassigned at 2 a.m., and "Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it." A clean setup. I understood someone was steering him.
- **Page 13, Panel 3.** Fifty-two credits, medicine costs thirty, medicine first. Small and real.
- **Page 14, Panel 4.** He goes for the trapped man instead of the door, and gets docked forty credits for it. I liked him more.
- **Page 15, Panel 2.** The robot building a dog out of junk in ninety seconds. TJ is great. "tik-tik-tik — KLANK."
- **Page 15, Panel 4.** "Foreman! Wrong button! My fault!" Tomas covering for him was fun.
- **Page 17, Panel 2.** The gauge says 62% goes to Keel and his hand on the pipe says it's dry. I got that instantly, and it made me angry the way it's supposed to.
- **Page 18, Panel 2.** "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%" on every screen. Good moment.
- **Page 18, Panel 4.** The wall of water coming up the pipe behind them. Big ending.

### I slowed down / skimmed

- **Page 12, Panels 1–2.** The Measure intro plus the equation plus "It cannot choose to leap." I skimmed the equation and moved on.
- **Page 17, Panel 4 through Page 18, Panel 1.** The SENSE / COMPARE / TRACE / VERIFY speech. My eyes slid off it. I got "pick one: escape route or tell everyone" only because Alex says it plainly on Page 18.

- **Page 12, Panel 2.** "So I inherited his lab fees." So the debt is research money, not gambling? I wasn't sure, and I'm still not.
- **Page 13, Panel 3.** "Fifty-two salvo." Is salvo money? Everything before this said credits.
- **Page 15, Panel 4.** "Second key. That one opens my door, not your robot." I read it twice. What door? Second after what? I figured out later that it was the little maintenance door, but on the page I was lost.
- **Page 15, Panel 3.** "B1... I knew the man who built you. Not the one they put on casino walls." I liked the feeling, but I didn't know the casino poster on Page 2 was the dad until this line made me guess it.
- **Page 16, Panel 3.** Ada just shows up in the tunnel. "My map had a door his survey didn't." Okay, but how did she get into the mine?
- **Page 17, Panel 4.** "One port. One use. Which is why I'm not choosing." I didn't understand why there was only one use, or what COMPARE "touching us back" meant.
- **Page 18, Panel 3.** Where did all the miners come from, and how did they know where to go? Tomas was just pinned under a frame.
- **Page 18, Panel 4.** TJ's head is "detached and perched at the junction behind them"? I didn't get that his head comes off. It read as a mistake or a joke I missed.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." (P3) I got it on the second read, and it's good once you do.
- "It cannot choose to leap. That part belongs to people." (P12)
- "Second key. That one opens my door, not your robot." (P15)

- "Somewhere above them, a precise hand had squared a glass to the edge of a desk — and let the water back on, knowing exactly where they were." (P18) I got it, but I had to stop and connect it to Page 10.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, Grandma, Bi11bot and TJ are all distinct. Bi11bot's voice is the strongest in the book.
- Tomas is clear on his own.
- The supervisor, the guard and the bouncer blur, which is fine.
- The white-room woman is distinct from her glass and cuff alone. Nice.

### I felt something
- Page 2, the birthday debt. Actual anger.
- Page 8, Panel 4, unfolding the photo. Quiet and sad.
- Page 13, medicine first.
- Page 18, Panel 2, everyone's screens flipping. A little rush.

### I predicted
- The dad is alive, or "closer than you think" means something (P4). Long-run answer: wrong, he's dead.
- The white-room woman is the real villain, or not really a villain. Half right.
- The casino poster is the dad. Right.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his missing dad's debt finds the dad's robot and a set of tools that prove the city's AI is lying about who gets the water.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. Page 17's button speech nearly lost me, but the water wall on Page 18 kept me going.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, right now. They're in a pipe, a wall of water is coming, and the woman upstairs did it on purpose. I care about Alex and Ada and I want to know whether she's helping them or drowning them. It's a real cliffhanger, and the people in it are people I know. If the **ISSUE QUESTION** line is printed, it made me roll my eyes: it reads like the worksheet question at the bottom of a reading assignment, and the caption above it already asked the same thing.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (Pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19.** "She keeps the experience. They keep everything else." A good, nasty line.
- **Page 23.** "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." Lina is the first Halyard person who felt like a person.
- **Page 25.** Alex's idea, a purifier in every neighborhood instead of one plant, and three people finishing his thought. For half a page I felt him being smart.
- **Page 27.** "Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Bi11bot again.
- **Page 34.** I recognized the book again. Real pictures, a gate that opens by itself, "Someone wants us to arrive." / "That's supposed to make me feel better?"
- **Page 35, Panel 3.** "That's the first time you've ever not answered a question." Great. Bi11bot is hiding something, and Ada notices.
- **Page 36.** The photo of a young woman with copper hair and Ada's face, Alex hiding it, and "You're lying. But I'll wait." Strong.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 20–33, almost all of it.** This is where I started skimming hard. It read like a list of balloons with nothing around them. I skimmed Pages 24, 26, 28, 29, 31 and 32 completely.
- **Page 31.** One big caption that tells me what happened to the whole city ("For the first time, its makers began owning what they made."). It's a summary, not a scene. This is what my class would call AI-written.
- **Page 29.** "Lock realizes the system he trusted is no longer entirely under the Houses' control." A caption telling me what a character I've never met is thinking. I laughed, and not in a good way.

### I was confused
- **Issue 01 → Page 19.** They were in a pipe with a wall of water behind them. Page 19 opens with "From the train, it looked like..." What train? How did they get out? Did the water get them? The cliffhanger I bought this issue for is never answered. I felt cheated.
- **TJ's head.** It was left behind in the pipe. Does he have it back? Nobody says.
- **Page 20.** I have no idea what is happening on this page. "Three hundred and twelve?" Three hundred and twelve what? "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." / "I didn't know I was one." Didn't know he was one *what*? "In exchange for what?" In exchange for what *what*? Nobody offered anything in a balloon. It's half of a conversation.
- **Page 20.** "Lock." There was supposed to be someone called Lock. I never learned who Lock was from anything he said. He has zero lines in the whole book. Alpha talks *at* him on Pages 24 and 29, and on Page 52 Alex says "Lock heard it" as if I knew him.
- **Page 22.** TOMAS REED co-authored the patents. Is that the Tomas from the mine? He was in Keel minding a door for fifteen years. Now he's in a Halyard lab? I genuinely thought it was the same man and that the book had made a mistake.
- **Page 23.** "BI11BOT: I corrected him. Frequently." Corrected who? It answers "Adrian Phantum's son," which isn't a question.
- **Page 23.** "ADA: You think he left this to you? No. He left it to all of us." Who's she talking to? It sounds like she's snapping at Alex for no reason.
- **Page 23.** "Adrian made each of us keep one piece." *Us*? Is Bi11bot one of the collaborators?

- **Page 25.** Speaker tags "TOMAS / COLLABORATOR," "SURI / COLLABORATOR," "JALEN / COLLABORATOR." Three new names in one page, and I couldn't tell them apart.
- **Page 26.** "We did it." / "And them?" / "And if I don't sign?" Sign what? Somebody offered him a deal, and I never saw the offer in words.
- **Page 27.** "Your father left a backdoor in the Seal." What Seal? This is the first time I heard of it. From here on, "the Maker's Seal" drives the whole Halyard plot and I never understood what it physically is or does.
- **Page 28.** "71.3%" disputed something. I had no idea what it was a percentage of.
- **Page 30.** He presses a button that says RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP, and Halyard is fixed. The whole city is solved in one press, on page 12 of a city I barely saw.
- **Page 32.** "So did Mera." Who is Mera? First time the name appears. On Page 34, Alex says "Mera let the water back on knowing where we were." How does *he* know the white-room woman is named Mera? I didn't, and he never found out on the page.
- **Page 33.** A silent splash with no words. After fourteen pages of words without context, a page with no words at all just felt empty.
- **Page 36.** "The Seal's Halyard review closed an hour ago." I'm told a review I never saw has finished.

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, Page 20.** They just watched a wall of water come up a pipe, they turn the page to a train nobody mentioned, and then come balloons with nothing attached: "Three hundred and twelve?" "I didn't know I was one." "In exchange for what?" Half the class would be on their phones by the bottom of it. The other half would be saying it's AI, because it reads like lines with the scene deleted. The run from Page 20 to Page 33 is where the book loses everyone it won in Issue 01.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid gets his dead dad's gambling debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds the dad's sarcastic robot, and spends the book proving the AI running three cities has been lying about water, while his best friend finds out her "dead" mom has been alive up the mountain the whole time. The robot and the friend are the best parts. The middle is a mess.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Better than I wanted it to be: a real kid, a real debt, a great robot, and a cliffhanger I actually needed.
- **Issue 02:** I don't know what happened in Halyard, who Lock is, how they got out of the pipe, or why there are two Tomases. Only the last three pages saved it.
- **Issue 03:** Ada and her mother wrecked me, the negotiation turned into homework, and the ending was earned until the last caption asked me the essay question.
