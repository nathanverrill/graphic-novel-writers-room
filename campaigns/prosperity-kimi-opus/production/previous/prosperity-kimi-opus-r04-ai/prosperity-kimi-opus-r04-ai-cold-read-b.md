# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, in order, one sitting, the night before it's due. I did not want to like it.

---

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
Page 2, panel 3. The scanner display — "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / EARNED TODAY: 40 / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS" — is the first thing in the book that made me feel something, and it's just numbers on a screen. "Happy birthday, Phantum" landed. I was in from there.

Page 5, the bookshelf clicking open. Obvious move, but it works because the grandmother is so calm about it. Page 7, Bi11bot waking up — "He bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't" — first line in the book I actually liked as a line.

Pages 14–18 are the best stretch in the issue. The accident, the wage forfeiture ticking on screen while he decides, the dog built out of spare parts, Tomas's wrist guard. I read these fast and didn't skim any of it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 1. A full page of city description with four captions that are just names. I get that it's pretty. I learned nothing I cared about. If this were a shop copy I'd have flipped past it.

Page 11, panel 3 — the history lecture. "Twenty years of water crises... Alpha was built to analyze, predict, and coordinate. Then, in the emergency, it was asked to decide." I felt the book sit down and start explaining itself. I skimmed and waited for the characters to talk again.

### I was confused
Page 9, panel 4 and Page 10. The pulse traveling up the mountain, then a woman from behind in a white room pressing TRACK. I understood *something* was watching, but I didn't know who she was, and the book never tells me in this issue. I assumed she was the villain. (She sort of is and sort of isn't, I found out two issues later.)

Page 17, panel 4: "SENSE tells you what's moving. COMPARE touches Alpha's line... TRACE maps a private way out. VERIFY sends the truth to Keel." Four capitalized functions dumped in one balloon while doors are closing. I read it twice and still only kept VERIFY. The choice on page 18 worked anyway because Alex says it in plain words right after.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Alpha" — used on page 1 (the control nodes) and page 10 before anyone explains it on page 11. I guessed "the computer that runs everything" and was basically right, but the book leaned on the name before earning it.
- "The water wars" — referenced in Bi11bot's lecture, never actually shown this issue. Fine as a hook, but it's a name I was carrying around unexplained.
- The chip and the equation (A = K + ρU) — shown matching on page 12 and never explained what the equation *is*. I still don't know. I noticed I stopped expecting to.
- "Safeguards" — not named yet, but the Measure/Seal/Covenant structure is clearly a set of three and the book assumes I'll track that. I did, barely.

### Lines I read twice
- "He didn't hide it in the mine. He hid it in the lie." (p.17) — read twice, liked it the second time.
- "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." (p.3) — read twice, still not sure it parses. I moved on.
- "Safer for whom?" — fine. "Which is why he built an answer. Sit down — it starts with a pump you already know." — the second half of that balloon is doing too much; I stumbled.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandmother, Bi11bot — clear, distinct voices. Tomas is clear once he talks. The supervisor, guard, bouncer, foreman are furniture, fine. The woman in the white room is deliberately a mystery, but she's the one I most wanted a name for and didn't get.

### I felt something
Page 8. The hologram, and the grandmother saying "I heard that" from the doorway with her hands stopped. That got me. Also page 13, panel 3 — fifty-two credits, medicine is thirty, medicine first. Quiet and it hurt a little.

### I predicted
That the father was dead and the debt was a frame or at least more complicated than "gambling." That the woman in white was connected to the father. That Ada's "I'm finding out who decided it had to stay this way" meant she'd turn out to be connected to someone important — I called that on page 13 and felt smug at page 36.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his father's debt and his father's hidden tools for proving the system is lying, and has to decide whether to expose it or escape it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 1, honestly — a pretty establishing page with no person in it is a bad first page for a reader who didn't choose the book. If I'd made it to page 2, the scanner display would have kept me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but weakly. The water coming up the conduit behind them is a decent physical cliffhanger, and "a precise hand had squared a glass" tells me the mystery woman is playing a longer game — that's the part that pulls me, not the flood. The "ISSUE QUESTION" printed on the page annoyed me. I can ask my own questions; being handed one felt like homework, which this already is.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 1; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (Pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
Page 23, Lina Vey: "Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution." Best line in the issue; I read it out loud. Pages 27–30 — the Seal waking up, "It remembers everyone," "Only those the system was taught to forget" — this stretch moved and I didn't skim.

Page 36, the unsealing and the copper-haired woman. I said "oh no" out loud. Best page-turn in the book so far.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 19–22 are a slog. The arrival caption is one long paragraph of "Every building promised... Every screen celebrated... Every creator seemed to work for someone else" — I got the point at the first "Every" and skimmed the rest. Then the Registry scene is Alex asking questions ("What does 'intellectual estate' mean?" "In exchange for what?") with answers I mostly didn't get on the page. Page 20's locked dialogue has Alex saying "I didn't know I was one" and I genuinely could not tell what "one" referred to without re-reading — a Charter House? An estate holder? Still not sure.

Page 25's montage — Tomas, Suri, Jalen each saying what Adrian didn't understand — felt like a team-assembling montage in a movie. I skimmed it and lost nothing.

### I was confused
Page 19: the caption says "From the train, it looked like that might even be true" — but the staging has them climbing out of a storm drain. There is no train anywhere. I stopped and flipped back looking for the train. There isn't one. That broke the page for me completely.

Page 22: "TOMAS REED" is listed as a collaborator with a portrait of a young bearded engineer — but Tomas in Issue 01 is the old miner from Keel. Same name? Same guy? If same guy, when was he an engineer in Halyard, and why is he mining in Keel? Nobody says. I spent two pages distracted by this.

Page 24: Lock appears with Alpha talking to him, two lines, no introduction. Who is Lock? I inferred "Halyard's Mera-equivalent" and was never confirmed or denied. He matters to the plot (page 26, 29) and I never learned who he works for or what he wants beyond "the Houses."

Pages 32–33: the Covenant record shows a sealed third identity, and then page 33 is a full silent splash with no caption. A whole page of nothing but a view, ending the Halyard section. It felt like a printing error.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The three Houses (Aqualis is named once, page 23; the other two I never learned). "Water, energy, mobility" insignias on page 1, never connected by name.
- "The Charter Houses" vs "the Houses" vs "Charter" — used interchangeably; I never knew if these were the same thing.
- "The Maker's Seal" — appears page 27 as "something has been awakened" and I had to reconstruct that it's the second of the three tools. The book never says "this is tool two of three" in this issue. I knew only because Issue 01 said "two more tools after it."
- Mera — Alex says "Mera let the water back on" on page 34. First time her name is spoken. The woman from page 10, I assume — but the book never once connects them explicitly. I had to do that work myself.

### Lines I read twice
- "Your father left many things badly labeled." (p.20) — twice, liked it.
- "I didn't know I was one." (p.20) — three times, gave up.
- "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." (p.28) — twice, worth it.
- "Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." (p.27) — twice, smiled.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lina is distinct. Suri and Jalen are names with one trait each (microbial balance, network security) — I could not tell them apart by page 26 and stopped trying. Lock is a silhouette of a character. Tomas Reed vs. Tomas the miner actively confused me (see above).

### I felt something
Page 28: "They didn't steal the Seal. They stole its purpose." And page 30, Alex refusing to claim it all — "If I claim it all, I become exactly what they wanted." I believed him. First time I fully trusted Alex as a character.

### I predicted
The third custodian being Ada's mother — I called the Ada connection in Issue 01, so page 36 confirmed rather than surprised, but the execution (Alex closing the image before she sees, and Ada instantly knowing he's lying) was better than my prediction.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about who gets credit for what people make — the system that erases names versus the people who build things together.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 19, at the train that doesn't exist. If a book contradicts itself on the first page of an issue, a reader who didn't choose the book is gone.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes — the strongest yes of the three issues. "You're lying. But I'll wait." is a real cliffhanger: a question about someone I care about, not a loud noise. The printed "ISSUE QUESTION" again annoyed me and again wasn't needed — the page already did the work.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 19; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS (Pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
Pages 38–41. The reunion is the best writing in the book. "You have her height." / "I have your face." The wooden bird. "That wasn't protection. That was amputation." / "Yes. And I would do it again, and it would still be amputation. Both are true." I read these pages slowly on purpose.

Pages 43–47, the negotiation. I did not expect a trade summit to be gripping, but "Tell me about Tuesday," Elias unsealing the folio, "He went first," Mara releasing thirty days of reserve — I was genuinely hooked. Ada's "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" is the moment the whole book turns on, and it earned it.

Page 50. The log: PUMP ACCESS: DENIED — VENTILATION: DENIED — ESCAPE ROUTE: SEALED. And then "MANUAL OVERRIDE REQUESTED: M. VALE — OVERRIDE: REJECTED." I had to put the script down for a second. The book made me feel for Mera in one line after three issues of distrusting her.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 37's opening captions ("Oasis had learned how to provide everything. It had never learned how to need anyone.") — the book telling me its theme again. I skimmed and waited for the people.

Pages 48–49 slow down hard after the negotiation high. The Covenant activation is mechanical (shutters, clocks, engraved terms) and then Adrian's *second* posthumous recording arrives to explain the theme again ("What will you build when no one is left to tell you what comes next?"). I felt the book not trusting me to have gotten it.

### I was confused
Page 42: Mera offers the deal, and Alex's answer is "Call the three cities." I understood it, but the jump from "I'm not saying yes or no" to "convene the cities" happened fast, and then page 43 just... has the meeting already assembled, with three brand-new named characters (Mara, Frank, Elias) introduced mid-argument. I had to re-read page 43 panel 1 twice to attach names to cities. Frank Reed — *another* Reed, and the script has him say "no relation to Tomas Reed" as a joke, which only reminded me I was already confused about the first Tomas.

Page 49: "SUCCESSION TRANSFER: ACTIVE" — Alex is suddenly the designated successor to... Alpha? The succession? This is a huge reveal delivered as screen text, and I wasn't sure what Alex was actually being handed until page 52 spells it out (PERMANENT EXECUTIVE OVERRIDE). The two pages between left me guessing.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Mara Doss, Frank Reed, Elias Renn — three delegates who speak for whole cities, introduced in one panel each, never seen before. I accepted them because they were well-drawn, but they're a lot to absorb at page 43 of 54.
- "The Charter" (p.53 wall text: "THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE") — what Charter? First mention. On the last pages.
- The association-risk order — page 38 says ACTIVE, page 53 is the court case about it. That tracked fine, actually. One of the few setups that paid off cleanly.

### Lines I read twice
- "I thought she left after she died. I built a whole story where you died AND abandoned me. In that order." (p.39) — three times. Best line in the book.
- "It is a machine for not having to know." (p.51) — twice, underlined it mentally.
- "He never wanted the crown, Adrian. He wanted it to be his choice." (p.52) — twice; Bi11bot talking to the dead man got me.
- "No. Just on purpose, this time." (p.54) — twice. Good last line.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Mara, Elias, and Frank are sharply distinct — counting fingers, sealed folio, paper ledger. Leona and Mera are distinct and their contrast (the one who left vs. the one who stayed and controlled) is the book's best character work. The anonymous delegates on page 53 are fine as furniture. Suri and Jalen, mentioned again on page 47's ledger, I still couldn't pick out of a lineup.

### I felt something
Page 46, panel 4: Leona with her hand over her mouth — "the gesture her daughter makes" — and Ada seeing it. That destroyed me. Quietest panel in the issue and the one I'll remember.

### I predicted
That Alex would be offered the crown and refuse it. By page 52 it was obvious where it was going, and the refusal speech ("Not because I don't trust me. Because nobody should have to.") was good enough that I didn't mind predicting it. I also predicted Mera's override rejection before it was revealed — the book had been humanizing her too carefully for her to be the one who let Adrian die on purpose.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether people can be trusted to decide things together when no one is forced to — and the book's answer is "yes, barely, on purpose."

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue. It's the strongest of the three by a distance.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the end of the book. The closing image — the chip finally still in his open hand, "Just on purpose, this time" — works as an ending. The final caption ("Can people accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee?") is the third time the book has asked me its own essay question, and I liked the book *despite* these, never because of them. If there were a next book I'd buy it, mostly to see whether the agreement holds and whether Ada and Leona get more pages.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 19. The caption describes a train that the art says never happened, on the first page of the issue, right after the book's best cliffhanger. Half the class would check out there and never come back. Runner-up: page 1, for having no person on it.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a mining city inherits his dad's debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds a hidden robot and three tools his dad built, and uses them to prove the system that runs three cities has been lying — and then has to decide whether to take the system over himself or make the cities learn to share power. Also his best friend's dead mom isn't dead.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: A debt counter and a hidden workshop — the world is rigged and the proof is under the floor.
- Issue 02: The names they erased — slower, talkier, but the Seal waking up and the copper-haired photo make it worth the slog.
- Issue 03: The negotiation and the reunion — the book's brain and heart finally in the same room, and the best issue by far.
