## Page 1 (right) — A boy keeps a dead pump alive under three cities that don't know his name.

**Panel 1.** Full-page splash. A towering vertical view of the mountain basin, 2043. At the top, above the cloud line: Oasis — pearl-white towers, broad blue reservoirs, controlled green. Mid-slope: Halyard — bronze and gold fabrication towers, engineered terraces. At the bottom, in shadow: Keel — rust, charcoal, dust, a cramped sprawl around an exhausted open-pit mine. One massive water conduit drops through all three tiers like a spine — enormous at Oasis's reservoirs, narrowing to almost nothing by the time it reaches Keel. A small identical white-and-amber node sits on the pipe at each tier. In the immediate foreground, low in the frame, inside a dark pump hall open to the basin: ALEX PHANTUM (17, lean, warm brown skin, thick unruly black curls, worn charcoal-and-rust work jacket with a patched "K" shoulder mark) stands alone at an aging patched pump, eyes closed, head tilted, bare palm pressed flat to the vibrating housing — listening to the machine the way a doctor listens to a chest. His work-light is the only warm point in Keel's shadow.

CAPTION: 2043.

SUPERVISOR (small, from the hall door): That's not the approved repair.

ALEX: Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.

PAGE CHECK: The reader meets a person doing something before any world-building; the narrowing pipe carries the inequality wordlessly. Page-turn: who is this kid, and what happens to people like him here?

## Page 2 (left) — On his seventeenth birthday, the system hands Alex his father's debt and locks the gate.

**Panel 1.** End of shift, the exit checkpoint. A silent queue of exhausted workers, each with a small credit balance floating overhead — most in the tens of thousands. Alex presses his palm to a flat white-and-amber Alpha scanner. A GUARD watches, bored.

SFX: BEEP

GUARD: Happy birthday, Phantum.

**Panel 2.** Extreme close-up on the scanner display. Cold, clinical typography, sterile white-and-amber — the cleanest thing on the page.

DISPLAY:
ALEX PHANTUM — AGE VERIFIED: SEVENTEEN
PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE
GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED
DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS
EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS
PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS
TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

**Panel 3.** Wide on the checkpoint. Above Alex's head, his balance blazes enormous beside the small numbers of the queue. The other workers glance up. No one says anything. Alex stands frozen, hand still on the scanner.

ALEX (quiet): I've had better surprises.

**Panel 4.** Morning street. Alex walks past a shuttered casino, THE LAST CHANCE, its faded lights still flickering; on the wall, a peeling poster of a man's face, mostly torn away. Alex doesn't look at it. Half-hidden in his pocket, his scarred fingers turn a worn dark-ivory casino chip with a tarnished brass rim. On his wrist, the band glows faintly: RESTRICTED.

PAGE CHECK: The debt lands as math the reader does without being asked; "GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED" plants the missing father as a question, not an explanation. The chip appears in the hand, unexplained.

## Page 3 (right) — On the overlook, Alex and Ada look at the same mountain and see opposite futures.

**Panel 1.** Late afternoon, the overlook on the pit rim — a rusted railing worn smooth where they always sit. Keel in shadow below, Halyard's terraces catching gold, Oasis white in the sun far above. Alex sits on the railing, shoulders down. ADA VEYRA (20, long copper hair tied back, freckles, light teal-blue eyes, dark high-collared top with a rivet-studded strap, muted moss-green wrap) arrives with two battered metal mugs and holds one out.

ADA: Thought you might need this.

ALEX: The birthday special? Not sure I can afford it.

**Panel 2.** Close on their hands around the mugs, steam rising in rust-gold light. Alex's other hand turns the casino chip between his fingers. Ada's thumb rests on a strap rivet.

ADA: It's on me. You don't owe anybody anything today.

ALEX: Everything has a price, Ada.

ADA: Not everything.

**Panel 3.** Two-shot, side by side on the railing. The laughter has gone out of the light. Ada watches Alex; Alex watches the distance.

ADA: I heard about this morning.

ALEX: Everyone did.

**Panel 4.** Wide, from behind them. The railing cuts the frame diagonally. Alex's head tilts up toward sunlit Oasis; Ada's gaze angles down toward Keel in shadow, her copper hair lifted by the wind. Between them, the chip has stopped turning in Alex's fist.

ALEX: One day, I'm leaving.

ADA: Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving.

PAGE CHECK: The split eyelines carry the argument the dialogue refuses to have; Ada's last line ties Alex's escape dream to the missing father from Page 2's screen — the page ends on a question about a person, not a theme. Ada is named in dialogue on her first page.
