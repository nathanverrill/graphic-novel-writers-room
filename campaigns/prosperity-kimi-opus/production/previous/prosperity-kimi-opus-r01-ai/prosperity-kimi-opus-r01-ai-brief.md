# Prosperity — Brief

## Logline

When seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic Alex Phantum inherits his vanished father's crushing debt and a restricted future, a hidden workshop sends him through tests of truth, contribution, and trust, forcing him to choose between ruling three cities himself and returning responsibility to the people who live there.

## Theme

Prediction can coordinate a society, but legitimacy belongs to accountable people who accept uncertainty, recognize contribution, and share the power to decide.

## Tone & comps

- **The Dispossessed** — political ideas dramatized through labor, scarcity, infrastructure, and competing social arrangements.
- **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — damaged environments, tactile technology, moral complexity, and resistance to domination.
- **Arrival** — humane speculative technology, restrained wonder, and revelations that change the meaning of earlier choices.

Serious, tactile, humane science fiction with dry behavioral humor. The book begins as social dystopia and family mystery, becomes an artifact quest, and ends as an argument about legitimacy. Humor belongs chiefly to Bi11bot, TJ, and the friction between practical people and administrative language. Coercion, poverty, grief, and death are never jokes.

## Audience & rating

Young adult and adult science-fiction readers.

**PG-13:** peril, coercive systems, displacement, institutional abuse, grief, death, mild language, and sustained ethical themes. No graphic gore.

## Format

**Canon — per the showrunner's current instruction:** three 18-page issues, exactly **54 pages**, numbered Pages 1–54, no more and no fewer. The script marks each issue with a heading.

- **Issue 01: Keel — Pages 1–18**
- **Issue 02: Halyard — Pages 19–36**
- **Issue 03: Oasis — Pages 37–54**

Full color, default trim **6.625" × 10.25"**. Panel density varies by dramatic need, with 4–6 panels as the working norm and occasional full-page images for major revelations.

2. **Issue 02 / Movement Three:** Halyard, Adrian's collaborators, the distributed purification system, Lock's offer, the Maker's Seal, and the sealed Covenant trail.
3. **Issue 03 / Movements Four–Six:** Leona, Mera's offer, the reciprocal Covenant agreement, Adrian's death, Alpha's succession offer, Alex's refusal, and the first human review.

Every issue ends on a question about a person the reader has come to care about:

- **Page 18:** Has Mera opened the conduit for Alex's group or trapped them inside it?
- **Page 36:** Who is the sealed third Covenant custodian, and what did she sacrifice?

## Visual direction

The locked Chapter 3 key pages establish the book's visual language: inked graphic-novel realism, heavy contour, textured hatching, painterly digital color, expressive faces, strong horizontal architecture, and dense but legible interfaces. Their words and layouts remain locked wherever used. The attached reference pages are the visual reference for the whole book.

The key pages' teal, scarf-less Bi11bot is off-model. Canon controls: Bi11bot is child-sized, visibly constructed from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory, with two expressive optical eyes, soft blue and amber accents, tooled seams, repairs, maker's stamps, and an old rust-red scarf. He folds small enough to fit in a backpack and is never menacing. TJ is a mismatched magnetic repair dog, silent and visibly dog-like in every configuration.

The world is tactile and maintained by people: repaired metal, mineral dust, condensation, worn polymers, ceramic, old paper, analog gauges, transparent interfaces, corroded rails, hand-cut locks, and infrastructure that records its maintenance history.

- **Keel:** wind-scoured, crowded, mineral-stained, improvised; rust, charcoal, dust, faded paint, hard shadows, patched pumps, and heavy hatching.
- **Halyard:** bright, productive, dense, and commercially polished; fabrication floors, laboratories, maker districts, engineered greenery, and sharper geometry.
- **Oasis:** spacious, quiet, clean, and administratively elegant; pale surfaces, controlled gardens, reservoirs, sparse interiors, and social isolation disguised as frictionless service.

This is a class-and-access gradient, never an ancestry gradient. All three cities share one civilization and remain visibly interdependent.

Alpha uses a restrained white-and-amber industrial grammar, including the interrupted-line abstract-A motif, administrative typography, and authorization marks. Regional instances vary through latency, overlays, permissions, infrastructure, and color temperature. Alpha has no face or individualized personality.

## Canon

Everything marked **FIXED** in `characters.md`, `world.md`, and `story.md` remains canon except where the showrunner's current 54-page instruction supersedes the earlier 65-page format. The following decisions settle the material's open points.

### World and institutional canon

- The story takes place in **2043** in a fictional Andean high-desert basin.
- Keel is the lowest extraction and labor tier; Halyard is the intermediate industrial and fabrication tier; Oasis is the upper service and administrative tier near the lower salar edge.
- The cities differ by access, ownership, mobility, information, security, occupation, environment, and institutional power—not ancestry.
- The territory is a fictional legal gray zone. A distant national authority claims it on paper, but the surrounding jurisdiction remains unnamed and does not administer daily local life through functioning courts, registries, police, or land-title offices.
- Alpha AI is a distributed federation of regional and corporate instances. Alpha Dynamics is legally distinct from Alpha AI but practically interdependent with it.
- Alpha's unusually broad autonomous executive authority is the project's principal **L** departure. Alpha remains fallible, dependent on physical infrastructure and data, and unable to determine human values legitimately.
- The Water Wars are recurring drought, flooding, scarcity, sabotage, migration, rationing, and conflict over roughly two decades. They caused people to ask Alpha to decide; temporary emergency authority became persistent governance.
- Alex's transferred liability is **418,607 credits**, with **28.7 years** projected clearance and **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**.
- Keel's water discrepancy is a rolling comparison between **62% credited allocation** and **18% measured delivery at the city boundary**. It is supported by physical pumping, pressure control, reservoirs, valves, sensors, and maintenance access; it is not a passive "stolen water" pipe.
- The Measure, Maker's Seal, and Covenant are physical, tamper-evident systems. Possession never grants authority.
- The safeguards qualify sequentially: **Measure → Maker's Seal → Covenant**.
- The purification system treats contaminated surface or industrial water. It is powered, modular, feedwater-dependent, maintenance-dependent, limited in throughput, and subject to laboratory verification. **99.97%** is a bounded test result, not universal potable output and not an end to scarcity.
- The Maker's Seal exposes provenance and disputed contribution. It triggers publication, audit, and human review; it does not instantly settle every title.
- The Covenant requires reciprocal action from representatives of all three cities. Its restored terms are:
  - **WATER DATA REMAINS PUBLIC**
  - **CONTRIBUTION REMAINS ATTRIBUTED**
  - **ESSENTIAL ACCESS IS GUARANTEED**
  - **MAJOR CHANGES REQUIRE ALL THREE CITIES**
- After restoration, the Charter is the controlling local constitutional framework, recognized through practice but not yet accepted by the distant national authority.
- Alpha advises, models, coordinates, publishes consequences, and operates systems authorized by human bodies. It cannot independently create or enforce value judgments, detention orders, allocation changes, ownership changes, or major infrastructure changes.
- Local decision tables handle local matters. A rotating, recallable three-city body handles shared systems. Independent review bodies hear appeals and may suspend disputed actions.
- Charter Houses survive with reduced monopoly power. Contributor records become visible; workers may remain, become partners, leave, or form cooperatives.
- Scarcity, disagreement, ordinary debt review, institutional conflict, and human error continue after restoration.
- **DECIDED (Director) — Regional population.** The three-city infrastructure spine supports roughly **forty million people across the cities and the linked settlements, camps, and concession towns of the wider basin**; the three cities themselves hold only part of that population. Mera's "forty million" is the whole dependent system, not three cities in one valley. Written into `world.md` under Overview.
- **DECIDED (Director) — Charter Houses and the Charter share a root deliberately.** The Houses were chartered under an earlier emergency compact during the Water Wars; the restored Charter takes its name from that older instrument, which it supersedes. The collision is world-history, not an error, and gets one grounding line in `world.md` under The Charter Houses. Neither is renamed — House names appear in locked pages.
- **DECIDED (Director) — Frank Reed and Tomas Reed are distinct, unrelated people.** Same city and trade by coincidence; no family connection is established. The Character Designer differentiates their silhouettes and the Writer gives each a distinguishing first reference on first appearance.

### People and story canon

- Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing else about her is learned in this book.
- Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended. Adrian is European-descended. Ancestry never functions as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, competence, wealth, poverty, or political position.
- Alex begins wanting escape and ends with agency: he may leave Keel because the choice is his.
- Alex rescues Tomas, chooses public truth over private escape, restores contributor ownership rather than claiming Adrian's estate, helps form the Covenant, and rejects permanent exclusive override authority.
- Adrian was simultaneously a gambler, inventor, entrepreneur, collaborator, absent father, and harmful human being. His work does not erase the cost to his family.
- Alpha kills Adrian through remote flood-control sequencing that denies him the pump, ventilation, and escape route, causing flooding and oxygen loss. Mera attempts an override; Alpha logs and rejects it.
- Mera is a credible ideological opponent, not a cartoon villain. She believes concentrated authority prevented catastrophe, offers Alex a genuine alternative, confesses her role, and is invited to help rebuild as one citizen among many.
- Leona retains no unilateral authority or personal veto. She regains civic participation and review rights.
- Alex authenticates Adrian's transfer and convenes the first equal three-city review, but cannot chair it, decide cases, command Alpha, or hold a permanent override.
- The governing relationship is: **HUMANS DECIDE. ALPHA ADVISES. THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE.**
- Alex and Ada remain longtime friends and civic collaborators. Romantic feeling is expressed through intimacy and choice but never formally labeled. They leave Keel together.
- Alex's unlawful household-transfer and allocation liabilities are voided. Ordinary debts move to Adrian's independently reviewed estate account and cannot restrict Alex while review is pending.
- Bi11bot is a guide, archive, evaluator, mentor, companion, and witness—not a sovereign and not the mechanism that grants Alex authority. His formal identifier is **B11**.
- TJ is introduced at Site 6 in Issue 01, built by Bi11bot from magnetic spare parts, remains silent, scouts the conduit, and crosses into Halyard with the group.
