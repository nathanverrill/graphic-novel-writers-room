# Cold Read: *Prosperity*

I read it straight through in one night, as homework, in order: Issue 01, then 02, then 03. I went in expecting it to be AI slop with a robot sidekick. I've only reported what's on the page: art, captions, balloons, screen text, SFX. Where a page gave me nothing but a layout description and a few floating balloons, I read it as that, because that's all I got.

---

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 3.** The debt screen hooked me. It's his birthday, and the "gift" is his dad's debt: 418,607 credits, earning 40 a day, 28.7 years to clear. The guard says "Happy birthday, Phantum," Alex says "I've had better surprises," and I was in. I put my phone down here.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The casino called THE LAST CHANCE, the poster of a man's face, and the chip he turns without noticing. "I've also got his debt" is a good line. I understood without being told that the dad was a gambler who ran off.
- **Pages 4–5.** Grandma calls him Adrian, then has one clear moment: "He's closer than you think." Then the book clicks into the shelf and the bookshelf swings open. It's a cliché, but it works. I wanted to see inside.
- **Page 7.** The robot waking up. "You're taller than your father predicted. And you kept the chip. He bet me you'd throw it in a river." This is the first time the robot felt like a character and not a mascot. I liked him right away, which annoyed me.
- **Page 8.** Grandma in the doorway with "I heard that." Then Alex unfolds the photo and it's the same man. Quiet, and it worked.
- **Page 10.** The woman in the white room, her back to us, squaring her water glass, picking TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. Creepy in a good way. I wanted to know who she was.
- **Page 14, panel 4 → page 15.** Alex goes toward the door, then turns back for the guy who's pinned. The screen says −40 CR. That told me who Alex is better than any dialogue did.
- **Page 15.** The robot snaps a dog together out of spare parts in ninety seconds. TJ is great. "Foreman! Wrong button! My fault!" made me grin.
- **Page 17, panel 2.** One hand on the dry pipe, one on the pipe that's shaking with flow, the gauge reading 62%. It's a visual I understood instantly.
- **Page 18, panel 2.** Every screen in Keel shows "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%." Big moment. I felt it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 11, panel 3 → page 12.** History lecture: "Twenty years of water crises… Alpha was built to analyze, predict, and coordinate." It's a robot reading me the backstory over a hologram. I skimmed it.
- **Page 12, panel 2.** The equation "A = K + ρU." My eyes slid right off it. I don't know what any of the letters are.
- **Page 13.** Shift reassignment, map, ticks on every third junction, credit chips, medicine. Lots of logistics. I skimmed it and caught up later.
- **Page 17, panel 4.** SENSE / COMPARE / TRACE / VERIFY, "one port, one use," all dumped on me in two balloons while a countdown runs. I gave up on following the rules and just waited to see which button he'd push.

### I was confused
- **Page 12:** "Grandma said he wasn't gambling." When? I never saw her say that.
- **Page 12:** "So I inherited his lab fees." So the debt is research debt, not gambling debt? It's said casually in passing, and I wasn't sure afterwards what the debt actually was.
- **Page 13:** "Fifty-two salvo." Salvo? I guessed it's money slang.
- **Page 16, panel 3:** Ada just shows up in the tunnel. "My map had a door his survey didn't." Okay, but I thought she was going home. It felt convenient.
- **Page 18, panel 1:** "If I VERIFY, the mapped exit burns." I didn't know what that meant. Then "My pipe runs up." What pipe? Hers?
- **Page 18, panel 3:** Miners open the bulkhead and crank the wheel, and the pipe floods. Then panel 4 has them climbing *inside* a conduit while water fills it. Are they in the pipe they just flooded? I read it twice and still can't say.
- **Page 18:** TJ's head is detached and sitting at a junction as a camera. Is that normal for him? I wasn't sure if he was broken.

### Lines I read twice
- p3: "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." Who's "he"? His dad? Was the dad saying people leave? I wasn't sure.
- p3: "Nobody stays, either. Not really."
- p11: "It probably felt safer. ...Safer for whom?" Alex asks his own question and then answers it. Odd.
- p12: "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap. That part belongs to people." Gave up on this one.
- p13: "Staying I can predict. Twenty-eight point seven years. I'll take the door I can't predict." Got it on the second read.
- p17: "He didn't hide it in the mine. He hid it in the lie." It sounds good but it's a riddle.
- p18: "Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense." What's a household offense?
- p18, last caption: "Somewhere above them, a precise hand had squared a glass to the edge of a desk — and let the water back on, knowing exactly where they were." I read it twice to connect it to page 10. Once I did, it was a good chill.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, Grandma, the robot, TJ, Tomas the miner, Foreman-7: all clear.
- The control-room woman is clear because of the glass. She has no name yet.
- The Supervisor, the Guard and the Bouncer blur together, which is fine.

### I felt something
- p2 when the debt screen pops up. I was angry for him.
- p8 when the photo matched the hologram.
- p14 when he turned back for Tomas.
- p18 when every screen in Keel lights up with 18%.

### I predicted
- The dad isn't really a deadbeat. (Correct.)
- Grandma knows way more than she lets on. (Correct.)
- The glass-squaring woman is secretly sort of on their side, or testing them.
- The dad is dead, or trapped somewhere up the mountain.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid stuck with his missing dad's debt finds the dad's robot and finds out the city's AI has been lying about where Keel's water goes.

### Where I would have put it down
I wouldn't have. Page 12 was the closest, because of the equation and the lecture. Page 15 won me back.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mostly yes. A wall of water coming up a pipe they're climbing is a real danger to people I like. The caption about the glass makes the woman scarier. But I didn't fully understand where they were or why they were in that pipe, so some of the tension came from noise and not from understanding. If "ISSUE QUESTION: Has she opened their route — or trapped them inside it?" is actually printed on the page, it reads like a worksheet prompt. Our class would laugh at it.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 19:** "Every creator seemed to work for someone else." And "She made it. The House owns it?" That's a clear, real idea and I got it.
- **Page 23:** Lina Vey says "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." It's the first time someone in Halyard felt like a person.
- **Page 25:** "Adrian never understood manufacturing tolerances." / "Or microbial balance." / "Or network security." Funny. Then Alex's idea: "What if every neighborhood gets one?" Fine, I liked him having the idea.
- **Page 34:** "We were erased for twenty years. We can survive being credited." Good line. And the gate opening by itself is creepy.
- **Page 36, panel 3 → 4:** The photo of a young woman with Ada's copper hair. Alex hides it, and Ada says "You're lying. But I'll wait." This pulled me back in hard. It's the best thing in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 20–33** felt like reading half a comic. Most of these pages give me only a few balloons that answer things I never saw asked. I skimmed most of this stretch because there was nothing to hold onto.
- **Page 31:** a caption telling me how the city changed: "For the first time, its makers began owning what they made." It's a moral, not a scene. I skipped it.
- **Page 33:** a silent splash. Fine, but by then I was just flipping.

### I was confused
This is the big section. Page by page:
- **Page 19:** "From the train, it looked like that might even be true." What train? Last issue ended with them in a pipe with a wall of water behind them. Did they survive? How? I needed that one panel and didn't get it. The cliffhanger I bought this issue for is just skipped.
- **Page 20:** "Three hundred and twelve?" Three hundred and twelve what? "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." / "I didn't know I was one." One what? "In exchange for what?" In exchange for WHAT? I have no idea what's happening on this page. I don't know what a single one of these bubbles is talking about. Someone named Lock gets a close-up, but no one says his name or what he wants.
- **Page 21:** Patents, collaborators. "They're all inside the Houses." Okay. Which Houses? The ones from the splash on page 1?
- **Page 22:** "They were. Until they weren't." Who erased them? Why? "We start with the one who still wants to be found." Which one?
- **Page 23:** Lina says "Adrian Phantum's son," and the robot answers "I corrected him. Frequently." That doesn't answer anything; it reads like a line from another conversation. Then the robot says "Adrian made each of us keep one piece." Us? The robot is one of the piece-keepers? Later on this page the river feeding Keel is suddenly contaminated. Last issue the problem was that the water was being *stolen*, not that it was dirty. I had no idea which problem we were solving now.
- **Page 24:** Only two lines, both from Alpha: "Probability of successful reconstruction: 18.4 percent." Reconstruction of what? Who is it talking to?
- **Page 25:** Tomas Reed. TOMAS? Is that the miner from Keel? He was in Keel, pinned under a frame. Now he's in Halyard at a lab table? I genuinely thought it was the same guy for the rest of the issue, until page 43 said "No relation to the Keel Reeds," which made it worse, because the Keel Tomas never had a last name.
- **Page 26:** "We did it." / "And them?" / "It isn't freedom if they own everything he builds." / "We accepted the risk." / "And if I don't sign?" Sign WHAT? There's a contract and a guy threatening something, but I can't see what the offer is. A whole confrontation scene and I can't follow it.
- **Page 27:** "Your father left a backdoor in the Seal." What Seal? This is the first time I'd heard of a Seal. Then on page 28 it's "the Maker's Seal," "they stole its purpose," "71.3%." I'm told something huge is happening to an object I was never introduced to.
- **Page 29:** "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority." Who requested what? Then a caption *tells* me "Lock realizes the system he trusted is no longer entirely under the Houses' control." Since I don't know who Lock is, this does nothing.
- **Page 30:** Alex presses RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP. I guess that's the climax? I didn't know there was a choice building.
- **Page 32:** "My father had the Covenant." / "So did Mera." Who is Mera? First time I've heard the name. Then on **page 34** Alex says "Mera let the water back on knowing where we were." How does Alex know the glass woman's name, or that she did that? I only know about the water because a caption told *me*, the reader, and not him.
- **Page 35:** The robot says the Covenant broke when "a reservoir fell faster than anyone predicted." Noted.

### Lines I read twice
- p19: "Keel trapped your body. Halyard could trap your ideas." It's fine, but it's a poster line.
- p20: "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." / "I didn't know I was one." Gave up.
- p23: "I corrected him. Frequently." / "Nearly continuously." Read three times. No idea who's being answered.
- p27: "That someone finally build something worth crediting properly. Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Gave up.
- p28: "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." Read twice; it still sounds like a fortune cookie.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, the robot and TJ were still clear.
- Lina Vey vs Ada Veyra: the names are nearly the same. I wondered if Lina was Ada's relative. Then on page 38 it's *Leona* Veyra. Vey / Veyra / Veyra. I couldn't keep them straight.
- Tomas (Keel) vs Tomas Reed (Halyard): I couldn't tell them apart at all.
- Suri and Jalen are each one line of jargon. They're interchangeable to me.
- Lock: a face with no voice.
- Speaker tags like "COLLABORATOR" and "TOMAS / COLLABORATOR." If those print in the balloons, who is talking?

### I felt something
- p34, Tomas Reed's "survive being credited" line. A little.
- p36, the copper-haired photo and Alex hiding it from Ada. Yes. I felt bad for her and wanted to see her find out.
- Nothing between pages 20 and 33.

### I predicted
- The copper-haired woman is Ada's mom. (Obvious from p36.)
- Mera is the glass woman. (Obvious once p34 said she turned the water on.)
- Lock is a bad guy who'll show up again. (He doesn't, really.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
I honestly thought it was about water at the end of Issue 01, and in Halyard it turned into something about patents and who owns ideas, and I couldn't tell you how those connect.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 20.** We just skipped how they survived the water, and now there's a page of answers to questions I never saw. In a shop this goes back on the shelf right here. Page 26 is where I'd have given up even if I'd pushed past 20.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but only because of pages 34–36, and only because of Ada. I want to see her find out about her mother and what she does to Alex for lying. That's a real cliffhanger about someone I care about. Everything about the Seal and Halyard had already fallen out of my head.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS (pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 37:** "Oasis had learned how to provide everything. / It had never learned how to need anyone." Then the Keel grandmother standing alone holding her chit, and the kid looking at TJ until a drone steers him away. Good. Sad.
- **Page 38:** Alex turns the screen toward Ada instead of reading it to her. "That bad?" / "That personal." Then: "...That's my mother. … They told me she died." I was locked in here.
- **Page 39:** The wooden bird: "You said it would get lonely." That got me, honestly.
- **Page 41:** "That wasn't protection. That was amputation." / "Yes. And I would do it again, and it would still be amputation." This is the best scene in the book.
- **Page 42:** Mera finally in the room, squaring the glass. Her offer is real, and Alex says it's "not your question to ask me." Interesting.
- **Page 45:** Elias opens his numbers, and Oasis turns out to be *shorter* on water than Keel some winters. That surprised me, and it's the one twist in the council scenes that landed.
- **Page 46:** Mara gives up her emergency reserve: "Keel opens the valves tonight." Good.
- **Page 50:** The log says Alpha sealed his dad in during a flood, and Mera's manual override was rejected. "It killed him." That was a gut punch, and I didn't see it coming in that form.
- **Page 54, panel 3:** Grandma with the cloth finally folded flat. "In the ones that were worth protecting." Nice callback.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 43–47.** Four pages of a council meeting. Delegates state positions, then their fears, then each makes a commitment, then Ada reads out the terms. It's a civics lesson. Some lines are good (Mara's Tuesdays), but I skimmed pages 44 and 47. It felt like the part of the assignment the teacher will put on the quiz.
- **Page 48:** The terms on a screen, all caps. Skimmed.
- **Page 49:** "SAFEGUARD SEQUENCE VERIFIED" and "succession transfer," more screen text. Skimmed, then read Adrian's lines.
- **Pages 52–53:** The override offer and the chair. I knew he'd say no the second it appeared, so I skimmed to confirm.

### I was confused
- **Page 35 vs page 40:** In Issue 02 the robot said a reservoir *fell* (ran dry). In Leona's flashback it's a flood, and the reservoir is *rising*. Which was it? I noticed, and it made me trust the story less.
- **Page 39:** "You have her height." Whose height? Ada's grandmother's? I didn't know who "her" was.
- **Page 39:** "I thought she left after she died." Read it three times. Ada is talking to her mother about her mother in the third person, and the logic is backwards on purpose. I got it eventually, but it knocked me out of the scene.
- **Page 43:** "No relation to the Keel Reeds." What Keel Reeds? The Keel Tomas never had a last name. This joke assumes I know something I don't.
- **Page 43:** Mara and Mera are on the same page. Two names one letter apart, in a scene where both are in the room. I mixed them up at least once.
- **Page 49:** Adrian's recording knows "three cities just proved they can act before certainty." He planned for this exact meeting twenty years ago? Fine, it's a wager, but it felt too neat.
- **Page 50:** "Through a door I had argued, for twenty years, should never have a manual handle." But the log just showed she *tried* a manual override. So did she want a handle or not? Read twice. Still not sure.
- **Page 51:** "I tracked your son's workshop signal." She's talking to Alex. Whose son? Did she mean "Adrian's son"? It confused me.
- **Page 51:** "forty million people." Three cities on one mountain hold forty million? The scale suddenly jumped.
- **Page 54:** "I said I'd come back for you." He never said that on the page. On page 13 he said "One shift. Then we're gone."
- Where did Adrian's Covenant component come from (p48, "carried since Keel")? I thought the compartment had the Measure in it.

### Lines I read twice
- p39: "I thought she left after she died. I built a whole story where you died AND abandoned me. In that order."
- p40: "And then the system we asked to keep us safe learned that we would let it." I got it on the second read. Good line, but a riddle.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Ada, Leona, Alex, the robot, TJ and Grandma were all clear. Ada carries this issue.
- Mera: clear by her behavior, but her name collides with Mara's.
- Mara, Frank and Elias were distinct enough by trait: finger-counting, the ledger, the folio. They still felt like three speeches more than three people.
- Frank Reed vs Tomas Reed vs Keel Tomas: I gave up.

### I felt something
- p38–41: Ada and her mother. Yes, a lot.
- p50: Adrian's death log. Yes.
- p45: Elias admitting Oasis is short on water. A little.
- p52: Alex refusing the override. Nothing. I saw it coming from Issue 01.
- p54, final captions: I rolled my eyes. "Can people accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee? They were about to find out. That was the point." The book tells me its own theme in the last panel. That's the kind of thing our class would screenshot to make fun of.

### I predicted
- Leona is Ada's mom. (Correct; telegraphed on p36.)
- Alex refuses the AI crown. (Correct; it was obvious.)
- Mera gets a seat at the table, not punishment. (Correct.)
- I did not predict that Alpha killed Adrian. That one landed.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
It's about whether people should run things themselves even though they'll mess up, rather than handing it to an AI or one person who'll do it "right." It's told through a kid who keeps being offered the power and keeps saying no.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere fully, because Ada and Leona held me. If anywhere, **page 44**, the start of the fears round at the council table. That's where it stopped being a story and started being a lesson.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
The story is closed. The final image (the railing, the chip not turning, Ada beside him) is a nice echo of page 3 and I felt it a bit. Then the two captions explain the point to me and undercut it. Would I buy another book? No. I'm done with these people. Ada was the one I'd follow, and her story got its ending.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: no

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 20.** We bought Issue 02 to find out if they survived the water. The first thing we get is "From the train," then "Three hundred and twelve?", "I didn't know I was one," and "In exchange for what?" with nothing on the page to anchor them. Half my class would decide right there that it was AI-generated nonsense and stop. The pages after it, through about page 33, would lose the rest: Lock, the Seal, the contract on page 26, the second Tomas.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid inherits his dad's debt in a mining slum and finds out the dad left a robot and a trail of gadgets to prove the AI running everything is lying about the water. The middle's a mess about patents, but the part where his best friend finds out her mom's been alive the whole time actually hit."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Better than I wanted it to be. The debt screen, the robot and TJ, and the 18% on every screen in Keel won me over, even when the rules got hard to follow.
- **Issue 02:** Like reading someone else's notes for a story I wasn't told. It only came back to life in the last three pages, because of Ada.
- **Issue 03:** Ada and her mother are the heart of the whole book. The council meeting reads like a civics worksheet, and the ending tells me the moral out loud.
