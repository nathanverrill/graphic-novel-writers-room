# Prosperity Prologue — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- **FIXED.** This prologue is set in two linked periods: about 2070, in an ordinary comfortable home, and 2043, in Keel’s pump hall. It is exactly four pages. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md; input/pitch.md)`
- **FIXED.** The 2070 world must feel warm, ordinary, comfortable and earned, not dystopian. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **FIXED.** The visible economic world is built around independent companies. These companies license Alex’s patents and compete with one another and with the Houses. Nothing in the book shows a co-op, collective or giveaway. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED.** Ownership is written down and protected. The wall shows patents, licensed designs, independent shops, competing businesses, a failed prototype, and a debt paid back in full. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`
- **FIXED.** Money is called **credits** everywhere. There is no slang word for money or for a balance. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **FIXED.** The book does not present free markets, free enterprise, private ownership, honest business, or business serving customers as villains. Monopolists, cronies and central planners may be villains in the wider project, but none appears in this four-page prologue. `(rules/stakeholders.md)`
- **FIXED.** The prologue communicates its economic and social world visually, not through a lesson or explanation. The wall carries the evidence; no dialogue names the partner, the backer, the missions, or a broader political position. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Overview

The prologue presents a future in which a comfortable household can display the material record of a working inventor’s career: patents, machines, licensed products, independent businesses, competition, failure, and settled debt. The future is not defined by spectacle or luxury. It is a quiet ordinary street, a maintained home, solar tiles, a vegetable garden and a ten-year-old car. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The economic system visible on the wall rewards invention, repair, ownership, licensing and competition. Alex’s inventions are not necessarily manufactured by Alex’s own company: a purifier patent is used in schools by “somebody else’s company,” and dozens of founders operate separate water-unit businesses under different shop signs. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The earlier setting is Keel in 2043, specifically a pump hall with rust and charcoal around the machinery. The prologue establishes Keel through its industrial infrastructure rather than explaining the town’s full history or administration. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Setting

### The 2070 home

- A quiet, well-kept house stands on an ordinary street in warm evening light. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Solar tiles are installed on the house. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A vegetable garden is maintained on the property. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A ten-year-old car sits in the drive. The household is comfortable but not flashy. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Inside, the hallway wall is covered in framed patents, photographs of machines and news clippings. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The wall functions as a visible record of invention, ownership, commercial use, collaboration, failure and repayment. No character explains this function aloud. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Keel, 2043

- Keel has a pump hall with industrial machinery, rust and charcoal-dark surroundings. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The pump hall contains a pump with a bracket that corresponds to the grease-stained sketch displayed in the 2070 house. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The broader geography, civic structure, climate, water system and relationship between Keel and other locations are not established in this prologue. `(input/pitch.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Economics, Ownership and Work

### Currency and accounts

- The currency is credits. Dialogue and visible account language must use “credits,” not slang or casino terminology. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- The framed final account statement reads: **PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS**. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The statement establishes that at least one significant financial obligation has been completely settled and that the account records a zero remaining balance. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The amount associated with Alex’s original debt is **418,607 credits** in Chapter 1 material, while the prologue dialogue refers to “four hundred thousand credits I’d never borrowed.” The exact relationship between those figures is not explained in this prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Independent businesses and licensing

- Independent shops license Alex’s patents rather than operating as a collective or giveaway system. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Licensed businesses compete with each other and with the Houses. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A wall clipping reads: **“40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER.”** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Another clipping reads: **“SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN.”** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A photograph shows dozens of founders, each with an individual water unit and a different shop sign. This indicates separate ownership and visibly differentiated businesses rather than one unified provider. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A purifier patent is used in a school by another company. The visible economic arrangement separates inventing a design from manufacturing or deploying it. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The names, legal structures, size and powers of the Houses are not established in this prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Failure and repayment

- A scorched, cracked prototype is framed with the handwritten note **“v1 - didn't work.”** Failure remains part of the inventor’s visible record rather than being erased. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The wall also preserves a final account statement marked **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS.”** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The prologue establishes repayment, not cancellation, bailout or giveaway, as the outcome of the debt. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`
- The creditor, terms, interest, collateral, payment schedule and mechanism by which the debt was repaid are not established here. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Technology

- Household food production includes a vegetable garden. No irrigation, automation or ownership details are established. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A ten-year-old car remains in use, suggesting that durable older vehicles can remain ordinary household property. Its propulsion system and fuel source are not specified. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Water technology

- Water purification is a commercially licensed technology by 2070. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The clipping identifies a product called the **Phantum Purifier**. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- A purifier diagram appears among the framed patents. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The purifier is installed in at least one school, and the company operating it is not necessarily the inventor’s own company. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Multiple independent founders have their own water units and shop signs. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The exact purification method, energy requirements, capacity, maintenance procedures and failure modes are not established in the prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Industrial machinery

- Keel’s pump hall contains large industrial pumps and repairable mechanical assemblies. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The pump-bracket sketch is a crude design or repair drawing rather than a patented invention. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The prologue does not define the pump hall’s operator, output, supply network or technical specifications. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Institutions and Power

### Independent shops

Independent shops are visible commercial actors with their own signs, products and licenses. They compete to develop or sell water-related designs. The number “40TH” on the clipping indicates that at least forty independent shops have licensed the Phantum Purifier by the time of the 2070 home scene. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Houses

The Houses are established as competing commercial powers in the 2070 news clipping. They race with small makers for the next design. Their number, internal structure, legal authority, relationship to government, resources and ability to enforce ownership are not established in this prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Schools

Schools are customers or sites of deployment for commercial purification technology. At least one school has a purifier made by a company other than Alex’s. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Records and written ownership

Patents, licenses, framed clippings and account statements make ownership and financial obligations visible and durable. The wall treats documents as evidence of what was invented, who uses it commercially and whether an obligation remains outstanding. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

The prologue does not specify the patent office, licensing authority, banking system, courts or other institutions that make these records enforceable. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Environment and Material Conditions

- The 2070 domestic environment shown is maintained and comfortable, with vegetation, solar infrastructure and a functioning private vehicle. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The material environment of Keel’s pump hall is industrial, rusted and charcoal-toned. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Water is important enough to appear in purifier patents, school infrastructure and multiple competing water units. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The prologue does not establish whether water scarcity, contamination, war, climate change or another condition created the demand for purification. `(rules/decisions.md; input/pitch.md)`

## Daily Life and Lived Texture

The 2070 household’s visible daily life is quiet and practical: a maintained home, food grown in a garden, an older car still kept in service and family members moving through a hallway lined with working records. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The wall is visually busy with framed documents and images, but each item supplies a concrete record:

- patents;
- machine photographs;
- news clippings;
- a photograph of four collaborators laughing in a lab;
- a photograph of many independent founders with separate water units and shop signs;
- a scorched failed prototype;
- a final account statement;
- a rough grease-stained drawing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The economic world is communicated through ordinary artifacts rather than public slogans. There are no speeches about free enterprise, no named educational organizations, no political messaging and no explanation of what the wall is meant to teach. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Language and Public Terminology

- The currency term is **credits**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- The visible account terminology includes **PAID IN FULL** and **BALANCE 0 CREDITS**. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The public/business terminology visible in the clippings includes **INDEPENDENT SHOP**, **LICENSES**, **PURIFIER**, **SMALL MAKERS**, **HOUSES** and **NEXT DESIGN**. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The prologue’s dialogue does not explain the economic system, the patent system or the institutions behind the records. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The language gradient between Keel, Halyard and Oasis is a rule for the wider project. This prologue supplies only Keel and the later domestic setting; it does not establish Halyard or Oasis on the page. `(rules/vocabulary.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## What the Prologue Does Not Establish

- The government or governments of this world.
- The legal definition or enforcement mechanism of a patent or license.
- The formal structure of the Houses.
- The cause, scale or history of any water crisis.
- The technical operation of the Phantum Purifier.
- The source of household electricity beyond the presence of solar tiles.
- The nature of the 2043 debt, its creditor, its interest rate or its collateral.
- The relationship between Keel and the later 2070 economy.
- Any casino, casino chip or gambling-based financial system. The casino chip is not part of this book. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Any co-op, collective, giveaway, debt cancellation or windfall. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

## Key props

### Adrian’s old steel watch

- **Appearance:** A worn steel wristwatch, slightly too large for Alex, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that stays dark. Adrian’s looping equation is engraved on the steel caseback. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Owner and appearances:** Alex wears it on his left wrist in the 2070 house. The same watch is associated with Alex in the 2043 material. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Handling:** Alex keeps it close and half hidden, pulling his sleeve down over it or covering its face with his thumb. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Meaning:** It is Adrian’s keepsake and a physical link between Alex’s later life and the earlier Keel period. The engraving silently matches the chalkboard and a later instrument; the prologue does not explain that mechanism. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Restrictions:** It has no readout and counts nothing. There is no casino chip anywhere in the book. `(rules/decisions.md)`
