# Prosperity Prologue — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

This four-page prologue establishes Alex through the ordinary, comfortable, earned life he reaches by about 2070. The people should be understood through what can be seen: the worn tools and clothing, the wall of patents and records, the failed prototype, the paid account statement, the damaged sketch, the watch, and the way Alex answers his daughter. Nothing explains a lesson, names the partner or backer, or turns the characters into lecturers. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

Alex remains visibly shaped by Keel even after moving upward. His success is associated with independent invention, repair, ownership written down and protected, separate companies licensing his patents, and work that serves people while making money. `(rules/look.md; rules/decisions.md)`

No character in this prologue presents a co-op, collective, giveaway or charitable business model. The businesses represented on the wall are independent companies that license Alex’s patents and compete with one another and with the Houses. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Characters

### Alex — about 2070

**Role:** Independent inventor and repairer in his mid-40s. He owns or co-owns the patents represented on the hallway wall and licenses them to separate manufacturing and deployment companies. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** Alex is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, with a day’s stubble. He is still dressed like a mechanic: a worn work jacket over a T-shirt, sleeves pushed up, scuffed boots, nothing proper or polished. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

He wears Adrian’s old steel wristwatch on his left wrist. The watch is a little big for him, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that stays dark. Adrian’s looping equation is engraved on the steel caseback. The watch shows no readout and counts nothing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alex keeps the watch close and half hidden. He pulls his sleeve down over it or covers its face with his thumb. In the prologue, this is a recurring visual gesture rather than an explained piece of backstory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

In the 2070 hallway, Alex taps a framed photograph of four people laughing in a lab: Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** Alex speaks plainly, concretely and without polish. In the prologue his dialogue is:

- “Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me.”
- “Not in the beginning.”
- “In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed...” `(rules/decisions.md)`

His language should not become civic, academic, activist or bureaucratic. He describes evidence, work and immediate consequences rather than principles. His voice is short, dry, skeptical and mechanical, with sarcasm available when defensive. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

The prologue’s line “four hundred thousand credits” is a rounded conversational description of the exact 418,607-credit debt balance established in Chapter 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Who he is:** Alex is a person who makes, repairs and works with other people rather than claiming sole credit. His answer to his daughter distinguishes among making some inventions, fixing most things and working with people smarter than himself. `(rules/decisions.md)`

He has become successful without becoming polished or detached from physical work. His clothing, hands-on manner and attachment to the damaged pump-bracket drawing keep his earlier repairer’s identity visible inside the later inventor’s life. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/look.md)`

His success is earned and recorded rather than presented as a windfall. The hallway wall shows patents, licensed designs, independent companies and a paid-in-full account, while the failed prototype remains visible among the successes. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

Alex’s past includes deep debt and rationed water. The same debt eventually reaches a zero-credit balance, and the prologue’s framed statement records that the Chapter 1 obligation was repaid in full. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:** Alex is the father of a daughter who is about nine. He answers her questions directly and allows her to discover the wall’s meaning by looking at it. He does not lecture her about his achievements. `(rules/decisions.md)`

His relationship with his former lab collaborators Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren is represented by the framed photograph of the four of them laughing in a lab. They are peers with distinct technical abilities whose ideas or work influenced the inventions on the wall; they are not presented as employees or as a collective business. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Adrian is connected to Alex through the old steel watch Alex keeps and wears. Adrian’s exact identity and relationship to Alex remain open. The watch signifies concealed history and continuity, but the prologue does not state its backstory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Alex taps or touches meaningful objects rather than explaining them. He taps the photograph of Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. When confronted with the crude sketch, he looks at it and then down at the watch, with his thumb covering the watch face. `(rules/decisions.md)`

He keeps his sleeve pulled down over the watch or covers its face with his thumb. This gesture links the older Alex to the watch without explaining Adrian. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** By about 2070, Alex has accumulated patents and worked with former lab collaborators. He has also kept a failed, scorched, cracked prototype marked in his own hand “v1 - didn’t work.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

He retains a crumpled, grease-stained sheet showing a crude pencil sketch of a pump bracket, with smudged fingerprints and no patent number. The sketch is a loose sheet mounted inside a mismatched frame: the frame is what looks different, while the paper remains visibly damaged and informal. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The pump-bracket sketch mattered because it solved a real problem, while patenting it added no necessary value. The story does not supply a technical, legal or financial explanation for this. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The first visible example of Alex’s ability to fix real machinery under pressure is the 2043 Keel pump repair. That repair itself is not the invention that later makes him successful; it becomes part of the history of the practical ability that supports his later invention and licensing work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** The supplied material contains no draft chapter or page sequence beyond the settled four-page prologue breakdown. `(input/notes.md; input/pitch.md)`

In the prologue, Alex appears in two periods: as a mid-40s father in about 2070 and, in the final transition panel only, as a seventeen-year-old in Keel’s pump hall with his palm flat on the pump. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Arc:** Within the prologue, Alex’s established position changes from a successful older inventor being questioned about his achievements to a remembered seventeen-year-old at the beginning of the pressure that shaped him. The final transition identifies the earlier Alex with debt, rationed water and the Keel pump hall. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The prologue does not establish the full arc from the seventeen-year-old to the successful inventor. It establishes the endpoint—earned ownership, independent licensing, failure remembered rather than hidden, and the same debt eventually paid in full—then opens the earlier story. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alex — seventeen in Keel, 2043

**Role:** A seventeen-year-old Alex at the beginning of the story’s Keel period. His exact occupation or status at this moment is not specified in the prologue material. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** He appears only in the final, full-width transition panel, in Keel’s pump hall, with his palm flat on the pump. The panel transitions from the later Alex’s sketch into rust and charcoal. No further appearance details are established. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The steel watch worn by the older Alex is a continuity object linking the two Alexes, but the prologue does not separately specify whether or how it is worn by seventeen-year-old Alex in this panel. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** No dialogue is assigned to seventeen-year-old Alex in the prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Who he is:** He is the earlier version of Alex at the point where the story’s debt, rationed water and Keel circumstances begin. The prologue establishes the pressure around him but does not yet define his complete personality or motivation at seventeen. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** At this point he is connected to the Keel pump hall and the pump-bracket repair that later appears as a damaged drawing in the 2070 hallway. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc:** The prologue establishes him as the starting point for Alex’s later development, not as a completed character arc. His transformation into the older inventor remains outside the four-page prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alex’s daughter — about 9

**Role:** Alex’s daughter. She is about nine years old and looks at the framed material on the hallway wall. Her name is not given. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Look:** No specific physical appearance, clothing or distinguishing features are established beyond her age and the action of standing on tiptoe to examine the wall. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** Her speech is direct, curious and enthusiastic. Her established lines are:

- “Did you make *all of these?”
- “That one's in my school!”
- “This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?”
- “Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

She asks concrete questions about what she can see rather than asking for an abstract account of Alex’s life. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Who she is:** She is curious about her father’s work and initially reads the wall as a collection of impressive inventions. She notices the difference between the formal patents and the informal drawing, even though she does not notice the framed paid-in-full account statement. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Her enthusiasm creates the emotional pressure that makes Alex distinguish the later success from the difficult beginning. She is not presented as a student receiving an explained lesson; she is a child looking closely and asking what the objects mean. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

**Relationships:** She and Alex have an affectionate, familiar father-daughter relationship. Alex answers her without boasting and lets the wall, photographs and objects carry most of the history. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** She stands on tiptoe to examine the wall, points at the patent with a purifier diagram, stops at the frame that does not match the others, and beams while imagining inventing as fun. `(rules/decisions.md)`

She does not notice the small framed statement reading **PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS**. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** The supplied material contains no draft beyond the settled prologue breakdown. `(input/notes.md; input/pitch.md)`

**Arc:** Her brief movement is from admiring the visible inventions to waiting for Alex’s answer when he says the beginning was not fun. She looks up at him in the final page and waits, creating the handoff into the 2043 story. `(rules/decisions.md)`
