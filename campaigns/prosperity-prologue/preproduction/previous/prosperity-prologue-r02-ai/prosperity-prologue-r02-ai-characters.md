# Prosperity Prologue — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

The prologue presents Alex in an ordinary, comfortable, earned life about 2070, with his daughter at the wall of framed patents, photographs, and clippings. The people should communicate the ending's meaning through what is visible rather than through explanation: Alex has built a successful life, ownership is written down and protected, independent companies license his patents and compete, failure taught him something, and a debt was paid back in full. Nothing in the prologue names the partner, the backer, the missions, Alpha, the safeguards, or the artifacts. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alex and his daughter are the only active characters in the prologue. Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren appear only as unnamed people in one framed photograph. Bi11bot, TJ, and everyone else do not appear. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## Characters

### Alex

**Role:** The central character of the prologue. In about 2070, he is a father whose ordinary home and wall of framed work show the life he has built. He is an independent inventor and repairer who owns or co-owns the patents represented on the wall and licenses them to separate manufacturing and deployment companies. In the transition at the end of the prologue, he appears at seventeen in Keel's pump hall in 2043. `(rules/decisions.md; input/pitch.md)`

**Look:** In about 2070, Alex is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, and wears a plain sweater. He wears Adrian's old steel wristwatch on his left wrist. The watch is a little big for him, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass, and a small display window that stays dark. Adrian's looping equation is engraved on the steel caseback. The watch shows no readout and counts nothing. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

In the final transition panel only, Alex is seventeen, with his palm flat on the pump in Keel's pump hall. The pump-bracket sketch seen earlier matches the repair begun in Chapter 1. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Voice:** Alex speaks plainly and without explaining the larger meaning of his life. His lines in the prologue are:

- “Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me.”
- “That one's in a lot of schools. Somebody else's company put it there.”
- “Because it never needed to be.”
- “Not in the beginning.”
- “In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

His speech is concrete and understated. He answers his daughter's questions directly, gives credit to other people, and does not turn the wall into a lesson. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Who they are:** Alex is an independent inventor and repairer. He owns or co-owns the patents represented on the wall and licenses them to separate manufacturing and deployment companies. He is capable of making and repairing things, but does not claim sole credit for everything visible on the wall. He distinguishes between what he made, what he fixed, and ideas he borrowed from people more knowledgeable than himself. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The wall establishes that his work has reached schools and that another company's product has put one of his inventions there. His success is connected to independent companies licensing his patents and competing with one another and with the Houses, not to a co-op, collective, giveaway, or single centralized enterprise. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alex's response to the daughter's enthusiasm about inventing is qualified by lived experience. He first says that inventing was “Not in the beginning,” then identifies the beginning with owing 400,000 credits he had never borrowed. The exact Chapter 1 balance is 418,607 credits; his prologue wording is a rounded conversational figure for that same debt. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:** Alex is the father of the approximately nine-year-old girl. He answers her questions patiently and allows her to inspect the wall, but he does not volunteer every meaning attached to it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alex has a past relationship with Adrian, whose old steel watch he keeps and wears. Adrian's exact identity and relationship to Alex remain open. In the prologue, the watch signifies concealed history and continuity rather than a stated backstory. The watch is emotionally significant: when Alex looks at the crumpled pump-bracket sketch, he looks down at it, and his thumb covers its face in the same gesture established in Chapter 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren are Alex's former lab collaborators: peers with distinct technical abilities whose ideas or work influenced the inventions represented on the wall. They are associated with Alex through a photograph of four people laughing in a lab. Alex taps the photograph while speaking about people smarter than him, but none of them is named in the prologue. They are not presented as employees or as a collective business. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Alex keeps the watch close and half hidden. He pulls his sleeve down over it or covers its face with his thumb. In the prologue, he looks down at it after examining the grease-stained pump-bracket sketch, and his thumb covers its face. `(rules/decisions.md)`

He taps the framed photograph of Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren while acknowledging that he borrowed ideas from people smarter than himself. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** Alex has made, repaired, or otherwise contributed to the inventions represented on the wall. His work includes a purifier whose patent diagram is framed among the others; by about 2070, that purifier is used in many schools, though another company's product put it there. Alex owns or co-owns the patents represented on the wall and licenses them to separate manufacturing and deployment companies. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A framed photograph records Alex with Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren laughing in a lab. They are his former lab collaborators, peers with distinct technical abilities whose ideas or work influenced the inventions represented on the wall. The prologue does not identify their individual abilities or explain which invention each influenced. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A framed prototype is scorched and cracked, marked in Alex's handwriting: “v1 - didn't work.” This is an observable record of failure that he keeps among the patents and photographs. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A crumpled, grease-stained sheet carries a crude, smudged pencil sketch of a pump bracket, with fingerprints and no patent number. The sheet is mounted inside a mismatched frame. The frame is what the daughter identifies as different, while the paper remains visibly damaged and informal. Alex says it “never needed to be” a patent. The reason remains implicit: the drawing mattered because it solved a real problem, while patenting it would have added no necessary value. The story never supplies a technical, legal or financial explanation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The wall also contains a framed final account statement reading **PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS**. The statement records the same debt Alex names: the Chapter 1 obligation is eventually repaid in full, leaving a zero-credit balance. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc:** The prologue establishes a contrast between Alex's comfortable, successful life in about 2070 and the seventeen-year-old Alex who begins in Keel's pump hall in 2043. It does not show the intervening character arc. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Within the prologue, Alex moves only from answering his daughter's questions about visible achievements to naming the difficult financial beginning behind them: “In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed.” His success, ownership, failure, collaboration, and paid debt are shown on the wall rather than explained as a moral. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alex's Daughter

**Role:** Alex's approximately nine-year-old daughter. She accompanies him inside the house and discovers the meaning of the wall through her questions. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Look:** She is about nine years old. In the hallway, she stands on tiptoe in front of the wall of framed patents, photographs, and news clippings. No other physical description or clothing is established. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** She is direct, curious, and enthusiastic. Her dialogue in the prologue is:

- “Did you make *all of these*?”
- “That one's in my school.”
- “This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?”
- “Inventing stuff must have been *so much fun*.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

Her questions focus on the visible differences between the objects on the wall: what Alex made, what appears in her school, why one drawing is not a patent, and whether inventing was enjoyable. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Who they are:** She is old enough to read the wall as a collection of achievements but young enough to approach it with uncomplicated wonder. She notices that the grease-stained drawing does not look like the framed patents and asks why it is different. `(rules/decisions.md)`

She does not notice the small framed account statement reading **PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS**. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:** She is Alex's daughter. She treats the wall as part of her father's history and expects him to explain what she sees. Alex responds to her without lecturing, giving short, concrete answers. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** She stands on tiptoe to examine the wall, points at the patent with the purifier diagram, and stops at the mismatched frame containing the crumpled pump-bracket drawing. The frame, not the damaged paper itself, is what she identifies as different from the others. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** No personal history is established beyond her family relationship with Alex and her connection to the school that uses one of the patented purifiers. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc:** During the prologue, her understanding shifts from seeing the wall as a collection of things her father made to hearing that his beginning involved a debt he had never borrowed. The prologue ends before she responds to that revelation. `(rules/decisions.md)`
