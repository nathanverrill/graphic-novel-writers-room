# prosperity-prologue — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type: explicit decision and showrunner direction.** This is a self-contained four-page prologue titled **“The Wall,”** set in about 2070, that opens *Prosperity* not on dystopia but on the ordinary, comfortable, earned life Alex eventually reaches. `(rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md; input/pitch.md)`

The prologue should let the reader see, without being told, that the story ends in:

- earned success;
- ownership written down and protected;
- an independent business serving people and making money;
- many separate companies competing with one another and with the Houses;
- failure that taught Alex something;
- a debt repaid in full through income from later inventions and licensing agreements, rather than through a bailout, cancellation, windfall or giveaway. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The wall carries this information visually. No dialogue names the Young Americans Center, Bill Reynolds, the missions, the Houses, or a lesson about free enterprise. Nothing on the wall presents a co-op, collective, giveaway or charitable distribution. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

The intended emotional destination is a warm domestic moment that turns into the beginning of Alex's harder past. The daughter sees the visible evidence of her father's life and asks about it. Alex answers honestly but incompletely: the success was not fun at first. His answer opens the story onto Keel in 2043, where the reader will encounter the debt and rationed water that the prologue has already placed in the reader's mind. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The reader should understand the immediate action on a first read: a father and daughter examine a wall of framed evidence; she asks what he made; he distinguishes making from fixing and collaboration; she asks about a drawing; he answers; then his answer changes the question from “Was inventing fun?” to “What happened in the beginning?” The final transition begins Chapter 1 but does not tell any of Chapter 1's subsequent story. `(rules/reader.md; rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## Premise

In about 2070, Alex, now in his mid-40s, lives with his approximately nine-year-old daughter in a comfortable but lived-in home in the Prosperity basin. A wall of patents, photographs, clippings and personal objects shows the earned path from his early repair work to independent invention and licensing. When his daughter asks whether he made all of it, Alex's restrained answers reveal that the visible success began in debt and rationed water. The prologue ends by turning the wall's damaged sketch into the rust and charcoal of Keel's pump hall in 2043, with seventeen-year-old Alex, a young Keel repairer or maintenance worker permitted or expected to work on the pump, beginning the story. `(rules/decisions.md; input/pitch.md)`

The central dramatic engine is the movement from visible result to concealed origin:

1. The daughter sees the finished life.
2. Her questions move from achievement to collaboration, failure and the value of an unpatented drawing.
3. Alex's private gesture toward Adrian's watch connects the comfortable present to an unspoken past.
4. His final answer supplies the threshold: “Not in the beginning.”
5. The page turn into Keel reveals where that beginning occurs. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The prologue's story is not that Alex has already explained his success. It is that the wall gives the reader enough evidence to recognize what has been earned, while the dialogue leaves the difficult beginning just ahead.

## Story So Far

### Sequence: The wall in 2070

**Material type: explicit decision.** The entire sequence takes place in and around Alex's home at evening in the Prosperity basin, about 2070. It occupies exactly four pages. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The future is near-future rather than fantastical: most elements are present-day infrastructure aged and repaired, with emerging technologies made ordinary and a small amount of genuinely new technology. The home is not present-day suburbia. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md; rules/look.md)`

The prologue opens on the life Alex reached, not on the deprivation or institutional conflict that preceded it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Page 1 — The house and the question

**Material type: explicit decision.** Page 1 establishes the comfortable present and introduces the wall as a visual record of Alex's work.

#### Panel 1

A wide view shows Alex's house at evening in the Prosperity basin in 2070. The house is lived in, a little worn and comfortable, not flashy. The mountains and the basin's terraces sit behind it.

The roof has pearl-white composite and water-capture panels. The panels collect precipitation and atmospheric moisture where available. A small purifier unit stands by the door, and a rooftop cistern is visible. The home remains connected to external water supply for shortages and dry periods. A delivery drone crosses the dusk sky.

These details show that the water technology that was exceptional to Oasis in 2043 is ordinary by 2070. The image establishes prosperity as practical infrastructure integrated into daily life rather than as spectacle. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

#### Panel 2

Inside the house, a hallway wall is covered with framed patents, photographs of machines and news clippings.

Alex stands with his daughter. Alex is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, with a day's stubble. He still dresses like a mechanic: a worn work jacket over a T-shirt, sleeves pushed up and scuffed boots. He is not dressed formally or polished for the success represented on the wall.

Adrian's old steel watch is on Alex's left wrist. The watch is worn, slightly too large for Alex, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that stays dark. Alex keeps it close and half hidden, pulling his sleeve over it or covering its face with his thumb. Its caseback bears Adrian's looping equation engraved into the steel, though the caseback is not displayed in this panel. Adrian's identity and relationship to Alex remain unresolved until the wider story requires an answer; the prologue adds no backstory. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/characters.md)`

Alex's daughter, about nine years old, stands on tiptoe in front of the wall. Her position makes the wall's evidence feel large and accumulated from her perspective. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Panel 3

The camera moves close to the wall's clippings. The text is seen but not read aloud:

- “40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER”
- “SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN”

A photograph shows dozens of founders, each with an individual water unit and a different shop sign.

The visual information establishes that Alex's work has reached many independent companies, that those companies license his designs, and that they compete with one another and with the Houses. The Houses are large commercial organizations that own or control substantial manufacturing, distribution and infrastructure capacity; they remain competitors rather than the government itself. The businesses remain separate; the image does not show a co-op, collective or giveaway. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/look.md)`

Alex or his companies retain recorded patent ownership and grant separate manufacturing or deployment licenses to independent shops. The licenses specify permitted designs, production rights, attribution and payment. Ordinary legal and commercial enforcement protects those agreements. This mechanism is visible only through the wall's evidence in the prologue; it is not explained in dialogue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

All framed photographs and clipping images are rendered in the same inked graphic-novel style as the surrounding panels. They are not photographic and not black-and-white photo-realistic inserts. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

#### Panel 4

The daughter asks:

> **DAUGHTER:** “Did you make *all of these*?” `(rules/decisions.md)`

This question creates the first dramatic problem: the wall appears to credit Alex with a whole visible world of inventions, but the truth of his role is more complicated than the daughter assumes.

### Page 2 — Making, fixing and failure

**Material type: explicit decision.** Page 2 answers the daughter's first question while expanding the wall's evidence from individual achievement to collaboration, failure and repayment.

#### Panel 1

Alex answers:

> **ALEX:** “Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

As he speaks, he taps a photograph of four people laughing in a lab: Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. They are not named in the image or dialogue. They are Alex's former lab collaborators and peers. Their later choices turn on professional judgment, ambition, loyalty to former colleagues and personal risk, with each person's contribution tied to a different invention or stage of the licensing work. They are not presented as employees or as a collective business. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

The answer changes the daughter's apparent story of solitary invention. Alex claims responsibility for some work, identifies repair as the larger part of his experience, and acknowledges collaborators rather than treating the wall as a monument to himself.

#### Panel 2

The daughter points at a patent showing a purifier diagram and says:

> **DAUGHTER:** “That one's in my school!” `(rules/decisions.md)`

The line connects Alex's work to an ordinary institution in the daughter's life. The invention is no longer merely an artifact on a wall: it is a device she recognizes as part of her daily world.

#### Panel 3

Among the patents is a framed photograph of a scorched, cracked prototype. In Alex's handwriting, the image carries the notation:

> **v1 - didn't work** `(rules/decisions.md)`

Neither Alex nor his daughter remarks on it. The failed prototype remains a visual fact for the reader, showing that the successful objects on the wall include failed attempts and that failure is part of the path rather than an erased embarrassment.

#### Panel 4

A small, almost hidden frame contains a final account statement:

> **PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS** `(rules/decisions.md)`

The daughter does not notice it. The reader may notice it. The statement records the same debt Alex later names in Chapter 1. The Chapter 1 obligation is eventually repaid in full through income generated by Alex's later inventions and licensing agreements, leaving a zero-credit balance. The repayment is earned commercial success, not a bailout, cancellation, windfall or giveaway. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The page therefore places the debt's resolution in the comfortable future without explaining the complete course of repayment.

At the end of the page, the daughter stops at one frame that does not match the others. This redirects the sequence away from the patents and toward the personal drawing.

### Page 3 — The drawing and the hidden history

**Material type: explicit decision.** Page 3 shifts from public evidence of invention to a damaged, informal object that has no patent number.

#### Panel 1

Close on a crumpled, grease-stained sheet bearing a pencil sketch of a pump bracket. The paper has crude lines, smudged fingerprints and the marks of handling. It is a loose sheet mounted inside a mismatched frame. The frame is what looks unlike the other frames; the paper remains visibly damaged and informal. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/characters.md)`

The sketch is the same pump-bracket design that will match the opening repair in Chapter 1. The connection is visual and is not explained in the prologue. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Panel 2

The daughter asks:

> **DAUGHTER:** “This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?” `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alex answers:

> **ALEX:** “Because it never needed to be.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

The reason stays implicit. The drawing mattered because it solved a real problem; patenting it would have added no necessary value. The story does not provide a technical, legal or financial explanation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The exchange introduces an important distinction between making something, recording ownership and solving an immediate problem, but it does so through the object and the question rather than an explanation.

#### Panel 3

The daughter, beaming, says:

> **DAUGHTER:** “Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

Her statement is a new interpretation of the wall: she sees the patents, the school purifier, the founders and the successful future, and assumes the process was pleasurable.

#### Panel 4

Alex looks at the sketch, then down at Adrian's old watch. His thumb covers the watch's face, repeating the gesture from Chapter 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The gesture makes the watch a continuity object and a sign of concealed history. Its exact relationship to Adrian remains unstated. The prologue does not explain Adrian or the watch's backstory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The movement from drawing to watch changes the emotional register. Alex is no longer simply answering a child's question about inventions; he is confronting the beginning that the wall leaves out.

### Page 4 — The answer and the threshold

**Material type: explicit decision.** Page 4 turns the domestic conversation into the opening of Chapter 1.

#### Panel 1

Alex answers:

> **ALEX:** “Not in the beginning.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

This is the first direct contradiction of the daughter's interpretation. It creates the prologue's central unanswered question: what happened in the beginning?

#### Panel 2

The daughter looks up at him, waiting. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The pause gives the line weight. Alex has opened the past but has not yet described it.

#### Panel 3

Alex says:

> **ALEX:** “In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed...” `(rules/decisions.md)`

This line establishes the two immediate conditions that will govern the next sequence: debt and water rationing. Alex's “four hundred thousand credits” in the prologue is a rounded conversational figure; the exact Chapter 1 debt balance is **418,607 credits**. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The rationing responds to a prolonged basin-wide water shortage and is enforced through pump schedules, household allotments and local access controls. The prologue does not explain these mechanisms in dialogue; it plants the conditions that Chapter 1 will make concrete. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Panel 4

A full-width transition dissolves the sketch into the rust and charcoal of Keel's pump hall. Seventeen-year-old Alex has his palm flat on the pump. He wears Adrian's old steel watch on his left wrist. The watch is physically identical to the 2070 watch: worn steel, slightly too large, plain cream dial, scratched glass and a dark display window. Its history remains unexplained.

Alex is a young Keel repairer or maintenance worker who is permitted or expected to work on the pump. The exact employment arrangement is left for Chapter 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The caption reads:

> **Keel. 2043.** `(rules/decisions.md)`

This is the only panel in which the seventeen-year-old Alex appears. It is the threshold into Chapter 1, not a scene from Chapter 1 beyond the transition. The book must not continue into Chapter 1 in this prologue. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The pump repair is not itself the invention that later makes Alex successful. It is the first visible example of his ability to fix real machinery under pressure, an ability that later becomes part of his invention and licensing work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Exact panel proportions, gutters, lettering placement and transition geometry remain a later production decision for the Layout Agent, provided the fixed panel order and dialogue are preserved and the final image remains the full-width Keel reveal. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Causality and Change

### Immediate causality

1. The wall of patents, clippings and photographs prompts the daughter's question about whether Alex made everything. `(rules/decisions.md)`
2. Alex answers by distinguishing making from fixing and collaboration. His answer directs attention to the photograph of Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren. `(rules/decisions.md)`
3. The daughter recognizes one purifier from her school, making the wall's history part of her present life. `(rules/decisions.md)`
4. The failed prototype and paid-in-full statement remain mostly unspoken visual evidence. They complicate the apparent success without interrupting the conversation. `(rules/decisions.md)`
5. The daughter notices the mismatched frame and asks about the drawing. The story shifts from public patents to a private, practical object. `(rules/decisions.md)`
6. Alex says the drawing did not need to be a patent. The answer leaves its value grounded in solving a real problem rather than in formal recognition. `(rules/decisions.md)`
7. The daughter interprets invention as fun. Alex's look toward the sketch and watch shows that the wall's success has concealed a harder beginning. `(rules/decisions.md)`
8. Alex says it was not fun in the beginning and names debt and rationed water. The daughter waits for the story behind the wall. `(rules/decisions.md)`
9. The sketch dissolves into Keel's pump hall, making the first repair the visual beginning of the larger story. Young Alex wears Adrian's watch, preserving the continuity object without explaining it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### What changes

- **For the daughter:** she begins by seeing a wall of her father's accomplishments. By the final page, she understands that those accomplishments began in difficulty, although she has not yet heard the details. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **For Alex:** he begins in the role of a father answering questions about finished work. The daughter's assumption and the drawing force him to acknowledge the concealed beginning. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **For the reader:** the reader first sees the outcome—comfortable life, independent licenses, useful inventions and a paid debt—then learns that debt and rationed water preceded it. The reader is carried from the future into the past by a specific object and image rather than exposition. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **For the larger story:** the domestic prologue establishes the destination and then hands the narrative to seventeen-year-old Alex, a young Keel repairer or maintenance worker, in Keel in 2043. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Stakes

### Personal stakes

Alex's later comfort is set against the personal burden of beginning in deep debt and rationed water. The wall shows that his success is not an abstract reputation: it affects the home, the daughter, the school she attends and the ordinary water systems around her. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Familial stakes

The daughter is forming a story about who her father is. She sees the wall as evidence of effortless invention; Alex must decide how much of the concealed past to reveal. The prologue ends when he chooses to begin telling her the truth. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Financial stakes

The story establishes a debt of 418,607 credits in Chapter 1, rounded by Alex to “four hundred thousand” in the prologue, and establishes that the same debt is eventually repaid in full through income from his later inventions and licensing agreements. The prologue does not show the repayment process or identify the lender. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Material and civic stakes

Water is not merely a background technology. The prologue's 2070 home has water-capture panels, a purifier and a cistern; the wall includes a purifier used at the daughter's school; Alex says that in the beginning their water was rationed. The transition therefore connects domestic comfort and useful invention to an earlier material shortage. The rationing is enforced through pump schedules, household allotments and local access controls. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Professional stakes

The wall shows Alex's patents being licensed by independent companies that compete with one another and with the Houses. Alex or his companies retain recorded ownership and grant separate manufacturing or deployment licenses specifying permitted designs, production rights, attribution and payment. His later success depends on useful work, recorded ownership and competitive licensing rather than a collective business, giveaway or bailout. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`
