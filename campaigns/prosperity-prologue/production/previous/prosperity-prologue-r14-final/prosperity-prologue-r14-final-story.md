# prosperity-prologue — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type: explicit decision and showrunner direction.** This is a self-contained four-page prologue titled **“The Wall,”** set in about 2070, that opens *Prosperity* not on dystopia but on the ordinary, comfortable, earned life Alex eventually reaches. `(rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md; input/pitch.md)`

The prologue should let the reader see, without being told, that the story ends in:

- earned success;
- ownership written down and protected;
- an independent business serving people and making money;
- many separate companies competing with one another and with the Houses;
- failure that taught Alex something;
- a debt repaid in full through income from later inventions and licensing agreements, rather than through a bailout, cancellation, windfall or giveaway. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The wall carries this information visually. No dialogue names the Young Americans Center, Bill Reynolds, the missions, the Houses, or a lesson about free enterprise. Nothing on the wall presents a co-op, collective, giveaway or charitable distribution. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/stakeholders.md)`

The intended emotional destination is a warm domestic moment that turns into the beginning of Alex's harder past. The daughter sees the visible evidence of her father's life and asks about it. Alex answers honestly but incompletely: the success was not fun at first. His answer opens the story onto Keel in 2043, where the reader will encounter the debt and rationed water that the prologue has already placed in the reader's mind. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The reader should understand the immediate action on a first read: a father and daughter examine a wall of framed evidence; she asks what he made; he distinguishes making from fixing and collaboration; she asks about a drawing; he answers; then his answer changes the question from “Was inventing fun?” to “What happened in the beginning?” The final transition begins Chapter 1 but does not tell any of Chapter 1's subsequent story. `(rules/reader.md; rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## 1. Structure

### Format and dramatic spine

**Canon:** Exactly four pages. Pages 1–3 contain four panels each. Page 4 contains three dialogue panels and one full-width transition panel.

The prologue is a compressed reveal structure:

1. **Visible prosperity:** The reader sees the comfortable result before learning its cost.
2. **Qualified authorship:** Alex rejects the daughter's assumption that he made everything alone.
3. **Material evidence:** Collaboration, school deployment, failure and repayment complicate the wall's apparent success.
4. **Personal artifact:** The daughter's attention moves from public patents to the damaged pump-bracket sketch.
5. **Emotional reversal:** She calls invention fun; Alex answers that it was not fun in the beginning.
6. **Threshold reveal:** Debt and rationed water lead through the sketch into Keel, 2043.

### Acts and turning pages

#### Act I — The earned future, Pages 1–2

The wall establishes Alex's later life as useful, recorded and commercially successful. The daughter's first question creates the immediate dramatic problem: she assumes the wall represents solitary invention, while Alex knows the record is more complicated.

- **Act I turn — Page 2, Panel 1:** Alex's answer changes the story from “Alex made everything” to “Alex made some, fixed most and worked with others.”
- **Page 2 reveal:** The paid-in-full statement plants the future resolution of the debt before its origin is known.

#### Act II — From public record to private origin, Page 3

The daughter notices the mismatched frame and asks about the unpatented drawing. Alex's answer preserves the drawing's practical value while refusing to explain it. Her admiration turns the past into an imagined adventure.

- **Act II turn — Page 3, Panel 4 / page turn:** Alex studies the sketch, looks at Adrian's watch and covers its face. The reader understands that the drawing has reached concealed personal history.

#### Act III — The answer and threshold, Page 4

Alex contradicts the daughter's assumption, then names the beginning's material conditions: debt and rationed water. The sketch becomes the physical pump in Keel.

- **Climax / final reveal — Page 4, Panel 4:** The damaged bracket drawing matches the real pump hardware. Seventeen-year-old Alex has his palm flat on the pump beneath the caption **“Keel. 2043.”**
- **Ending question:** What happened to Alex, and how did this practical repair become the beginning of the life represented on the wall?

### Causal engine

The daughter's questions cause each new layer of revelation:

- The wall causes her authorship question.
- Alex's qualified answer directs attention to collaboration.
- The wall's evidence connects invention to her school and exposes failure and repayment.
- Her scrutiny finds the mismatched frame.
- The drawing prompts the patent question.
- Alex's refusal to explain the drawing leaves room for her romantic assumption.
- Her assumption forces Alex to acknowledge that the beginning was different.
- His acknowledgment opens the past.
- The sketch physically carries the reader into Keel.

No event is decorative: each answer redirects what the daughter and reader look at next.

## 3. Page plot

### Page 1 — The wall asks its first question

**Page function:** Establish the warm 2070 endpoint, then make the wall and daughter’s curiosity the active engine.

**Page-turn status:** No principal reveal; Page 1 ends on the authorship question, which is answered after the turn.

- **Panel 1:** Quiet exterior of Alex’s lived-in 2070 home at evening in the Prosperity basin. Pearl-white roof water-capture panels, purifier by the door, rooftop cistern, mountains and terraces, and a delivery drone establish ordinary future comfort without spectacle. The exterior remains brief by approved decision.
- **Panel 2:** Interior hallway. Alex and his daughter stand before the dense but organized wall of framed patents, machine photographs, clippings and personal artifacts. Alex is visibly a mechanic and keeps his left wristwatch partly hidden.
- **Panel 3:** Wall-focused view. The two required headlines and the separate-founders photograph dominate. The collaborator photograph, purifier diagram, machine photographs and other records remain unmistakable framed evidence without requiring internally legible detail. Exact text is reserved for lettering.
- **Panel 4:** The daughter, on tiptoe and looking at the wall, asks: **“Did you make *all of these*?”** Reserve a clean upper-right balloon area without covering her face, pointing gesture or wall evidence.

**Change:** The wall is no longer background decoration; it becomes the subject of a question.

### Page 2 — The wall qualifies success

**Page function:** Reframe Alex's success as repair, collaboration and useful deployment while planting failure and repayment for the reader.

**Page-turn status:** Page 2 ends with the daughter finding the mismatched frame, redirecting attention to the personal sketch.

- **Panel 1:** Alex taps the framed photograph of four collaborators laughing in a lab. He says: **“Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me.”** The photograph remains the only appearance of Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren.
- **Panel 2:** The daughter points directly to the purifier diagram, not the adjacent clipping, and says: **“That one's in my school!”** Alex acknowledges the connection without adding dialogue. Reserve separate quiet balloon zones for the daughter and Alex as approved by the lettering plan.
- **Panel 3:** The wall presents the scorched, cracked prototype with the handwritten notation **“v1 - didn't work”** as a visible artifact. Neither character comments on it. The panel may include nearby machine photographs and patents, but the failed object must remain unmistakable.
- **Panel 4:** The daughter notices the mismatched frame containing the damaged pump-bracket sketch. A neighboring account-statement frame has a stable, clean area for **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS,”** subordinate through scale and contrast rather than illegibility. The daughter’s sightline and attention remain on the mismatched sketch, not the statement.

**Change:** The reader now knows that the wall includes collaboration, failure and a paid obligation; the daughter has found the object that changes the conversation.

### Page 3 — The drawing changes the question

**Page function:** Move from public achievement to private practical history, then end on Alex's concealed reaction.

**Page-turn status:** Page 3 ends on the watch-covering gesture. Page 4 begins with Alex’s answer.

- **Panel 1:** Close view of the crumpled, grease-stained pump-bracket sketch in its mismatched frame. The paper has crude pencil lines, smudged fingerprints and no patent number. The mismatch belongs to the frame; the paper remains visibly damaged.
- **Panel 2:** The daughter asks, **“This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?”** Alex answers, **“Because it never needed to be.”** Reserve two readable balloon zones while keeping both faces and the sketch visible.
- **Panel 3:** Give the daughter enough width and quiet space to preserve her bright face, turn toward Alex and the complete balloon: **“Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun.”** The wall recedes so the emotional beat is not crushed by detail.
- **Panel 4:** Alex studies the sketch, then looks down at the old steel watch on his left wrist and covers its face with his thumb. The cream dial, scratched glass and dark display window must be identifiable before the thumb covers it. Do not show the caseback, equation or a readout. Keep the sketch edge in a separate background plane.

**Change:** The daughter's innocent conclusion forces Alex to confront the unshown beginning; the watch makes the transition personal without explaining Adrian.

### Page 4 — The threshold into Keel

**Page function:** Deliver the emotional correction, name the beginning's immediate conditions and transform the sketch into the pump hall.

**Page-turn status:** Principal reveal page after the Page 3 turn.

- **Panel 1:** Alex answers: **“Not in the beginning.”** Use a restrained reaction shot or shared hallway view. Keep the daughter present and reserve a clean balloon field.
- **Panel 2:** The daughter looks up at Alex and waits. Repeat the paid-in-full statement only as faint background continuity in its established wall position if lettering remains genuinely subordinate; otherwise keep it visible as art evidence without a new lettering destination. Her sightline must point toward Alex, not the statement.
- **Panel 3:** Alex says: **“In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed...”** Reserve the upper band for the balloon, away from the sketch frame and faces. Do not add the exact debt figure, creditor, terms or repayment explanation.
- **Panel 4 — full-width transition:** The damaged bracket sketch becomes the physical rust-and-charcoal bracket on a Keel pump through a clear geometric match. Young Alex, visibly seventeen and narrower than his older self, has his palm flat on the corresponding pump surface. His left wrist carries the same oversized worn steel watch. Reserve a stable quiet caption field for **“KEEL. 2043.”** The image is the threshold of Chapter 1, not a subsequent Chapter 1 scene.

**Change:** The reader leaves the comfortable outcome and arrives at the material beginning: young Alex, a real pump and the unresolved pressure of debt and rationed water.

## 5. Reader vs. character knowledge

### Reader knows more than the characters

- The reader may notice the account statement **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS”** before Alex names the debt.
- The reader understands that the same obligation is eventually repaid through later invention and licensing income; the daughter does not.
- The reader sees the failed prototype and understands that the wall preserves failure; the daughter does not comment on it.
- The reader sees the pump-bracket sketch before seeing the physical pump and can anticipate the match.
- The reader sees Alex’s watch-covering gesture as a continuity signal; the daughter has no stated knowledge of Adrian or the watch’s meaning.
- The reader understands that the wall’s independent founders represent separate competing companies, while the daughter mainly sees a collection of impressive achievements.
- The reader knows the final pump hall is the beginning of the main story; the daughter only hears that her father’s beginning involved debt and rationed water.

### Characters know more than the reader

- Alex knows the circumstances of the debt, but the prologue does not identify its creditor, terms, cause or repayment mechanics.
- Alex knows why the pump-bracket drawing never needed to become a patent; he answers only, **“Because it never needed to be.”**
- Alex knows the meaning of Adrian’s watch and the identity of Adrian; both remain concealed.
- Alex knows the full history of Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren, but the prologue shows only their collaborator photograph.
- Alex knows what happened in Keel in 2043 after the transition; the prologue ends before those events begin.
- The daughter knows her school has the purifier, but she does not know its commercial or personal history.

### Deliberate knowledge gap at the ending

The reader should finish with a clear immediate question, not confusion:

> How did seventeen-year-old Alex, facing debt and rationed water in Keel, turn practical repair into the later life represented on the wall?

The prologue must not answer that question early through exposition.

## 6. Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

### Decision needed

- Whether **“The Wall”** appears anywhere on the finished pages or remains an internal title. The current story does not place it in the art or lettering.
- Whether the paid-in-full statement receives a faint continuity repeat in Page 4, Panel 2, or remains only as background evidence after its full Page 2 lettering.
- Whether the final rendering leans toward painterly realism or cleaner graphic realism, provided the approved warm-to-industrial contrast, human-scale camera and lettering clarity remain.

### Proposal

- Retain the approved Page 1 exterior-to-interior movement as a deliberate exception. Keep Panel 1 brief and quiet, with only the house, roof capture panels, garden or lived-in exterior texture, mountains, terraces and delivery drone permitted. Panels 2–4 carry the active story in the hallway.
- Treat Page 1, Panel 3 as a hierarchy problem rather than a density problem: the two headlines and separate-founders photograph are dominant; the collaborator photograph, purifier diagram and machine photographs are recognizable but not internally legible until their later panels.
- Give Page 3, Panel 3 the widest practical lower-row panel and reserve its balloon before rendering. Do not shorten or alter the daughter's approved line.
- Stage the Page 4 transition with equal priority for the bracket match, young Alex's readable identity and palm-on-pump action, and the caption field. No single element may obscure the others.
- Repeat the paid-in-full statement on Page 4 only if it remains a subordinate continuity clue and does not redirect the daughter's attention.

### Canon protection

No proposed execution choice may:

- add dialogue;
- alter the approved dialogue;
- introduce the exact debt figure into the prologue;
- name Adrian, the creditor, the Houses' structure, Alpha, safeguards or artifacts;
- show any absent character outside the approved collaborator photograph;
- imply a co-op, collective, charitable distribution, giveaway, bailout, cancellation or windfall;
- turn the pump repair into the invention that later makes Alex successful;
- show Chapter 1 events beyond the threshold image.

## Ending

The prologue ends on the full-width transition into Keel's pump hall:

> **KEEL. 2043.**

Seventeen-year-old Alex has his palm flat on the pump and wears Adrian's old steel watch on his left wrist. This is the beginning of Chapter 1, not an additional Chapter 1 scene. The prologue does not show Bi11bot, TJ, Adrian, Alpha or any other character beyond Alex and his daughter, Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren in the framed photograph, and seventeen-year-old Alex in the final panel.

The ending promises that the next sequence will explain the debt, rationed water and practical origin of the pump-bracket drawing. It does not explain the safeguards, Alpha or artifacts. Adrian's identity and relationship to Alex remain unresolved, and the watch remains a concealed-history and continuity object.

The approved panel order and dialogue remain fixed. The Layout Agent may determine exact panel proportions, gutters, lettering placement and transition geometry during production, provided every panel retains one clear focus and the final image remains the full-width Keel reveal.
