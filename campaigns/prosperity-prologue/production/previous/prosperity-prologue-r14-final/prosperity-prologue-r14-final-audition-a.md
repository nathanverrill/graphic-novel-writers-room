## Page 1 (right) — Establish the 2070 home and wall

**Panel 1.** Quiet exterior of Alex’s lived-in 2070 home at evening in the Prosperity basin. Pearl-white composite roof and water-capture panels catch the warm dusk light. A small purifier unit sits by the door, and a cistern rests on the roof. In the background, the basin's mountains and terraces rise against the sky. A delivery drone crosses the air. No solar tiles, no garden, no car. 

**Panel 2.** Interior hallway. Alex’s daughter stands on tiptoe, her energetic, forward-reaching posture aimed at a dense but organized wall of framed patents, machine photographs, and clippings. She is approximately nine years old with warm-brown skin, a child’s compact build, an open expressive face, bright attentive eyes, and two low uneven braids. She wears a mustard cardigan over a pale blue school shirt, a navy skirt, white socks, and red sneakers. Alex stands beside her. He is a man in his mid-40s with medium-brown skin, a practical mechanic’s build, average height, and broad, work-hardened hands. He has a broad, tired face, close-cropped dark hair grey at the temples, dark eyes, and day-old stubble. He wears a worn charcoal-brown work jacket over a pale T-shirt, sleeves pushed up, practical dark trousers, and scuffed work boots; his silhouette is compact, useful, and slightly stooped. On his left wrist, partly hidden, is Adrian’s old steel watch.

**Panel 3.** Wall-focused view. The hierarchy is clear: the two approved headlines and a photograph of dozens of different founders standing by separate water units under different shop signs dominate the space. Other frames (collaborators, diagrams) are visible but not internally legible yet.

## Page 2 (left) — The wall qualifies success

**Panel 1.** Alex’s broad, work-hardened hand taps a framed photograph of four collaborators laughing in a lab. His gesture directs the flow.

ALEX: Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me.

**Panel 2.** The daughter's fingertip lands directly on the purifier diagram, not the adjacent clipping. 

DAUGHTER: That one's in my school!

**Panel 3.** Following the same wall search, a scorched, cracked physical prototype sits in a frame. Neither character comments on it. 

ART NOTE: Reserve space for the label beneath the prototype.
LETTERING (LABEL): v1 - didn't work

**Panel 4.** The daughter visibly stops, her sightline and attention settling entirely on a mismatched frame holding a damaged sketch. Nearby, subordinate through scale and contrast rather than illegibility, is an account-statement frame. She does not notice the statement.

ART NOTE: Reserve a stable, clean area on the account statement.
LETTERING (STATEMENT): PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS

PAGE CHECK: The reader now knows the wall includes collaboration, failure, and a paid obligation; the daughter has found the object that changes the conversation.


## Page 3 (right) — The drawing changes the question

**Panel 1.** Close view of the crumpled, grease-stained pump-bracket sketch in its mismatched frame. The paper has crude pencil lines and smudged fingerprints. There is no patent number. The mismatch belongs to the frame; the paper remains visibly damaged.

**Panel 2.** The daughter looks at the sketch. Alex looks guarded.

DAUGHTER: This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?

ALEX: Because it never needed to be.

**Panel 3.** Wide lower-row panel. Give the daughter enough width and quiet space to preserve her bright face and her turn toward Alex. Her face is full of innocent, uncomplicated admiration. The wall recedes so the emotional beat breathes.

DAUGHTER: Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun.

**Panel 4.** Alex studies the sketch, then looks down at the old steel watch on his left wrist and covers its face with his right thumb. The watch is slightly too large, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass, and a small dark display window that stays dark. It has no readout. Keep the sketch edge in a separate background plane. Silence.

PAGE CHECK: The daughter's innocent conclusion forces Alex to confront the unshown beginning; the watch makes the transition personal.
