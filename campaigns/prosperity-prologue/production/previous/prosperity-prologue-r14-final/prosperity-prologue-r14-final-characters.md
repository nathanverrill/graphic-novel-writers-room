# Prosperity Prologue — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX — ABOUT 2070

**Visual lock** — Alex is a man in his mid-40s with medium-brown skin, a practical mechanic’s build, average height and broad, work-hardened hands. He has a broad, tired face, close-cropped dark hair grey at the temples, dark eyes, and day-old stubble. He wears a worn charcoal-brown work jacket over a pale T-shirt, sleeves pushed up, practical dark trousers and scuffed work boots; his silhouette is compact, useful and slightly stooped from years of hands-on repair. On his left wrist he wears Adrian’s old steel watch, slightly too large for him, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small dark display window that stays dark; it has no readout and counts nothing. He keeps the watch partly hidden beneath his sleeve or covers its face with his thumb. Adrian’s looping equation is engraved on the caseback, which is never displayed in this prologue. Alex remains visibly a mechanic and repairer despite his later success; he is not polished by prosperity. `(Canon: rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/look.md)`

**Voice** — Alex speaks plainly, concretely and without polish. His sentences are short, dry, skeptical and practical; he describes evidence, work and immediate consequences rather than principles. He does not become civic, academic, activist or bureaucratic in speech. His approved lines are: “Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me.” and “In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed...” `(Canon: rules/decisions.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Independent inventor and repairer in his mid-40s; he owns or co-owns the patents represented on the hallway wall and licenses them to separate manufacturing and deployment companies.
- **Immediate want:** Answer his daughter honestly without turning the wall into a speech about himself.
- **Deeper need / unresolved contradiction:** He needs to acknowledge that the visible prosperity began in hardship, repair and collaboration, while part of him still protects the concealed past represented by the watch and sketch.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can repair real machinery under pressure, recognize practical solutions, invent useful designs, collaborate with technically stronger people and build income through independent licensing.
- **Vulnerability:** Debt, rationed water, failure and the unresolved history attached to Adrian’s watch remain emotionally exposed beneath his competent exterior.
- **Mistaken belief / incomplete model:** He believes he can keep the difficult beginning contained in objects and gestures rather than having to speak about it.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not boast, claim sole credit or explain Adrian’s identity and the watch’s history.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** When his daughter calls invention fun, he stops presenting the wall as finished success and says, “Not in the beginning.”
- **Relationship that pressures change:** His approximately nine-year-old daughter’s concrete admiration makes silence feel dishonest and pressures him to begin telling the truth.

**Arc in this book** — Alex begins as a father answering questions about finished work and ends by admitting that the beginning was defined by deep debt and rationed water. His proof of change is the choice to say, “Not in the beginning,” followed by the approved debt-and-water line; he opens the door to the past without explaining more than the prologue can hold. `(Canon: rules/decisions.md)`

### ALEX — SEVENTEEN, KEEL 2043

**Visual lock** — Seventeen-year-old Alex is a visibly younger, narrower version of the older Alex, with medium-brown skin, close-cropped dark hair, determined dark eyes and a lean repairer’s build. He wears grease-marked, worn work clothes appropriate to a dense industrial pump hall. His silhouette is narrow and alert, with one arm extended and his palm flat against the pump. On his left wrist he wears Adrian’s old steel watch, physically identical to the older Alex’s watch: worn steel, slightly too large, plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that stays dark. `(Canon: rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice** — No dialogue is assigned to seventeen-year-old Alex in this prologue. His presence communicates through his palm on the pump, his alert posture and the practical machinery around him. Example lines for this prologue: none; do not add dialogue. `(Canon: rules/decisions.md)`

**Causality**

- **Public role:** A young Keel repairer or maintenance worker permitted or expected to work on the pump.
- **Immediate want:** Make contact with the failing pump and begin solving the real mechanical problem in front of him.
- **Deeper need / unresolved contradiction:** He needs practical control in a life shaped by debt and rationed water, but the prologue has not yet established how he understands those pressures.
- **Leverage / capability:** Hands-on mechanical ability and the willingness to work directly on machinery under pressure.
- **Vulnerability:** He is seventeen, working inside Keel’s water pressure and industrial scarcity.
- **Mistaken belief / incomplete model:** Not established in this prologue.
- **Boundary at the start:** Not established in this prologue.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He places his palm flat on the pump, turning the damaged wall sketch into a real mechanical problem.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** The relationship to Adrian represented by the watch remains concealed and unresolved.

**Arc in this book** — The prologue does not complete his arc. It establishes him as the starting point from which the older Alex’s repairer identity, debt, rationed water and later invention will develop. The proof is the full-width transition into Keel, 2043, with his palm on the pump and the caption **“Keel. 2043.”** `(Canon: rules/decisions.md)`

### ALEX’S DAUGHTER — ABOUT 9

**Visual lock** — Alex’s daughter is approximately nine years old with warm-brown skin, a child’s compact build and an energetic, forward-reaching posture. She has an open, expressive face, bright attentive eyes and two low uneven braids. She wears a mustard cardigan over a pale blue school shirt, a navy skirt, white socks and red sneakers. Her silhouette is defined by the cardigan, braids and red shoes; she often stands on tiptoe to reach or inspect something above her eye level. She is the only active character besides Alex in the 2070 sequence. `(Canon: rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md; continuity ledger)`

**Voice** — She speaks directly, concretely and enthusiastically, asking about what she can see rather than requesting an abstract explanation. Her two approved example lines are: “Did you make *all of these*?” and “Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun.” Her other approved lines are: “That one’s in my school!” and “This one’s just a drawing. Why isn’t it a patent?” `(Canon: rules/decisions.md)`

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Alex’s daughter and the viewer of the wall’s framed record.
- **Immediate want:** Understand whether her father made the objects and images surrounding her.
- **Deeper need / unresolved contradiction:** She wants to admire Alex’s success as something joyful and simple, but the wall keeps presenting evidence that is more complicated than her first reading.
- **Leverage / capability:** Close observation, concrete questions and emotional openness.
- **Vulnerability:** She has only the finished wall and her father’s partial answers to understand the hardship beneath it.
- **Mistaken belief / incomplete model:** She initially believes inventing the things on the wall must have been fun and assumes the wall represents one person’s making.
- **Boundary at the start:** She does not intrude on private history she cannot yet see; she does not notice the paid-in-full statement.
- **Decision only she would make:** She stops at the mismatched frame and asks why the damaged pump-bracket drawing is not a patent.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Her affectionate, admiring relationship with Alex pressures him to replace a safe partial answer with an honest beginning.

**Arc in this book** — She moves from admiring a wall of apparent solo accomplishments to recognizing that Alex’s beginning was difficult. Her proof of change is the final waiting reaction: after Alex says, “Not in the beginning,” she looks up at him and waits for the story behind the wall. `(Canon: rules/decisions.md)`

## Ensemble Test

- **Alex notices:** Damaged mechanisms, practical failures, ownership records, useful outcomes and the emotional danger of the watch and sketch.
- **Alex’s daughter notices:** Framing, visible objects, school connections, differences between a formal patent and an informal drawing, and the emotional change in her father.
- **Alex values:** Practical repair, accountable ownership, collaboration, useful work and honest acknowledgment of difficulty.
- **Alex’s daughter values:** Curiosity, admiration, concrete understanding and the joy she associates with invention.
- **Alex can do:** Repair machinery, invent, collaborate, license useful designs and answer difficult questions with restrained honesty.
- **Alex’s daughter can do:** Observe closely, ask the question that changes the scene and pressure Alex without intending to.
- **Alex fears losing:** Control of the concealed past and the ability to remain only the successful father shown by the wall.
- **Alex’s daughter fears losing:** The simple, admirable story she has built about her father.
- **Alex thinks the problem is:** How to answer questions about the wall without exposing everything behind it.
- **His daughter thinks the problem is:** Whether Alex made all the impressive things and why the damaged drawing is not a patent.
- **Collaborators’ function:** Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren are visual evidence against solitary genius; they appear only in one framed photograph and have no active scene choices in this prologue.
- **Adrian’s function:** The watch preserves an unresolved personal history and links the two Alexes without introducing Adrian as an active character.
- **Primary dramatic contrast:** Alex answers through restraint and qualified ownership; his daughter advances through concrete curiosity and emotional openness.
