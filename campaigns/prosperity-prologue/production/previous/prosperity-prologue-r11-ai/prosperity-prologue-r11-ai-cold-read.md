# cold-read.md

Read once, straight through, as the assigned reader: 15–18, homework, the night before, looking for a reason to call it bad. I only went by what lands on the page: the art, the balloons, the labels and the one caption. The book is four pages, all in Issue 01.

---

## Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 3: "v1 - didn't work."** This was the first thing that felt like a real person instead of a brochure. A burnt, cracked thing hanging on the wall of fame with a joke label on it. I smiled a little, against my will.
- **Page 2, panel 2: "That one's in my school!"** This tells me fast that Dad's stuff is everywhere, and the kid saying it makes it feel offhand rather than bragging. That works.
- **Page 3, panel 4: the thumb over the watch.** He looks at his wrist and covers the watch. I didn't know why, but I noticed it, and it was the first moment where I thought something was being hidden. This is "confused but intrigued."
- **Page 4, panel 1: "Not in the beginning."** This is a good answer to "so much fun." It's short and it shuts the happy mood down. This is where I actually started paying attention.
- **Page 4, panel 4: the drawing turning into the real pump.** The cut from the crumpled sketch to a rusty, grimy pump hall is the best-looking moment in the book. The place suddenly has texture, dirt and a different colour. I wanted more of *that* place.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, all of it.** A nice house, a dad, a kid, a wall of frames, and "Did you make *all of these*?" Honestly this is where I went "okay, here we go, inspirational dad story." Nothing is wrong yet, but nothing is at stake either. I skimmed panel 3 because I can't read anything in the frames. It's just a wall of rectangles.
- **Page 2, panel 4 / the "PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS" frame.** I almost missed it. The kid doesn't look at it, nobody mentions it, and it's small. When I did see it I thought "weird thing to frame," then moved on. It only half-clicked when he says "deep in debt" on page 4, and I had to flip back to find it.
- **Page 3, panel 3: "Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun."** It felt like a line written so the dad can say something sad next. I could see the setup coming and skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 3, panel 2: "Because it never needed to be."** I don't know what this means. Is the drawing not important? Too important? Something you'd never patent on purpose? I read it twice and still don't know, and nothing on page 4 answers it.
- **The watch.** Why does he cover it? Is it his? Is it broken? I got that it matters, but the book gives me nothing. Intrigued, but only just. If the next issue doesn't pay it off fast, it turns into a leak.
- **Page 4, panel 4: is the kid at the pump the dad?** I *guessed* yes because of the match cut and the same watch, but nobody says so. If I'd missed the watch on page 3, I'd just think it's some random teenager.
- **"Our water was rationed..."** Who's "our"? His family? The whole place? I can see water panels on the roof on page 1, but I didn't connect that to water being a big deal until this line. It's mentioned in passing in one balloon, right at the end.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |

| The dad's name | Never said on the page | No. I don't know what he's called. |

| 2043 | Page 4, panel 4 | I know it's a year. I don't know what year "now" is, so I can't tell how far back we went. |

### Lines I read twice
- "Because it never needed to be." (page 3) I gave up on it.
- "Made some. Fixed most. Worked with some people smarter than me." (page 2) This one is fine. I just read it twice to check if "fixed most" was a hint. I think it is?
- "In the beginning I was deep in debt, our water was rationed..." (page 4) I re-read it to see if it was the real start of the story, and then flipped back to the PAID IN FULL frame.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Dad and daughter: easy, there are only two people.
- Older dad vs. the teenager on page 4: I *think* they're the same person, but only by the watch and the cut. Nothing tells me.
- The four people in the lab photo: just a photo, faces with no identity.

### I felt something
- A small laugh at "v1 - didn't work."
- A small drop at "Not in the beginning." The mood turning was real.
- Mostly, though, page 1 through page 3, panel 3 felt warm in a way I didn't trust. The polished family-success hallway with the cute kid on tiptoe gave me the "this was made by a program for a program" feeling. The burnt prototype and the grimy pump hall were the only bits that felt like someone *meant* them.

### I predicted
- From page 1, panel 4: the dad will say it wasn't easy. Right.
- From page 3, panel 4: the watch belonged to someone who's gone, probably dead, maybe one of the lab people. I'm fairly sure of this, but nothing confirms it.
- From page 4: the next part is a "young guy grinds his way out of poverty" story, and the crumpled bracket drawing is the first thing he made. The PAID IN FULL frame means he got out of debt.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A successful inventor tells his daughter that his life of patents started with being poor, in debt and short on water as a teenager in a grim place called Keel, and something about a watch he won't talk about.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 3**, around panel 3 ("Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun"). In a shop, that's where I'd have decided it was a wholesome dad story and closed it. I only kept going because it's four pages and I'd almost finished anyway.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 4 is the first page that's interesting. "Not in the beginning" plus the cut into the rusty pump hall finally gives me a problem: debt and rationed water. But that problem shows up in the very last balloon. I'm not attached to it yet, and I don't know the guy at the pump, so it isn't a cliffhanger. Nobody's in danger and there's no question about a person I care about. It's a door opening, not a hook. I'd read the next issue because Keel *looks* like a place where something will finally happen and I want to know about the watch. But I'd wait a week, not turn the page right now.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 3; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 1.** A pretty house, a dad, a cute kid and a wall of awards, with "Did you make *all of these*?" Half my class would have decided "inspirational AI dad comic" right there and not given page 2 a fair shot. The good stuff (the burnt prototype, the covered watch, the pump hall) comes after they've already checked out.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"It's like a dad showing his kid his inventions, then he goes 'it wasn't fun at first' and it flashes back to him as a teenager in this dirty factory place where there's no water. That's literally where it stops."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Three pages of a nice hallway I didn't trust, then one page that finally felt like the story starting, just as it ended.
