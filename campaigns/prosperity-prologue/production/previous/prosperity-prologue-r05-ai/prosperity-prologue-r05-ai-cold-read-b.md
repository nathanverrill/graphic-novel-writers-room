### Pulled me forward
Page 4, panel 3. The line "In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed." That was the first time I actually cared about what was happening. Before that, it just felt like a dad giving a museum tour of his own hallway. The jump to him as a 17-year-old in a rusty factory (Page 4, panel 4) actually looks like a story.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 1 and Page 2. I completely skimmed the text on the framed documents. "40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER" sounds like a fake textbook headline. Watching two people stand in a hallway looking at a wall of paper for three pages is really boring. 

### I was confused
Page 3, panel 4. The watch. He covers it up and it has a "dark display window with no readout." Is it a tracker? A prison monitor? A broken Apple watch? I don't know why we are zooming in on it. 

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Phantum Purifier" (Page 1, panel 3) - I guess it cleans water, but no idea what it really is.
- "Credits" (Page 2, panel 4) - Standard sci-fi money, I assume.
- "Keel" (Page 4, panel 4) - Is this a city? A planet? A company? A ship? No idea, it's just a rusty place.

### Lines I read twice
- "Because it never needed to be." (Page 3, panel 2). Read it twice because it felt like a riddle. Why didn't it need to be a patent? Did he steal it? Did he give it away? 
- "In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed." (Page 4, panel 3). Read it twice because it was actually a good line.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Super easy. It's just an older dad, a little girl, and then a younger version of the dad. 

### I felt something
Page 4, panel 3 and 4. I felt bad for the 17-year-old version of Alex. Getting slapped with massive debt you didn't even borrow and having to work in a grease-stained pump hall sucks. 

### I predicted
I predicted that he invented something really important to pay off his debt, but the big corporations stole the rights to it. That's why he has a "0 credits" balance on the wall (Page 2, panel 4) and why someone else's company put the purifier in his kid's school (Page 2, panel 2). 

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A dad tells his daughter the story of how he was forced to become a mechanic/inventor to survive a massive, unfair debt when he was a teenager.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 2. If I wasn't reading this for an assignment, I would have closed it while they were still staring at the "v1 - didn't work" frame. It takes way too long to get to the actual plot.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, actually. The transition from the boring hallway to the rusty pump hall with the 400k debt reveal is a real hook. I want to know who forced that debt on him and how he gets out of it.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 2; next issue: yes

Page 1. Starting a comic with a quiet house and a guy looking at framed patents is a guaranteed way to make high schoolers pull out their phones.

It's about a guy who got slammed with 400k in fake debt as a teenager and had to invent water pumps in a rusty factory to survive.
