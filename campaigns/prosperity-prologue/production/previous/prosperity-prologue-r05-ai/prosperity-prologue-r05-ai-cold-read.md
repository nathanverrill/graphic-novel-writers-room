# Cold Read: prosperity-prologue

I read it as the assigned reader: 16, homework, the night before, looking for a reason to call it AI slop. The book is four pages long. I read it once straight through, then went back to check the lines I quote below. I'm reporting only what the art, captions and balloons gave me. Some of the panel descriptions say what a moment is "for" (for example, "establish the first repair under pressure"), and I ignored those because a reader never sees them.

---

## Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 4, panel 3.** "In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed." This was the first line in the book that made me want an answer. How do you owe money you never borrowed? I actually asked myself that. It's the whole hook, and it arrives on the last page.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** The cut from the pencil bracket to the real rusted bracket landed. I got straight away that the drawing on the wall came from this place. Going from a warm hallway to rust and charcoal is a big enough change that I sat up a bit.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** "Somebody else's company put it there." That was a small pull. Why would he let someone else's company have his thing? It's the first sign this guy did something unusual.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** The watch with the dark window that shows nothing. I noticed him covering it with his thumb, and the blank display seemed deliberate. It made me mildly curious, which isn't the same as hooked.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, all of it.** A nice house, solar tiles, a garden, a normal car. I skimmed it. Nothing is wrong and nobody wants anything. It looked like the opening of an ad.
- **Page 1, panel 3, the headlines.** Two small framed headlines full of words I didn't know. I read the first one and slid past the second.
- **Pages 2–3 as a spread.** Most of it is a kid pointing at frames and asking about them, one frame after another. By the "v1 - didn't work" frame (page 2, panel 3) I knew where it was going and started skimming the art.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** I didn't see the "PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS" statement on my first pass. The panel points me at the sketch, so I looked at the sketch. I only went back for it after the debt line on page 4.

### I was confused
- **Page 1, panel 3:** "SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN." I don't know who or what the Houses are. Companies? Families? Royalty? The capital H says it matters, and the book never tells me.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** He says "that one" is in schools, and somebody else's company put it there. Did he give it away, get it stolen, or sell it? I couldn't tell whether he's proud or bitter about it.
- **Page 3, panel 2:** "Because it never needed to be." I don't know what that means. Is the bracket too small to patent? Did he give it away? Is he dodging the question? Confused, but a little intrigued.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** Is the 17-year-old the same guy? I assumed so because it's a flashback straight after he says "in the beginning", but nobody says it. I worked it out by guessing.
- **Present-day year.** If 2043 is "the beginning" and he's grey now, the hallway is maybe the 2060s or 2070s. But page 1 looks exactly like today with solar tiles. I couldn't tell when "now" is.

That's a lot of unknowns for four pages. The Houses and Keel are the two I'd actually raise my hand about.

### Lines I read twice
- "Because it never needed to be." (Page 3, panel 2). Read it twice and still don't know what it means.
- "Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me." (Page 2, panel 1). Not confusing, but I reread it on the second pass because of the debt line. He "borrowed" ideas and owed money he "never borrowed", and I couldn't tell if that echo was on purpose.
- "SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN." (Page 1, panel 3). I gave up on it.
- "I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed." I read this twice, but because it's good, not because it's hard.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- **The dad and the kid:** easy, there are only two of them.
- **The dad as a person:** quiet, humble, guarded. Beyond that I couldn't say much. He comes off like a nice-grandpa type in a cereal ad until page 4.
- **The daughter:** basically a question machine. She asks, he answers. Her "Inventing stuff must have been *so much fun*" (page 3, panel 3) is a line that only exists so he can say "not in the beginning." I could see the setup coming.
- **The four lab people:** faces in a photo. Not people to me.
- **The 17-year-old:** I could only connect him to the dad by assuming.

### I felt something
- **Page 4, panel 3.** Some sympathy. It's the first time the dad isn't comfortable. Saying the debt plainly to a nine-year-old landed harder than if he'd been dramatic about it.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** A tiny "oh, something's wrong" when he looks at the watch and covers it.
- Before that, honestly nothing. Pages 1–2 felt like a setup I've seen a hundred times: a kid asks about the old stuff on the wall, then cut to a flashback. That's exactly the kind of template that makes me suspect AI or a committee, and pages 1–3 didn't talk me out of it. Page 4 partly did.

### I predicted
- As soon as the kid said "so much fun", I knew he'd say it wasn't. Correct.
- I predicted the flashback, though not the debt. The debt surprised me.
- Now I'm predicting: the debt is a trap from some company or "House", he gets out by fixing that pump with the bracket, the four people in the photo are his friends from Keel, and the watch is connected to the debt (a counter? a tracker?). Paid in full is the ending, and the frame on the wall already told me so.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A guy who ended up a successful, humble inventor started out as a teenager stuck in a debt he didn't take on, in some industrial place called Keel, and the story is how he got out.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 3.** In a shop I'd have flipped through a nice house, a kid pointing at frames, a "v1 - didn't work" sign, and a dad being humble, then put it back before turning to page 4. Nothing on pages 1–3 is wrong. It's just quiet, and nobody wants anything. The only reason I turned the page was that it was assigned, and page 4 was the page that earned it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but it's closer to "wait a week" than "right now." It's a real cliffhanger, not a cheap one: "owed money I never borrowed" is a question I want answered, and seeing the 17-year-old with his hand on a rusty pump makes it about a person. What holds me back is that I don't know him yet. He's a nice dad and a kid in a caption. And "KEEL. 2043." is a place name I can't picture, so the final beat is a label rather than a feeling. I want to know how the debt works. I don't yet care whether he gets out.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 3; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Page 1.** A pleasant house in honey-colored light, a wall of awards, and a kid asking "Did you make *all of these*?" Half my class would decide right there that it's a feel-good AI story about a nice inventor and stop paying attention. They wouldn't make it to the debt line that would have caught them.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"It starts super boring with a dad showing his kid his inventions wall. Then on the last page he says he started out owing like 400k he never borrowed, and it flashes back to him as a teenager fixing pumps in some place called Keel. The last page is actually decent."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** Three pages of a nice dad in a nice hallway that I'd have shelved, then one good line about a debt he never borrowed that made me want to know how that happened.
