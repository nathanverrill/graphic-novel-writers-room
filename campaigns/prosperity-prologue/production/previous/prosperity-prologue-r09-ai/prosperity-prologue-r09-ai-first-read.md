# First Read: prosperity-prologue, Issue 01, pages 1–3 (audition)

I read A first, then B. The two versions are close to the same scene with the same dialogue, so my read of B was not cold: I knew every line before I got to it. Where B differs, it is mostly in what I *can't* read, and that turned out to matter.

I read only what a reader gets: the art as described, the balloons and the lettered text. There are no captions in either version. Nothing on the page tells me the year, the place, or anybody's name except "Alex," and I only get that from the speaker tag. Notes about who the watch belonged to, what the frames "mean" and what the reader should feel are not things I can see, so I've left them out.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **A, page 2, panel 3: "v1 - didn't work"** under a scorched, cracked prototype. This is the first thing that felt like a real person made it. It's a little funny, and it's the dad's joke on himself. I liked him for a second.
- **A, page 2, panel 4: "PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS."** I noticed it even though she didn't. Why would you frame a bank statement? Was he in debt? Did inventing ruin him? This is the only spot where I leaned in, curious about something specific.
- **A, page 3, panel 4.** He covers the watch with his thumb after she says "so much fun." I could tell this was the point of the scene. I don't know why, but I knew it was meant to hurt. That's a small hook.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **A, page 1, panels 1–2.** A house at dusk, then a hallway. Nothing happens. I'd have flipped past panel 1 in a second. The drone is the only sign that it's the future.
- **A, page 1, panel 3, the headlines.** I read them and got almost nothing from them (see below). After two headlines I didn't understand, I stopped trying to read the rest of the wall.
- **A, page 2, panels 1–2.** "Made some. Fixed most." and "That one's in my school!" are fine, but it's wall-tour small talk. I was waiting for it to be about something.

### I was confused
- **"40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER."** What is a Phantum? Is that misspelled? Why is a shop *licensing* it a headline? Why the 40th? I genuinely don't know if this is good news or bad news.
- **"SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN."** Who are "the Houses"? It's capitalised, so it's obviously a Thing. Companies? Families? Rulers? Nobody tells me.
- The photo of "dozens of founders by water units under different shop signs." I can see lots of people and lots of shops. I don't know why that's on Dad's wall or what it means to him.
- **Why is the drawing in a fancy frame?** I get that it's important. I don't get why.
- The watch has a small dark window that's dark. Is it broken? Off? Supposed to show something? Confused but intrigued, just barely.

| "the Houses" | A, p1 p3 | No |

### Lines I read twice
- "40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER." Read it three times. Gave up.
- "SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN." Read it twice. Gave up on "the Houses."
- "Because it never needed to be." Read it twice. It sounds deep, but I can't tell what it means. Never needed a patent because it was free? Because it was stolen? Because it was someone else's? It's a riddle answer to a kid's simple question.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Dad and daughter: easy. Two people, very different. She's bright and pointy; he's tired and stooped.
- The four in the lab photo: a blob. I couldn't tell any of them apart and didn't try.
- The founders photo: a crowd. Nobody.
- I can't say if the watch is meant to point to another person. If it is, I didn't get who.

### I felt something
- A small warm thing at "v1 - didn't work."
- A small uneasy thing at page 3, panel 4. She's so happy ("*so* much fun") and he hides the watch. I felt the gap between them. I don't know what's in the gap, but I felt it.
- That's it. I don't care about either of them yet. She's cute. He's sad. That's all I have.

### I predicted
- Someone (probably the owner of the watch, or one of the lab people) actually made the drawing, and Dad didn't. Or Dad took it. Or that person died.
- Inventing was not fun. It cost money, or a friend, or both. The "0 credits" statement is the clue.
- Page 4 will flash back, or show the actual pump bracket.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A dad whose wall says he's a successful inventor is hiding that the most important thing on it, a drawing that "never needed" a patent, came with a cost or a person he won't talk about.

### Where I would have put it down
**A, page 1, panel 3.** Two headlines full of words I don't know ("Phantum," "the Houses," "licenses"), on a page where nothing has happened yet. That's the moment I'd think "this is one of those books" and pick up my phone. If I got past it, page 2's "v1 - didn't work" would have kept me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 3 ends on a silent close-up of a thumb over a watch. It's a real beat, not a fake-out: something hurts this man. But I don't know who he is, what the watch is or what the drawing is, so all I'm left with is "huh, he's sad about something." With only one page to go, I'd turn it because it's short. If this were the end of an issue, I'd wait, maybe not bother. It's a question about a mood, not about a person I care about.

## Version B

(Second read. I already knew every line.)

### Pulled me forward
- Same as A: **B, page 2, panel 3, "v1 - didn't work"** is still the best moment. In B it's a close-up with the two of them at the edge of the frame, so the burnt thing feels more like *theirs*. I slowed down on it more than in A.
- **B, page 2, panel 4.** Dad is at the edge of the panel *watching her* find the sketch. I noticed him watching. That made me nervous for him a page earlier than in A, which I liked.
- **B, page 3, panel 2.** Her hand hovers near the glass without touching it. That's a nice small thing; it makes the drawing feel untouchable.
- **B, page 3, panel 4.** Same thumb over the watch. Same small hook.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **B, page 1, panels 1–2.** Same empty opening. Panel 2 is a lot of standing.
- **B, page 1, panel 3.** I can see two big news clippings, but I can't read them. No text. So I looked, saw "newspapers," and moved on. Weirdly, this was *faster* than A because I wasn't stuck on words I didn't know. But it's also emptier. The wall is just "lots of achievements" with nothing specific.
- **B, page 2, panels 1–2.** Same small talk.

### I was confused
- What the clippings say. They're the biggest things on the wall, they're clearly meant to matter, and I can't read them. Is that on purpose?
- Why the drawing is framed: same as A.
- The dark window on the watch: same as A, confused but mildly intrigued.
- I don't even get A's half-clues ("small makers," "independent shop"), so I have less idea what world this is. Just a house with water stuff on the roof.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| What the big clippings are about | B, p1 p3 | No. Unreadable |
| Why the house has water panels, a cistern and a purifier | B, p1 p1 | No |

B has fewer unexplained *words* than A. It has just as many unexplained *things*.

### Lines I read twice
- "Because it never needed to be." Same problem as in A.
- Nothing else. There's no headline text to trip on.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Dad and daughter: easy, same as A.
- Lab four and founders: anonymous crowds, same as A.

### I felt something
- Same warm "v1" moment. Slightly more uneasy in the middle, because he's shown watching her get close to the sketch.
- Same gap at the end: her "*so* much fun," his covered watch.
- I still don't care about them as people yet.

### I predicted
- Same as A: the sketch belongs to someone else, or cost him someone, and the watch is tied to it. B's "Dad watching her" panel made me surer of this.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A dad hiding the painful story behind a crumpled drawing his daughter thinks is just another happy invention.

### Where I would have put it down
**B, page 1**, the whole page. It's a house, a hallway, a wall I can't read and one line from a kid. Nothing confused me badly; nothing grabbed me either. I'd have stuck around about one page longer than in A because nothing pushed me out, but nothing pulled me in until "v1 - didn't work."

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Same ending as A, and the same verdict: a real emotional beat about someone I barely know. I'd turn to page 4 because it's one page. I wouldn't hunt for an issue 2 off this alone.

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
They're almost the same scene. Neither one wins me on the dialogue, because the dialogue is identical. What split them was **page 1, panel 3, the headlines.**

- In A, I could read them, and they lost me: "PHANTUM," "the Houses," "licenses." That's the spot where I nearly quit. But they're also the only text in either version that hints at a bigger world (small makers against someone called the Houses), and once I got past them, that hint made the "0 credits" statement more interesting.
- In B, the clippings are blank to me, so I glided through. Nothing tripped me, but there was also nothing for me to wonder about. B makes up for it a little in the middle: Dad watching her (p2 p4) and her hand hovering at the glass (p3 p2) made the ending land a bit harder.

So: A confused me more and gave me more. B was smoother, and the ending hit a little harder. Honestly, they pull about equally for different reasons. The deciding moment for both is **page 2, panel 3, "v1 - didn't work."** That's where each version first stopped feeling like homework.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A sad dad, a happy kid, and a wall of headlines I couldn't decode, with a burnt "didn't work" that made me like him for a second.
- **B:** The same sad dad and happy kid, easier to get through, quieter, and a little emptier, ending on a thumb over a watch I want explained.
