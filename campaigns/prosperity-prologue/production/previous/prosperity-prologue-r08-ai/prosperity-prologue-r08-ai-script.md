## Page 1 (right) — Establish Alex’s ordinary, earned prosperity and let his daughter turn the wall into a question.

**Panel 1.** Wide exterior in warm evening light. An ordinary, well-kept house on a quiet street. Solar roof tiles catch the honey-colored sun. A maintained vegetable garden borders the walk. An ordinary ten-year-old car sits in the drive. Comfortable, never flashy.

**Panel 2.** Human-scale hallway view. Alex stands beside a dense but orderly wall of framed patents, machine photographs and clippings. He wears a plain charcoal-brown sweater over a pale shirt; grey shows at his temples. His left wrist is partly hidden beneath his sleeve. His approximately nine-year-old daughter stands on tiptoe before the wall, studying the objects one by one.

**Panel 3.** Close enough for the wall’s variety to read without becoming a diagram: the framed headlines **“40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER”** and **“SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN,”** a photograph of separate founders beside separate water units and different shop signs, patent diagrams and machine photographs. Among them, the framed photograph of four unnamed collaborators laughing together in a lab.

**Panel 4.** The daughter points into the wall, then looks back toward Alex. Her finger lands between the patent records and machine photographs.

ALEX’S DAUGHTER: Did you make *all of these*?

**PAGE CHECK:** The reader begins in a warm, ordinary home, then understands that the wall is a record of Alex’s working life. The daughter’s question shifts the page from observation to conversation. Page turn pulls toward Alex’s answer.

## Page 2 (left) — Alex qualifies authorship, makes licensing concrete through school, and plants failure and repayment before the private sketch takes focus.

**Panel 1.** Medium shot. Alex reaches up and taps the framed photograph of the four unnamed collaborators laughing in the lab. The photograph remains behind glass; none of the figures becomes a separate active character. Alex’s expression is calm and matter-of-fact.

ALEX: Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me.

**Panel 2.** The daughter has moved one step along the wall. She points at the framed purifier patent, where the purifier diagram is visible beside the clipping about the fortieth independent shop. Alex remains in the same hallway space, slightly behind her.

ALEX’S DAUGHTER: That one's in my school.

ALEX: That one's in a lot of schools. Somebody else's company put it there.

**Panel 3.** Close on a framed, scorched and cracked prototype among the cleaner patent records. A handwritten label is clearly legible:

**v1 - didn't work**

Neither Alex nor his daughter comments. Their partial silhouettes at the edge of the frame keep the object connected to the wall rather than making it a display by itself.

**Panel 4.** The mismatched frame containing the crumpled pump-bracket sketch sits low on the wall. Its grease-stained paper and crude pencil geometry are visible through the glass. Nearby, smaller and partly surrounded by other frames, the account statement is legible to the reader:

**PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS**

The daughter’s head has tilted toward the mismatched frame. Her attention is on the sketch, not the statement. Alex is present at the edge of the panel, watching her notice it.

**PAGE CHECK:** Alex has shifted the wall from a record of solitary achievement to evidence of repair, borrowed knowledge and independent deployment. The failed prototype and paid-in-full statement complicate the polished record without dialogue. The daughter’s attention lands on the mismatched frame, creating the next question.

## Page 3 (right) — Move from public records to the private origin, then end on Alex’s silence and concealed watch.

**Panel 1.** Close on the mismatched frame. The paper inside is visibly crumpled and grease-stained, with smudged fingerprints and a crude pencil drawing of a pump bracket. There is no patent number. The frame is visibly older and unlike the orderly frames around it; the damaged sheet remains informal inside it.

**Panel 2.** The daughter faces Alex, still slightly raised onto her toes. One hand hovers near the frame without touching the glass. Alex stands beside her, his posture quiet and guarded but not stern.

ALEX’S DAUGHTER: This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?

ALEX: Because it never needed to be.

**Panel 3.** The daughter turns from the sketch toward Alex with a bright, uncomplicated smile. The wall fills the background: polished patents, photographs, clippings and the rough frame occupy the same field.

ALEX’S DAUGHTER: Inventing stuff must have been *so much fun*.

**Panel 4.** Close, quiet panel. Alex studies the pump-bracket sketch. His gaze drops to his left wrist. The old steel watch is partly hidden beneath his sleeve: slightly too large, worn steel, cream dial, scratched glass and a small dark display window with no readout. His thumb comes over the watch face, obscuring it while leaving enough of the cream dial and dark window visible to identify the object. His other hand remains at his side. No dialogue.

**PAGE CHECK:** The sketch has become personal rather than merely unfinished. The daughter’s admiration presses against what Alex knows about the beginning. His hand covering the watch withholds history and creates the page-turn question: what was the beginning really like?

## Page 4 (left) — Alex names the debt that began his story, then the sketch becomes the first repair in Keel.

**Panel 1.** Alex remains beside the wall, the mismatched sketch frame behind him. He looks toward his daughter rather than at the records.

ALEX: Not in the beginning.

**Panel 2.** The daughter looks up at him and waits. Her bright expression has gone still, not frightened, simply attentive. The wall remains behind them; the small **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS”** statement is still visible to the reader. No dialogue.

**Panel 3.** Alex faces her. His voice stays plain and understated. The wall’s polished records and the rough sketch share the frame behind him.

ALEX: In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed.

**Panel 4 — FULL-WIDTH TRANSITION.** Visual match from the grease-stained pump-bracket sketch to the actual metal bracket: the same crude geometry fills the foreground, first as pencil lines on damaged paper, then as rusted, grease-dark machinery. The warm hallway is gone. **KEEL. 2043.** Seventeen-year-old Alex, visibly younger and narrower, stands in the rust-and-charcoal pump hall with grease-marked work clothes. His palm is flat on the pump. The bracket match is immediately readable; the industrial setting and Alex’s hand establish the first repair under pressure.

CAPTION: KEEL. 2043.

**PAGE CHECK:** Alex has answered the emotional question without explaining the debt. The full-width match turns the framed sketch into a real machine and hands directly into Chapter 1. The new question is how seventeen-year-old Alex came to owe a debt he never borrowed—and what he will do with the pump in front of him.
