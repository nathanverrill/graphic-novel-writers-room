## Page 1 (right) — Establish the quiet, earned life before questioning its origins.

**Panel 1.** Wide exterior of Alex’s home in warm, honeyed evening light. It’s an ordinary, comfortable house on a quiet street. Solar tiles line the roof. A well-maintained vegetable garden grows in the yard. A ten-year-old car sits parked in the drive. Nothing is flashy; everything is cared for.

**Panel 2.** Inside the hallway, human-scale. The wall is a dense but orderly archive of framed patents, machine photographs, and clippings. Alex’s nine-year-old daughter stands on tiptoe, her head tilted forward, studying the frames. She wears a mustard-yellow cardigan over a pale blue school shirt. Alex stands a step behind her. He is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, wearing a plain charcoal-brown sweater. On his left wrist, half-hidden by his sleeve, sits Adrian’s old, slightly oversized steel watch. 

**Panel 3.** Close view of the wall’s public record. The frames show independent commercial success without a single corporate monolith. We see a photograph of dozens of different founders standing by separate water units under different shop signs. Two framed news clippings are clearly legible: **“40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER”** and **“SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN.”**

**Panel 4.** The daughter points a finger toward the center of the dense display, looking back over her shoulder at Alex.

DAUGHTER: Did you make *all of these*?

PAGE CHECK: The reader turns the page to hear Alex's answer and discover the reality of the wall.


## Page 2 (left) — Broaden the definition of success and plant the hidden complications.

**Panel 1.** Alex rests his work-worn hand on a framed photograph of four unnamed collaborators laughing in a lab. He is not claiming the whole wall for himself.

ALEX: Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me.

**Panel 2.** The daughter points to a framed patent diagram of the Phantum Purifier. 

DAUGHTER: That one's in my school.

ALEX: That one's in a lot of schools. Somebody else's company put it there.

**Panel 3.** A framed object sits quietly among the polished patents: a scorched, cracked physical prototype. A handwritten note mounted beneath it simply reads: **“v1 - didn't work.”** 

**Panel 4.** A wider view of a lower section of the wall. Tucked in the corner of a frame is a small, quiet account statement that reads: **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS.”** The daughter doesn't notice it. Her attention has settled entirely on a different frame nearby—one made of mismatched wood, holding a piece of crumpled paper.

PAGE CHECK: The polished public record gives way to the private, damaged history.


## Page 3 (right) — Shift from public achievement to concealed history.

**Panel 1.** Close on the mismatched frame. Inside is a crumpled, grease-stained sheet of paper. It bears a crude pencil sketch of a pump bracket, smudged with dark fingerprints. There is no patent number, no corporate seal. It is raw and damaged.

**Panel 2.** The daughter studies it, her head holding its signature inquisitive tilt. 

DAUGHTER: This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?

ALEX: Because it never needed to be.

**Panel 3.** The daughter turns to Alex, beaming. Her face is bright with uncomplicated admiration. 

DAUGHTER: Inventing stuff must have been *so much fun*.

**Panel 4.** Alex looks down at his left wrist. His right thumb slides over the face of Adrian’s old steel watch, covering it. We can just see the edge of the plain cream dial, the scratched glass, and the dark, lifeless display window beneath his thumb. He does not speak.

PAGE CHECK: The watch is covered; the silence holds. The reader must turn the page to get the answer.
