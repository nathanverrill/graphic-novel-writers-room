# Prosperity Prologue — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Explicit decision.** This is the four-page prologue, **“The Wall,”** set about 2070 and turning into Keel in 2043. It opens not on a dystopia but on where Alex ends up: an ordinary, comfortable, earned life. The ending should let the partner and backer see, quietly and visually, that the story ends in earned success, protected ownership, independent businesses licensing and competing, a business that serves people and makes money doing it, failure that taught something, and a debt paid back in full rather than cancelled. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

The prologue must communicate these ideas through what is seen on the wall, never through explanation. Nothing names the partner, the backer or the missions. No line explains a lesson. There is no co-op, collective or giveaway: the businesses are independent companies that license Alex’s patents and compete with one another and with the Houses. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The intended emotional movement is from quiet, recognizable prosperity to curiosity about the past, then to the first image of the debt and hardship that made the ending possible. The daughter’s questions guide the reader through Alex’s wall of evidence. Her final question leads to his first direct admission about the beginning, and the page turn into Chapter 1 is a visual and temporal reversal: the warm house becomes Keel’s rust and charcoal pump hall. `(rules/decisions.md; input/pitch.md)`

The central dramatic question of the prologue is: **How did this ordinary, earned life begin?** The answer is withheld until the final page: Alex began with a debt he had never borrowed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The prologue is made for high-school readers who have no prior knowledge of Keel, Alpha, the Water Wars or the safeguards. This piece therefore establishes no additional background and does not assume the reader knows the larger story. It ends at the threshold of Chapter 1 rather than explaining the world beyond the transition. `(rules/reader.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

## Structure

**Format:** Exactly four pages.

### Act I — The visible outcome: Pages 1–2

The reader enters Alex’s warm, ordinary 2070 home and reads the wall as evidence of a successful life. The daughter’s questions establish that Alex’s work is larger than his solitary authorship: he repaired, collaborated, borrowed ideas and licensed designs through independent companies.

**Turn on Page 2:** The wall’s polished record is complicated by failure and a nearly hidden paid-in-full statement. The daughter then identifies the one object that does not belong: the rough pump-bracket sketch in its mismatched frame.

### Act II — The private beginning: Page 3

The sketch shifts the conversation from public achievement to personal history. Alex says it never needed to be a patent. The daughter’s uncomplicated belief that inventing was fun forces him to distinguish the successful outcome from the difficult beginning.

**Turn on Page 3:** Alex’s covered watch and withheld response create the question the final page must answer: what was the beginning really like?

### Act III — The debt and the threshold: Page 4

Alex corrects her: “Not in the beginning.” He names the debt. The final full-width panel does not explain it; it places the reader at its physical and temporal beginning in Keel.

**Final state:** The question changes from *What did Alex make?* to *How did seventeen-year-old Alex begin under a debt he never borrowed?*

## Page plot

### Page 1 — The wall

**Page-turn function:** Establish the warm destination, then end on the daughter’s question so the reader turns to hear Alex’s answer.

1. **Panel 1:** Wide exterior of Alex’s quiet, well-kept 2070 home in warm evening light: solar roof tiles, vegetable garden and an ordinary ten-year-old car. The house is comfortable, not flashy.
2. **Panel 2:** Human-scale hallway view. The daughter stands on tiptoe before a dense but legible wall of patents, machine photographs and clippings. Alex stands nearby in a plain sweater, grey at the temples; Adrian’s old steel watch is on his left wrist.
3. **Panel 3:** Close view of the wall’s public record: the two approved headlines, the photograph of separate founders with separate water units and shop signs, patent diagrams and machine photographs. The visual evidence establishes licensing and competition without narration.
4. **Panel 4:** The daughter points into the wall and asks, “Did you make *all of these*?” **Page-turn reveal:** the reader turns to Alex’s qualified answer.

### Page 2 — What counts as his

**Page-turn function:** Let Alex’s answer broaden the meaning of success, then plant the hidden complication before the daughter chooses the sketch.

1. **Panel 1:** Alex taps the framed photograph of four unnamed collaborators laughing in a lab. He says, “Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me.” The photograph remains the only appearance of Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren.
2. **Panel 2:** The daughter points to the framed purifier patent and says, “That one's in my school.” Alex answers, “That one's in a lot of schools. Somebody else's company put it there.” The patent and the daughter’s school connection make licensing concrete.
3. **Panel 3:** A framed scorched, cracked prototype, visibly marked **“v1 - didn't work.”** Neither character comments. The failure sits among the polished records.
4. **Panel 4:** A small, almost hidden framed statement reads **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS.”** The daughter does not notice it. In the same panel, her attention settles on the nearby mismatched frame containing the crumpled drawing. **Page-turn reveal:** the private object is now the subject.

### Page 3 — The drawing

**Page-turn function:** Shift from public achievement to concealed history and end on Alex’s withheld response.

1. **Panel 1:** Close on the mismatched frame: a crumpled, grease-stained sheet with a crude pencil pump-bracket sketch, smudged fingerprints and no patent number. The frame is visibly unlike the others; the damaged paper remains informal.
2. **Panel 2:** The daughter asks, “This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?” Alex answers, “Because it never needed to be.” The answer is complete but not explanatory: the drawing solved a real problem.
3. **Panel 3:** The daughter beams and says, “Inventing stuff must have been *so much fun*.” Her face and posture express uncomplicated admiration.
4. **Panel 4:** Alex studies the sketch, then looks down at Adrian’s old watch. His thumb covers its face. The watch’s cream dial, scratched glass and dark display window are visible; it gives no readout. **Page-turn reveal:** the reader turns to Alex’s correction and debt admission.

### Page 4 — The beginning

**Page-turn function:** Deliver the principal verbal reveal on the left-hand page after the Page 3 turn, then use the full-width final panel to enter Chapter 1.

1. **Panel 1:** Alex, still beside the wall, says, “Not in the beginning.” The line answers the daughter’s assumption without explaining the past.
2. **Panel 2:** The daughter looks up at him and waits. Her unfinished response is held in the silence; the wall remains behind them, including the reader-visible paid-in-full statement.
3. **Panel 3:** Alex says, “In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed.” This is the rounded conversational form of the exact 418,607-credit Chapter 1 balance.
4. **Panel 4 — full-width transition:** The grease-stained pump-bracket sketch visually matches into the actual pump bracket in Keel’s rust-and-charcoal pump hall. Seventeen-year-old Alex has his palm flat on the pump. The only caption is **“KEEL. 2043.”** This is the first visible example of Alex fixing real machinery under pressure, not the later invention that makes him successful.

## Reader vs. character knowledge

### The reader knows, or may know, before Alex says it

- The wall contains evidence of independent licensing and competition.
- Alex’s work reaches schools through another company.
- A failed prototype was preserved rather than hidden.
- The account statement reads **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS.”**
- The mismatched frame contains a pump-bracket sketch that visually resembles the final Keel machinery.
- Alex’s watch is emotionally significant and deliberately concealed.
- The daughter does not notice the paid-in-full statement.
- The daughter does not yet know that the wall’s success began under debt.

### Alex knows that the daughter does not

- The wall’s records are only the visible outcome of a much harder beginning.
- The paid-in-full statement records the same obligation he is about to name.
- The pump-bracket sketch belongs to a practical repair history, not necessarily to formal invention.
- The watch connects him to concealed history, though the prologue does not state what that history is.
- The debt’s exact Chapter 1 balance is 418,607 credits; his spoken figure is rounded to four hundred thousand.

### The daughter believes, but the reader should question

- The objects on the wall are all things Alex made.
- Inventing was enjoyable from the beginning.
- A valuable invention should naturally be a patent.

Her questions expose these assumptions; Alex corrects them without turning the exchange into a lesson.

## Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

### Decision needed

- Whether the title **“The Wall”** appears anywhere in the finished pages or remains a working title. This does not affect the plot.
- Whether the visual treatment leans slightly painterly or cleanly graphic, provided the warm-to-industrial reversal and human-scale staging remain.
- Whether the small paid-in-full statement should be easy for a careful reader to find or remain genuinely incidental. The current plot keeps it legible but subordinate to the daughter’s discovery of the mismatched frame.

### Proposal

- Use frame age and mounting quality as a quiet visual distinction: public patents and clippings are orderly, while the pump sketch and account statement feel more private and handled. This adds no new story event and does not alter canon.
- Include small signs of ongoing household maintenance—such as a cleanly repaired latch or mended furniture—only if they remain background texture and do not compete with the wall.

### Canon protection

No proposed change adds a partner, backer, mission, Alpha, safeguards, artifacts, creditor, repayment mechanism, additional character, casino imagery, collective economics, cancellation, bailout or windfall.

## Ending

The prologue ends on the full-width transition into Keel’s pump hall, not on an explanation of the debt. Seventeen-year-old Alex stands with his palm flat on the pump beneath the caption **“KEEL. 2043.”** Chapter 1 begins after this final panel; no Chapter 1 scene or dialogue is added here.

The ending links the present and the past through three continuities:

- Adrian’s old watch remains on Alex’s wrist as concealed history; Adrian’s identity and relationship to Alex remain unstated.
- The crumpled pump-bracket sketch matches the Chapter 1 repair. It solved a real problem but did not need to become a patent.
- The rounded debt figure links to Chapter 1’s exact 418,607-credit balance. The framed **“PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS”** statement records that same obligation after eventual repayment.

The final question is not answered: **How did seventeen-year-old Alex come to owe a debt he never borrowed, and what will he do with the machinery in front of him?**
