# First Read: prosperity-prologue, pages 1–3 (audition)

I read this as the reader in `rules/reader.md`: 16, it's homework, it's the night before, and I'm looking for a reason to say it's bad or AI-made. **I read A first.** The two versions use the same dialogue word for word, so B was not a cold read. By B, I already knew the watch was the payoff.

One thing about both: I only see the art and the balloons. **Nobody says a name out loud in either version.** The script calls him "Alex" and the watch "Adrian's", but on the page he's "the dad" and it's "a watch". I'm reporting it that way.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **Issue 01, page 3, panel 4.** This is the only moment in the three pages where something happened to me. She says inventing must have been "so much fun". He doesn't answer, looks at his wrist and puts his thumb over his watch. He's hiding something, from her or from himself. I sat up a little.
- **Page 2, panel 2:** "Somebody else's company put it there." That's an odd thing to tell a nine-year-old. It sounded slightly bitter, or maybe proud. I couldn't tell which, and that made me curious.
- **Page 2, panel 3:** the burnt prototype labelled "v1 - didn't work." I liked looking at it. It's the only object on the wall that looks like it has a story.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, panels 1–3.** A nice house, solar tiles, a vegetable garden, a wall of frames. I flicked past the exterior. It felt like the opening shot of an ad for a bank or an insurance company.
- **Page 1, panel 3, the headlines.** I skimmed them. "40TH INDEPENDENT SHOP LICENSES PHANTUM PURIFIER" is a lot of words I don't care about yet, on the first page.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** I didn't notice the "PAID IN FULL · BALANCE 0 CREDITS" statement on my first pass, and the girl doesn't either. When I went back and found it, I didn't know what to do with it.

### I was confused
- **Page 3, panel 2:** "Because it never needed to be." I didn't know what he meant. Was the sketch too small to bother patenting, did someone give it away, was it made in an emergency? It was confused but intrigued, just barely, because the watch beat came straight after it. On its own it would have been confused and detached.
- The "PAID IN FULL" statement. Paid what? A loan, a debt, a house? Why frame it? It's set up like it matters and I have no idea why.
- "THE HOUSES" in the headline. Houses like homes? Houses like companies? Houses like families in a fantasy book? I couldn't tell.
- **Page 3, panel 4:** the watch has a "dark display window". So it's an analog watch with a small dead screen in it? I wasn't sure whether it's broken, switched off, or meant to show something it no longer can.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First appears | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Phantum Purifier | p1 panel 3 | Partly. It's in schools and it's for water, I think. I never learned why it matters. |
| "Independent shop" licensing / why the 40th one is news | p1 panel 3 | No |
| "The Houses" | p1 panel 3 | No |
| "Water units": why water needs units at all | p1 panel 3 | No. I guessed water is a big deal in this world. |
| Credits (as money) | p2 panel 4 | Sort of. I assumed it's money. |
| What was paid in full | p2 panel 4 | No |
| Who drew the sketch / why it's in a special frame | p3 panel 1 | No |
| Whose watch it is / why it's dead | p3 panel 4 | No. It's slightly too big for him, so I guessed it belonged to someone else. |
| The dad's name, the girl's name | never | No. I'd call them "the dad" and "the kid". |

### Lines I read twice
- "Because it never needed to be." I read it twice and still didn't get it.
- "Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me." Not because it's hard. It read like a quote on a motivational poster, which set off my AI-written alarm. The three-beat rhythm is too neat. I went back to check whether a real dad would say it. Maybe.
- "SMALL MAKERS AND THE HOUSES RACE FOR THE NEXT DESIGN." Twice, because of "the Houses".

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Only two people, so that's easy. The kid in the yellow cardigan, the grey-haired dad in the brown sweater. The four laughing people in the lab photo are a blur, and I assume one of them matters (watch owner? sketch drawer?), but I couldn't pick one out.

### I felt something
Page 3, panel 4. It was a small "oh," not a big one. The kid's "so much fun" against his silence worked. Before that I felt nothing. The wholesome house and the humble-genius dad felt like they wanted me to admire them.

### I predicted
- The watch belonged to someone who's dead, probably the person who drew the pump-bracket sketch.
- Inventing was not fun. Something bad happened around water, and the purifier came out of it.
- The "paid in full" is a debt he spent years paying off, probably connected to the same thing.
- The next page is him either telling her or not telling her.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A successful inventor is hiding from his daughter that his success came out of something painful, involving whoever owned that watch.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 1.** If I'd picked it up in a shop, I'd have shut it after the house and the wall of headlines. Nothing on page 1 is a problem, a person in trouble or a question. It's a nice house and a proud wall. I only got to the watch because I was made to.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 3 is the last page I was given. It ends on a real question, not a loud noise: why won't he answer her, and whose watch is it? But the question belongs to a guy I met three pages ago and don't know the name of. I'd turn the page if it was right there. Would I go and get it next week? No. It's a quiet hook, not a need.

## Version B

*(Read second. I already knew where it was going.)*

The reader sees the same balloons, the same wall and the same ending as in A. The end of the B file has a "Decision needed" note about dialogue sources. A reader never sees that, so I ignored it.

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 3, panel 4,** same as in A: he says nothing and covers the watch. In B he looks at the sketch first and then down at the watch. If that comes across in the art, it tied the sketch and the watch together for me more than A did: same person, I assumed. Either way it's the moment.
- **Page 3, panel 2:** the dad already looks "guarded" when she asks about the drawing. That's a hint one panel earlier than in A that the sketch is the sore spot. Because of it, "Because it never needed to be" read less like a riddle and more like someone dodging the question.
- **Page 2, panel 2:** "Somebody else's company put it there." Same small itch as in A.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, panels 1–3,** same as A. The house, and a wall I'm told to read. Panel 3 is even busier in B: both headlines, the founders photo, patents, machine photos and the lab photo all at once. I didn't read it. I looked at it like wallpaper.
- **Page 2, panel 3:** the "v1 - didn't work" prototype. On my second read I went past it faster.
- **Page 3, panel 1:** the sketch was already shown on page 2, panel 4, so the close-up felt like seeing it a second time instead of finding it.

### I was confused
- The same as in A: "Because it never needed to be," "PAID IN FULL", "the Houses", and the dead little screen on the watch.
- **Page 2, panel 4:** the sketch and the "PAID IN FULL" statement are in the same panel, near each other. That made me think they were connected: did the sketch pay something off? I was more confused about the statement here than in A, but also a bit more curious.

### Things I was expected to already know
Same as A, nothing new and nothing explained: Phantum Purifier, independent shop licensing, the Houses, water units, credits, what was paid in full, who drew the sketch, whose watch it is and why it's dead, and the dad's and kid's names (never said).

### Lines I read twice
Same three as in A: "Because it never needed to be," "Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas…" (the AI-poster alarm again), and the "Houses" headline.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Dad and kid, easy. The four in the lab photo are still faces I couldn't tell apart. The silhouettes at the edge of page 2, panel 3 didn't change anything for me.

### I felt something
The same small "oh" at the watch. It was a little weaker for me, but that's because I'd seen it in A, not because B did it worse.

### I predicted
The same as in A, a bit more firmly: the sketch and the watch belong to the same person, that person is gone, and the "paid in full" is part of it.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A man whose inventions made him comfortable is hiding from his daughter what it cost, and that cost is tied to the person who drew the sketch and wore the watch.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 1,** same reason. Panel 3 being even more crowded made it slightly easier to put down.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Same as A. It's a real but quiet question about someone I barely know. I'd turn the page if it was in my hand, and I wouldn't go looking for it.

---

## Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
Honestly, they pull about equally. They're the same scene with the same words, and both live or die on page 3, panel 4. If I have to lean, I lean very slightly toward **B**, because of **page 3, panel 2**, where the dad looks guarded at the sketch question. That made "Because it never needed to be" feel like a dodge instead of a riddle, and it made the watch beat land as part of the same thing. A's last panel is a cleaner, tighter image on the wrist, though, and I'd have been fine with either. Neither one gets me past page 1 if I'm not made to read it.

## One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A pretty, polite wall of achievements that only comes alive when a dad covers a dead watch with his thumb.
- **B:** The same polite wall and the same thumb on the watch, with the dad's discomfort showing one panel sooner.
