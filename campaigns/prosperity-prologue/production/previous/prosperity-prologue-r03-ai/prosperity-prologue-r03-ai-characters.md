# Prosperity Prologue — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX

**Visual lock** — Alex is a man in his mid-40s in the 2070 scenes, of average height with a lean, capable repairer's build, squared shoulders and work-worn hands; he has a long, calm face, grey at the temples, close-cropped dark hair, medium-brown skin and steady dark eyes, and wears a plain charcoal-brown sweater over a pale shirt with practical dark trousers. His silhouette is quiet and functional rather than fashionable. On his left wrist he wears Adrian's old steel wristwatch, slightly too large for him, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass and a small display window that stays dark; Adrian's looping equation is engraved on the steel caseback. The watch has no readout and counts nothing. Alex keeps the watch partly hidden beneath his sleeve and covers its face with his thumb after looking at the pump-bracket sketch. In the final transition panel only, Alex is seventeen, visibly younger and narrower, with the same dark hair and determined eyes, wearing grease-marked work clothes in Keel's pump hall, his palm flat on the pump. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Voice** — Alex speaks plainly, concretely and without turning his history into a lesson. He gives credit away rather than claiming sole authorship, answers his daughter's questions directly, and remains understated even when naming the debt. His approved lines are: “Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me.” / “In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed.” `(rules/decisions.md; rules/vocabulary.md)`

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Independent inventor and repairer; he owns or co-owns the patents represented on the wall and licenses them to separate manufacturing and deployment companies.
- **Immediate want:** To answer his daughter's questions about the wall without turning the moment into a lecture or exposing more of the past than he is ready to share.
- **Deeper need / unresolved contradiction:** He needs to acknowledge that his comfortable life was built through repair, borrowed knowledge, collaboration, failure and repayment, not solitary genius; he presents the evidence publicly while keeping its most painful origin private.
- **Leverage / capability:** He can make and repair real machinery under pressure, recognize useful solutions, preserve evidence of work, and distinguish invention from licensing, deployment and ownership.
- **Vulnerability:** The wall's success records are inseparable from an unexplained debt and concealed personal history. The mismatched pump-bracket frame and Adrian's watch can pull him out of the present.
- **Mistaken belief or incomplete model:** He assumes he can let the wall speak for itself and limit the conversation to visible achievements; his daughter's innocent questions make that incomplete.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not explain the creditor, terms, collateral, repayment mechanism, Adrian's identity or the larger history behind the debt.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** Instead of claiming the wall as proof of his genius, he says, “Made some. Fixed most. Borrowed a few ideas from people smarter than me,” then admits that the beginning was defined by debt.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** His approximately nine-year-old daughter, whose curiosity turns private artifacts into direct questions he cannot dismiss.
- **Pressure sequence:** Daughter asks whether he made everything → Alex credits repair and collaborators → she recognizes the purifier from school → Alex distinguishes his patent from somebody else's company → her attention reaches the pump sketch → she calls inventing fun → Alex looks at the sketch and covers the watch → he tells her the beginning was not fun and names the debt.

**Arc in this book** — Alex moves from allowing his wall to represent a comfortable, successful life to admitting that its beginning was difficult and financially inexplicable. He does not confess the whole history; he gives one decisive truth, then the story returns to his seventeen-year-old self in Keel. **Proof of change:** he breaks his own restraint and says, “In the beginning, I owed four hundred thousand credits I'd never borrowed.” His refusal to explain further is also deliberate: the prologue ends at the threshold of that history.

### ALEX'S DAUGHTER

**Visual lock** — Alex's daughter is approximately nine years old, short and slight with a child's soft, round face, warm-brown skin, large dark eyes and thick dark hair gathered into two low, slightly uneven braids; she wears a mustard-yellow cardigan over a pale blue school shirt, a knee-length navy skirt, white socks and scuffed red sneakers. Her silhouette is small, upright and inquisitive, and she repeatedly rises onto her toes to reach the wall with her attention. Her signature detail is the slight forward tilt of her head when she discovers something that does not fit. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

**Voice** — She is direct, curious and enthusiastic, treating visible objects as evidence and asking the next question before the previous answer has fully settled. Her approved lines are: “Did you make *all of these*?” / “This one's just a drawing. Why isn't it a patent?” `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Alex's daughter and the reader's guide through the hallway wall.
- **Immediate want:** To understand what her father made and why the objects on the wall are different from one another.
- **Deeper need / unresolved contradiction:** She wants a simple story of achievement, but the wall contains collaboration, failure, ordinary commercial use, repayment and a debt she did not expect.
- **Leverage / capability:** She notices concrete visual differences adults might overlook: the purifier from school, the mismatched frame and the gap between a rough drawing and a patent.
- **Vulnerability:** She begins with uncomplicated admiration and has no framework for her father's unexplained debt.
- **Mistaken belief or incomplete model:** She believes the wall is a record of things Alex made and that inventing must have been enjoyable from the beginning.
- **Boundary at the start:** She does not knowingly push past Alex's privacy; she asks only about what she can see.
- **Decision only she would make:** She treats the mismatched frame and unpatented drawing as important enough to interrupt the celebratory story and ask why it is not a patent.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex, whose short answers encourage her curiosity but whose final debt reveal changes what she thinks she knows about him.
- **Pressure sequence:** She asks whether Alex made everything → points out the purifier from school → stops at the mismatched frame → asks why the drawing is not a patent → calls inventing fun → waits when Alex says, “Not in the beginning.”

**Arc in this book** — She shifts from seeing the wall as a collection of her father's achievements to recognizing that it records a more complicated beginning. **Proof of change:** after saying, “Inventing stuff must have been *so* much fun,” she looks up and waits for Alex's answer to the debt reveal. The prologue ends before she can resolve or answer it.

## Ensemble Test

Alex notices the wall's differences as evidence of work, ownership, failure and repayment; his daughter notices those differences as a child-reader looking for a simple story. Alex values useful work, acknowledgment and directness; his daughter values discovery and emotional wonder. Alex can repair machinery, interpret records and carry difficult history; his daughter can connect the purifier to school, detect the mismatched frame and force the next question.

The four collaborators differ from Alex by being present only as a shared photograph: they supply a visual history of borrowed expertise without becoming active characters, named speakers or a collective business. Adrian differs from everyone visible by being represented only through a concealed watch. TJ and Bi11bot remain outside the prologue entirely.

The central contrast is therefore not between competing personalities but between Alex's guarded adult knowledge and his daughter's open attention. Her questions cause his disclosures; his disclosures change the meaning of the wall; the pump-bracket sketch and watch carry the unresolved past into Keel.
