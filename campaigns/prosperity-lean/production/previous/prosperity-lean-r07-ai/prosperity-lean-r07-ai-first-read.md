# First Read — Issue 01, pages 1–3, both auditions

I read A first, then B. My read of B wasn't fully cold. I already knew there was a hidden pipe, so B's reveal on page 2 had less surprise for me than it would for a new reader.

---

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- Issue 01, page 1, panel 1 into panel 2: the sign says NORMAL and the tap goes dry. I got that straight away and it's a decent hook. The sign is lying, fine, I'm listening.
- Page 1, panel 4: an eye glowing inside the bag. That's the moment I actually leaned in. Something is hiding in his bag.
- Page 2, panel 3: the metal finger pointing down without a word. Creepy-cute, and I liked that it didn't talk.
- Page 2, panel 2: "The board is lying." Short, and he says it like he's sure. Good.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 3, panel 2: the queue lines about "dispatch" and "upper blocks". I skimmed them as generic angry-crowd noise.
- Page 3, panel 1: him clearing dirt. Nothing happens there.

### I was confused
- Page 3, panel 4: the "forbidden old mine". I only know it's forbidden because I was told. On the page it's faded stripes on a wall far in the background. Why does he go down at all? Is he trying to fix the pump, or find out who's stealing? I couldn't tell.
- "...drag." on page 2, panel 1. What drag? I guessed it's mechanic talk. Confused, and it didn't make me curious.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Keel" (page 1): a place name and nothing else. Never explained.
- "Allocation" (page 1): I got it as water rationing from context.
- Bi11bot (page 1, panel 4): I don't get the name from the art alone. I only have it here because the panel description says it, so to a reader it's just "an eye".
- Adrian's watch (page 2, panel 1): who is Adrian? A reader would only see a big watch. Never explained.
- Dispatch / upper blocks (page 3): never explained.
- The forbidden mine (page 3): never explained.

### Lines I read twice
- "...drag." (page 2, panel 1). I gave up on it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex is clear. The eye/robot is clear. The queue is one blur: two "QUEUE VOICE"s I couldn't tell apart.

### I felt something
- A little, at page 3, panel 2: people "here since dawn" with empty jugs. That's mild sympathy and nothing more. Alex himself barely talks, so I don't feel anything about him yet.

### I predicted
- Someone powerful is stealing the water, and the pipe leads into the mine. I called it on page 2.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A quiet mechanic kid with a hidden robot finds out the town's water is being secretly stolen and follows it underground.

### Where I would have put it down
- I wouldn't put it down within three pages, because it's short and moves fast. If I did, it would be page 3, panel 2, where it turns into crowd noise.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
He goes down into the dark. I'd turn the page, but only from mild curiosity about the pipe. I don't care about him yet. It's a "what's down there" pull, not a "will he be okay" one.

---

## Version B

### Pulled me forward
- Page 1, panel 3: "It is hurting." / "Pumps don't hurt." / "This one does." This is the moment I stopped wanting to hate the book. The robot has a personality and so does Alex.
- Page 1, panel 4: "Give me three minutes." / "You have two." Quick, and I laughed a little. Now there's a clock.
- Page 2, panel 4: "That isn't a failure." Good line to turn the page on, because it means somebody did this on purpose.
- Page 3, panel 4: the "Quietly." exchange. Charming, and I'd read more of these two talking.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 2, panels 1–2: the gauge, the fitting, "pressure drops here". I skimmed it as fix-it procedure. Having already read A, I knew where it was going.
- Page 3, panel 3: "It's not a route. It's a maintenance gap." / "It is if someone asks." I slowed down here, but it was confusion more than boredom.

### I was confused
- Page 3, panel 3, the route vs. maintenance gap thing. I can tell there's some rule about where he's allowed to go, but I don't know the rule. I was confused but intrigued, so it worked on me more as a hook than a leak.
- Page 2, panel 1: "The allocation's fine." Is he agreeing with the sign? It took me a second to get that he means the numbers are fine and the water isn't.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Keel: same as A, never explained.
- "Authorized allocation" / "uphill": I got rationing from context. "Uphill" stays vague.
- Bi11bot: he never says his own name, and the name isn't in any balloon. As a reader I'd just call him "the robot in the bag".
- Adrian's watch: same as A. To a reader it's just a big watch, and the caseback detail (page 2, panel 4) means nothing to me yet.
- The forbidden mine boundary (page 3, panel 2): again, all I get is hazard paint. Never explained.
- What a "route" is and who would "ask" (page 3): never explained.

### Lines I read twice
- "It's not a route. It's a maintenance gap." / "It is if someone asks."
- "The allocation's fine."

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex and Bi11bot are very distinct. Alex is clipped and the robot asks questions. The QUEUE WORKER has one voice and an attitude ("You have two"), so the queue has a face. I also noticed the child's jug.

### I felt something
- Page 1, panel 4: the child's empty jug, a small pang. I also felt warm toward the robot all through. I actually like these two.

### I predicted
- Same as A: deliberate theft, pipe into the mine. I also predicted the watch is going to matter, and that Adrian is someone who's gone.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A gruff teen mechanic and his curious hidden robot find out someone is secretly diverting Keel's water, and break a rule to follow it.

### Where I would have put it down
- Page 2, panels 1–2 is the dip, the procedure stretch. I wouldn't actually put it down, because the robot keeps cutting in.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. I'd turn now. It isn't because the cliffhanger is big (it's the same "step into the dark" as A). It's because I want to stay with these two. It's a real pull because I care who's standing in it, though the danger is still vague.

---

### Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
B. It was decided on page 1, panel 3, with "It is hurting." / "Pumps don't hurt." / "This one does." In A, the robot is a cool eye and a finger. In B it's a character, and Alex becomes one by answering it. A is cleaner and quicker to read, but I watched it rather than felt it.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **A:** A moody, efficient mystery setup where I liked the glowing eye more than the boy.
- **B:** A boy and his talkative robot I'd happily follow into a hole, even though I still don't know why the hole is forbidden.
