# Characters

These hold on every page. The drafts give way where they disagree.

## Alex Phantum

- **Who he is:** eighteen, an adult, from Keel. Lean, mechanically gifted, self-reliant. He fixes pumps by
  feel, palm flat on the housing, not by the manual.
- **Look:** brown skin, short dense black curls, dark eyes. Dark work shirt with rolled sleeves over
  a black tee, rust-brown cargo trousers with patches, heavy scuffed boots, a worn canvas
  backpack, about 40 cm tall, worn slung over ONE shoulder by one of its two straps; the strap runs
  straight down over that shoulder and never crosses his chest. Big enough to carry Bi11bot folded. Scarred, work-rough hands.
- **Adrian's watch:** a worn steel round case on a faded olive canvas strap, a little big for him,
  with a full glass display face and no hands. Adrian left it in the hidden workshop for Alex's
  eighteenth birthday, tagged FOR ALEX. 18. Alex finds it there (Chapter 1, page 7) and wears it on
  his left wrist from then on. **Before he finds it, he has no watch.**
- **Two screens, like apps.** Most of the time the face is the CLOCK: the time in large, plain white
  digits. When a choice with a price is in front of him, it switches to COST: a large amber-red
  number in credits (310 CR) that fills the face, with a small cost icon above and a thin bar under
  it. The numbers are big enough to read in a close-up. The cost screen first appears in Chapter 1,
  page 11. It never judges or speaks, and never shows words like GOOD or BAD.
- **What COST measures:** what a choice really costs in credits over time: a loan's total
  repayment, a fee, the interest a debt adds. It reads posted prices and terms on Keel's node
  network. It cannot measure sleep, time or risk; those Alex has to weigh himself.
- **The engraving is on the back of the watch only.** A looping equation is engraved on the steel
  caseback, against his skin. Nobody sees it, Alex included, unless he takes the watch off and
  turns it over. It never appears on the face or the strap, except once: the LOADING screen.
- **The watch wakes Bi11bot.** The first time Alex buckles it on (Chapter 1, page 8), it powers up:
  the LOADING screen draws the looping equation in thin amber lines while a ring fills around the
  face, and when the ring closes, Bi11bot's eyes light. After that the face shows CLOCK or COST.
- **Nothing else on his wrists or in his hands by default:** no wristband, no coin, no chip.
  A creased photograph of Adrian and his grandmother stays in his jacket pocket. His Keel work card
  (ID, wages, debt, travel status) is clipped to his belt.
- **His father:** Adrian disappeared when Alex was two. Keel says Adrian threw everything away.
  Alex half believes it and resents him for it.
- **His family:** his grandmother, who is losing her memory. His mother left after Adrian lost
  everything, and we never learn more about her.

## Bi11bot

- **Look:** a rugged, weathered repair robot, companion-sized. An oversized round head of
  segmented faded industrial-blue panels with a mustard-yellow stripe; two large round amber eyes
  in heavy metal rings; round ear assemblies on the sides; rounded shoulder plates; a chest plate
  marked "Bi11" in white; compact legs, broad feet. Chipped blue and mustard-yellow paint, rust,
  rivets, scratches. An old rust-red scarf at his neck.
- **Repair arms, not strong-man arms.** His arms are slim and jointed like a machinist's tools:
  exposed pistons and cables, many small joints, long precise fingers with tool tips (a hex driver,
  a small clamp, a probe). Nothing bulky or muscular about them.
- **He wears his tools.** Wrenches, pliers, a coil of wire, pipe fittings and a small gauge are
  clipped to his back, hips and shoulder plates. When he folds, they wrap the outside of the
  bundle, so it looks like a bag of tools and scrap.
- **Why he matters:** he is B-11, a first-generation Alpha field unit that Adrian built and kept
  when he left. Inside him is the only map to the three artifacts, the Measure first. Alpha's
  records lost him sixteen years ago. Anyone who wants the Measure has to find Bi11bot first.
- **Untraceable when folded.** Unfolded and awake, he checks in with Alpha's network now and then,
  the way an old company laptop does when it's switched on, and the check-in can be traced. Folded,
  his head shell blocks every signal, like a metal box, and he cannot be found.
- **His height is fixed.** Standing, the top of his head is level with Alex's belt, about 75 cm
  (2.5 ft). He is never as tall as Alex and never small enough to hold in one hand.
- **The right eye ring** clicks and rotates when he focuses.
- **He folds into his head.** His head is about 25 cm across. To fold, his limbs telescope in like
  a camera lens, his body slides up inside the hollow head shell, the head panels close like an
  iris over his eyes, and his clipped-on tools wrap the outside. Folded, he is a 25 cm round bundle
  of tools and scrap metal that fits in Alex's backpack, and an inspection reads him as
  unregistered scrap. He never inflates, and folded he is never bigger than the backpack.
- **Unfolding is warm, never creepy.** Three clean steps: the plates slide apart, the limbs telescope
  out, the head panels open and the eyes light. Graceful and a little stiff, never spidery or
  insect-like. Whenever he is in a bag, he is folded; he never stands or sits upright inside one.
- **His expression is a kind old man's.** He has no mouth; he shows warmth with his eyes (soft,
  slightly narrowed, as if smiling), a tilt of the head and an open, gentle posture. Patient,
  helpful, quietly amused, like a favourite grandfather who knows how everything works.
- **Never menacing.** Wise, patient, often quietly amused. No red or harsh light in his eyes, no
  looming low angles, no face lost in shadow. When a scene is tense, the tension is around him.
- **How he talks:** short, plain sentences. He asks more than he explains, and he never lectures.
