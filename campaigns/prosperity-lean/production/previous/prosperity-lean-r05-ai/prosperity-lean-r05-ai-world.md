## Overview

The year is **2043**. Three cities occupy different levels of one mountain basin in the high desert. They are joined by a single spine of infrastructure: a huge water conduit, power lines, a rail line, and identical small white-and-amber control nodes positioned at every level. `(input/world.md)`

Water is the pressure on everything. The **Water Wars** ended a generation ago, but the systems built afterward keep people alive and keep them in place. `(input/world.md)`

The cities are economically and physically stratified:

- **Keel** occupies the bottom of the basin, built in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
- **Halyard** sits midway up the mountain and is the manufacturing and ownership center.
- **Oasis** rises above the cloud layer and holds the greatest visible abundance.

The three cities share ancestry but not living conditions. Differences in health, space, clothing, speech, infrastructure and access make the class structure visible. `(input/world.md)`

## Setting and Geography

### The mountain basin

The basin is high desert terrain. Its vertical geography determines access: Keel is at the bottom, Halyard in the middle, and Oasis at the top. The infrastructure spine follows this ascent, carrying water, power and rail between the three levels. `(input/world.md)`

The cities are not independent settlements. Their placement and survival are tied to the same conduit, power network, rail line and control system. The mountain is therefore both a physical slope and an administrative hierarchy. `(input/world.md)`

### Keel

Keel is built in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.

Visible conditions include:

- stacked housing;
- rusted mining structures;
- patched pumps;
- crowded lanes;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- metered public taps;
- dust;
- digital checkpoints displaying what residents owe.

People queue at the public taps with jugs. Machinery is repaired rather than replaced. The dominant colors are rust, charcoal and oxidized orange. `(input/world.md)`

Travel upward from Keel requires a permit. A permission gate crosses the rail line to Halyard, making the boundary between the two cities physically visible as well as bureaucratic. `(input/world.md)`

### The mines below Keel

Beneath the open pit are old lithium works: shafts, haul tunnels and galleries dug before Keel became a city.

The entrance on the pit rim is:

- sealed with concrete;
- marked by faded hazard striping;
- forbidden to enter.

Beyond the seal, the old works are dark, cold and damp. They contain rusted rails, riveted pipes, analog gauges and manual regulator wheels.

The works are not entirely abandoned. Newer water-control equipment has been installed inside the old systems, out of sight. This allows official readings and actual water flow to differ. `(input/world.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard sits midway up the slope. It contains:

- fabrication towers;
- workshops;
- foundries;
- greenhouse terraces;
- elevated rail;
- cargo cranes;
- patent exchanges.

Halyard is where things are made, and where ownership of what is made is contested or enforced. It is richer and more orderly than Keel, but workers still pass through gates. Its visual palette is warm bronze, gold and engineered green. `(input/world.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis rises above the cloud layer. It contains:

- pearl-white architecture;
- designed gardens;
- wide reservoirs;
- open fountains;
- quiet automated transit;
- a distant spacecraft launch structure.

A spacecraft lifts toward orbit from the launch structure. Oasis has visibly greater access to space, water, room and automated services. Its palette is pearl white, silver, cool blue and controlled green. `(input/world.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars ended a generation before 2043. The material does not specify their exact dates, combatants, causes or political settlement.

Their lasting consequence is a society organized around water control. The postwar systems preserve life while restricting movement and access. `(input/world.md)`

### The lithium extraction era

The old lithium works were dug before Keel became a city. The open-pit mine around which Keel was built is now exhausted, but its structures and underground systems remain part of the city's physical environment.

The mine's infrastructure has been adapted rather than discarded. Old analog systems coexist with newer hidden water-control equipment. `(input/world.md)`

### Postwar infrastructure

After the Water Wars, a single intercity infrastructure spine became essential to the three-city system. It includes:

- a major water conduit;
- power lines;
- a rail line;
- identical white-and-amber control nodes at each level.

The material does not establish which government, company or authority originally built this spine, or whether its ownership changed after the wars. `(input/world.md)`

## Institutions and Civic Systems

### The water system

The water system is the essential civic system connecting the basin. It moves through a huge conduit and distributes water through channels, pipes, pumps, public taps, reservoirs and water-control equipment.

In Keel, residents receive water through metered public taps and narrow channels. In Oasis, water is visibly abundant enough for wide reservoirs and open fountains. The difference indicates unequal allocation or access, though the governing formula is not established. `(input/world.md)`

The water system depends on pumps and control equipment. Some newer controls are hidden inside the old mines, where official readings can diverge from actual flow. `(input/world.md)`

### Digital checkpoints

Digital checkpoints operate in Keel and display what people owe. The material does not specify whether the displayed obligation is a water debt, labor debt, general civic debt or a combination.

The checkpoints are part of ordinary movement through the city, making debt or administrative status visible at the point of passage. `(input/world.md)`

### Travel permissions and gates

Travel up the mountain requires a permit. A permission gate crosses the rail line between Keel and Halyard.

Workers in Halyard also pass gates. Access is therefore tied to administrative status or authorization, not simply to physical proximity or employment. The permit system's issuing authority, criteria and penalties are not established. `(input/world.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Three Charter Houses run the largest towers in Halyard. Their domains are:

- energy;
- water;
- space.

The Houses possess institutional power over the major systems associated with their domains. Halyard is explicitly a place where the question of who owns what gets made is central. The material does not establish the Houses' legal status, internal structure, relationship to one another, or whether they govern the cities directly. `(input/world.md)`

### Patent exchanges

Halyard contains patent exchanges. They are part of the city's manufacturing and ownership economy, where inventions, processes or designs can be claimed, traded or controlled.

The material does not establish who may use a patent, how inventors are compensated, or how patent ownership relates to the Charter Houses. `(input/world.md)`

### Space-launch infrastructure

Oasis has access to a distant launch structure from which a spacecraft lifts toward orbit. The launch structure and the Charter House responsible for space are part of the top city's visible technological and institutional power.

The material does not establish who may travel into orbit, what the spacecraft is for, or how launch access is allocated. `(input/world.md`)

## Governance and Power

Power is organized through control of essential systems:

- water determines survival;
- permits determine movement up the mountain;
- gates enforce access;
- checkpoints expose what residents owe;
- the Charter Houses control major Halyard towers in energy, water and space;
- ownership determines who controls what Halyard makes.

The three cities are connected but not equal. Keel is dependent on repaired infrastructure and metered public access. Halyard controls manufacturing and ownership systems. Oasis displays the highest level of water, space and automation. `(input/world.md)`

The material establishes restriction but not a named central government. It is not yet clear whether the Charter Houses answer to a civic government, replace one, or share authority with it.

### Enforcement and legitimacy

Enforcement is visible through:

- sealed and forbidden mine entrances;
- digital checkpoints;
- permits;
- rail permissions gates;
- worker gates in Halyard;
- control nodes along the infrastructure spine.

The material does not establish the legal penalties for unauthorized travel, tampering with infrastructure or entering the old mines. It also does not establish what public justification is given for the access hierarchy. `(input/world.md)`

## Economics, Labour and Ownership

### Extraction and decline

Keel was built around lithium extraction. The open-pit mine is exhausted, leaving behind old mining structures, underground works and a population living amid infrastructure whose original industry is gone. `(input/world.md)`

### Manufacturing and ownership

Halyard is the production center. Its workshops, foundries, fabrication towers, cargo cranes and patent exchanges make it the place where goods and technologies are produced and where ownership is negotiated or imposed.

The central economic question is not only who performs the work, but who owns what the work produces. `(input/world.md)`

### Labour conditions

Workers in Halyard still pass gates, indicating that employment does not provide unrestricted movement or civic access. Keel's residents pass checkpoints that show what they owe, suggesting that work, access and obligation are connected.

The material does not establish wages, currency, contracts, working hours, unions, unemployment support or the exact relationship between labor and debt. `(input/world.md)`

### Ownership

The three Charter Houses run the largest towers in Halyard, in energy, water and space. Their control makes ownership of infrastructure and production a central source of power.

No ownership structure is established for Keel's homes, pumps, public taps or old mine systems. `(input/world.md)`

### Control nodes

Identical small white-and-amber control nodes appear at every level of the basin's infrastructure spine. Their exact function is not specified, but their repetition marks the continuity of the network and its control architecture. `(input/world.md)`

### Water-control systems

The old mines contain newer water-control equipment installed inside obsolete mine systems. Official readings can differ from real flow, indicating that the system's instrumentation and its physical operation are not necessarily aligned.

The old works retain analog gauges and manual regulator wheels. The system therefore requires human observation and intervention alongside newer controls. `(input/world.md)`

### Maintenance culture

In Keel, machinery is repaired rather than replaced. Patched pumps and old pipes are part of the ordinary environment. Maintenance is a survival practice and a visible sign of the city's resource constraints. `(input/world.md)`

### Transit and access

Rail connects the levels of the basin, but travel upward requires authorization. A permission gate crosses the rail line to Halyard.

Oasis has quiet automated transit, marking a technological and class contrast with Keel's crowded lanes and manually maintained machinery. `(input/world.md)`

## Daily Life and Lived Texture

An ordinary day can involve:

- queuing with a jug at a metered public tap;
- living among narrow water channels and old pipes;
- relying on patched pumps and repaired machinery;
- passing a digital checkpoint that displays what one owes;
- encountering permits and gates when attempting to travel upward.

Keel's systems make scarcity and obligation public. Water is collected in queues, debt or status appears on screens, and movement upward is visibly conditional. `(input/world.md)`

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

The basin is high desert. Water scarcity remains the dominant ecological and political pressure after the Water Wars. The exhausted lithium mine has left a built environment of pits, shafts, haul tunnels, rusted structures and contaminated-looking industrial legacy, though the material does not specify pollution levels or ecological remediation.

Water is unevenly visible across the vertical city:

- Keel queues for metered public taps.
- Halyard supports greenhouse terraces.
- Oasis maintains wide reservoirs and open fountains.

The underground works are cold and damp despite the high-desert setting, and their hidden water-control infrastructure can produce a difference between official readings and actual flow. `(input/world.md)`
