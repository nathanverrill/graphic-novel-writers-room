## Premise

**Canon** — During a public pump failure, seventeen-year-old Keel mechanic Alex Phantum must restore water to a cut-off queue before the supply is redirected elsewhere. With his concealed robot companion Bi11bot, he discovers that the pump is being deliberately diverted through hidden Water House controls in the forbidden old mine. Alex enters the mine, finds Adrian’s maintenance mark, and chooses to reopen the manual regulator—restoring Keel’s water but triggering an unauthorized-system alert.

The chapter’s immediate dramatic question is: **Can Alex restore Keel’s water without exposing himself and Bi11bot?**

The chapter-ending question is: **Who was Adrian trying to reach, and who is now coming for Alex?**

## Structure

### Act I — The pump lies (Pages 1–3)

Establish Keel’s scarcity, Alex’s tactile repair skill, Bi11bot’s concealed companionship, and the public cost of a failing pump. Alex discovers that the displayed allocation is normal while the physical flow is wrong. His choice to trust his hands over the official reading points him toward the sealed mine.

**Act turn:** Alex identifies a hidden control line beneath the pump and decides to follow it into the forbidden works.

### Act II — The buried system (Pages 4–9)

Alex and Bi11bot cross into the old mine. They navigate an older manual water system that has been grafted to newer control hardware. Alex uses Adrian’s watch and its looping equation to recognize the control architecture. The discovery changes the problem from “repair a broken pump” to “someone is redirecting Keel’s water.”

**Midpoint turn:** Adrian’s initials and maintenance mark appear inside the control housing.

**Act II crisis:** The diversion is not a passive fault. The system is actively routing water away from Keel, and the node registers Alex’s intervention.

### Act III — The unauthorized repair (Pages 10–12)

Alex must either leave the system untouched and let Keel lose its water or reopen the manual regulator and expose himself to the network. He chooses the people above him. The flow returns, but the system begins a lockdown or inspection response.

**Climax:** Alex forces the manual regulator open while the white-and-amber node wakes.

**Ending:** Alex and Bi11bot are trapped in the cold control chamber with evidence that Adrian worked here and a response coming toward them.

## Page plot

### Page 1

A crowded Keel water queue gathers around a patched public pump while the digital allocation display reports normal service. The pump coughs, spits mineral-stained water, and stops. Alex pushes through with his tool bag as the queue begins to break apart. Bi11bot is concealed inside the bag.

### Page 2 — Page-turn reveal

Alex lays his scarred palm against the pump housing and reads its vibration. Inside the opened casing, a pressure gauge drops while the official display remains steady. **After the turn:** a thin secondary pipe pulses behind the pump wall, carrying water away from the public line.

### Page 3

Alex follows the hidden pipe through patched conduit beneath the pump station. The queue is told the remaining water will be redirected elsewhere if the fault is not cleared. Alex finds the pipe disappearing toward the sealed mine works. He looks from the forbidden route to the waterless taps and chooses the route.

### Page 4 — Page-turn reveal

Alex uses a maintenance breach beneath the station to reach the concrete-sealed mine entrance. Bi11bot unfolds just enough to help, his amber eyes soft and readable. **After the turn:** the breach opens onto a cold mine tunnel with rusted rails, damp rock, riveted pipes, and a faint active gauge light far below.

### Page 5

Alex and Bi11bot descend through the old works. Alex follows pressure, vibration, and the direction of condensation rather than signage. Bi11bot asks short questions and points out that one pipe is warmer than the others. They reach an old regulator gallery where manual wheels sit beside a newer cable and control line.

### Page 6 — Page-turn reveal

Alex scrapes mineral crust from a newer control assembly grafted onto the manual system. Its white-and-amber node is identical to the nodes visible along the basin infrastructure spine. He compares its repeating marks to the looping equation on Adrian’s watch. **After the turn:** the watch’s equation aligns with the assembly’s calibration pattern.

### Page 7

Alex opens the watch caseback and uses its engraved equation to identify the sequence of ports on the control housing. Bi11bot holds a work light while Alex removes the panel. Beneath it, an old maintenance mark is nearly hidden by later hardware.

### Page 8 — Page-turn reveal

Alex cleans the mark with his thumb. It resolves into Adrian’s initials beside a dated maintenance notation. Alex recognizes the handwriting from the few old papers he kept. **After the turn:** behind the mark is a live control channel routing water away from Keel toward the basin spine.

### Page 9

The control assembly rejects Alex’s attempt to redirect the flow. A white-and-amber indicator flashes; the old gauge climbs toward dangerous pressure. Bi11bot braces the housing while Alex reaches for the seized manual regulator. He realizes the manual override will register as tampering but is the only route back to Keel.

### Page 10 — Page-turn reveal

Alex and Bi11bot force the manual wheel together. The mechanism breaks free with a violent jolt, spraying cold water across the chamber. **After the turn:** a broad surge races through the old pipe toward Keel as the remote node wakes and begins transmitting.

### Page 11

Above ground, water returns to the public taps and the queue surges forward. Below, Alex sees the node’s signal traveling through the control assembly. A lockdown shutter begins closing over the gallery exit. Alex folds Bi11bot down, then changes his mind and carries him openly as they run for the remaining gap. The system has identified the breach.

### Page 12 — Final image

Alex and Bi11bot reach a colder control chamber beyond the closing shutter. The white-and-amber node wakes behind them, illuminating damp rock, rails, and the old manual system. Alex holds the opened watch against the node’s matching equation while a distant mechanical response begins in the tunnels. The chapter ends with Alex realizing Adrian was connected to this system—and that someone is coming.

## Reader vs character knowledge

### Reader knows

- The public pump’s official display reports normal allocation while its physical pressure is failing.
- A concealed route diverts water beneath the pump station.
- The old mine contains active newer equipment.
- The white-and-amber node belongs to a basin-wide control architecture.
- Adrian’s initials are present inside the control housing.
- Alex’s manual override triggers a system response.

### Alex knows

- The pump is behaving incorrectly before he can prove why.
- Adrian’s watch bears the looping equation.
- The maintenance mark resembles Adrian’s hand.
- Keel needs the water immediately.
- Opening the manual regulator is unauthorized and dangerous.

### Bi11bot knows or infers

- Alex is likely to trust physical evidence over official readings.
- The mine is an unsafe place but contains an active route.
- The control assembly is newer than the surrounding mine equipment.
- Alex is emotionally affected by Adrian’s mark, even when Alex does not say so.

### Characters do not know

- Whether Adrian is alive.
- Whether Adrian installed, investigated, or merely passed through the control system.
- Who receives the tamper alert.
- Why Keel’s water is being diverted.
- What the equation fully means.
- Bi11bot’s original purpose or connection to the system.

### Dramatic irony

By Page 12, the reader understands that Alex’s repair has restored Keel’s water but exposed him to a larger system. Alex understands only that Adrian was connected to the system and that an automated response is approaching. The chapter preserves Adrian’s fate and the diversion’s ultimate purpose as open questions.

## Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

- **Proposal:** The pump’s immediate failure is caused by a hidden diversion valve that is functioning within specification, while Keel’s public display is reporting the upstream allocation rather than the water physically reaching the taps.
- **Proposal:** The control assembly’s white-and-amber node can register unauthorized manual intervention and initiate a remote inspection or local lockdown. The identity of the responding authority remains open.
- **Proposal:** Adrian’s mark proves only that he worked on, investigated, or passed through the system. It does not establish whether he was loyal to the Water House, opposed it, or was coerced.
- **Proposal:** The equation on the watch is a calibration or maintenance key that lets Alex recognize the control architecture. It does not yet decode a message or reveal Adrian’s full objective.
- **Proposal:** Alex’s choice to restore Keel’s water is the chapter’s first meaningful movement from isolated repairer to accountable public actor. He does not become formally political or heroic yet.
- **Proposal:** Bi11bot’s assistance establishes practical trust without resolving his manufacture, former ownership, or original function.
- **Decision needed:** Who receives the tamper alert: the Water House directly, a Basin Charter monitoring center, a local inspection crew, or an automated system whose human operator remains unknown?
- **Decision needed:** Does the mine shutter fully trap Alex and Bi11bot, or does it create a narrow route into a deeper section of the old works?
- **Risk:** The chapter could make Adrian’s connection feel too conclusive. The mark should be unmistakable as evidence of presence but ambiguous as to motive and fate.
- **Risk:** The pump mechanics, mine route, control assembly, and watch equation could overload twelve pages. Keep exposition visual and make every technical detail serve Alex’s immediate repair choice.
