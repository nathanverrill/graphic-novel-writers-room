# Prosperity Lean — Development Brief

## 1. Logline

When a failing Keel water pump reveals that the city’s official numbers are false, seventeen-year-old mechanic Alex Phantum must enter the forbidden mine with the concealed robot Bi11bot to restore the flow—and discovers the first evidence that his missing father may have been trying to expose the system.

## 2. Theme

A society that calls exploitation “stability” can be challenged by people who learn to trust one another—and to trust what their own hands can feel.

## 3. Tone & Comps

- **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — ecological pressure, engineered systems, and a young protagonist whose practical compassion becomes political.
- **The City of Ember** — enclosed civic infrastructure, failing public systems, and discovery through maintenance rather than abstract heroics.
- **Children of Men** — stratified social control, restricted movement, and hope carried through a dangerous human connection.

The book is tense, tactile, and hopeful without becoming cheerful. Its politics are shown through queues, gates, meters, repairs, and unequal access rather than speeches.

## 4. Audience & Rating

Young adult and adult crossover. **PG-13 equivalent:** peril, authoritarian control, industrial danger, family abandonment, implied historical violence, and restrained emotional intensity. No graphic gore, sexual content, or sadistic violence.

## 5. Format

- **Chapter 1:** exactly 12 pages, Pages 1–12.
- Single graphic-novel chapter, full color.
- Default trim: 6.625" × 10.25".
- Usually 4–6 panels per page, with one or two larger images for the mine-entry reveal and closing image.
- Page 12 ends the chapter; it does not add a bonus page or epilogue.

## 6. Visual Direction

Tactile, production-minded science fiction: weathered metal, repaired mechanisms, mineral dust, water glare, and readable human faces. Keel is compressed and crowded, built from rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, faded industrial blue, and hard white water light. The old mine is colder, darker, and more spatially open, with damp rock, rusted rails, analog gauges, riveted pipes, and isolated amber instrument light.

Use visual contrast as class storytelling:

- **Keel:** patched, crowded, noisy, low and horizontal.
- **Halyard:** bronze, gold, engineered green, vertical fabrication towers and controlled movement.
- **Oasis:** pearl white, silver, cool blue, open space and quiet automation.

The story remains physically legible. Establish the pump, the sealed mine entrance, and the route into the old works before compressing action. Do not use murky shadow to create artificial mystery.

Alex is always recognizable by his brown skin, short dense black curls, dark work clothes, rust-brown patched cargo trousers, scuffed boots, canvas tool bag, scarred hands, and Adrian’s oversized steel watch on his left wrist. Bi11bot remains companionable and readable: no red eyes, menacing posture, looming low angles, or face-hidden shadow.

## 7. Canon

The following are approved project truths. All fixed material in `characters.md` and `world.md` remains canon; this section adds the Director’s decisions.

### Story canon

- Chapter 1 begins and ends in Keel.
- Alex works as a maintenance mechanic for Keel’s public water pumps. He is not formally powerful or licensed to alter the larger system.
- The chapter’s immediate problem is a public pump failure during a water queue. Alex must restore it before the queue is cut off and the remaining water is redirected elsewhere.
- Bi11bot is already Alex’s hidden companion at the start of the chapter. Alex found him in discarded mine equipment several months earlier and repaired enough of him to make him mobile. Their trust is practical and still developing.
- Alex conceals Bi11bot because an inspection would classify him as unregistered scrap and remove him.
- Alex’s repair reveals that the pump is not merely failing: a newer control module is diverting water away from Keel while the public display reports normal allocation.
- Alex and Bi11bot enter the forbidden old works through a maintenance breach beneath the pump station. They do not reach Halyard.
- Inside, Alex finds a newer control assembly grafted onto the old manual system. Its white-and-amber node matches the nodes on the basin-wide infrastructure spine.
- Adrian’s looping equation is a calibration key or maintenance notation connected to this control architecture. Chapter 1 reveals the connection, not its full meaning.
- Alex finds Adrian’s initials and an old maintenance mark inside the control housing. This is the first present-tense evidence that Adrian worked on, investigated, or passed through the system after Keel believed he had “thrown everything away.”
- Alex chooses to reopen the manual regulator and restore water to Keel, knowing the intervention will register as unauthorized tampering.
- The restored flow is temporary. The system identifies the breach, and a remote signal begins a lockdown or inspection response.
- The final image is Alex and Bi11bot in the mine’s cold control chamber as the white-and-amber node wakes behind them; Alex holds the opened watch against the matching equation. The chapter question is: **Who was Adrian trying to reach, and who is now coming for Alex?**

### World canon

- The basin’s infrastructure is administered by a **central postwar authority called the Basin Charter**, whose operating power is delegated to the three Charter Houses. The Charter Houses are not identical to the civic authority, but the water, energy, and space Houses control essential systems through contracts and enforcement.
- The **Water House** controls allocation, pump telemetry, permits, and the water-control equipment in the old works. The Energy and Space Houses remain background institutions in Chapter 1.
- Keel’s digital checkpoint obligation is a combined **water-and-labor account**: households accrue water allocation and maintenance charges, while registered workers receive credits for assigned labor. The display shows the net amount owed, not a neutral civic identity.
- Travel permits are issued by the Basin Charter through local administrative offices. A Keel resident may apply through employment, emergency service, or sponsorship; approval is discretionary. Unauthorized upward travel risks detention, debt escalation, and loss of work access. These penalties remain background, not an on-page arrest in Chapter 1.
- The repeated white-and-amber control nodes are network relays. They authenticate readings, route control commands, and report tampering across the infrastructure spine.
- Keel’s public pumps and taps are Basin Charter property operated by local maintenance crews. The old mine’s concealed water-control equipment belongs to the Water House, though Keel workers are not meant to know it exists.
- The Water Wars remain intentionally undefined in their combatants and exact settlement. The book treats them as the origin of the control regime, not as a history lesson.
- The spacecraft and launch structure in Oasis remain outside Chapter 1 and undecided.

## 8. What We Take From the Material

- **Alex Phantum:** take all fixed appearance, history, mechanical ability, watch, photograph, grandmother, mother, resentment, and Keel pressures from `characters.md`. Develop his arc from isolated repairer to someone who makes a dangerous repair for other people.
- **Bi11bot:** take the fixed appearance, folding mechanism, voice, patience, questions, and non-menacing visual rules from `characters.md`. Use him as Alex’s existing but concealed companion; set aside any need to explain his manufacture in Chapter 1.
- **Adrian:** take the disappearance, Keel’s accusation, Alex’s uncertainty, watch, and photograph. Use the watch actively; do not resolve Adrian’s fate.
- **Grandmother and mother:** retain as emotional context. They do not require a full scene in this 12-page chapter.
- **Keel and the old mines:** use the public pump, queue, checkpoint pressure, sealed entrance, hidden equipment, analog controls, and forbidden descent from `world.md`.
- **Halyard and Oasis:** retain as the larger vertical system and visual promise, but do not visit them in Chapter 1.
- **Water-control discrepancy:** use it as the chapter’s central discovery and causal engine.
- **Charter Houses and governance:** use the Water House and Basin Charter as the settled institutional frame; keep their broader politics offstage.
- **Idea-draft status:** no supplied plot beats are being copied as a draft. The book keeps the material’s strongest connected elements—repair, scarcity, hidden controls, Adrian’s watch, and Bi11bot—and forms them into one causally linked opening chapter.

## 9. Proposals Under Consideration

- Adrian may still be alive, dead, imprisoned, or operating under another identity.
- Bi11bot may have been built for mine maintenance, Water House inspection, or a function unrelated to the control system.
- The looping equation may be a calibration sequence, a map, a synchronization key, or a message disguised as maintenance notation.
- The concealed water diversion may be corruption, emergency triage, a planned system failure, or part of a larger attempt to force Keel’s relocation.
- Alex may eventually travel to Halyard and Oasis, but the route, allies, and cost remain open.
- The grandmother may retain fragments of useful knowledge about Adrian, but her memory loss must not become a convenient exposition device.

## 10. Non-Negotiables

- Chapter 1 is exactly 12 pages.
- Alex and Bi11bot are the principal defined characters.
- Alex wears Adrian’s steel watch on his left wrist and has no other wrist or hand accessory by default.
- Bi11bot is never menacing and never uses red or harsh eye light.
- Keel remains materially poor, water-scarce, repaired, crowded, and visibly controlled.
- The three-city vertical hierarchy, shared infrastructure spine, permits, public taps, digital obligations, old mine, and white-and-amber nodes remain coherent.
- The chapter must show action through machinery, bodies, space, and consequence rather than explain the world in speeches.
- Adrian’s ultimate fate is not resolved in Chapter 1.
- The chapter ends with a changed question and a credible reason to continue.

## 12. Open Questions

- Is Adrian alive, and what was he attempting when he used the water-control system?
- What is Bi11bot’s original function, and why was he discarded?
- Who receives the control system’s tamper alert?
- Is the Water House diverting water for profit, emergency allocation, or a larger relocation plan?
- How advanced is the grandmother’s memory loss, and what does Alex owe her beyond daily care?
- What will Alex and Bi11bot have to sacrifice to reach Halyard?
- Does the Basin Charter remain the visible antagonist, or will the true conflict divide the Charter Houses from one another?
