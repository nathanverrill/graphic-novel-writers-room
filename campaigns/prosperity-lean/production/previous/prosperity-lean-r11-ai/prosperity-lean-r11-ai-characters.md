# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is eighteen, with brown skin, short dense black curls, dark eyes, a lean build, scarred work-rough hands, a dark rolled-sleeve work shirt over a black tee, rust-brown patched cargo trousers, heavy scuffed boots and a worn canvas backpack hanging from one straight shoulder strap. His silhouette is narrow, practical and slightly forward-leaning, with his hands often held ready to test machinery. He wears his Keel work card clipped to his belt and carries a creased photograph of Adrian and his grandmother in his jacket pocket. Before the workshop discovery he has no watch; afterward Adrian’s worn steel round watch on a faded olive canvas strap remains on his left wrist. The watch is slightly oversized, has a full glass display face with no hands, and bears a looping equation engraved on the caseback only. Alex’s signature detail is the habit of placing one scarred palm flat against a machine housing to read its vibration.

**Voice** — Alex speaks plainly and dryly. Under pressure he uses short deflections, understatement and occasional sarcasm rather than explaining his feelings. Example lines: “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” “Then we start here.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Eighteen-year-old Keel pump worker and repairer.
- **Immediate want:** To leave Keel and reach a future he can choose.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He must replace inherited reputation with tested evidence, but he distrusts inherited systems while still wanting a simple escape from them.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reads machinery by touch, repairs failing equipment with salvaged parts and notices physical evidence others overlook.
- **Vulnerability:** His work card, wages, debt and travel status are controlled by Alpha; he has no direct memory of Adrian and is emotionally exposed by his grandmother’s memory loss.
- **Mistaken belief:** Leaving Keel is the clearest form of freedom, and Adrian’s public reputation may be the only truth available.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly risk his work card or future mobility for a story about Adrian.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He chooses the sealed mine as his first destination because it offers evidence, even though entering it could cost him his work card.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada challenges his private escape fantasy; Adrian’s absence and Bi11bot’s warning force him to question both the public story and the inheritance.
- **Decision chain:** Alex repairs the pump by feel → the repair is flagged, but the supervisor records it as routine maintenance → Alex’s debt transfers at eighteen → he pockets Danny’s loan slip → he follows his grandmother to the blue book → the workshop, watch and Bi11bot become evidence → he hears Adrian’s warning → he presses the Measure marker and begins the investigation.

**Arc in this book** — Alex begins wanting only to leave Keel and believing that inherited debt and Adrian’s reputation have already determined his future. He ends by treating the public account as a theory rather than a fact and choosing evidence over safety: **“Then we start here.”** He does not forgive Adrian, accept Adrian’s account, or enter the mine yet. His proof of change is the deliberate acceptance of a risk he previously would have avoided.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a companion-sized but substantial first-generation Alpha field unit, approximately 75 cm standing, with the top of his head level with Alex’s belt, and never menacing. His faded industrial-blue segmented head has a mustard-yellow stripe, two large amber eyes in heavy metal rings, round ear assemblies and a chest plate marked **Bi11** in white. He has slim jointed repair arms with exposed pistons and cables, long precise fingers and tool tips, compact legs with broad feet, rounded shoulder plates, chipped working surfaces, rust, rivets and scratches. An old rust-red scarf remains at his neck. Wrenches, pliers, a coil of wire, pipe fittings and a small gauge clip to his back, hips and shoulder plates. His right eye ring clicks and rotates when he focuses. When folded, his limbs telescope inside his head shell, the panels close like an iris and the tools wrap the outside, forming a 25 cm round bundle of scrap metal and tools. His signature detail is the patient head tilt paired with a softly narrowed amber eye.

**Voice** — Bi11bot speaks in short, plain sentences. He asks more than he explains, never lectures, and sounds patient, dry and quietly amused. Example lines: “Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes.” “That's a theory. The mine is evidence.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Lost B-11, a first-generation Alpha field unit and Adrian’s repair companion.
- **Immediate want:** To guide Alex to the beginning of the Legacy Protocol and keep the trail moving.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants to preserve Adrian’s work without turning Adrian into an authority Alex must obey.
- **Leverage / capability:** He carries the only map to the three artifacts, understands repair systems and can project Adrian’s message and the Legacy Protocol map.
- **Vulnerability:** His awakened, unfolded body checks in with Alpha and can be traced; his knowledge of Adrian is partial and fallible.
- **Mistaken belief:** He may initially assume that providing the correct evidence and route is enough to prepare Alex for the consequences.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not present Adrian as flawless or tell Alex to obey without question.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He directs Alex toward the sealed mine while reducing the danger to a dry logistical problem: “Then we don't get caught.”
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s skepticism forces Bi11bot to distinguish guidance from control.
- **Decision chain:** Alex wears the watch → Bi11bot awakens → he identifies Adrian’s mistakes rather than defending him → he projects the message → he reveals the Legacy Protocol and the Measure → his check-in exposes the investigation to Alpha.

**Arc in this book** — Bi11bot begins as dormant equipment and becomes Alex’s guide and witness. He moves the inheritance from a hidden room into an active investigation while preserving uncertainty about Adrian. His proof of function is that he gives Alex a destination without taking away Alex’s choice.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is seen in Chapter 1 through a creased photograph and a holographic recording. He has the same visible face in both: a narrow, expressive face, steady tired eyes and a restrained half-smile that can become an apology without becoming self-pity. His hologram is waist-up, rendered in soft blue light that shifts toward burgundy when he speaks seriously. He wears practical inventor’s work clothes, visibly lived-in rather than polished. His signature detail is the directness of his gaze into the recording lens, as if addressing Alex across the years.

**Voice** — Adrian begins with a small joke, then speaks carefully and directly. He admits failure without asking Alex to absolve him or finish his work. Example lines: “If you're watching this, I wasn't there when you needed me. I'm sorry.” “Whatever he tells you... question everything. Especially me.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Missing father, failed borrower and cautionary figure in Keel’s public story.
- **Immediate want:** To give Alex access to the hidden work while leaving the final choice to him.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants his work to outlast him, but knows his own failures make his legacy unsafe to trust.
- **Leverage / capability:** He built the workshop, created or maintained Bi11bot, designed the COST function and established the three-artifact trail.
- **Vulnerability:** He abandoned Alex when Alex was two; his absence has allowed others to define him.
- **Mistaken belief:** He may have believed that a carefully prepared inheritance could compensate for the damage caused by leaving.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not command Alex to complete the Legacy Protocol unchanged.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He leaves Alex both a map and an explicit instruction to question the map’s keeper, himself and every inherited account.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s absence from Adrian’s life makes every apology consequential but insufficient.
- **Decision chain:** Adrian conceals the workshop → entrusts the mechanism to Grandmother → leaves the watch, Bi11bot and message for Alex’s eighteenth birthday → apologizes and warns Alex → remains absent and unresolved.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian shifts from public cautionary legend to contradictory private evidence: a flawed inventor who prepared a deliberate inheritance and admits he was absent. He does not receive a present-time resolution. His proof of refusal to simplify himself is the warning: **“Especially me.”**

### ALEX’S GRANDMOTHER

**Visual lock** — Alex’s grandmother is an elderly Keel woman with a frail hand, a small, careful posture and a face that alternates between fogged confusion and startlingly clear attention. She sits in a worn chair in the modest apartment, wearing layered, repaired domestic clothing in muted rust, brown and faded blue. Her hands repeatedly fold and refold a small cloth. Her silhouette is slight against the tall bookshelf that conceals Adrian’s workshop. Her signature detail is the sudden steadiness of her gaze when she recognizes that Alex has turned eighteen.

**Voice** — Her speech can drift, repeat or confuse Alex with Adrian, but becomes deliberate when she reaches Adrian’s instruction. Example lines: “Eighteen. Oh. Then there's something your father wanted you to have.” “Especially the things worth protecting.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex’s grandmother and guardian in Keel.
- **Immediate want:** To preserve the household and deliver Adrian’s inheritance at the correct moment.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** Her memory is failing, but she has protected one precise responsibility for years.
- **Leverage / capability:** She controls access to the blue book, the hidden mechanism and the workshop.
- **Vulnerability:** Memory loss threatens her ability to explain what she knows or protect the secret indefinitely.
- **Mistaken belief:** She may believe that preserving Adrian’s instructions is enough, even though Alex needs answers she cannot provide.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not reveal the workshop before Alex is eighteen.
- **Decision only she would make:** In a clear moment, she sends Alex for the blue book rather than explain the secret herself.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s gentleness allows her to act without shame, while Adrian’s old instruction gives her a final duty.
- **Decision chain:** She mistakes Alex for Adrian → Alex tells her he is eighteen → she recognizes the handoff moment → she directs him to the blue book → she watches him cross into the workshop → Adrian’s message makes her stop folding and listen.

**Arc in this book** — She moves from apparent confusion to a deliberate act of guardianship, releasing Adrian’s inheritance without pretending she can explain all of it. Her proof of change is the handoff itself: she chooses Alex’s present over the comfort of keeping the secret.

### ADA

**Visual lock** — Ada is twenty, with long wind-tangled copper hair, freckles and teal-blue eyes. She wears a dark high-collared top with a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder and a muted moss-green wrap at her neck. Her stance is steady and rooted, contrasting with Alex’s forward-leaning restlessness. She carries a ration card and two battered metal mugs when she meets Alex at the pit rim. Her signature detail is the way she looks down toward Keel while Alex looks up toward Oasis.

**Voice** — Ada speaks directly and steadily, teasing Alex before allowing concern to surface. She is comfortable letting silence sit after a difficult question. Example lines: “Not everything has a price, Alex.” “So everything else is what people told you. What if they were wrong?”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex’s close companion in Keel.
- **Immediate want:** To care for Alex and challenge his resignation on his birthday.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants Keel changed rather than abandoned, but has no immediate power to make that happen.
- **Leverage / capability:** She notices Alex’s emotional evasions, gives up part of her ration and asks questions he cannot dismiss as machinery.
- **Vulnerability:** Her hope is materially costly; every saved ration is something she goes without.
- **Mistaken belief:** She may be too ready to believe that Adrian’s disappearance has a hidden explanation because she needs the world to contain alternatives.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not let scarcity turn every act of care into a transaction.
- **Decision only she would make:** She spends part of her ration on Alex and refuses to treat the gift as a debt.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s desire to leave forces her to defend the possibility of changing Keel from within.
- **Decision chain:** She saves water → brings Alex two mugs → challenges his view of Adrian → states that Adrian would not simply leave → sends Alex home carrying a question rather than an answer.

**Arc in this book** — Ada begins as Alex’s material and emotional anchor. She ends as the first person to make his inherited story unstable, arguing that Adrian may not have simply abandoned them. Her proof of change is that she converts care into a challenge, not reassurance.

### DANNY

**Visual lock** — Danny is about twenty, lanky and narrow-shouldered, with a buzz cut, an open grin and the restless energy of someone already moving toward his next purchase. He wears worn Keel pump-hall work clothes and carries a used console under one arm with a game-shop bag in his hand. His work card and visible debt balance belong to the same public administrative system as Alex’s. His signature detail is the way he waves the loan slip as if the weekly payment were the whole object.

**Voice** — Danny is enthusiastic, casual and focused on the immediate benefit of a choice. Example lines: “Alex! Look what I got!” “Ten a week. That's nothing!”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Keel pump-hall worker and Alex’s coworker.
- **Immediate want:** To own the used console now.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants relief and pleasure in the present while treating the future as too distant to weigh.
- **Leverage / capability:** He normalizes borrowing by making the weekly payment sound ordinary.
- **Vulnerability:** His existing debt and low wages make the total repayment more dangerous than he admits.
- **Mistaken belief:** The meaningful cost is the ten-credit weekly payment, not the 310-credit total.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not give up a present want merely because the future cost is larger.
- **Decision only Danny would make:** He celebrates a 120-credit advance as “nothing” while carrying a 310-credit obligation.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s later judgment is complicated by Bi11bot’s explanation of why tomorrow can make today look bigger.
- **Decision chain:** Danny wants the console → he takes the payday loan → he shows Alex the slip → the slip falls → Alex pockets it → the watch later reveals its total cost.

**Arc in this book** — Danny does not receive a visible change in Chapter 1. He functions as the first human example of the difference between posted price and real cost. His refusal to see the total cost is the pressure that makes Alex’s later COST reading meaningful.

### PUMP-HALL SUPERVISOR

**Visual lock** — The pump-hall supervisor is a Keel maintenance worker in a weathered dark work uniform, with a compact, sturdy build, close-cropped hair and a face trained into skeptical concentration. Their forearms and boots show the same dust and oil as the pump hall. They stand with arms folded while Alex works, then lean close to the terminal when choosing how to classify the repair. Their signature detail is the pause between looking at the red unauthorized-repair flag and selecting the green routine-maintenance option.

**Voice** — The supervisor uses clipped procedural language and rarely wastes words. Example lines: “That's not the approved repair.” “...Works.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Alex’s supervisor at the Keel pump hall.
- **Immediate want:** To keep the pump running without attracting an administrative problem.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** They want procedural safety but depend on workers willing to improvise when approved parts fail.
- **Leverage / capability:** They can flag Alex’s repair or reclassify it as routine maintenance.
- **Vulnerability:** A failed pump or visible unauthorized work could threaten their own standing.
- **Mistaken belief:** They may believe that quiet classification can keep the system stable indefinitely.
- **Boundary at the start:** They will not openly endorse Alex’s unauthorized method.
- **Decision only they would make:** They record the working repair as routine maintenance instead of punishing Alex.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s competence forces the supervisor to choose between Alpha’s procedure and Keel’s functioning machinery.
- **Decision chain:** They challenge Alex → the pump keeps working → Alpha flags the repair → they select routine maintenance → the balanced water-drop mark remains visible beneath the pump.

**Arc in this book** — The supervisor begins as procedural resistance and ends by making a small, quiet exception. The proof is the green classification: they protect a working repair without declaring that the system is wrong.

### THE LAST CHANCE SHOPKEEPER

**Visual lock** — The Last Chance shopkeeper is a Keel lender and pawn broker in practical, slightly over-neat clothing chosen to remain presentable amid dust and rust. They stand in the doorway of **THE LAST CHANCE — PAWN & LOANS**, framed by used goods, loan forms and the glowing promise **CASH TODAY · EASY TERMS**. Their face is sharp, their smile is thin and appraising, and they recognize Alex immediately as Adrian’s son. Their signature detail is the gesture of tipping their chin toward Adrian’s torn default notice while speaking as if offering helpful advice.

**Voice** — The shopkeeper uses teasing, predatory promotional language and turns public shame into sales pressure. Example lines: “Care to see if your luck runs better than his?” “Your father always believed the next loan would change everything.”

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Pawn shopkeeper and payday lender in Keel.
- **Immediate want:** To keep customers borrowing and to make Adrian’s reputation commercially useful.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** They present loans as easy while depending on borrowers not examining the total cost.
- **Leverage / capability:** They control immediate access to cash and can turn a family’s public failure into a warning.
- **Vulnerability:** Their business depends on the appearance that the terms are ordinary and manageable.
- **Mistaken belief:** They believe the public story about Adrian is more useful than any hidden truth.
- **Boundary at the start:** They will not offer Alex sympathy when shame can sell.
- **Decision only they would make:** They invoke Adrian’s failure while Danny is still carrying a new loan.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s later COST reading exposes the gap between the shopkeeper’s language and the obligation beneath it.
- **Decision chain:** Danny borrows → the shopkeeper displays the weekly terms → they point to Adrian’s notice → Alex pockets the slip → Adrian’s watch later reveals the 310-credit total.

**Arc in this book** — The shopkeeper remains a stable face of Keel’s immediate-price economy. Their proof of function is the contrast between **EASY TERMS** and the total obligation the watch reveals.

### OASIS CONTROL-ROOM WATCHER

**Visual lock** — The Oasis control-room watcher is an unnamed woman seated alone in a minimalist pearl-white and graphite control room, seen first from behind and kept visually small against a wall-sized live display of the mountain. Her build and face remain indistinct in Chapter 1. She wears precise, quiet administrative clothing with no visible insignia. A glass of clean water sits on the desk beside her monitor. Her signature detail is the exacting alignment of that glass with the desk edge and the monitor frame.

**Voice** — She has no spoken dialogue in Chapter 1. Her decisions are communicated through controlled hand movements, the selection of **TRACK** and her forward lean toward the intermittent signal. If dialogue is later required, it should be concise, neutral and operational.

**Causality**
- **Public role:** Unnamed Oasis monitor of Alpha network alerts.
- **Immediate want:** To identify and acquire the returning B-11 signal.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She values control and exact information, but the signal is intermittent and belongs to a legacy device outside her immediate command.
- **Leverage / capability:** She can select **TRACK** and direct Alpha’s monitoring toward Keel.
- **Vulnerability:** She does not yet have a stable signal or a confirmed view of Alex.
- **Mistaken belief:** Her choice to track may assume that a legacy device can be contained once identified.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not leave a significant alert unresolved.
- **Decision only she would make:** She selects **TRACK** rather than **REPORT**, turning a detection into an active pursuit.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Her relationship to Alpha, the Charter Houses and the emergency authority is unresolved; the signal itself becomes the pressure.
- **Decision chain:** B-11 checks in → she reads the legacy-device alert → she chooses TRACK → the signal remains intermittent → she leans toward the display as the ring begins acquiring it.

**Arc in this book** — The watcher begins as an unseen institutional observer and ends as the first active pursuer of Alex’s investigation. Her proof of change is the transition from passive monitoring to deliberate tracking.

## Ensemble Test

- **Alex** notices vibration, improvised mechanical solutions and physical evidence. He values competence, autonomy and proof. He can repair, test and choose under material risk. He fears permanent confinement and the possibility that Adrian truly abandoned him.
- **Ada** notices emotional resignation and social patterns. She values care, shared survival and changing Keel from within. She can challenge Alex without withdrawing affection. She fears that escape will leave Keel unchanged.
- **Bi11bot** notices mechanisms, timing and incomplete explanations. He values useful truth over loyalty to reputation. He can interpret devices, project records and guide Alex. He fears neither physical danger nor embarrassment, but his traceability makes his presence dangerous.
- **Adrian** notices systems, long-term consequences and the cost hidden behind immediate choices. He values agency but has failed to protect the people closest to him. He can leave tools and evidence across time. His vulnerability is the damage caused by his absence.
- **Grandmother** notices family continuity and the right moment for a promise. She values protection and remembrance. She can preserve a secret despite memory loss. Her vulnerability is the fading reliability of her own memory.
- **Danny** notices the immediate pleasure of a purchase. He values relief and present access. He can normalize debt through casual confidence. He fears the deprivation that makes tomorrow feel less real than today.
- **The supervisor** notices whether machinery is actually functioning. They value procedural safety but need practical repairs. They can alter the administrative record. They fear being held responsible for either failure or deviation.
- **The shopkeeper** notices sales opportunities and public shame. They value immediate transactions and repeat debt. They can convert reputation into pressure. They fear losing the appearance that their terms are easy.
- **The watcher** notices network anomalies and alignment. She values control, precision and acquisition of information. She can turn an alert into a pursuit. Her authority and motive remain unknown.

Together, the principals do not make the same dramatic choice: Alex risks status for evidence; Ada spends resources for care; Bi11bot preserves uncertainty while guiding; Adrian delegates choice; Grandmother protects timing; Danny chooses the present; the supervisor quietly bends procedure; the shopkeeper monetizes pressure; and the watcher converts information into control.
