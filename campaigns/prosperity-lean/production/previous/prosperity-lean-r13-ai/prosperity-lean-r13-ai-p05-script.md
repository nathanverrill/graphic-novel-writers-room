## Page 5 (right) — Grandmother recognizes Alex’s eighteenth birthday and releases the blue book

**Panel 1.** Wide interior of the modest Keel apartment at dusk. Handmade quilts, repaired furniture, shelves of old books, faded family photographs, potted plants in recycled containers and two water jugs by the door with ration marks. Grandmother sits in a worn chair at center, folding and refolding a small cloth. A tall bookshelf dominates the far wall; one worn blue book lies alone on the top shelf. Alex stands in the doorway, backpack on one straight shoulder.

GRANDMOTHER: Adrian?

ALEX: No, Grandma. I'm Alex.

**Panel 2.** Medium. Alex kneels beside Grandmother at eye level. Her hands continue folding the cloth. His smile is gentle and tired.

GRANDMOTHER: Of course. Alex. I knew that.

ALEX: And today's my birthday.

GRANDMOTHER: Really? How old?

ALEX: Eighteen.

**Panel 3.** Tight on Grandmother. The fog clears from her face; her gaze becomes startlingly steady and deliberate. Behind her, Alex still smiles, unaware.

GRANDMOTHER: Eighteen. Oh. Then there's something your father wanted you to have.

**Panel 4.** Medium-to-tall composition. Grandmother points toward the top shelf. The worn blue book bears a faint balanced water-drop mark on its spine. Alex follows her finger, puzzled, then reaches up on his toes as the warm room light falls behind him.

GRANDMOTHER: Get me the blue book, would you? Top shelf.

GRANDMOTHER: Your father never let anyone else touch it.

ALEX: He had opinions about everything.

GRANDMOTHER: Especially the things worth protecting.

PAGE CHECK: Grandmother’s confusion gives way to deliberate guardianship; the blue book is visibly isolated and marked before it becomes the mechanism.
