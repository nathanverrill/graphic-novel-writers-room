# Cold Read: Issue 01

I read it once, straight through. I didn't want to read it and I went in looking for reasons to hate it.

## Issue 01

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 2.** The debt screen got me. 418,607 credits handed to him on his birthday, with "28.7 YEARS" under it. That's the first time I stopped looking for my phone. "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "I've had better surprises." That line is good.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The supervisor clicking ROUTINE MAINTENANCE and muttering "...Works." Small and nice. I liked him for a second.
- **Page 5, panel 3 to page 6.** Grandma's face clearing when he says "Eighteen" is where the book actually had me. The bookshelf swinging open is an old trick, but I turned the page fast.
- **Page 8, panel 4.** "...Well. That took longer than expected." The robot is funny right away. I'd read more because of him.
- **Page 10.** "Which means your grandmother still refuses to throw anything away." / "I heard that." That made me laugh, and it was the warmest moment in the issue.
- **Page 11, panel 3.** The watch turning 120 into 310 is the first time I understood what the gadget actually does. Concrete, and I got it.
- **Page 12, panel 4.** The woman lining up the water glass is creepy in a good way.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1.** Three questions in captions, all at once, all over a big picture. They read like a movie poster. I skimmed them and couldn't tell you which question went with which city.
- **Pages 3–4.** The whole rim conversation. Two pages of looking up and looking down, "Ada lets the silence sit" twice, and "Because nobody leaves" / "People stopped trying to make it worth staying." It sounds like a speech about the theme. I skimmed most of page 4. The grandma-forgets material is repeated on page 5 anyway.
- **Page 7.** No words, a lot of stuff in the room. It's fine, but the "same equation in two places" beat takes three panels, and I got it in one.
- **Page 10.** The middle of the hologram message ("every failure taught me something", "you may find a better way") sounds like a graduation card. I skimmed until "question everything."

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 2.** Why does Danny's balance get huge, and why does everyone look up? I thought it meant something. Then on page 2, panel 3 he's taken a loan, so maybe the loan is the balance? But you see it at the checkpoint, before the shop. Confused and detached.
- **Page 2, panel 3.** I couldn't tell if Danny's loan came from the pawn shop or the game shop. Two shop signs and two kinds of business in one panel.
- **Page 4, last line.** "He wouldn't just leave." Does Ada know Adrian? She's twenty, so she was four. This is confused but intrigued, and I want to know.
- **Page 9, panel 3.** He puts the watch on, then takes it straight back off so the robot can look at the back. I lost track of where the watch was.
- **Page 11.** "Anyone caught past that seal loses their work card." / "Then we don't get caught." So they're going? Tonight? I didn't know when or how.
- **Page 11, panel 4.** Alex touches the map and that sends a signal? Or did the robot waking up send it (page 8)? I couldn't tell what tipped off the woman.
- **Page 12, caption.** "I told myself I was only checking." Whose voice is that? The woman's? It's the first time anyone has narrated in first person. Intrigued, but I wasn't sure.

### Lines I read twice
- "Eighteen: the age Keel decides you're old enough to owe." Fine, but I read it twice to get the joke.
- "Everyone in Keel knew where they had been born. / Few believed they could choose where they belonged."
- "That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying."
- "When tomorrow looks bad, today looks bigger." **Read it three times.** I still only half get it.
- "That's a theory. The mine is evidence." Evidence of what?
- "Most of the true things in my life started that way."
- "Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes." Cool line, but I read it twice.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Grandma, Bi11bot, Adrian: all clear.
- Ada: I could tell her apart, but I don't know who she is to Alex. Friend? Neighbour? Crush? She mostly says the theme out loud. She seems to know something, and that's the most interesting thing about her.
- Danny: clear, but he only exists to be the dumb loan guy.
- The supervisor, the guard and the shopkeeper blur together into "adults in Keel." The shopkeeper's line about his father is the only reason I remember him.
- The Oasis woman: clear as a mystery.

### I felt something
- The debt number on his birthday. That's unfair, and I felt it.
- Ada saving her water for him. A small thing, and it worked.
- "I heard that." Warm.
- Alex unfolding the photo and the face matching the hologram (page 10, bottom). A small ache.

### I predicted
- When the grandma said the father "was working," I guessed he wasn't a deadbeat. Correct. The book wants that to be a twist, and I saw it coming from page 2.
- Ada knows more about Adrian than she's saying.
- Adrian is alive, probably in Oasis or connected to the woman.
- The woman chose TRACK over REPORT, so she's secretly on his side, or she will be. I'd bet she's the narrator of the whole book.
- Danny's loan is going to ruin him later.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid who inherits his missing dad's debt finds out his dad was secretly building a way out, and has to follow a trail up through the three cities while someone at the top watches.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 4.** After the debt screen grabbed me, two pages of rim talk lost me. If I were flipping in a shop, I'd have put it back before the bookshelf. Page 5, panel 3 is what saves it, and I'd only get there if someone made me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mostly yes. The woman pressing TRACK and lining up her glass is a real hook, and the caption makes her a person, not just a villain. But Alex isn't in danger yet on the page, the mine trip hasn't started, and I still don't know what the Measure is. So it's "I'd read it this week," not "I'd read it tonight." The thing I actually want to see is the robot in the mine.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 4; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 4. Two kids at a railing saying the theme to each other, right after the best page in the issue.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A broke kid gets his deadbeat dad's debt on his 18th birthday, then finds a secret lab behind the bookshelf with a sarcastic robot, and somebody rich is spying on him."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: Starts strong with the debt, sags on the railing, and is saved by Grandma, the robot and the creepy water glass.
