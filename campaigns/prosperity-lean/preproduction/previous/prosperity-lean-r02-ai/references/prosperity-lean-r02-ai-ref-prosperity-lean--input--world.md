# The world of Prosperity

The year is 2043. Three cities are stacked up one mountain basin in the high desert, joined by one
spine of infrastructure: a huge water conduit, power lines, a rail line and identical small
white-and-amber control nodes at every level. Water is the pressure on everything. The Water Wars
ended a generation ago; what came after keeps people alive and keeps them in place.

## Alpha

After the Water Wars, an emergency council of the three cities built Alpha, an AI to run water,
food, power, work and travel. It was meant to be temporary and it stayed. The small white-and-amber
nodes at every level are Alpha's. Every machine Alpha has ever registered checks in with it when it
comes online, like a company laptop reconnecting to its office network, so a device that has been
dark for years shows up the moment it is switched back on. A watcher in Oasis sees those check-ins.

## Keel, the bottom

Keel is built in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Stacked housing, rusted mining
structures, patched pumps, crowded lanes, dust. Water runs in narrow channels and old pipes, and
people queue with jugs at metered public taps. Workers pass digital checkpoints that show what they
owe. Travel up the mountain needs a permit, and a permission gate crosses the rail line to Halyard.
Machinery is repaired, never replaced. Colours: rust, charcoal, oxidized orange.

On Keel's main street is THE LAST CHANCE, a pawn shop with a payday-loan window ("CASH TODAY · EASY
TERMS"), and next door a small game shop. People pawn what they own and borrow against their next
wages there. Keel's story about Adrian Phantum is that he pawned and borrowed everything away; a
torn default notice with his face is still on the shop's wall. There is no casino in Keel.

## The mines below Keel

Under the pit are the old lithium works: shafts, haul tunnels and galleries dug long before Keel
was a city. The entrance on the pit rim is sealed with concrete and faded hazard striping, and going
in is forbidden. Getting there means sneaking down past the seal, not travelling anywhere. Inside it
is dark, cold and damp, with rusted rails, riveted pipes, analog gauges and manual regulator wheels.
Some of it is not abandoned: newer water-control equipment has been installed inside the old works,
out of sight, where official readings and real flow can differ.

## Halyard, the middle

Halyard sits midway up the slope: fabrication towers, workshops, foundries, greenhouse terraces,
elevated rail, cargo cranes, patent exchanges. It is where things are made, and where the question
is who owns what gets made. Three Charter Houses, for energy, water and space, run the largest
towers. Richer and more orderly than Keel, but its workers still pass gates. Colours: warm bronze and
gold with engineered green.

## Oasis, the top

Oasis rises above the cloud layer: pearl-white architecture, designed gardens, wide reservoirs,
open fountains, quiet automated transit. A spacecraft lifts toward orbit from a distant launch
structure. Its people differ from Keel's in health, space, clothes and speech, not in ancestry.
Colours: pearl white, silver, cool blue, controlled green.
