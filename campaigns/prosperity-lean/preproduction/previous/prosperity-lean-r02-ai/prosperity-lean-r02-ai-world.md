## Overview

The year is **2043**. Three cities occupy different elevations of one mountain basin in the high desert: **Keel** at the bottom, **Halyard** in the middle, and **Oasis** above the cloud layer. They are joined by one vertical infrastructure spine containing a huge water conduit, power lines, a rail line and identical small white-and-amber control nodes at every level. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Water is the pressure on nearly every part of life: housing, employment, travel, debt, public services and political authority. The Water Wars ended a generation ago, but the emergency systems built afterward remain in force. The result is a society that keeps people alive while also keeping them in place. `(input/world.md)`

The three cities share ancestry but not living conditions. Keel is crowded, dry, mined-out and heavily restricted; Halyard is more prosperous and industrial; Oasis is spacious, clean and administratively powerful. The differences are expressed through access to water, maintenance, mobility, space, health and speech rather than through separate peoples. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Setting

### The mountain basin

The basin rises in three urban tiers:

- **Keel:** the bottom tier, built in and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
- **Halyard:** the middle tier, located midway up the slope.
- **Oasis:** the upper tier, rising above the cloud layer.

The infrastructure spine runs vertically through all three tiers. The water conduit is enormous at Oasis's reservoirs and narrows dramatically by the time it reaches Keel's channels. Access gates multiply toward Keel, including a permission gate across the rail line between Keel and Halyard. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

A sealed mine entrance sits on the rim of Keel's pit. It is marked with faded hazard striping, and entry is forbidden. The old mine works extend beneath the pit through shafts, haul tunnels and galleries. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Keel

Keel is built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. Its visible environment includes:

- stacked housing;
- rusted mining structures;
- patched pumps;
- crowded lanes;
- aging factories;
- narrow water channels;
- old pipes;
- metered public taps;
- digital checkpoints;
- a sealed mine entrance on the pit rim;
- a permission gate across the rail line toward Halyard.

Its palette is rust, charcoal and oxidized orange. Machinery is repaired rather than replaced. Dust hangs in the air, and workers queue with jugs at public taps. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel's main street includes **THE LAST CHANCE**, a pawn shop and payday-loan business. Its window advertises:

> CASH TODAY · EASY TERMS

A small game shop, **NEXT LEVEL GAMES**, stands next door. A torn default notice bearing Adrian Phantum's face remains on the pawn shop's wall as a public cautionary image. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

A public tap on the street displays a ration of **4 L**. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### The mines below Keel

The old lithium works beneath the pit were dug long before Keel became a city. They contain:

- shafts;
- haul tunnels;
- galleries;
- rusted rails;
- riveted pipes;
- analog gauges;
- manual regulator wheels;
- newer water-control equipment installed inside the old works.

The newer equipment is hidden from ordinary view. It allows official readings and real water flow to differ. The mine is officially sealed, and reaching the interior requires sneaking down past the concrete seal rather than travelling through an ordinary public route. `(input/world.md)`

### Halyard

Halyard sits midway up the mountain slope. It contains:

- fabrication towers;
- workshops;
- foundries;
- greenhouse terraces;
- elevated rail;
- cargo cranes;
- patent exchanges;
- advanced industrial facilities.

Its palette is warm bronze and gold with engineered green. Halyard is more prosperous and orderly than Keel, but workers still pass through gates. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Three **Charter Houses** run the largest towers. They control major sectors identified as energy, water and space. Their towers carry restrained, distinct insignia. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Oasis

Oasis rises above the cloud layer. It contains:

- pearl-white architecture;
- designed gardens;
- broad reservoirs;
- open fountains;
- civic spaces;
- quiet automated transit;
- a distant spacecraft launch structure.

Its palette is pearl white, silver, cool blue and controlled green. A spacecraft rises toward orbit from the launch structure, carrying the same space-and-mining insignia associated with the largest Halyard tower. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Oasis has visibly greater space, health, cleanliness and access to water than Keel. Its control room is minimalist and pearl white, with graphite surfaces, a wall-sized live display of the mountain and a glass of clean water placed precisely on a desk. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

The Water Wars ended a generation before 2043. Their aftermath produced the institutional order that still governs the basin. Water remains scarce enough that the memory of the wars is built into public space: a weathered plaque on Keel's rim reads **WATER WARS MEMORIAL**, above a dry, cracked drinking fountain that no longer runs. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### The emergency council and Alpha

After the Water Wars, an emergency council representing the three cities built **Alpha**, an artificial intelligence intended to run water, food, power, work and travel. The system was meant to be temporary but remained in place. `(input/world.md)`

Alpha's control nodes are small, identical, white-and-amber units installed along the infrastructure spine at every level. A node is present in Keel's pump hall, at the rail permission gate and throughout the higher tiers. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### The old lithium works

The lithium works beneath Keel predate the city. Their obsolete physical systems—analog gauges, manual regulator wheels, riveted pipes and rails—remain in place beneath the sealed pit. Newer water-control equipment has been installed inside them, hidden from official view. `(input/world.md)`

### First-generation Alpha field units

Bi11bot is identified as **B-11**, a first-generation Alpha field unit. Alpha's records lost the unit sixteen years before the events of Chapter 1. When an old registered machine is switched on, it checks in with Alpha's network, like a company laptop reconnecting to its office network. The check-in can be traced by a watcher in Oasis. `(rules/characters.md; input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Institutions and networks

### Alpha

Alpha is the basin-wide AI authority for:

- water;
- food;
- power;
- work;
- travel;
- registered machinery;
- administrative status.

It operates through the white-and-amber control-node network and through digital checkpoints. Its systems record work, wages, debt, identity, travel permission and machine registration. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Alpha's authority is infrastructural rather than merely governmental: the same network that manages water and transit also observes workers, records repairs and detects dormant registered devices when they return online. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Alpha can fail or be bypassed in ways that create competing realities. In the old mines, hidden water-control equipment permits official readings and actual flow to diverge. A device can also remain untraceable while folded if its physical shell blocks its signal. `(input/world.md; rules/characters.md)`

### The Charter Houses

Three Charter Houses dominate Halyard's largest towers and represent the sectors of:

- energy;
- water;
- space.

They control major fabrication and infrastructure interests. The space-and-mining insignia of the largest Halyard tower also appears on the spacecraft launched from Oasis. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The material does not establish the Charter Houses' formal relationship to Alpha, the emergency council or one another. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Keel's administrative checkpoints

Keel workers pass digital checkpoints that display what they owe. A checkpoint can verify age, transfer household liability, record wages and show travel status. On a worker's eighteenth birthday, parental household liability can transfer to the adult's record. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The checkpoint display can show:

> TODAY: DEBT TRANSFER DAY  
> AGE VERIFIED: EIGHTEEN  
> PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE  
> GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED  
> DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS  
> EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS  
> PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS  
> TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED

The checkpoint guard can treat this as an ordinary civic milestone, saying:

> Happy birthday, Phantum.

`(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Oasis monitoring

A watcher in Oasis observes device check-ins on Alpha's network. When B-11 comes online after sixteen years, the Oasis control room receives:

> LEGACY DEVICE ONLINE  
> UNIT B-11 · ALPHA FIELD UNIT, FIRST GENERATION  
> REGISTERED TO: A. PHANTUM — RECORD CLOSED  
> LAST CHECK-IN: 16 YEARS AGO  
> HOLDS: MEASURE LOCATION  
> [ REPORT ] [ TRACK ]

The watcher can select **TRACK**. The resulting display reads **SIGNAL INTERMITTENT · ACQUIRING**. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Governance and power

Alpha's systems determine or enforce access to essential resources and movement. A person's work card carries identity, wages, debt and travel status. Travel up the mountain requires a permit, and the rail line between Keel and Halyard has a permission gate. `(input/world.md; rules/characters.md)`

The lower a person lives, the more visible the restrictions become. Keel workers pass checkpoints, queue for rationed water, repair obsolete machinery and can lose their work cards for violating a mine seal. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The mine seal is both a physical barrier and an administrative boundary. Anyone caught beyond it risks losing their work card. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The hierarchy is also visible through infrastructure:

- Keel receives narrow channels and old pipes.
- Halyard contains manufacturing, workshops and controlled industrial access.
- Oasis has broad reservoirs, open fountains, automated transit and a dedicated monitoring room.

The material establishes Alpha's practical reach but does not establish whether Alpha is legally sovereign, who currently directs it, or whether the Charter Houses can override it. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Economics, labour and ownership

### Currency and debt

The currency is measured in **credits**, abbreviated **CR**. Debt is attached to work and household records. Workers' checkpoint displays show current debt, daily earnings, projected clearance time and travel status. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel's pawn and payday-loan economy operates through immediate cash advances against future wages or possessions. The Last Chance advertises:

> CASH TODAY · EASY TERMS

A loan slip from the shop reads:

> ADVANCE 120 CR · REPAY 10 CR / WEEK · 31 WEEKS

The total repayment is **310 CR**, not the original 120 CR advance. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The loan system creates a market in which people exchange future wages for present necessities, entertainment or a chance to change their circumstances. The exact legal terms, interest calculation and enforcement mechanism are not established beyond the displayed repayment schedule. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Work

Workers maintain the basin's water and industrial systems. Keel's machinery is old enough that repair skill is essential, and approved procedures can conflict with what keeps equipment functioning. A pump-hall worker can make an unauthorized repair, have it flagged by an Alpha node, and still have a supervisor reclassify it as routine maintenance if the pump works. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The checkpoint records earned wages. One displayed worker earns **40 credits** in a day against a debt balance of **418,607 credits**, with a projected clearance time of **28.7 years**. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Halyard's economy is based on fabrication, energy, water, space infrastructure, workshops, foundries, patent exchanges and cargo movement. Ownership of what is made is an explicit social question; the three Charter Houses run the largest towers. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The material does not establish whether workers own inventions, tools, patents or the products of their labour. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Technology

### Alpha's node network

The small white-and-amber control nodes are Alpha's physical network points. They appear at every tier along the infrastructure spine and at workplaces and checkpoints. A node can:

- log a worker's shift;
- flag an unauthorized repair;
- record a supervisor's maintenance decision;
- verify identity and age;
- transfer household liability;
- display debt and wages;
- report travel status;
- detect an online registered machine;
- transmit a traceable check-in through the basin.

`(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### COST interface

The watch's COST screen is a visual interface for the financial consequences of a choice. It reads posted prices and terms on Keel's node network and displays the real credit cost over time, including a loan's total repayment, a fee or the interest added to a debt. `(rules/characters.md)`

COST cannot measure sleep, time or risk. Those remain matters for a person to weigh. It does not judge, speak or display moral language such as **GOOD** or **BAD**. Its screen shows a large amber-red number, a small cost icon and a thin bar. `(rules/characters.md)`

The first observed reading is **310 CR**, generated from the payday-loan slip showing a 120-credit advance repaid at 10 credits per week for 31 weeks. `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Machine registration and traceability

Every machine Alpha has registered checks in when it comes online. A device that has been dark for years appears on the network as soon as it is switched back on. An awakened, unfolded B-11 can check in periodically and be traced. `(input/world.md; rules/characters.md)`

B-11 becomes untraceable when folded. Its head shell blocks every signal like a metal box, and an inspection reads the folded unit as unregistered scrap. `(rules/characters.md)`

### Repair technology

Keel depends on mechanical repair rather than replacement. Its pumps, pipes and mining systems combine obsolete physical equipment with Alpha-connected monitoring. Visible machinery includes patched brackets, old valves, manual regulator wheels, analog gauges and riveted pipes. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

A worn pump can continue operating even when a repair violates the approved maintenance procedure. Alpha's system may flag the repair, but a supervisor can alter the recorded classification. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Transit and communications

The infrastructure spine carries a rail line between the tiers. The rail line is controlled by a permission gate between Keel and Halyard. Oasis has automated transit. Alpha's network carries machine check-ins and monitoring signals through control nodes and connected infrastructure. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

A spacecraft can launch toward orbit from a structure above Oasis. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Infrastructure and daily life

### Water

Water is transported down the mountain through a huge conduit. At Oasis it feeds broad reservoirs and open fountains; by Keel it has narrowed into channels and old pipes. Keel residents queue at metered public taps with jugs, and a street display can state a ration of **4 L**. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Water access is visibly unequal. Oasis has clean water available as an ordinary object in a control room and fountains in public space, while Keel residents measure and queue for their daily supply. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Housing and domestic life

Keel housing is stacked and crowded. A Keel apartment can still be warm, dignified and carefully maintained despite scarcity, with:

- handmade quilts;
- repaired furniture;
- shelves of old books;
- faded family photographs;
- potted plants in recycled containers;
- water jugs by the door;
- ration marks scratched into the jugs.

`(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Work and bureaucracy

A Keel worker's ordinary day can include working in a pump hall before dawn, using a digital node to log a shift, passing a checkpoint at the end of work, seeing debt and wages displayed, and negotiating whether to follow an approved repair procedure. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Administrative status is carried physically and digitally. A work card contains ID, wages, debt and travel status and is clipped to a worker's belt. Losing it can threaten employment and movement. `(rules/characters.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Food and public life

The material establishes Alpha's responsibility for food, but does not specify the food supply, distribution system or ration levels. Keel's public life includes crowded lanes, pawn shops, a game shop, water queues, work checkpoints and the pit rim. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

### Social conduct

People in Keel notice one another's debt. At a checkpoint, workers' balances can be visible to the queue, and a sudden increase draws silent attention. Public debt is both administrative information and social stigma. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

People also exchange help under scarcity. Saving part of a water ration for another person is a meaningful act because water is measured and limited. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

The balanced water-drop symbol appears on old equipment and on the spine of a hidden blue book. Its broader cultural or institutional meaning is not established. `(drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

## Environment and extraction

The high-desert basin is dry, dusty and defined by steep elevation. Water infrastructure runs from the reservoirs above the cloud layer down toward the mining city below. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`

Keel occupies an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. The visible mine structures are rusted and aging, while the sealed works below contain both obsolete mining infrastructure and newer concealed water-control equipment. `(input/world.md)`

The basin's environmental pressure is not abstract: dust hangs over Keel, public taps are metered, water channels are narrow, the memorial fountain is dry, and the official and actual water flows may not match. `(input/world.md; drafts/chapter-01-draft.md)`
