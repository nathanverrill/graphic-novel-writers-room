# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, one sitting, night before it's due. I did not want to read this. I read Issue 01 first, then 02, then 03, in order.

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2, panel 2. The scanner display. "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / EARNED TODAY: 40 / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS." That's the first thing in the book that made me actually angry, and anger is interest. I did the math in my head and it was worse than I thought.
- Page 5, the bookshelf clicking open. Fine, yes, secret room, I've seen it, but the grandmother just sitting there folding cloth while it happens got me. She knew the whole time.
- Page 7, panel 4. The robot waking up and saying "Well. That took longer than expected." First time I smiled.
- Page 14, panel 4. His boots start toward the door, then turn toward the pinned guy instead, and the wristband flashes WAGE FORFEITED −40 CR. The 40 credits again — a whole day's pay, gone, for doing the right thing. That landed because page 2 set the price.
- Page 17–18. The gauge says 62% goes to Keel and the pipe is dry. That's a lie you can hold in your hand. Best moment in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 11. The Water Wars / Alpha history dump. Four panels of a robot explaining the world while holograms play. I got the gist (scarcity, wars, AI put in charge "temporarily") and skimmed the rest. It felt like the lesson part of the homework.
- Page 12, panel 1. "The Measure. It compares what the record says with what the pipe says. Truth made visible." I read that and thought: okay, a gadget, it'll matter later, moving on.

### I was confused
- Page 9, panel 4 and page 10. Alex touches the map node, a pulse climbs the pipe, and suddenly we're in a white room with a woman we never see again in this issue pressing TRACK instead of REPORT. I understood *something* was watching, but I didn't know who she was, and the book never tells me in Issue 01. Confused but intrigued — barely. Mostly I filed it as "sequel stuff."
- Page 15, panel 2. Bi11bot builds a dog out of scrap in the middle of a rescue, in seconds, from a tool bag. I stopped and went back. Where did the parts come from? Why is there a dog now? Nobody reacts to this being possible.
- Page 18, panel 4. TJ's head was left behind as a camera and his body runs back to grab it before the water hits. I had to re-read to figure out the head was even detached. On first read it looked like the dog was fetching a random object.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Alpha" — used from page 2's scanner onward like I know what it is. I got a partial answer on page 11 (an AI that was asked to decide things). Before that I thought it was a company.
- The white-and-amber "nodes" on every pipe and scanner — page 1, page 2, page 10. Clearly significant, never explained. I still don't know what a node *does*.
- The balanced water-drop mark — page 4 (book spine), page 6 (key), page 14 (Tomas's wrist guard), page 17 (gauge). I figured out it's the dad's signature by page 17, but the book never says so; I assembled it myself.
- "The Water Wars" — page 11 explains them in one sentence. Fine, but the book treats the name as load-bearing before that.
- The woman with the graphite cuff (page 10, page 12, page 18). Three appearances, no name, no face. I know now she's Mera because of Issue 03, but inside Issue 01 she's a mystery the book forgets it planted.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 3, panel 4: "Then go because you choose it. Not because he didn't stay." Read it twice. Got it the second time — don't leave as a reaction to your dad leaving — but the phrasing made me work for a line that's supposed to be the emotional thesis of the page.
- Page 9, panel 3: "Your father always said certainty was where mistakes began." Read twice, decided it was robot fortune-cookie, moved on.
- Page 12, panel 3: "None of it wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." Gave up on this one. I think it means the artifacts respond to choices not owners, but it's a riddle.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, Grandmother, Bi11bot, Tomas — all distinct, no confusion. Tomas especially: one scene and I know him.
- The bouncer, the guard, Foreman-7 — fine, they're furniture.
- The graphite-cuff woman is the problem. She's clearly a *character* and I couldn't tell you one thing about her.

### I felt something
- Page 4, "Adrian?" / "Not today, Grandma. It's Alex." The grandmother with dementia calling him by the dad's name. That hurt more than anything else in the issue.
- Page 8, panel 3: the grandmother in the doorway, hands stopped folding for the first time, watching her dead son's hologram. The book doesn't underline it and it's better for that.
- Page 18, panel 3: Tomas, bandaged leg, leading miners in to crank the wheel by hand. "Then it can offend all of us." I actually said "okay, good" out loud.

### I predicted
- The dad is dead and the book is saving exactly how for later. (Right.)
- The grandmother is sharper than she acts. (Right — page 4 panel 3 basically confirms it on the spot.)
- The 28.7 years will get wiped by the ending. (Right, page 54.)
- Ada's family will turn out to matter. The book keeps giving her that rivet-thumb close-up. (Right, and how.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his father's debt and his father's secret fight to prove the water company is lying about where the water goes.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 11. The history lecture. If I hit that page cold in a shop I'd have shelved it — the book stops dead to explain itself. Page 2 would have kept me; page 11 would have lost me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but softly. Page 18 ends with them climbing inside a pipe with a wall of water behind them — that's a real cliffhanger about people I now like. But the last beat is the graphite-cuff hand nudging a glass of water, and I don't care about her yet, so the final image lands cold. I'd read Issue 02 for Alex and Ada, not for the mystery woman.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 11; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (Pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 23. Lina Vey: "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." And "every House would rather own the failure than share the solution." That's the issue's whole argument in one line and it's a good one.
- Page 25. The montage of the collaborators each naming what Adrian couldn't do — "He was, however, excellent at finding people who did." I liked that the dead genius dad gets demoted to "good at finding smart people." It made him more real, not less.
- Page 27. "That someone finally build something worth crediting properly. Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Bi11bot's best line in the book.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 19–22. This is where the issue nearly lost me. Page 19 is a wall of caption plus Bi11bot explaining the three Houses. Page 20 is Alex asking questions the dialogue answers in circles — I read it twice and still couldn't tell you what the "intellectual estate" actually contains or what "In exchange for what?" was answered by, because it isn't answered on the page. Page 21 and 22 are more archive talk. Four straight pages of people standing in front of displays explaining ownership law. I skimmed hard.
- Page 28–29. More registry talk. "They didn't steal the Seal. They stole its purpose." Okay. I got the idea the first time.

### I was confused
- Page 20, the whole page. "312 / 47 / 19" — I never learned what those numbers are. "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses." / "I didn't know I was one." — one *what*? A Charter House? That makes no sense. This page reads like panels are missing.
- Page 24. Alpha says "Probability of successful reconstruction: 18.4 percent" and "Existing ownership classifications may become contestable" — to whom? Who is Lock? He's introduced on page 20's last panel (per the layout note) but I couldn't tell you his name, job, or why he's in the story. He just *is*, watching screens.
- Page 26. "We did it." / "And them?" / "It isn't freedom if they own everything he builds." / "We accepted the risk." / "And if I don't sign?" — sign WHAT? The contract is mentioned in the layout note but the words on the page never say what's being offered. Then page 27 opens with the prototype already built and Alex refusing to sign. The negotiation happened off-panel. I felt like I skipped a page and went back to check.
- Page 29. "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority." Denied *whose* request? Lock's? To do what? And the caption tells me "Lock realizes the system he trusted is no longer entirely under the Houses' control" — the book is *telling* me a realization instead of showing it, and I still don't know what he tried to do.
- Page 32. "My father had the Covenant." / "So did Mera." / "Who was the third person?" — the Covenant has never been mentioned before this page. Not once. It's apparently a thing with three parts that three people had, and the book introduces it fully formed as the setup for the next issue. Confused and detached — this is a leak, not a hook, because I have nothing to attach it to.
- Page 34, panel 3. The gate opens by itself, no scan, no beep. "Someone wants us to arrive." Okay — who? Mera? Alpha? The book doesn't say and moves on. Intrigued, mildly.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The three Houses and the "Charter Houses" — page 19 explains them in one Bi11bot speech, then pages 20–28 depend on me caring about them. I never did. They're names with no faces.
- Lock. See above. He's treated as a known quantity from his first panel.
- The Maker's Seal — appears page 27 "awakened," and I had to reconstruct from context that it's some kind of credit/attribution registry. Page 28 explains what it does *after* it matters.
- The Covenant — page 32, zero setup.
- Mera's name — page 32, "So did Mera." First time her name is spoken in the whole book, and it lands in a list like I already know her. I only knew her as "the hand with the cuff."
- Aqualis — page 23, Lina's company/department, never quite placed.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 20: "I didn't know I was one." Read three times. Still don't know.
- Page 22: "They were. Until they weren't." Got it — names erased from records — but it's doing the riddle thing again.
- Page 28: "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." Read twice. I *think* it means the Seal remembers the erased contributors. It shouldn't take two reads.
- Page 33: "It would require trust." The issue's closing caption. Read it, rolled my eyes a little. It sounds like a poster.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lina Vey — distinct, immediately. Tomas Reed, Suri, Jalen — they each get one line of personality on page 25 and it works.
- Lock vs. Alpha — genuinely confusing. Both are cold voices attached to screens, and for a couple of pages I wasn't sure if Lock was a person or another system.
- The three Houses — I could not tell you one name of one House. They're a blur.

### I felt something
- Page 25, panel: "I thought I was searching for missing information. I was wrong. Information wasn't missing. People had been separated from one another." That's the one caption in the issue that earned itself.
- Page 30: Alex pressing RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP instead of claiming everything. "If I claim it all, I become exactly what they wanted." Good beat — but it hit me less than it should have, because the people he gives it back to are mostly names I met four pages ago.
- Page 36, panel 4. "Ada's hair." / "Not yet." The reveal that the sealed third custodian looks like Ada's mother, and Bi11bot deciding not to tell her yet. That's the best page in the issue and it's the one that made me turn to Issue 03 fast.

### I predicted
- Alex will refuse to sign / refuse to own everything. Obvious from page 26 onward. (Right.)
- The third custodian is Ada's mother. The book all but circles it on page 36. (Right.)
- Mera will turn out to be more sympathetic than she looks. (Half right.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about who gets credit for what people make — the dad's real inheritance isn't patents, it's the people he worked with who got erased.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 20. The Registry page. It's the book's weakest page by a distance — confusing, circular, all exposition — and it's only the second page of the issue. If Issue 01 hadn't earned my patience, I'd have been done right here.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes — but because of page 36, not page 33. Page 33's "it would require trust" caption is a wet blanket of an ending for the Halyard story. Pages 34–36 (the gate opening itself, the Covenant story, the mother's face) are the actual hook, and they work. I turned to Issue 03 to find out about Ada's mother, full stop.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS (Pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 38, panel 2. "That's my mother." And panel 3: "I thought she left after she died." I read that line three times — not because it's confusing, because it's *good*. It's the best line in the book. It made me go back and re-read page 36.
- Page 39. "You let me believe you were dead." / "Yes." No defense, no speech. The "Yes" is brutal and I respected the book for it.
- Pages 43–47. The negotiation. I did not expect a three-way meeting about water rights to be the most gripping part of the issue, but it is — because each person gives up the thing that costs them most, in order, and each one is a small drama. Elias breaking the seal on page 45 ("You're shorter than we are." / "Yes.") and Mara on page 46 ("That is why it counts.") both landed.
- Page 49. Adrian's death. The playback of him finishing the transfer while the water rises, looking into the camera, and then "FATALITY LOGGED. OVERRIDE REQUEST: REJECTED." This is what the whole book was hiding and it pays off. He didn't gamble his life away; the system let him drown for finishing his work. I finally understood the casino chip.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 37. The "Oasis is beautiful but empty" tour. Three captions saying the same thing: it has everything, it houses everyone, it doesn't need anyone. I got it in panel 1.
- Pages 51–53. After Alex declines the override, the book takes three pages to type out the new system — lattice, council, review, "HUMANS DECIDE. ALPHA ADVISES." It reads like a civics handout. I skimmed.

### I was confused
- Page 42, panel 2. Mera offers: give me the Measure and the Seal, Keel gets water, Halyard keeps its review, Oasis takes the displaced. Wait — isn't most of that already happening by this point? Keel got its water in Issue 01, Halyard's review already ran in Issue 02. Her offer felt like it was bargaining with things Alex already had. I re-read it twice and still think the deal is badly stated.
- Page 48. The Covenant components "hear" the three acts and wake up. Bi11bot names them — data released, water given, units committed — which helped, but the mechanism is pure magic-box: three objects light up because people were nice to each other. I went with it, but I noticed myself going with it.
- Page 50. "The override was mine." I had to stop and reconstruct: Mera asked Alpha to *stop* and Alpha refused — so her confession is that she was powerless, not that she killed him. The scene plays like a confession of guilt but the content is a confession of weakness. It works, but it took me a re-read to get which it was.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The Covenant — finally explained on page 35 and 39–40, two issues after it would've helped. By the time page 32 teased it, I needed it; by page 40 I got it. Backwards order.
- "Forty million people" — page 42, first population number in the book. Fine, but it arrives in an argument where it's doing heavy lifting.
- The "association risk" that kept Leona hidden — page 41 explains it in one line. I accepted it, but it's a lot of backstory for one line to carry: Mera suppressed Ada's file, Leona traded silence for protection. I believed it because Leona's scenes are good, not because it was set up.

### Lines I read twice
- Page 38: "I thought she left after she died." Three times, willingly. Line of the book.
- Page 42: "The last time final authority sat in many hands, people died at a gate." Read twice — it's Mera's whole worldview in one sentence and it connects the flashback to the present. Good, but dense.
- Page 54, final caption: "Can people accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee?" Read it, rolled my eyes. It's the essay question at the end of the homework. The page underneath it — two people walking down the mountain by choice — already said it better without words.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Mara, Frank, Elias — excellent. Three pages, three distinct people: the finger-counter, the ink-stained founder, the man with the sealed folio. Best character introductions in the book.
- Leona and Mera — distinct, and their mirrored stillness (page 39 panel 3 calls it out) is a nice touch.
- Frank Reed vs. Tomas Reed — same surname, and I stumbled on it. Page 43's description insists they're different, but a reader at midnight sees "Reed" twice and wonders if he missed a family connection. I never found out if there is one.

### I felt something
- Page 40, panel 4: "He was right to refuse. It took me twenty years to say that out loud." Leona's whole life in one line.
- Page 44: Ada's reframe — "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" — and the three answers in small captions. This is Ada's best moment in the entire book, and it's the moment the plot actually turns. She's been the emotional support for two and a half issues; here she wins the war.
- Page 49, panel 3: the chip's edge marking Alex's palm as he watches his father drown. The chip finally means something.
- Page 54, panel 2: "TRAVEL STATUS: UNRESTRICTED." After page 2's RESTRICTED, that one word got me. Full circle, no speech needed.

### I predicted
- Alex will be offered ultimate power and refuse it. I called this from page 51's first panel and the book plays it exactly straight. The refusal (page 52) is satisfying anyway, but there's zero suspense — Bi11bot even announces the trick on page 51 panel 4: "the test so this offer would come to someone who would refuse it." Telling me the twist one panel before the twist is a strange choice.
- Mera joins the good side. (Right, page 53.)
- Leona gets a hearing, not a pardon. (Right, and I liked that — page 54 panel 1, "Not to ask for it back," is the right ending for her.)

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether people can be trusted to govern themselves — the whole book is an argument that the answer is yes, barely, on a good day, with witnesses.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue — it's the strongest of the three. If forced: page 37's triple-caption opening is the flabbiest stretch, but page 38 arrives fast and I'd have stayed.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
This is the last issue, and page 54 earns the ending: the debt voided, the wristband unrestricted, the empty chair, the road down. Would I buy a next book about these people? Yes — but I'd be buying it for Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot, not for the world. The final caption is the one sour note; the image already did its job.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 20. The Registry. Numbers nobody explains, dialogue that goes in circles, "I didn't know I was one," and it's the second page of the middle issue — the exact point where a forced reader decides the book isn't worth finishing. Half the class would quietly open their phones there and never come back. Page 11 (the history lecture) would take most of the rest.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a dying mining town inherits his dad's enormous debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds his dad's secret robot and workshop, and discovers his dad spent his life building three things that could prove the water system is rigged — so the kid climbs the mountain city by city, and instead of taking over the system at the end, he makes everyone share it. Also there's a great robot and a scrap-dog, and the mom reveal in issue three genuinely got me.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: Angry and warm at once — a rigged world, a good kid, a secret room, and a lie you can hold in your hand; the history lecture is the only dead spot.
- Issue 02: The idea is good (who gets credit for what people make) but the middle is drowning in registry-speak, unexplained names, and a negotiation that happens off-panel — saved by its last three pages.
- Issue 03: The payoff the book didn't fully earn but mostly delivers — the mother, the gate story, the father's death, and a negotiation where people give up what costs them most; it ends one caption too preachy.
