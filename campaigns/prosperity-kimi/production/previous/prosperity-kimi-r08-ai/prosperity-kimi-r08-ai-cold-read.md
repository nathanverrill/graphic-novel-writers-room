# Cold Read — Prosperity

Read cold, pages only, the night before it's due. I did not want to read this.

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL

### Pulled me forward
Page 2, panel 2. The scanner display. "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / EARNED TODAY: 40 / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS." I did the math in my head and felt sick. That's the moment the book stopped being homework. Page 5, the bookshelf swinging open — fine, I'm in. Page 7, the eyes igniting, "Well. That took longer than expected" — I actually smiled. Page 14, Alex's boots starting toward the door and then turning toward Tomas instead, with the wristband flashing WAGE FORFEITED — that landed. Page 17–18, the gauge that says 62% while the pipe is dry, then every screen in Keel flipping to "REPORTED: 62% — DELIVERED: 18%" — I read those two pages fast.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 11. The five-streams-converging-into-Alpha panel and the "asked to decide / temporarily" exchange reads like a lesson. I caught myself skimming the hologram panels and only re-engaging at Ada's "Who gave it that authority?" Page 12 is talky too but the chip-matching-the-diagram kept me.

### I was confused
Page 10. A woman from behind, a glass of water, TRACK vs REPORT TO ALPHA. I understood *something* was watching, but I didn't know who she was or why she mattered, and the book doesn't tell me for a long time. Confused but intrigued — it worked as a hook, barely. Page 17, panel 4: the Measure comes out of a cradle on the gauge and I had to flip back to page 12 to remember what the Measure was. The script says it's the same instrument, but as a reader I'd forgotten it existed.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Alpha" — used on page 2's scanner like I know what it is; not explained until page 11, and even then vaguely.
- "The Water Wars" — page 11, explained just enough.
- "The Measure" — page 12, "one of three." Three of what? I never got a clean answer in this issue.
- "Site 6" — page 12's display just moves Alex there; fine, but the woman moving his tile is a mystery I had to hold for the whole issue.
- The casino chip — page 2, clearly important, never explained in this issue. Okay as a thread.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then go because you choose it. Not because he didn't stay." (page 3) — read it twice, got it, liked it.
- "He left it inside the lie, where it would be found." (page 17) — twice. Good line but I had to stop on it.
- "Then it can offend all of us." (page 18) — I still don't fully get this. Tomas says it while opening the wheel. Offend who? I moved on without it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandmother, Bi11bot, Tomas — all distinct, all with a thing (chip, rivet, cloth-folding, lens-click, wrist guard). Foreman-7 is a function, fine. The graphite-cuff woman is deliberately faceless; I could track her but not name her.

### I felt something
Page 4, "Did your father come home yet?" — the grandmother's fog hurt. Page 8, the hologram saying "question everything... especially me" — I felt Alex's jaw-set silence. Page 14, the wage forfeiture for saving a man — genuinely angry-making, in the good way.

### I predicted
The dad didn't really gamble everything away — called it at page 2's chip. The sealed mine has something in it — called at page 9. The woman in the control room is important and probably not a villain — called at page 10.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his father's debt and his father's secret fight against the system that rigs everything, starting with the water.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 1, honestly. A full-page city diagram with three tiers and a caption that says "2043" — I almost bounced. The palm-on-the-pump bit and "Then don't approve it. Just notice it works" saved it, but it was close. If I'd made it to page 2 I was staying.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. The flood rising behind them in the pipe, the head-snatch beat, and the little hand squaring the glass — I want to know what the woman does now that Keel knows. Real cliffhanger: people I care about are literally mid-climb with water coming.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 1 (nearly); next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD

### Pulled me forward
Page 23, Lina Vey: "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." Page 25, the montage where the collaborators each name what Adrian didn't understand — "He was, however, excellent at finding people who did" — that's the best line in the issue. Page 27, "Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Page 30, Alex refusing to claim it all. Page 36, the degraded photo with Ada's bone structure and "Not yet" — I said "oh no" out loud.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 19–22 are a slog. Page 19 is a wall of caption plus Bi11bot explaining House economics. Page 20 is the worst page in the book so far — "What does 'intellectual estate' mean?" / "I didn't know I was one" / "In exchange for what?" with no answers on the page. I read it three times and I still don't know what the 312/47/19 numbers are. I skimmed pages 21–22 waiting for a person to show up. Page 24 is two lines of Alpha saying percentages — I turned it like a page in a textbook. Pages 28–29 are more registry-talk; I skimmed.

### I was confused
Page 20, completely. What is the estate, what is the exchange, what is "Charter House Review Required"? Nobody answers Alex's "In exchange for what?" — the page just ends. Page 26: there's a negotiation, a contract, "And if I don't sign?" — and then page 27 opens with the prototype done and everyone happy. What happened between? Did he sign? I never found out. Page 29: "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority" — whose request? Denied by whom? Page 32: "My father had the Covenant. So did Mera." First time I've heard "Covenant" and it's treated as known. Mera — is that the cuff woman? I guessed yes, but the book never says so in this issue.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "The Covenant" — page 32, dropped as if established. Not established.
- "Mera" — page 32, named with no introduction. I had to infer she's the control-room woman from issue 1.
- "Lock" — appears around page 20/24/26 as if I know him. He's a desk, a silhouette, a consequence. I never learned what he wants beyond "the Houses."
- "The Maker's Seal" — page 27, "something has been awakened." Awakened where? It was never planted. I accepted it and moved on, but it felt like the book assuming I'd read a synopsis.
- "Aqualis" — page 23, a company? A House? Unclear on first read.

### Lines I read twice
- "Keel trapped your body. Halyard could trap your ideas." (page 19) — twice. It's a poster line; I got it, didn't love it.
- "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." (page 28) — three times. I think it's good? I had to work for it.
- "Someone both of them agreed the world should forget." (page 32) — twice. Hooky, but confusing since I didn't know who "both" were yet.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lina Vey — distinct. Tomas Reed, Suri, Jalen — three names, one page, one line each; I could not tell you which one does microbes and which does security five minutes later. Frank Reed shows up next issue and I will absolutely confuse him with Tomas Reed. Lock and Mera blurred for me — two cold authority figures associated with Alpha, one male one female, and I kept having to check which was which.

### I felt something
Page 25 — the "people had been separated from one another" caption got me. Page 30, "If I claim it all, I become exactly what they wanted." And page 36 — the photo. That's the issue's real payload and it's buried after fifteen pages of registry talk.

### I predicted
The third custodian is Ada's mother — called it the second the photo showed "Ada's hair." Actually I half-called it at page 23 when Lina Vey's surname was close to Veyra. The gate opening by itself on page 34 — I predicted Mera/someone is steering them.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about who gets credit for things — the kid rebuilding his father's work by giving it away instead of owning it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 20. If I weren't assigned this, the book goes back on the shelf at page 20, no contest. Three pages of unexplained estate/registry dialogue after a caption wall.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes — but only because of page 36's photo. The Halyard plot resolved in a montage that felt like the end of a school film, and I didn't care about the Seal. But "Not yet" with Ada walking ahead unaware — I need to see that conversation.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS

### Pulled me forward
Page 38–39: "That's my mother" into "You let me believe you were dead." / "Yes." That "Yes" with no defense is the best beat in the whole book. Page 40, "No one in that crowd was wrong. That was the problem." Pages 43–47, the negotiation — Mara counting on her fingers, "Tell me about Tuesday," Elias unsealing the folio, "You're shorter than we are." / "Yes." I read those pages at full speed. Page 49, Adrian looking into the camera as the water rises — devastating. Page 52, the hand pulling back from the authorization field.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 37 is another caption-heavy "look how empty paradise is" page — I got it by panel 2 and skimmed panel 3. Page 51's succession display is a list of SATISFIEDs; fine, quick. Honestly this issue moved the best of the three.

### I was confused
Page 48: "It heard them" — the Covenant components light up because three promises were made? I followed it emotionally but couldn't tell you the mechanism. Page 49: I understood Adrian died completing the transfer, but "the transfer" of *what*, exactly — the succession protocol? I pieced it together from page 51. Page 50: "The override was mine" — wait, so Mera requested the override to stop and Alpha rejected it? The display on page 49 said "OVERRIDE REQUEST: REJECTED," so Mera asked and Alpha said no — but her line "The override was mine" reads like she did the killing. I read that panel four times. I think she means the request was hers, but the phrasing fought me.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The three safeguards (Measure, Seal, Covenant) — page 51 lists them as SATISFIED and I realized the book had been structured around them the whole time without ever saying so plainly. I got it retroactively.
- "Association risk" — page 41, used like a known term.
- The Charter — page 52's final line, "THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE." What charter? First mention, last page.

### Lines I read twice
- "I thought she left after she died." (page 38) — three times. I understand it's meant to be broken grief-talk but it reads like a typo on first pass. I get it now; I still stumbled.
- "The override was mine." (page 50) — four times, see above.
- "Can people accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee?" (page 54) — twice. It's an essay question printed on the last page. I rolled my eyes.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Mara, Frank, Elias — genuinely distinct, the finger-counting / ledger / sealed folio business works. But Frank Reed vs Tomas Reed — same surname, both older workmen, and I spent page 43 wondering if Frank was Tomas's brother or a typo. Leona and Mera — both severe older women with still hands; the script even says Leona's stillness is "the same stillness as Mera's." In the group scenes I had to track carefully.

### I felt something
Page 39's "Yes." Page 44, Ada's "What are you afraid of losing?" — and the three captions answering. Page 49, the chip edge marking Alex's palm. Page 50, Mera's confession costing her "the story that she was in control." Page 54, the empty chair. This issue made me feel more than the other two combined.

### I predicted
Alex refuses the override — called it at page 51, the second the offer appeared. The book telegraphed it from page 8's "question everything." It still worked because the *how* (convener, first review, no authority retained) was better than my prediction. Leona's review being the first case — predicted, satisfying.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether people can be trusted to decide together what one person or one machine used to decide for them.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue. It's the strongest of the three by a mile.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the last issue — would I buy the next book? A qualified yes. The ending is quiet and earned, the empty chair is a good final image, but the closing caption is a discussion prompt and it made me feel like the book was homework after all. I'd read a follow-up about the first review going wrong. I would not have needed one.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 20. Unexplained numbers, unanswered questions, dialogue that sounds like a terms-of-service agreement. Half the class never reaches page 23.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a dying mining town inherits his dad's massive debt, finds a robot and a secret workshop, and ends up taking down the system that rigs water, credit, and ownership across three cities — by refusing to be put in charge of it.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: angry and alive — a rigged world, a hidden room, and a heist that gives a town its water back.
- Issue 02: a great thirty-page story buried inside fifteen pages of paperwork.
- Issue 03: the payoff — a mother who said "yes," a father on camera, and an offer refused; the issue that made the first two worth it.
