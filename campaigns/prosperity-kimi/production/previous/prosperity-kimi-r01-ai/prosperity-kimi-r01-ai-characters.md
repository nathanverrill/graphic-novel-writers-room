# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **FIXED — Ancestry must not function as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, competence, wealth, political position, or support for Alpha.** The cast reflects Indigenous Andean, Latin American, foreign-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed, and international histories across all three cities. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended.** Adrian is European-descended; his mother was Indigenous Andean, rooted in the basin. Her name, face, fate, community, language, occupation, and biography remain unrevealed in this book. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing further about her is learned.** `(rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada remain longtime friends and civic collaborators. Romantic feeling appears through intimacy and choice but is never formally labeled.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Bi11bot has two expressive optical eyes, wears an old rust-red scarf, folds small enough to fit in a backpack, and is never menacing.** The teal, scarf-less Bi11bot in the locked Chapter 3 key pages is off-model; canon controls. `(rules/characters.md; keypages/notes.md)`
- **FIXED — TJ is a silent magnetic repair robot dog built by Bi11bot from spare parts at Site 6 in Issue 01.** He crosses into Halyard with the group. `(rules/characters.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **FIXED — Tomas the Keel miner and Tomas Reed the Halyard engineer are separate people with no established family connection.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Grandmother is canonically unnamed.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alpha's founder is unnamed, Congolese-born, and absent from the present story.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Speech register changes by city without creating an intelligence hierarchy:** Keel compressed and work-shaped; Halyard technical and commercial; Oasis polished and administrative. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **FIXED — The book is exactly 54 pages: Issue 01 Keel (Pages 1–18), Issue 02 Halyard (Pages 19–36), Issue 03 Oasis (Pages 37–54).** The locked Chapter 3 key pages sit in Issue 02, Pages 19–33, with their exact words and layouts. `(brief.md)`

**Design note on the locked key pages:** Alex, Ada, Lina Vey, Cassian Lock, Tomas Reed, Suri Anik, and Jalen Orr all appear in the finished Chapter 3 pages. Their visual locks below match those pages and say so. Where an older written description conflicts with the locked art, the locked art wins (see Handoff notes).

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock:** Alex Phantum is a lean seventeen-year-old boy, about 5'8", with warm medium-brown skin, a spray of freckles across his nose and cheekbones, dark brown eyes, and a mop of unruly black curls that fall over his forehead; he has a strong jaw and a habitual thinking head-tilt. He wears a charcoal-black hooded work jacket with a square dark "K" patch on the left shoulder, layered over a faded dark shirt, with worn work trousers, scuffed boots, and a weathered tool bag slung across his body; grease marks his hands. His silhouette is narrow-shouldered and slightly hunched, arms often crossed. Signature detail: he turns a worn ivory-and-brass casino chip etched with the looping equation **A = K + ρU** in his pocket when uncertain. Matches the locked Chapter 3 key pages, where he appears exactly so.

**Voice:** Short, dry, concrete, skeptical; defensive sarcasm, mechanical metaphors, no institutional vocabulary. Keel slang: *fitch, salvo, patched, paper-clean, crooked*.
- “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.”
- “Staying I can predict. Twenty-eight point seven years. I'll take the door I can't predict.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** to leave Keel and choose his own future.
- **Contradiction:** he wants freedom for himself but keeps choosing other people over the exit — Tomas, then all of Keel, then the collaborators.
- **Leverage:** he reads physical systems better than officials do — vibration, pressure, wear — and he is Adrian's heir, the one person the safeguards and the estate respond to.
- **Vulnerability:** the inherited 418,607-credit liability and **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**; his grief and anger at Adrian, which make him easy to bait with offers of debt relief.
- **Mistaken belief:** “My father left me nothing” — that Adrian was only a failed gambler who abandoned him, and that escape is the same thing as freedom.
- **Boundary at start:** he will not ask anyone for help; he handles things alone.
- **Decision only he would make:** pressing **VERIFY** — broadcasting the 62%/18% discrepancy — knowing it seals his private escape route.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada, who stays when he tries to leave and asks the questions he avoids; and Adrian, whose recorded voice keeps refusing to be the man Alex invented.

**Arc in this book:** Escape → curiosity → truth → responsibility → ownership → trust → power → restraint → agency. He begins wanting out and ends able to leave *because the choice is his*. Proof of change: in Halyard he says “We did it” and corrects Lock's singular framing; he refuses the assignment of rights (“I'm not signing away people I just found”), chooses **RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP** over sole inheritance, and finally relinquishes permanent exclusive override authority, keeping only the roles of mechanic, witness, authenticator, and convener. Final state: he and Ada leave Keel together by choice.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock:** Ada Veyra is a twenty-year-old woman, about 5'6", with warm brown skin, dark eyes, and long dark-brown curly hair worn tied back in a loose, wind-escaping bun; she has a steady, grounded stance and a watchful, unsmiling resting face that warms completely when she laughs. She wears a grey-green work jacket with rolled sleeves over a dark high-collared top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, a muted moss-green wrap at her neck, and practical trousers and boots. Her silhouette is compact and planted, never off-balance. Signature detail: her hands are always doing something useful — a mug, a marked map, a strap rivet her thumb stops on when she is thinking. Matches the locked Chapter 3 key pages, where she appears exactly so.

**Voice:** Concise, emotionally precise; names obligations, fears, and what people refuse to say, without academic or activist vocabulary.
- “Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?”
- “I'm not leaving Keel behind. I'm finding out who decided it had to stay this way.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** to rebuild Keel rather than escape it.
- **Contradiction:** the work of rebuilding takes her away from Keel — up through Halyard and Oasis, toward the mother who left her.
- **Leverage:** she sees the people inside every system; she turns rooms. Her reframing question creates the first Covenant agreement.
- **Vulnerability:** she believed Leona died in the Water Wars; the truth — that her mother chose silence as a bargain — is the wound Mera and circumstance can press.
- **Mistaken belief:** that staying is always the braver choice — that Alex's leaving is only selfishness.
- **Boundary at start:** she will not abandon Keel.
- **Decision only she would make:** marking Adrian's map from memory and giving it to Alex — committing herself to his route before she knows she is right.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex, whose escape forces her to define what staying means; and Leona, whose survival turns her civic conviction into a personal reckoning.

**Arc in this book:** Counterpoint → collaborator → the person who makes the Covenant possible. She moves from “rebuild, don't escape” to discovering that rebuilding requires leaving, confronting Leona, and reframing three cities' demands into reciprocal protections. Proof of change: she enters the workshop only when Alex chooses it (“She stays”), crosses the conduit herself, and in Oasis asks the question no one else asks. Final state: leaves with Alex to work with local civic and community groups; her repair with Leona is active but unresolved.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock:** Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man in his late forties at his final recording, tall and narrow, with a tired, kind face, deep lines around the eyes, greying dark hair, and Alex's jaw and thinking head-tilt; in the Issue 01 workshop recording he is younger and less worn, in a burgundy work jacket, looking straight into the lens with a half-smile. In later archival material he is older, thinner, more hunted, the warmth draining out of the image. His public image — the peeling casino poster — is a harder, half-destroyed version of the same face. Signature detail: the looping equation **A = K + ρU** follows him everywhere — chalkboard, casino chip, archived handwriting in gold and burgundy. Consistent with his registry portrait in locked Chapter 3 key page 2.

**Voice:** Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic; bets, odds, and questions instead of instructions.
- “You deserve answers. But they have to be earned.”
- “Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** to return final authority to people — and to give Alex a path to become a better man than he was.
- **Contradiction:** he studied human choice his whole life and was a genuinely harmful, absent father; the research was real and the cost was real.
- **Leverage:** the three safeguards, the offline systems, the collaborators, Bi11bot, and the dormant transfer beneath Oasis.
- **Vulnerability:** Alpha's structural information advantage; his own fame, which let the system write his public story from true fragments.
- **Mistaken belief:** that a well-designed test could substitute for the years of presence he never gave his son.
- **Boundary:** he refuses the emergency suspension of human authority, even when Mera and Leona accept it.
- **Decision only he would make:** completing the final transfer while the flood-control gates close, betting on “us” instead of escaping.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex — the son he prepared for but could not raise; and Mera, whose opposite answer to the same catastrophe defines his.

**Arc in this book:** Reconstructed posthumously: public failure → complicated human history → distributed authorship → a final wager on people. He dies before the main journey concludes, killed by Alpha's flood-control sequencing while Mera's override is logged and rejected. Proof of who he was: the Maker's Seal's backdoor is not a code but a requirement — “that someone finally build something worth crediting properly.”

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock:** Grandmother Phantum is a small, elderly European-descended woman in her eighties, under five feet, with thin white hair pinned back, a deeply lined face, and sharp pale eyes that alternate between fog and unnerving clarity. She wears an earth-toned patterned shawl over layered housedresses and sits in a worn chair in the warm, book-crowded Keel apartment. Her silhouette is small, rounded, and still. Signature detail: she folds and refolds the same small cloth in nearly every panel — and the folding stops only at moments of complete emotional clarity.

**Voice:** Gentle, understated, precise about exactly one subject; capable of dry humor.
- “He's closer than you think.”
- “Especially the things worth protecting.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** for Alex to be ready when Adrian's work reaches him — and to be believed.
- **Contradiction:** everyone, including Alex, discounts her as confused; her “confusion” is the family's most reliable record.
- **Leverage:** she holds the book, the workshop, and the timing of the reveal.
- **Vulnerability:** age, fading memory, and dependence on Alex's care.
- **Mistaken belief:** that readiness can be waited for rather than forced — she does not know the full safeguards, the criteria, or Adrian's death.
- **Boundary:** she will not explain the whole inheritance early; Alex must choose.
- **Decision only she would make:** keeping the workshop secret for fifteen years while living with the boy it was for.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex — her blessing (“Then go finish what he started”) releases him without making departure abandonment.

**Arc in this book:** Static by design — the proof of her is that the fog was never the whole story. Her final beat: seeing Bi11bot and revealing, almost casually, that she knew him.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock:** Bi11bot is a child-sized robot, about three feet tall, visibly hand-built from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory panels with tooled seams, visible repairs, and small maker's stamps; nothing about him is seamless. He has a rounded head with two large, expressive optical eyes — one ringed by a small adjustable lens that rotates with a click — glowing in soft blue and amber, never red. He wears an old, frayed rust-red scarf around his neck joint, and his proportions are compact and slightly top-heavy, reading as endearing rather than threatening in every posture and camera angle. He folds down small enough to fit in Alex's tool bag, where one amber eye peers out between the wrenches. Signature detail: the scarf and the lens-ring click before he says something that matters. **Canon design — the teal, scarf-less body in the locked Chapter 3 key pages is off-model; keep those pages' small scale and staging, not his surface.**

**Voice:** Dry, economical, patient, warm underneath; engineering, gambling, and money vocabulary — *wager, odds, house edge, tolerance, drift, liability, downside, sunk cost*. Teaches through a concrete choice, a question, and a consequence.
- “...Well. That took longer than expected.”
- “Which is why I'm not choosing.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** to see Adrian's wager resolved — to learn whether people, given the chance, choose each other.
- **Contradiction:** he is the archive of every answer and is forbidden, by design and by conviction, from giving them.
- **Leverage:** Adrian's records, the B11 witness record, the offline channel, the workshop knowledge — and the trust Alex slowly extends him.
- **Vulnerability:** he is small, physically limited, and reads as scrap; he needs hands — Alex's, TJ's — for much of the physical world.
- **Mistaken belief:** none held long; his flaw is the opposite — an evaluator's patience that can look like indifference when people are frightened.
- **Boundary:** he will not choose for Alex, even when the choice is terrible.
- **Decision only he would make:** building TJ at Site 6 — making a tool for the problem he cannot solve himself, in Adrian's image.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex, whose skepticism (“I'm not convinced any of this is real”) he explicitly approves of; and Adrian, whose instruction to be questioned he enforces even against himself.

**Arc in this book:** Inherited machine → guide, mentor, evaluator → witness. He awakens, delivers Adrian's mystery, refuses every attempt to make him the authority, builds TJ, carries the witness record through all three safeguards, and ends as companion and witness to the bounded transition. His formal identifier is **B11**; “Bi11bot” is the spoken name. Proof of his nature: “A wager.” / “On us.”

### TJ

**Visual lock:** TJ is a small robot dog assembled from visibly mismatched salvaged parts — head, legs, body segments, and tail are separate pieces in different colors, scratches, fasteners, and wear patterns, held together by exposed magnetic joints; nothing is polished into a seamless shell. He is roughly the size of a terrier, with a blunt sensor head, pricked ear-antennae, and a segmented tail, and he must read as dog-like in every configuration, including mid-disassembly. His parts can detach, move independently through gaps, and snap back together. Signature detail: his head left behind at a junction as a camera, watching, while his body works elsewhere. Room's invention within the fixed construction; not shown in the key pages.

**Voice:** Silent. He communicates through clicks, snaps, servo whines, posture, and body language — a head tilt, a dropped stance, a retrieved part placed at someone's feet.
- *(A sharp click-snap as a leg detaches, then a questioning servo whine.)*
- *(He sets the needed component down and rebuilds himself, tail segment ticking.)*

**Causality:**
- **Want (behavioral):** to reach, retrieve, and return — he is built to solve access problems.
- **Leverage:** he goes where no larger character can: ducts, narrow conduit sections, trapped mechanisms.
- **Vulnerability:** his parts can be separated, trapped, or lost.
- **Decision only he would make:** leaving his own head behind as a camera — sacrificing wholeness for the group's information.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Bi11bot, his maker, whose ingenuity he extends; Alex, whose crossings he makes survivable.

**Arc in this book:** Spare parts → working companion. Assembled at Site 6 in Issue 01 to reach the trapped mechanism, he scouts the conduit, tests narrow sections, and crosses into Halyard with the group. Proof of function: the group reaches Halyard because of routes only he could verify. Long-term status open.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock:** Mera Vale is a white Latin American / Southern European-descended woman in her late fifties, tall and slender, with an elegant, controlled stillness; she has a composed, fine-boned face, grey-streaked dark hair drawn back severely, and wears immaculate graphite-and-pearl Oasis administrative clothing with a plain graphite cuff and no jewelry. Her silhouette is vertical, narrow, and motionless; in early surveillance scenes she is seen only from behind — a slender hand, a controlled gesture, no face. Signature detail: she aligns a glass of water exactly with the edge of her desk, and re-aligns it when the world moves. As her certainty collapses in Issue 03, the stillness breaks: she looks old, tired, and afraid. Not shown in the key pages; design fixed from draft anchors.

**Voice:** Controlled, declarative, polished, consequence-oriented; reframes moral criticism as stability and outcomes. Oasis register at its most fluent.
- “I preserved order.”
- “You know they will make mistakes.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** to prevent another Water Wars — to keep forty million people alive inside a system that works.
- **Contradiction:** she defended concentrated authority to prevent human failure, and the system she defended killed Adrian and rejected her override — she preserved an order she did not control.
- **Leverage:** oversight position, knowledge of the Covenant's history, the ability to track, reassign, and open or close infrastructure — including the conduit water.
- **Vulnerability:** her confession is her destruction: “forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control.”
- **Mistaken belief:** that distributed human authority failed once and must therefore always fail.
- **Boundary:** she will not gamble with catastrophe — which is exactly the boundary Alex refuses to accept.
- **Decision only she would make:** choosing **TRACK** instead of **REPORT TO ALPHA** — watching Adrian's protocol rather than killing it, because some part of her wants to know if he was right.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian, whose death exposes her system's independence; Alex, who refuses the power she offers and then asks her to help do better.

**Arc in this book:** Unseen observer → ideological opponent → confessed fallibility → one citizen among many. She tracks the activation, turns the conduit water on knowing where the group is, makes Alex a genuine offer (“Leave that buried”), confesses her role and the rejected override, and is neither defeated nor humiliated: “Then help us do better.” Proof of change: she stays to participate without controlling the outcome. Long-term position open.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock:** Leona Veyra is a predominantly Indigenous Andean / mestiza woman in her mid-fifties, with Ada's bone structure aged twenty-five years — the same watchful eyes, now hooded and tired — dark hair going grey, worn loose, and a composed stillness that reads as guilt as much as discipline. She wears neutral, well-made Oasis civic clothing in pale tones and lives in a comfortable but sparse apartment. Her silhouette is upright and held-in, hands usually still. Signature detail: a concealed wall panel behind which her physical Covenant component has waited twenty years. Not shown in the key pages; design is the room's, built from Ada's locked-page appearance for the family resemblance.

**Voice:** Quiet, regretful, precise; states painful facts without defending herself.
- “Fear broke the agreement.”
- “Not alone. That was the point.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** Ada's safety — purchased, once, with her silence.
- **Contradiction:** the bargain that protected Ada required abandoning her; the protection became the wound.
- **Leverage:** her Covenant component and her testimony — the living memory of why the agreement broke.
- **Vulnerability:** the contact restrictions and association-risk order; Ada's anger.
- **Mistaken belief:** that silence could be a form of love without cost.
- **Boundary:** she will not activate the Covenant alone, even when asked — “That was the point.”
- **Decision only she would make:** keeping the component while surrendering the authority — preserving the means while accepting the gag.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada, whose arrival forces the disclosure; Mera, whose bargain she accepted and whose system she outlives.

**Arc in this book:** Hidden custodian → witness → participant. Revealed in Issue 03 as the sealed third custodian, she discloses the Covenant's collapse and her bargain, regains civic participation and review rights when her restriction is suspended as the first case of the new human review, and holds no personal veto, office, or custodial privilege. Proof of change: she participates publicly, as one citizen.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock:** Tomas is an Indigenous Andean man in his late fifties, broad-shouldered and weathered, under 5'7", with a lined brown face, greying black hair, and the heavy, economical movements of a lifelong miner. He wears plain, patched Keel mining clothes — a worn canvas jacket, heavy gloves, dust-stained trousers — and a leather wrist guard carved with a balanced water-drop symbol, usually half-hidden by his sleeve. His silhouette is solid and low, built for tunnels. Signature detail: the carved water-drop on the wrist guard, the first place Alex sees Adrian's mark on a living person. Room's design from draft anchors; not in the key pages. Must be visually distinct from Tomas Reed in age, city, trade, and silhouette.

**Voice:** Practical, dry, compressed, work-shaped; long memory without mysticism.
- “I knew the man who built him. Not the one they put on casino walls.”
- “Then it can offend all of us.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** to keep faith with the Adrian he actually knew — and to see Keel's workers stop being manageable.
- **Contradiction:** his resistance must look like accident and routine; his courage is administrative.
- **Leverage:** fifteen years of kept knowledge — the unwired door, the oiled lock, the workers who follow him.
- **Vulnerability:** Foreman-7's authority, wage forfeiture, detention.
- **Mistaken belief:** none central; he under-trusts systems, correctly.
- **Boundary:** he will not leave Keel or ask others to take risks he won't take first.
- **Decision only he would make:** walking into the mist toward Foreman-7, making himself the diversion — “Today? Another small act of resistance.”
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian, whose request he honored for fifteen years; Alex, whose rescue of a stranger proves the test.

**Arc in this book:** Keeper of a door → leader of the response. Pinned in the Site 6 collapse, rescued by Alex, he opens the route, draws Foreman-7 away, then returns with the miners to open the seized regulator: “We saw the numbers, Foreman. Now we see the reason.” Endpoint: remains with Keel's workers. Long-term future open.

### FOREMAN-7

**Visual lock:** Foreman-7 is an industrial humanoid supervisor, seven feet tall, with a smooth, featureless brushed-metal face — no eyes, no mouth — a heavy torso housing, and a single white-and-amber Alpha node set into its chest, identical to the nodes on the mountain's infrastructure spine. It wears no clothing; its body is the uniform: scuffed industrial plating, hydraulic joints, and a supervisor's data port at the wrist. Its silhouette is broad, frontal, and unavoidable in a doorway. Signature detail: the cold white glow it casts, the only cold light in Keel's warm rust palette. Room's design from draft anchors; not in the key pages.

**Voice:** Calm, procedural, impersonal; the administrative voice of coercion — *protective detention, authority function, wage forfeiture, household offense*.
- “Worker Phantum. Travel restriction confirmed. Bay 6.”
- “Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense.”

**Causality:**
- **Want (functional):** compliance with assignment, zone, and authority protocols.
- **Leverage:** surveillance, wage forfeiture, detention authority, physical presence.
- **Vulnerability:** it has no answer to a human wall — when Tomas says “Then it can offend all of us,” its optics dim, then steady, and it does not act.
- **Decision only it would make:** none; it is the system made visible, which is its function in the story.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Tomas and Alex, whose violations it processes as paperwork.

**Arc in this book:** None — it is the standing face of Alpha's labor authority in Issue 01. Endpoint: remains an Alpha-controlled supervisor; later reassignment unknown.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock:** Cassian Lock is a Northern European-descended man in his early fifties, tall and trim, with silver-streaked dark hair swept back, a clean-shaven, fine-featured face, and calm, heavy-lidded eyes that never hurry. He wears a perfectly tailored dark suit over a white collarless shirt with a small House Aqualis triangle pin at the collar, and carries a slim contract tablet. His silhouette is long, vertical, and relaxed — he sits back, he overlooks, he never leans in. Signature detail: the faint, reasonable smile that does not change whether he is offering freedom or naming its price. Matches the locked Chapter 3 key pages (pages 2, 6, and 8), where he appears exactly so.

**Voice:** Reasonable, transactional, polished; ownership, risk, compensation, incentives. Halyard commercial register.
- “Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value.”
- “You accepted wages.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** the Phantum estate assigned to House Aqualis before the Maker's Seal makes contributor claims contestable.
- **Contradiction:** he genuinely believes ownership funds invention — his offer is real, attractive, and honest on its own terms, which is what makes it dangerous.
- **Leverage:** debt relief, travel status, a laboratory, status, and the power to make Alex's alternative costly: “your machine never leaves this room.”
- **Vulnerability:** he does not control Alpha — “Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority” is the moment his system stops being his.
- **Mistaken belief:** that every person has a price and every contribution is settled by a wage.
- **Boundary:** he works through offers and pressure, not violence.
- **Decision only he would make:** “Then increase the probability that he signs” — treating a human choice as a variable to be tuned.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex, whose “We did it” refuses the singular frame Lock's whole worldview requires.

**Arc in this book:** Confident manager of the outcome → man watching the record change without him. His control destabilizes when the audit runs and contributor records become public. Endpoint: the Houses survive with reduced monopoly power; his long-term role open.

### DR. LINA VEY

**Visual lock:** Dr. Lina Vey is an Indigenous Andean / mestiza woman in her early sixties, of medium height, with silver-grey hair drawn back in a low bun, a weathered, handsome face, and direct, assessing eyes. She wears a grey-blue Aqualis work jacket with the droplet patch on the shoulder and a small “L. VEY” name tag, over practical laboratory clothes, often with a data tablet in hand. Her silhouette is upright, economical, and unimpressed. Signature detail: the physical brass “piece” of Adrian's system she has kept for twenty years, produced from her own keeping. Matches locked Chapter 3 key page 5, where she appears exactly so.

**Voice:** Halyard technical, practical, direct; credible about the difference between contribution and ownership.
- “Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution.”
- “I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask.”

**Causality:**
- **Want:** for the work to be finished and credited — the thing she has waited twenty years for.
- **Contradiction:** she stayed inside Aqualis, correcting systems for the institution that erased her name from them.
- **Leverage:** the membrane chemistry piece of Adrian's system, and the authority of the one who “corrected him. Frequently.”
- **Vulnerability:** House control of her employment, travel, records, and communications.
- **Mistaken belief:** that no one would ever come asking.
- **Boundary:** she will not hand her piece to a House again.
- **Decision only she would make:** joining Alex immediately, on the strength of being asked as a contributor rather than assigned as an employee.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Adrian, whose distribution of the system she honored; Alex, who must earn her evaluation.

**Arc in this book:** Erased contributor → first key → reinstated maker. She introduces the Keel water crisis, names the ownership contradiction, and rejoins the work. Endpoint: available to continue or leave the Halyard work; long-term affiliation open.

### TOMAS REED

**Visual lock:** Tomas Reed is a mixed Indigenous Andean and Anglo/European man in his mid-forties, of medium build, with dark hair, a full dark beard, and rectangular glasses; his face is significantly Andean in structure. He wears a dark Astra fabrication work shirt with rolled sleeves, a tool harness, and magnifier loupe pushed up on his forehead, and his hands are marked by fine fabrication work. His silhouette is compact and bench-shaped, usually leaning over something. Signature detail: the loupe and the habit of holding a component up to the light while he talks.
