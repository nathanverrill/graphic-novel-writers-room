# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, one sitting, night before it's due. I did not want to read this. I read Issue 01 first, then 02, then 03, in order.

---

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2, panel 2. The scanner readout — "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / EARNED TODAY: 40 / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS" — is the first thing in the book that made me actually angry on someone's behalf. That's the hook, not the mountain splash on page 1.
- Page 5, the bookshelf swinging open. Fine, I'm in. Secret room behind grandma's shelf works on me even though I've seen it a hundred times.
- Page 7, the robot waking up and saying "Well. That took longer than expected." First line with a pulse.
- Page 14–16, the mine sequence. The cable snapping, the wristband flashing "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR" while he goes to help — that cost being a number he can't afford made the choice land. I read these pages fast.
- Page 18, every screen in Keel flipping to "REPORTED: 62% — DELIVERED: 18%." Genuinely satisfying. I wanted the miners to win and they did.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 1. Big pretty establishing splash, one exchange of dialogue, nothing at stake yet. If I weren't assigned this I'd have flipped past it.
- Pages 10–12. The control room woman with the glass of water, then the history lecture about the Water Wars and Alpha. I felt the book stop to explain itself. I skimmed Bi11bot's balloons on page 11 and only went back because page 12's "The Measure" seemed like it mattered later.
- Page 13. Planning scene around a table. I got the gist (reassigned shift, 2am, map) and moved on.

### I was confused
- Page 3, panel 4: "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." I read this three times. Who is "he"? The dad? Right about what? I still don't know what this sentence means.
- Page 12, panel 2: "Grandma said he wasn't gambling." / "Both were true. The research was real. So was the cost." Nobody said he was gambling before this. There's a casino chip and a torn poster, but nobody said "gambling" out loud. Confused and mildly detached, not intrigued.
- Page 16, panel 4: Ada is suddenly inside the old works with them. Last I saw she was at the kitchen table on page 13. The parenthetical says she came in through another access, but on the page she just appears in the crouch. I flipped back to check if I'd missed her. I hadn't.
- Page 17: the Measure was sitting on the gauge housing the whole time? Alex "lifts the Measure from the gauge housing" — I thought the Measure was the hand instrument Bi11bot showed on page 12. So it was planted there by the dad years ago? Or Bi11bot put it there? Unclear, and it matters because the whole climax runs on it.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Alpha" — used from page 2's scanner onward as if I know what it is. I get a partial answer on page 11. Until then it's just a name on things.
- The casino chip — page 2, turned in his fingers, never explained in this issue. I assume it's the dad's. Fine as a mystery, but it's doing a lot of work.
- "He" in Ada's page 3 line (above). Never resolved in this issue.
- The balanced water-drop mark — appears on the book spine, the key, the gauge, Tomas's wrist guard. I get that it's the dad's mark by page 17, but the book assumes I'm tracking it from page 4.
- Bi11bot's name — it's "BI11BOT" with ones. I read it as "Bilbot" for two pages.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." (p3) — gave up.
- "Sealed isn't the same as gone." (p9) — got it on the second read, fine.
- "None of it wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." (p12) — read twice, still fuzzy. Sounds wise, means unclear.
- "Every door closed but one. And someone made sure you'd notice it." (p13) — okay on second read.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, Grandmother, Bi11bot — clear, distinct voices.
- Tomas — clear enough once he talks.
- The graphite-cuff woman (Mera, though she's never named in this issue) — I know she's important because of the glass-of-water business, but she's a pair of hands. Deliberate, I guess, but I kept forgetting her between appearances.
- Foreman-7 vs the drones — interchangeable white-and-amber authority objects. Fine, they're supposed to be.

### I felt something
- Page 4, grandma calling him "Adrian." That hurt a little.
- Page 8, the hologram: "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me." Best beat in the issue.
- Page 14, the −40 credits. Anger.
- Page 18, the water hitting the Keel pipe. Actual satisfaction.

### I predicted
- The dad is dead or "dead" — predicted by page 8.
- The grandma's fog is at least partly an act — page 4, panel 3 makes it obvious. "He's closer than you think" is a neon sign.
- The 62% gauge is lying — predicted the moment the gauge and the dry pipe were in the same panel.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his missing father's debt and his hidden fight against the system that decides who gets water.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 1, honestly — pretty picture, no pull. If I got past that, page 11's lecture would be the next exit. But page 2's scanner readout is what would have kept me in a shop.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but mildly. The water win is complete and satisfying — which is the problem. The cliffhanger is a robot dog grabbing its own head and a hand nudging a glass. The glass hand is creepy but I don't know whose it is or why I should care yet. I'd read issue 2 because the 62/18 reveal promised more of that, not because of the last page itself.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 1; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (Pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 23, Lina Vey: "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." Good line, and the river-failing problem is concrete.
- Page 25, the montage of the collaborators each naming what Adrian didn't understand. "He was, however, excellent at finding people who did." I liked this page a lot — it's the issue's thesis and it has people in it.
- Page 27, the Seal waking up: "A requirement... That someone finally build something worth crediting properly. Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Made me smile.
- Page 36, the photo of the woman with "Ada's hair." Finally, a personal hook. This is the first time in the issue I needed the next page.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 19–22. This is a long stretch of people explaining ownership law to each other. "Intellectual estate," "Charter Houses," "the Registry." I understood maybe half of it and skimmed the rest. Page 20 especially — it's a list of questions Alex asks, and the answers aren't on the page.
- Page 24. Two lines from Alpha and a man at a desk. I get that Lock is being introduced, but nothing happens.
- Pages 28–31. The Seal's purpose, the contribution map, the decision, the montage of outcomes. It all washes together as "the system gets fairer." I skimmed page 31 entirely — it's a caption describing a better world, no scene.

### I was confused
- Page 19: "From the train." What train? They climbed out of a conduit full of water last issue. First line of the issue and I'm already checking whether I missed something.
- Page 20: "Three hundred and twelve?" Three hundred and twelve what? Patents? I never found out for sure.
- Page 20: "I didn't know I was one." One what? A Charter House? That can't be right. I read this panel four times and gave up.
- Page 22: "Then we start with the one who still wants to be found." Who? Next page introduces Lina, so I guess her, but the line floats.
- Page 26: "And if I don't sign?" — and then the page ends and page 27 opens with the prototype already done and stable. The negotiation, the contract, the consequence Lock was delivering — I never saw what the threat was or how it resolved. This is the biggest hole in the issue. Something got cut and I felt it.
- Page 29: "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority." Denied to whom? Lock asked Alpha for something and I don't know what.
- Page 32: "My father had the Covenant. So did Mera." First time "Covenant" appears (unless I missed it), used as if I know it. And "Who was the third person?" — third what? Custodian? The page assumes a structure I haven't been given.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The Charter Houses — page 19 onward. Three families, fine, but the book treats their mechanics as known.
- "The Registry" — page 20. Never explained, just capitalized.
- Lock — introduced page 20-ish (per the layout note) as a close-up, then he's at a desk with Alpha. I never learned what he wants or why he matters until page 29, and even then it's vague.
- "The Covenant" — page 32, dropped cold.
- Mera — named on page 32. The glass-of-water woman from issue 1, I assume? I've never seen her face. Confirming she's the same person took a flip back.
- Tomas Reed (p25) vs Tomas the miner from issue 1 — same first name, different people? I genuinely thought the miner had followed them until page 43 next issue spells out Frank vs Tomas. Confusing for two issues.

### Lines I read twice
- "I didn't know I was one." (p20) — gave up.
- "Keel trapped your body. Halyard could trap your ideas." (p19) — got it, but it's a poster, not a sentence.
- "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." (p28) — read three times. I think it's good? I think it means the Seal remembers the erased people. But it says "only those the system was taught to forget," which reads backwards.
- "It would require trust." (p33) — fine, but the whole closing caption is the book telling me its theme.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lina Vey — distinct, good.
- Tomas Reed / Suri / Jalen — three names, one voice. "Adrian never understood manufacturing tolerances / Or microbial balance / Or network security" is the same line said three times. I could not pick Suri or Jalen out of a lineup afterward.
- Lock — a silhouette of a man at desks. No voice.
- Ada — she nearly vanishes this issue. She has maybe four lines. I forgot she was there until page 36.

### I felt something
- Page 25, the team montage — warmth, the first time the book feels like it's about people plural.
- Page 36, "Ada's hair." / "Not yet." — a real chill. Best two lines in the issue, and they're whispers.

### I predicted
- Alex won't sign the contract — obvious from page 26's setup.
- The dad left a backdoor/test in the Seal — predicted before page 27 said it.
- The third custodian is Ada's mother — I called it at page 36, which I think is the intended beat, so fine.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about who gets credit for ideas — the kid rebuilding his father's work as something shared instead of owned. (Different sentence from issue 1, and the water-crisis thread from issue 1 mostly disappears, which I noticed.)

### Where I would have put it down
Page 20. The Registry scene is homework about homework. If I weren't assigned this, that's the shelf moment.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes — but only because of page 36, the photo. That's a real cliffhanger: a question about someone I know (Ada) that the characters are deliberately not answering. Pages 34–35's gate opening by itself and "Someone wants us to arrive" is fine but generic. The Ada photo is what would get me to issue 3.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS (Pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- Pages 38–41, Ada and Leona. "You let me believe you were dead." / "Yes." This is the best scene in the whole book. Leona not defending herself, "All those years. You were forty minutes away." / "Yes." I read these pages slowly on purpose.
- Page 42, Mera's offer and Alex's counter — "Then we don't choose your way or his. We convene all three cities." Clean, and I believed him.
- Pages 44–47, the negotiation. Ada's "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" actually works as a turn, and each representative giving something first — Elias's shortfall, Mara's thirty days, Frank shipping before licensing — I followed all of it and wanted each one to cave. Mara's "That is why it counts" got me.
- Page 49, the Adrian death playback. "FATALITY LOGGED. OVERRIDE REQUEST: REJECTED." Grim and it lands.
- Page 50, Mera's confession. "It logged my request and continued." The villain turning out to be a person who lost control of the machine — I felt that.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 37. Three captions telling me Oasis is clean but soulless. I got it in panel 1.
- Page 43. Three representatives introduced in three near-identical panels, each placing an object on a table. I mixed up Frank and Elias immediately and had to keep checking names for the rest of the issue.
- Pages 51–53. The offer, the refusal, the reconfiguration. I knew he would decline from the moment the offer appeared, so these pages had no suspense for me — I read them to confirm, not to find out.

### I was confused
- Page 38: "I thought she left after she died." I read this five times. I think it's a typo or a riddle. If it's a riddle, I failed it. Gave up.
- Page 42: Mera says "The last time your father's way was tried, people died at a gate." But page 40 told me Adrian refused the emergency suspension — so his way wasn't tried. Unless "his way" means the gate crowd? Confused and it matters, because it's her whole argument.
- Page 48: "Three safeguards. Three demonstrated choices. Adrian's transfer is awake." What transfer? Then the screen says "ADRIAN PHANTUM — TRANSFER COMPLETE" with a location from twenty years ago. Transfer of what — authority? His body? His consciousness? I never got a clean answer, and the next page is a death flashback, which made me more confused, not less.
- Page 49: Alpha denied Adrian pump access and sealed egress — so Alpha killed him by refusing to let him... do what? Save the facility? Save himself? The logistics of the death are muddy. I know Alpha let him drown; I don't know what he was trying to accomplish.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "The Covenant" — page 35 gave me a two-panel version, but pages 38–48 lean on its mechanics (three custodians, three components, authentication) harder than the setup supports. I pieced it together, but I was assembling furniture while people were arguing in the room.
- "Adrian's transfer" (p48) — never explained before it matters.
- The "interrupted-line Alpha motif" (p49) — first mention, presented as if recurring. Maybe it was visual all along, but as a reader I can't verify that.
- "Forty million" (p42, p50) — first population number in the book, dropped casually.

### Lines I read twice
- "I thought she left after she died." (p38) — gave up.
- "No one in that crowd was wrong. That was the problem." (p40) — read twice, good.
- "Your own claim isn't settled." / "No. That is why it counts." (p46) — twice, then it clicked, and it's the best exchange in the issue.
- "He did not build the answer." (p51) — fine once.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Leona and Mera — distinct, excellent. Leona's stillness costing her vs Mera's stillness as armor is clear on the page.
- Mara — distinct immediately (finger-counting, "Tell me about Tuesday").
- Frank and Elias — I swapped them twice during the negotiation. "Lean, angular, ink-stained" vs "tall, soft-spoken, silver temples" — the script tries, but in dialogue their cadence is similar and I kept checking.
- Frank Reed vs Tomas Reed — two Reeds in one book, one in Halyard, one in Keel, never acknowledged as related or not. I spent page 43 wondering if it was the miner's brother. Still don't know.

### I felt something
- Page 39, "Yes." — the single-word answer. This is the emotional peak of the book for me.
- Page 41, "You were forty minutes away." — anger and grief in one line.
- Page 49–50, the death and Mera's confession — the book's argument (systems vs people) finally has a body attached.
- Page 54, the empty chair and "That was the point." — quiet, earned.

### I predicted
- Alex declines the override — predicted the instant it was offered (p51), maybe earlier.
- Mera's "the override was mine" — I predicted she'd confess something, though the detail (Alpha ignored her) surprised me and is better than what I guessed.
- The debt gets voided — predicted from page 2, satisfied on page 54.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whether people can be trusted to govern themselves without one person — or one machine — holding the override. (Third different sentence. The book is really three books: water theft, ownership, governance. They connect through the dad, but I noticed the seams.)

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue — it's the strongest of the three. Slowest point is page 43, but by then I was invested.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Last page of the book. The final caption — "Can people accept responsibility for a future no system can guarantee?" — is the book asking me its essay question out loud, and I rolled my eyes a little. But the image before it (the road down, the full conduit, "That's the job now") is a good ending. If there were a next book I'd buy it on the strength of Leona and Mera, not on the caption.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 20. The Registry. Unanswered questions stacked on each other ("Three hundred and twelve?" / "I didn't know I was one."), jargon doing push-ups, nothing human in the panel. Half the class would be on their phones by the bottom of that page, and page 19's "From the train" would have already put the pedants on edge.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a dying mining town inherits his dad's massive debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds his dad's secret robot and workshop, and ends up taking down the system that controls water and credit across three cities — and it turns out the dad built a test to make sure no one person, including his own son, ever gets to run it. Also the robot dog is great.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: Angry, warm, and satisfying — the scanner readout and the 62/18 reveal are the book at its best.
- Issue 02: A long meeting about intellectual property rescued at the last second by a photograph of Ada's mother.
- Issue 03: The payoff — the mother scene and Mera's confession are worth the whole read, even though the ending explains its own theme to me.
