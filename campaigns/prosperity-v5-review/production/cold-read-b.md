# Cold Read B — EVOKE PROSPERITY

One version, read once, in order, late at night, wanting to be done with it. The title meant nothing to me before I started and still means nothing now — nothing in the book ever says it.

---

## Issue 01 — The Inheritance

### Pulled me forward
Page 2, the checkpoint. The scan dumping "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS / TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED" on a seventeen-year-old, and the guard's "Happy birthday, Phantum." That's the moment the book stopped being homework. I understood the whole world from one screen. The supervisor quietly re-flagging the repair as ROUTINE MAINTENANCE (page 2, panel 4) got me too — one small decent person. From page 5 onward (the book, the gap in the shelf, the hidden room, the robot, the father's hologram) I was genuinely turning pages.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 3–4, the railing. Two pages of two people sitting with mugs trading lines about looking up and looking down. It's doing relationship work, I know, but after the checkpoint it felt like the book parked itself. I skimmed the captions on page 1 — "never escaped the gravity of inequality" made me groan out loud. It smells like the poster in the classroom where this book gets assigned.

### I was confused
Page 11, "Just checking." Alex presses the map node and a pulse goes up the real pipes. Did he mean to do that? Did Bi11bot let him? Page 12 tells me it alerted the watcher, so I got it backwards, but in the moment I couldn't tell if it was an accident, a test, or a feature.

### Things I was expected to already know
- The white-and-amber node. It's on page 1, on the scanner, on the terminal, treated like I know what it is. Never explained in this issue.
- "Alpha." Page 12, screen text: REPORT TO ALPHA. No idea who or what Alpha is at that point. A person? A company? I guessed AI and was right, but the book made me guess.
- The surname "Phantum." I read it as "Phantom" every single time and snorted the first time. It reads like a placeholder nobody replaced.

### Lines I read twice
"Nobody stays either. Not really." (page 3). Took me two reads to get "people leave on the inside." It's fine, but it's the kind of line this book reaches for a lot.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandmother, the supervisor, the bouncer — all distinct immediately. No confusion.

### I felt something
The checkpoint — actual cold anger. And "Your father always believed the next hand would change everything." / "It did. Just not for him." (page 2) is a good enough line that I forgave the mug scene.

### I predicted
The moment the shelf was described with "one book-width gap at eye height" (page 5) I knew the book was a key. Knew the shape under the sheet was a robot. Knew Adrian wasn't a gambler — the grandmother's "crazy stories" plus Ada's "He wouldn't just leave" made that obvious by page 4. Did not predict the woman watching on page 12.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid trapped by inherited debt at the bottom of a rigged three-tier city finds his father's secret room and a way up.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 3. The checkpoint bought the book credit, and the railing spent it.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Not because of the woman — she's a stranger with a glass of water, atmosphere more than a question. I need it because I want to know what's under the sealed mine.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 3; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 — The Price of Departure

### Pulled me forward
The whole back half. From the cable snap (page 6) through Tomas, the sweating door, the chamber with the two pipes, to "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%" on every screen (page 12) — that's the best stretch in the book so far. I stopped skimming entirely around page 8 and didn't start again. "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." (page 8) is exactly my kind of scary. And the choice on page 11 — map your escape or send the truth and get caught — was laid out clean enough that I felt the weight of it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 3–4. Three rotating artifacts with caption labels felt like a video game inventory screen, and then page 4 gives me "A = K + ρU" floating in a hologram. I skipped the equation entirely — decorative math. "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap" is where the book starts talking in thesis, and my eyes slid.

### I was confused
The 52-dot panel (page 5). Medicine 30, token 50, food 10, pile of 52 — I stopped and did arithmetic on a Tuesday night. I got it (medicine first, earn the rest), but it cost me a full stop. Also page 14: "the credential Tomas gave you." Tomas gave him a key (page 8). The token came from the transit worker via Ada's network (page 13). So what is "the credential"? The key? I never found out.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "B1." Tomas says it (page 7) like the name's been established. I worked out it's short for Bi11bot, but the instant familiarity is doing work the page didn't do.
- "Charter House towers" (page 15) — named like I know them. I don't.
- Alpha itself gets properly explained on page 2, one issue after the book started using the word. Fine, but noted.

### Lines I read twice
"None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." (page 3) — the second sentence took two passes. "A leap can't be forced. And it can't be only for yourself." (page 4) — same. These are the rules of the whole quest and they're phrased like riddles.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Tomas (warm, limping, decent) and Foreman-7 (rules with optics) — clear. The rest of the miners are a crowd, which is fine.

### I felt something
The 62/18 reveal — genuine satisfaction, the kind where you sit up. Tomas's "Today? Another small act of resistance." (page 8). And Grandmother's "Your father said that once." (page 5) — quiet, ominous, perfect.

### I predicted
Knew Alex would send the truth instead of saving his exit — the book had already shown me who he is when he crossed the yellow line for Tomas. Knew Tomas would stay behind. Did not predict the follower on the train.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The system runs on a measurable lie, and proving it costs you your escape route.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 4. The equation panel is where the book briefly becomes the lesson it was assigned to be.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, properly. "THE BOY HAS THE MEASURE. / FOLLOW. DO NOT TAKE IT YET." — there's a person on the train with them. That's a real cliffhanger, not a loud noise.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 4; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 — The Price of Ownership

### Pulled me forward
Lock. "Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value" (page 3) is a great villain line — calm, true, and rotten. The all-nighter build (pages 12–13) moved fast and I liked that Alex's genius is his hands, not his brain — the rationing kid thinking smaller than the space engineer ("Took someone who grew up rationing to think of it," page 13). And Lock's counter on page 15 — "By evening, Aqualis will announce their own version... Your design freed itself. It didn't defeat us. There's a difference, and you'll learn it" — genuinely got me. I did not see the win shrinking like that.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 5–8 are the same scene four times: meet a collaborator, get a shard, hear a one-line philosophy, leave. By Suri I'd seen the pattern; by Jalen I was skimming for the shard handoff. Ren rescued it by being funny.

### I was confused
Page 4: four portraits, but five patent pieces. I flagged it and it resolved on page 8 (Ren has two), but for four pages it itched. Bigger: Issue 02 ended with three circled places on the map — "Which one holds the Seal?" — and this issue never visits a single one of them. Lina just produces the Seal from her uniform (page 14). So the whole train-scene setup was a feint the book itself forgot about. I noticed.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Charter House review." A court? A board? Who sits on it? Lock is "Director" — of what? Never said, all book.
- The Seal's rules from Issue 02 ("wakes for what they choose") — needed them here, had them, fine.

### Lines I read twice
"So was the idea that built it. That's probably the point." (page 13) — fortune cookie, read it twice, still not sure it means anything.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lina (sharp, dry) and Ren (blunt, funny) — vivid. Suri and Jalen blur into each other: the two quiet middle ones, one nervous, one gentle, and by the review scene I had to check which was which.

### I felt something
The cost triptych (page 15): nobody arrested, nobody fired, just watched, demoted, cut. "Halyard rarely needs to go that far." That felt true in a way the speeches don't. And Lina's "I've been waiting fifteen years to stop carrying it" (page 14).

### I predicted
Knew Alex wouldn't sign. Knew the Seal would wake for the five names and not for him alone — the book telegraphed its rule. Did not predict Lock's counter-move.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Whoever owns the ideas owns the future, and giving an idea away is the only move ownership can't absorb.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 7 — the third repetition of the shard-collection scene, where the formula showed.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but softer. Mera Vale finally has a name and a band, and "He'll want the third from me directly" sets up Oasis. It's mythology-pull, not danger-pull. The glass set down crooked was a nice touch — I've been noticing that glass since Issue 01.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 7; next issue: yes

## Issue 04 — The Broken Covenant

### Pulled me forward
The mother-daughter scene, pages 3–5 and 11. "I don't want tea, Leona." "Did you know where I was?" "...Yes. Within a year, yes." "You chose not to come back." / "I chose to keep you safe." / "Those aren't the same thing." This is the best two-person scene in the book — I read it slower than anything since the water reveal, on purpose. And the reveal that Leona was the deciding vote that handed Alpha its emergency power (page 7) recontextualized the whole series in one line. I put the book down for a second there, in the good way.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 8–10. The sepia flashback re-tells what Leona already told us on page 7, and then pages 9–10 are three people in a room agreeing with each other in complete sentences. "It wasn't humility. It was fear wearing humility's clothes" is a good line, but it's surrounded by a lot of thesis. I skimmed page 10's speeches and just waited for the bands to link.

### I was confused
Page 1: the gate flashes "VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED." Ada's surname is Veyra? She's been "Ada" for three issues. I had to flip back to confirm this was the first time her surname appeared. It is. The book held that card so long the reveal played as a typo for a second. Also: how did the three of them get to Oasis at all? Issue 03 ended in Halyard with the debt still live; Issue 04 opens with them stepping off a transit platform in the city that requires residency clearance. The travel problem that drove two whole issues dissolved off-panel.

### Things I was expected to already know
- That Ada had a missing mother at all. Issue 01–03 never mention her family once. "You said she disappeared" (page 2) — she did not say. Not to us. This is the biggest "expected to already know" in the book: the emotional core of the issue is a backstory the reader never received.
- "The wars" get referenced again with new details (the shortage, the broken agreement) as if established; the actual Water Wars have never been shown until page 8, and by then they're backstory to backstory.

### Lines I read twice
"Not too few hands holding it. Too few voices it actually answers to." (page 10) — I had to re-read to catch that Ada means the problem is the second one, not the first. The sentence fights itself on first pass.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona and Mera are beautifully distinct — one all careful smallness, one all stillness. No confusion. Alex is deliberately sidelined and the book knows it ("Guess I'm just along for this one," page 1) — I appreciated that it said so.

### I felt something
The toy at the bottom of the box (page 4) — "You kept this." That got me more than any speech. And "Don't tell me. Show me." (page 11) is the right answer to an apology and I believed Ada saying it.

### I predicted
Knew Mera's band would stay dark — "Two of three. Again." was visible from miles off. Knew Bi11bot was carrying Adrian's band the moment the compartment opened; the compartment was established in Issue 02 and I remembered it.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The people who built the cage also built the key, and their kids have to decide what forgiveness costs.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 9 — the third consecutive page of parlor philosophy, where I checked how many pages were left.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but on momentum, not hook. The crooked glass she doesn't straighten is a good character beat, but "END OF Issue 04" arrives with no question I need answered — Mera already said "Soon. Not here," so I know Issue 05 is the Adrian download. I'm reading on because I'm four issues in, not because the page pulled me.

VERDICT Issue 04: put down: page 9; next issue: yes

## Issue 05 — The Last Bet

### Pulled me forward
The death scene, page 6. The color draining panel by panel, the water rising, "He had spent his whole life putting a number on everything. This was the one moment he didn't try." — and then the amber eyes opening beside him, which I realized was Issue 01 page 8 replayed from the other side. That's the best single page in the book. I went back and checked Issue 01 to confirm. It lines up. Page 7's "He just looked at me the way you look at something you're betting on" and "It's been on us the whole time" — Bi11bot finally cracking — landed hard.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 3 and 5 are Mera narrating the stages of Adrian's destruction twice — once as archival insets, once as her confession. Both are good; together they're the same information at two temperatures. I skimmed the second pass. Page 8 is another "Mera says a true thing about herself" scene, and by then the issue had already made its point.

### I was confused
Page 4: "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." Dozens? We saw it once. And "since Issue 01" — the robot is referencing the comic's own issue numbering? I read that balloon three times. It broke the world for me. Also the timeline: Adrian sealed in a flooding room with "less than an hour left" (page 4) — but he had time to build Bi11bot, record the hologram, hide three artifacts across two cities, set up Tomas's door, and brief a grandmother's book trick? When? The book never says, and the more it shows me the death, the less the prep fits.

### Things I was expected to already know
- That Mera and Adrian were close enough that his death is "someone taken from her" (page 3). The book told me they built the Covenant together, but "took someone from me" implies something the pages never showed.
- The projector being "the same unit" (page 4) — fine, but the reveal depends on me recognizing a shoulder-mounted projector design I saw once, small, three issues ago.

### Lines I read twice
"What he found instead was a reason to stop looking for one and start becoming the other." (page 9 caption) — "one" and "other" doing too much work; took two passes to parse "stop looking for a father, start becoming one." Wait — becoming a father? Becoming his father? Becoming the reason? I still don't know, and it's the caption the issue ends its emotional arc on.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
No problem. Four people in a room, all distinct.

### I felt something
"He was a father who left. And he was someone who gave everything he had to a bet he couldn't see the end of." (page 9). And Alex's "I always thought that message came from somewhere safe" (page 4). This issue earns its sentiment more than the others because it finally shows instead of tells.

### I predicted
Knew Adrian died by drowning in that room from the moment the corridor had no Alpha nodes — the book's "abandoned mines don't sweat" logic taught me to read water where it shouldn't be. Knew Mera's override failed before she said it.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The father didn't lose a bet, he placed one — on his son — and the system that killed him was built by a woman who couldn't stop her own machine.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere, honestly. This is the issue where the book trusts itself. If I'd made it this far, nothing here loses me.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but it's the quietest hook yet — Mera standing alone in the room, straightening nothing. No cliffhanger at all. I want the next issue because I want to see what Alex does with three artifacts and a choice, not because the page demanded it.

VERDICT Issue 05: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 06 — The Fourth Choice

### Pulled me forward
Page 5, Ada refusing the co-pilot seat: "That's still two people deciding for everyone else. You just made the circle one person bigger." That's the moment the finale surprised me — I expected the duo to share power and the book to call it growth. Page 8's screen — "EXISTING ALLOCATIONS, OWNERSHIP RECORDS AND AGREEMENTS: UNCHANGED PENDING HUMAN PROCESS" — is the bravest beat in the issue: the machine says nothing is fixed, and the book lets a citizen say "That's it?" out loud. And page 13, the debt board: "PHANTUM, ALEX / DEBT BALANCE: 402,110 CREDITS." Still there. Smaller. Honest. I respected that enormously.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 6–7. Alex re-walks the three artifacts one at a time — "It reveals without deciding. It recognizes without controlling. It coordinates without ruling." — and I could hear the study guide being written. Then page 7 is one long speech that turns the artifacts into a constitution. I understood it and I still skimmed it, because it's the book explaining its own metaphor to me for the second time. Pages 9–11 (three cities reading screens, Leona arguing, Mera sitting among people) are good images but they're epilogue pace, and I read them at epilogue speed.

### I was confused
The mechanics of the ending. Alpha "rescinds emergency authority" and registers a "distributed verification protocol" because... Alex gave a speech near three glowing objects? What did the artifacts actually do? Did Alpha agree? Can it disagree? The screen says conditions were "satisfied" — by proximity and a designation — but Alex refused the designation and it worked anyway. I couldn't tell you what rule made the ending happen, and I've been tracking the rules since Issue 02. Also: Mera had "standing" to designate — where did that standing go? Did it dissolve? The book doesn't say.

### Things I was expected to already know
- That the three artifacts in proximity would trigger a "restoration protocol" at all. Page 2's screen is the first mention that this was ever a possibility. Adrian built a constitutional dead-man switch and nobody — not Bi11bot, who knew everything else — mentioned it in five issues. It reads as the ending inventing its own trigger.
- "B11 witness record" (page 7) — Bi11bot is suddenly B11 again, and a "witness record" is a new capability introduced in the final act's key sentence.

### Lines I read twice
"Certainty is confidence with poor manners" (Issue 05, but I re-read it here when Mera's certainty theme paid off). In this issue: "I just made sure nobody can keep it hidden, or keep it for themselves, without everyone seeing them do it." (page 8) — long sentence, two passes, worth it.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Everyone, easily — the cast had thinned back to the core. The named citizens are sketched fast but fine.

### I felt something
Grandmother holding out the cloth — "I don't need to keep this moving anymore. I think you finally are." (page 13). That's the payoff of a prop planted on page 5 of Issue 01, and it worked on me completely. And the final splash's bottom caption — "everyone could see exactly how far there still was to go" — is the first caption in the book I didn't groan at, because the book earned the modesty.

### I predicted
Knew Alex would refuse sole authority — the book had been building the refusal since Issue 02's "none wakes for whoever's holding it." Did not predict Ada refusing the shared version; that's the one genuine surprise in the finale. Predicted the debt would remain; glad to be right.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Power should work like the three objects: show the truth, name the workers, make promises visible — and never let one hand hold the answer.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 6 — the start of the artifact re-explanation, where the finale briefly becomes a recap of its own symbols.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
This is the last issue, so: would I buy the next book? Yes — but qualified. The mirror splash page is satisfying, the raised gate and the full conduit pay off page 1 of Issue 01 cleanly, and I'd read more of these people. But the ending's engine is a speech, and I know some readers who will feel the last issue talked its way across the finish line.

VERDICT Issue 06: put down: page 6; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Issue 06, page 7. One panel of a speech that converts three magic objects into a system of government, in a room full of people silently watching a teenager talk. Half the class would be gone by the second balloon — not because they disagree, but because it's the book doing the worksheet out loud. Runner-up: Issue 02, page 4, the floating equation.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a debt-trap mining city finds out his deadbeat dad was actually a rebel engineer, collects his three hidden inventions one city at a time, and when the all-powerful water computer finally offers him control of everything, he refuses to take it alone — and the refusal is the point. Also his best friend's mom turns out to be one of the three people who broke the world.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: The checkpoint screen is the whole world in one beep — best cold open in the book.
- Issue 02: The 62/18 reveal is the moment the book proves it can do plot, not just theme.
- Issue 03: Won the battle, lost the war to Lock's shrug — the most grown-up ending of the six.
- Issue 04: "You chose not to come back" / "I chose to keep you safe" / "Those aren't the same thing" — the scene I'll remember.
- Issue 05: The color draining out of the room as the water rises — the best page in the book, full stop.
- Issue 06: The debt is still on the board at the end, smaller — the book kept its promise not to lie to me.
