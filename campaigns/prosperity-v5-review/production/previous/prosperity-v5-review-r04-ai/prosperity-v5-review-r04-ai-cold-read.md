# Cold Read: EVOKE PROSPERITY (all six issues, 81 pages)

I read this the way the reader file describes: 16, assigned, reading it the night before, and expecting AI slop so I could say so in class. I read every issue in order, once, and gave each one the same attention. Where I started skimming, I say so.

---

## Issue 01: The Inheritance (pages 1–12)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1.** The splash is fine. A rich city on top and a poor one on the bottom is a poster I've seen before, but the captions are short and I got the idea in one look. It didn't hook me yet.
- **Page 2, panel 2: the debt display.** This is where the book got me. "DEBT TRANSFER DAY," then his birthday, then 418,607 credits against 40 earned today, then 28.7 YEARS. The line "Happy birthday, Phantum" / "I've had better surprises" works. I understood the whole world from one screen. Best panel in the issue.
- **Page 2, panel 3.** The bouncer line "luck runs in the family" tells me the dad was a gambler without anyone explaining it. Good.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The supervisor quietly clicking ROUTINE MAINTENANCE and muttering "...Works." I liked that someone in this place is decent and doesn't make a speech about it.
- **Pages 5–6.** Grandma saying "He's closer than you think," then the book in the gap, CLICK, RUMBLE, and the bookshelf opens. It's an old trick but it's a fun one, and it's fast. Grandma still folding her cloth in the background made me laugh.
- **Page 8, panel 5.** "...Well. That took longer than expected." The robot's first line is funny. It got a reaction out of me.
- **Page 9, panel 5.** "Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't." / "...I thought about it." Good exchange.
- **Page 10.** The hologram dad works, and the grandma "I heard that" joke works. Unfolding the photo at the end of page 10 got me a bit.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 3–4, the overlook with Ada.** I skimmed most of page 3. "You're officially old" / "I'm officially broke" and then the "two dreams" panel where one looks up and one looks down. It reads like a scene I've watched in ten movies. I picked back up at page 4, panel 3 ("That he wasn't gambling. That he was working.").
- **Page 11, panel 4.** "Brilliant... or completely mad" is a cliché. I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 12.** I followed the pulse going up the pipe. But who is the woman, what is "B1 NODE," what is "LEGACY PROTOCOL," and what is "Alpha"? It was confused-but-intrigued. Her squaring the water glass told me "control freak" without words, and I liked that.
- **Page 7.** The robot is "child-sized" under a sheet and there's a key next to it. Fine. But what is the chip actually for? It just matches a chalkboard. I didn't know if that mattered.
- **Page 10.** "Bi11bot has a map." Is it Bill-bot? Bi-eleven-bot? I didn't know how to say the name in my head for the whole book.

| Debt transfer at 17 | I1 p2 | Yes, the caption explains it |

### Lines I read twice
- I1 p2, panel 5 caption: "Everyone in Keel knew where they had been born. / Few believed they could choose where they belonged." Fine, just generic.
- I1 p3, panel 6: "Nobody stays either. Not really." I didn't know what she meant.
- I1 p11, panel 3: "Sealed isn't the same as gone." *(Note to self: this is the first of a lot of "X isn't the same as Y" lines in this book.)*

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandma, Bi11bot, the supervisor, the bouncer: all clear. The woman on page 12 is just "the woman," which is fine for now.

### I felt something
Page 2, panel 2 (the debt screen) made me a bit angry on his behalf. Page 10, panel 3 (Grandma crying in the doorway) got me a little.

### I predicted
Dad isn't a gambler, he was hiding something. The robot leads to a quest. Ada knows more than she's saying (p4, panel 6, "He wouldn't just leave"). The woman on page 12 is the main villain.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A broke kid in the bottom city finds his "deadbeat" dad's secret lab and a robot, and goes on a treasure hunt to get out of debt.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue. I almost skimmed out at page 3, and then the bookshelf opened.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Mostly yes. The cold ring closing around the one warm dot is a real image: someone powerful has noticed Alex, and he doesn't know. I don't know her, so the threat lands because I know *him*, not because of her. I'd read on tonight.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02: The Price of Departure (pages 13–27)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1 (I2).** "He hasn't slept. Or eaten." / "That sounds like him." Short and nice.
- **Page 2, panels 3–5: Alpha.** "Then, in the emergency, it was asked to decide." / "Temporarily." That small balloon is good, I got the joke straight away. "Safer for whom?" is good too.
- **Page 4, panels 6–7.** The mine entrance gets welded shut and his shift gets moved to Site 6, both shown silently. Then on page 5 "Every door closed but one." I liked that I put it together before Ada said it.
- **Pages 6–8: Site 6 and Tomas.** This is the best stretch in the issue. The cable snap, the wage-forfeit notice on his wrist when he goes to help (−40 CR), "Your plan needed you forgettable" / "Then we need a different plan." "You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you." Tomas hitting the lever and yelling "Wrong button! My fault!" I liked Tomas a lot.
- **Page 8, panel 6.** "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." Good.
- **Page 9.** The bolts going left. Ada's page actually mattering was satisfying.
- **Page 10.** Two pipes, one dry, and a gauge that says Keel gets most of the water. "Nobody checks what everybody trusts." I got it with no explanation needed.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** REPORTED 62%, DELIVERED 18%, on every screen at once. That was a real moment.
- **Page 13, panel 1.** The miners in the tunnel behind the Foreman. Yes, it's corny, and it still worked on me.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3 (I2): the three safeguards.** This is where I started to tune out. Three objects, each with a motto caption: "Truth made visible." "Ownership joined to responsibility." "Trust strong enough to move value between people." Then "None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." That's a textbook glossary. I skimmed every caption on this page.
- **Page 4, panel 3: the equation.** "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap." I didn't follow it and I didn't try.
- **Page 5, panel 5: the 52 glowing dots.** Medicine 30, food and lamp 10, token 50. I had to stop and do maths homework inside my homework. I still don't know what the token is for, or whether he ever paid for it.
- **Page 11: SENSE / COMPARE / TRACE / VERIFY.** Four capital-letter words. I skimmed the explanations and just followed the countdown and the bulkheads closing.
- **Page 14.** The countdown makes it move fast, but it's mostly packing.

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 4.** Ada: "Everything people need to survive." Bi11bot: "And nearly everything they need to choose their own future." I wasn't sure what that exchange meant.
- **Page 3, panel 6.** "Only for what they choose." What does that mean? I didn't find out until the Seal in Issue 03, and even then only sort of.
- **Page 5, panel 1.** "Kitchen table with the map, credential and schedule." What credential? Then page 14: "the credential Tomas gave you." Tomas gave him a *key*. The transit worker gave him the token. Was I supposed to know about a credential?
- **Page 5, panel 1.** "Measure first. Then the train to Halyard." How does he know the Measure is in the mine? Nobody said it was.
- **Page 11, panels 4–6.** I got "escape secretly or send the evidence." But "a trace out through the old tunnels" is a weird phrase for "map an escape route." Also: if he sends it and gets caught, how does Bi11bot hide the robot?
- **Foreman-7.** Robot or person? "Optics" suggests robot. The book never says. "Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense." What's a household offense?
- **Page 13, panel 6 and page 14, panel 1.** "Sixty minutes before Alpha notices," then suddenly a 22-minute countdown. Lost track of the clock.
- **Page 13, panel 5.** "One person can't buy freedom here. A city can still open a window." A neighbour is handing a bag to Ada at Alex's door. When did the whole city get organised? It happens offstage within minutes.

## Issue 03: The Price of Ownership (pages 28–43)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 4.** The engineer gets 14 credits and the House keeps her invention. "She keeps the experience. They keep everything else." I understood Halyard right away. Good.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** 312 PATENTS. A real jolt: the dad who left him debt also left him 312 patents.
- **Page 3: Director Lock.** I liked Lock. He's polite and never threatens anyone out loud. "Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value." "Take the day. Halyard isn't going anywhere." Creepy in a good way.
- **Page 9, panels 4–5.** Alex testing the connectors by feel, like the pump in Issue 01, and then "Well now. There you are." / "...What?" / "Nothing. Carry on." The best Bi11bot moment in the book. The kid has a skill, and the payoff goes back to page 2 of Issue 01.
- **Page 10.** Lock reciting all four surnames. Proper villain move. The 07:14:00 countdown appearing on its own was a nice creepy touch.
- **Page 15, panels 2–3.** "Your design freed itself. It didn't defeat us." And "Nobody was arrested. Nobody was fired. Halyard rarely needs to go that far." The only ending in the book that admitted winning cost something. I believed it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 5–8: the four collaborators.** It's a fetch quest. Go to a building, meet someone grumpy, they make a speech about Adrian, they hand over a shard, next building. Four times in a row. By Jalen (p7) I was skimming. Ren (p8) woke me up a little because she's the only one with any edge.
- **Page 12.** The argument round. Everyone gets one line of position. Skimmed.
- **Page 13.** Inventing things in a montage: fold the membrane, take turns, recycle the heat. It's neat that Alex solves each one, but the book is grading him. Every adult says some version of "that's not in our patents / your father never thought that small."
- **Page 14, panel 5 caption.** "It hadn't woken for a signature, or a claim, or a name on a patent. It had woken for five names, spoken aloud, by someone who didn't need to be the only one who mattered." Too long, and it explains the magic rule to me.

### I was confused
- **The Seal.** Lina had it the *whole time*? Then why did we need three nights of building? Does the medallion do anything except glow? After it wakes up, nothing changes in Halyard.
- **The four names.** Lock says "Dr. Vey. Ms. Anik. Mr. Orr. Ms. Okafor." I only knew them as Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren, and I had to work out who was who. Later, on page 14, Alex says them all again.
- **Page 5, panel 5.** Lina: "Try keeping up this time." This time? She'd just met him.
- **Page 7 vs page 12.** Jalen says "I'm not seeing you again," and then he's in the room on page 12 with no beat about it.
- **Page 10, panel 1.** Lina was at the build on page 9, then "Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren are gone for the night." Suri and Jalen were never at the build that I saw.
- **Page 16, panel 3: "The boy on the train had never belonged to a House. He had always belonged to her."** I read this three times. The message on the train said "THE BOY HAS THE MEASURE," and that boy is Alex. So I thought this caption meant *Alex* belonged to her. Is Alex secretly hers? Then I worked out it probably means the spy. Confused and detached.
- **Page 16, panel 5.** "He'll want the third from me directly." But the Covenant comes from Leona and Bi11bot. So what did she mean?
- **The Charter House review.** What does it actually decide? Then Alex says no and there's no ruling. The review just ends.

| "Contribution kiosk" | I3 p1 | Yes |
| Intellectual estate | I3 p2 | Yes, Ada explains |
| Charter House review | I3 p2 | Barely |

### Lines I read twice
- p3: "Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value." (I liked this one, though.)
- p8: "He wasn't building a machine. He was building an argument."
- p13: "So was the idea that built it. That's probably the point." Someone literally saying "that's probably the point."
- p16: "The boy on the train had never belonged to a House. He had always belonged to her." Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lock: very clear. Ren: clear (loud, welder). Jalen: clear (scared). **Lina and Suri blurred together**: both quiet, careful science women who say something wise about Adrian. Without the surnames page I'd have lost them completely. Ada is now mostly asking the question the reader would ask ("In exchange for what?" "Why didn't you leave?"). She's a narrator, not a person, this issue.

### I felt something
Bi11bot's "There you are." Lock's last line on the steps. The cost triptych on page 15. That's about it. I didn't feel much when the Seal glowed.

### I predicted
Lock works for Mera. *(Sort of right?)* Ada's backstory is coming. Lina would turn out to be holding the Seal. *(Right; I guessed it by page 5.)*

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid learns that sharing an invention beats selling it to a corporation, while the AI's boss watches from the top floor.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 7** (Jalen), the third "go meet a collaborator" scene in a row. Lock showing up on page 10 would have pulled me back, if I'd got there.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, mostly because the control-room woman finally has a name and a line. "Two found. He'll want the third from me directly" makes it sound like he's going to meet the villain. That's a real question. But that caption on page 16 almost lost me.

## Issue 04: The Broken Covenant (pages 44–57)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 4.** The gate opens for Ada instantly: "VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND — STATUS: UNRESOLVED." Best hook since the debt screen. I sat up.
- **Page 2.** Her mother is alive, in Oasis, with a job. Good twist. I didn't see it coming.
- **Page 3.** "I don't want tea, Leona." Calling her by her first name instead of "Mother" was sharp.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** The child's toy at the bottom of the box. That got me.
- **Page 5.** "Did you know where I was?" / "...Yes." This is the best scene in the book. It's short and blunt, and the mother actually admits it. "You chose not to come back." / "I chose to keep you safe." / "Those aren't the same thing." I felt that one.
- **Page 7, panel 3.** The deciding vote. So the whole AI dictatorship started because a mom wanted her kid back faster. That's a real reason, it's human, and I believed it.
- **Page 13, panel 5.** "Two of three. Again." The best Mera line. Short, and it lands.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 6, panel 4 and page 9.** Mera and Leona trading speeches: "Certainty kept this city standing." / "Certainty kept me from ever asking whether it still needed to." / "fear wearing humility's clothes." / "Safety isn't the same as agency." This is where it started to sound like a debate assignment written by the same person on both sides. Skimmed page 9.
- **Page 10.** Ada's Covenant proposal. I got the gist ("the people it affects should see the promise") and skimmed the rest. The caption "It was the first time anyone in the room had proposed fixing something instead of explaining why it broke" is the book telling me how to feel about her.
- **Page 11.** "Don't tell me. Show me." Seen it before.

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 3.** "You said she disappeared." *When?* Ada never told us anything about her mom in Issues 01–03. I didn't know Ada had a missing mother. It's mentioned once, here, as if I already knew, so the reveal is weaker than it should be.
- **The Covenant.** In Issue 02 it was three linked bands. Here, Leona has one, Bi11bot has had Dad's the whole time (why didn't he say?), and Mera has the third, which stays dark. So is the Covenant found or not? Bi11bot said "Two found. One remains" in Issue 03, and now it's two of three bands.
- **Page 7.** "Alpha could take emergency authority, but only if two of the three of us agreed." So the Covenant is *how* Alpha got power? And it's also a safeguard *against* Alpha? I didn't get how one thing is both.
- **Page 6, panel 2.** "I felt the record search this morning." Felt it?
- **Page 14, panel 5.** Mera's crooked glass. I noticed the glass gag every time, and this time I didn't know what it was telling me.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Ada's missing mother | I4 p2 ("You said she disappeared") | Never set up before |
| Ada's surname, Veyra | I4 p1 | Here, for the first time |
| Ada is from Oasis | I4 p1 | Yes, surprise |
| The civic council | I4 p13 | Sort of |
| The vote mechanism | I4 p7 | Explained here, late |

### Lines I read twice
- p6: "I am the reason there was still a city for your daughter to be found in."
- p9: "It wasn't humility. It was fear wearing humility's clothes."
- p13: "Your father built it to be difficult to cheat. He didn't build it to be difficult to use."
- p14: "Your father built something that worked. I built something that lasted. I'm beginning to understand those were never the same thing." *(Another "not the same thing." By now I was counting.)*

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Ada finally becomes a person this issue. Leona is clear. Mera is clear in the panels, but **in the arguments Mera, Leona and Bi11bot all talk the same way**: the same short, balanced, quotable sentences. Cover the names and I couldn't tell you who said "Certainty kept this city standing." Alex standing in the hallway is nice restraint, but he does basically nothing for 11 pages.

### I felt something
Yes. Pages 4–5 and page 8, panel 5 ("I traded a few terrible days I could see for fifteen years I couldn't"). This is the issue I'd defend in class.

### I predicted
Mera killed Adrian, or let it happen. Mera's band will matter in the finale. *(It doesn't, really.)*

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
It's turned into a book about parents who made bad choices "to keep you safe," and kids deciding whether to forgive them.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 9.** That's where the speeches started outweighing the people. I'd have kept going if I'd been reading at a normal pace, because pages 4–5 bought a lot of goodwill.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. "Will you tell me what happened to him?" / "Soon. Not here." That's a real question about someone I care about (Alex), and the answer is the dad mystery from page 2 of Issue 01. The control-room panel after it is just mood.

## Issue 05: The Last Bet (pages 58–67)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 4.** A corridor with no Alpha nodes. "I came down here fifteen years ago, and I never finished what I came to do." Creepy and good.
- **Pages 3 and 5.** Surveillance, then money frozen, then public liability, then pursuit. "Alpha didn't need to call him dangerous. It only needed to call him a risk. People stop helping a risk." That's the scariest idea in the book, and it reads like something that could actually happen.
- **Page 5, panels 3–4.** OVERRIDE DENIED. "I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing." Good.
- **Page 9, panel 3.** "He doesn't have to be only one thing for me to be allowed to miss him." That one actually hit me. Of all of Alex's lines in the book, it's the one that sounded true.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 7, panel 4 and page 8.** Bi11bot: "Fifteen years, Alex. It's been on us the whole time." Mera: "I preserved order." (long beat) Then more. Everyone takes a turn confessing in a dusty room. Skimmed.
- **Page 9, panels 4–5.** "I think what I actually inherited was a decision he made, not an answer he left." Then the caption "...start becoming the other." The book is writing my essay for me.
- **Page 10.** The lift goes up, Mera stands alone. Nothing happens on this page.

### I was confused
- **Page 4, panel 3. The robot says "since Issue 01."** "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." It's a speech balloon. A character in the story knows he's in a comic with issue numbers. That pulled me straight out, and it's exactly what my class would screenshot as proof a machine wrote this. It's worse because Alex then says "you've played me a hundred times," when I saw it played once.
- **Page 1, panel 3 vs everything.** Bi11bot: "I haven't been this deep since before you were born." But Alex was *two* when his dad vanished, and Bi11bot woke up at the moment his dad died (page 6). So when was Bi11bot ever down here before Alex was born?
- **Page 6, panel 5: the biggest confusion in the book.** Bi11bot "opens its eyes for the first time" beside Adrian in this flooding station in Oasis, "the exact amber ignition from Issue 01, Page 8, now revealed as this same instant." But in Issue 01, Bi11bot was in a workshop in *Keel*, behind Grandma's bookshelf, and *Alex* woke him with a key fifteen years later. Both can't be the first time. How did a robot in Oasis end up under a sheet in Keel? I gave up trying.
- **Did Adrian die?** Water at his knees, the door sealed, the screen goes grey. Then in the present the room is dry and dusty, with no body and nothing mentioned. Nobody says "he died." I *think* he drowned. I wasn't sure, and this is the answer the whole book was building to.
- **Page 9, panel 2.** "Lina said both were true." Bi11bot said that, in Issue 02. Is Alex misremembering, or is the book?
- **Page 4, panel 1.** "It's the same unit. He built mine to match it afterward." Afterward? He died.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| How Bi11bot got from Oasis to Keel | I5 p6 | Never |
| Whether Adrian is dead | I5 p6 | Only implied |
| "System stability intervention" | I5 p5 | Kind of |

### Lines I read twice
- p4: "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." Read it twice, then said "wait, what" out loud.
- p8: "Adrian would have told me certainty is confidence with poor manners."
- p9 caption: "What he found instead was a reason to stop looking for one and start becoming the other." The other what? A father? A father to whom?

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex finally talks like a person on page 9. Mera is clear. Ada stands in the doorway and asks one question; she's basically absent this issue.

### I felt something
Page 9, panel 3. Mera's shaking hands on page 5 a little. The dad's death should have been the biggest feeling in the book, and it wasn't, because I was busy working out how the robot could be in two places.

### I predicted
Mera hands Alex the power at the end. *(Right.)*

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex's dad wasn't a deadbeat. The AI hunted him down, he died making the robot for his son, and Alex has to make peace with it.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 4**, at the "since Issue 01" line. For a reader already hunting for proof it's AI-made, that was the proof.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. Mera standing in a dusty room isn't a question, it's a mood. The dad mystery is answered and all three objects are found, so I knew it was wrapping up and nothing pulled me into Issue 06. I'd only read it because it's assigned.

## Issue 06: The Fourth Choice (pages 68–81)

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2.** Every screen showing "AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY." That's a good image, and I wanted to know who.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** "You're still asking the same question you asked fifteen years ago. Just with my name in it instead of Alpha's." Smart line.
- **Page 5.** Ada: "You just made the circle one person bigger." That's the twist I didn't see coming. She refuses the power even when it's offered to the two of them, and that's in character for her.
- **Page 8, panel 3.** "That's it? Nothing happens?" I laughed, because that's what I was thinking.
- **Page 13, panel 3.** The debt goes from 418,607 to 402,110, still huge. "Nobody promised you a clean slate. Just an honest one." I respected that the book didn't wipe his debt.
- **Page 13, panel 4.** Grandma handing over the cloth. I didn't expect that to get me, and it did.
- **Page 14.** The last splash mirrors page 1. "...but this time, everyone could see exactly how far there still was to go." Fine. Bookends are nice.

### I slowed down / skimmed

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Tomas is back and still himself ("I still outrank you on seniority"). Grandma is still herself. Everyone else, including Ada, Alex, Mera, Leona and Bi11bot, now speaks in the same calm, wise, balanced sentence. In this issue I couldn't tell the voices apart, only the faces.

### I felt something
Grandma and the cloth (p13, panel 4). The debt still being on the board. Nothing during the climax itself.

### I predicted
That he'd refuse the power and spread it out. I called that back in Issue 04 when Ada proposed the Covenant thing. There was no surprise at the climax except Ada's "circle one person bigger."

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Nobody should have all the power, not an AI, not a CEO, not even the hero, and fixing things is slow and boring and everyone's job.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 6.** "Reveals without deciding / recognizes without controlling / coordinates without ruling." That's the moment it stopped being a story and turned into the lesson plan.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
The splash is a satisfying bookend, and a final image of gold lines running the height of the mountain is a nice thing to end on. Would I buy a next book? No. Everyone's arc got closed with a caption explaining what it meant, and nothing is left that I care to find out.

VERDICT Issue 06: put down: page 6; next issue: no

## The whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 05, page 4**, Bi11bot's line "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." Half my class goes in convinced this is AI-written, and a robot saying "Issue 01" in a speech balloon is the screenshot that ends the argument. Ada's "this whole chapter" in Issue 06, page 12 seals it. If it isn't that page, it's **Issue 02, page 3**: the three-safeguards glossary is where the kids who were never going to make it stop reading.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid from the poor city at the bottom of a mountain inherits his gambler dad's debt, then finds out his dad was actually fighting the AI that runs everything. He gets a sarcastic robot and goes up the mountain collecting three magic objects. The first two issues and the mom scene are actually good. By the end it's a civics lesson about sharing power, and everybody talks like a motivational poster."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** The debt-on-your-birthday screen and the bookshelf door got me, against my will.
- **Issue 02:** A lore dump up front, then Tomas and the dry pipe made it worth it.
- **Issue 03:** A fetch quest with a great villain and one great Bi11bot line, ending on a caption I still can't parse.
- **Issue 04:** The best issue: a mother admitting she knew where her kid was, then buried under speeches.
- **Issue 05:** The dad's death should have wrecked me; instead I was working out how the robot was in two cities and why it said "Issue 01."
- **Issue 06:** A climax that's a policy speech, saved a little by Grandma's cloth and the debt that didn't go away.
