# Cold Read: EVOKE PROSPERITY (Issues 01–06)

I read it straight through in one sitting, the night before, because I had to. I opened it expecting it to be AI slop. Below is what actually happened to me, page by page.

---

## Issue 01: The Inheritance

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1.** The picture of three cities stacked on one mountain, with the big pipe getting thinner as it goes down, worked on me before I'd read a word. I got it straight away: rich people on top, poor people at the bottom.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** The debt number appearing over his head on his birthday, the biggest in the line, and everyone looking up without saying anything. This is where the book got me. 418,607 credits and 28.7 years is concrete and it's awful. "Happy birthday, Phantum." / "I've had better surprises." He sounded like a real person.
- **Page 2, panel 4.** The supervisor quietly ticking "ROUTINE MAINTENANCE" and muttering "...Works." I liked him more than anyone else on the page. It was a small kindness and nobody made a speech about it.
- **Pages 5–6.** Grandma, the book, the bookshelf that clicks open. It's a cliché and I didn't care. The grandmother still folding her cloth in the background while a secret door opens is funny and creepy at once.
- **Page 8, panel 5.** "...Well. That took longer than expected." The robot's first line made me laugh. This was the moment I stopped wanting the book to be bad.
- **Page 9, panel 5.** "Your father bet me you'd throw it in a river. I said you wouldn't." / "...I thought about it." This is the best exchange in the issue.
- **Page 10, panel 3.** Grandma in the doorway saying "I heard that" to a hologram. It was funny and it got to me a little.
- **Page 12.** The pulse climbing the pipe all the way to a woman in a white room straightening her glass. That's creepy in a good way. I wanted to know who she was.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 3–4**, the overlook talk with Ada. It's fine, but it's two pages of mugs and sunsets and "Every birthday... I look up there... and wonder what it feels like to wake up somewhere you get to choose your own future." I skimmed the second half of page 4.
- **Page 7.** It's all picture and no words, and panel 4 basically repeats panel 1 (dust sheet, key, amber pulse). I went through it fast.

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 3.** I couldn't tell if the torn poster on the casino wall was his dad. I think it was? Nobody says so.
- **Page 4, panel 6.** "He wouldn't just leave." How does Ada know that? She sounds like she knows something about his father. That works as a hook, but the book never comes back to it.
- **Page 5, panel 4.** "He's closer than you think." I took that as a real clue: dad's alive, or dad is inside the robot. It never turned out to mean anything, as far as I could tell.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** "B1 NODE ACTIVE / LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED / [REPORT TO ALPHA] [TRACK]." I got that the alarm went off. I didn't know what B1 or Alpha was. Confused but intrigued, so it's a hook here.

|---|---|---|
| Keel / Halyard / Oasis | I01 p1 | Yes, from the picture. Fine. |
| "Pre-Alpha pipes" | I01 p7 (in the description; if lettered, it's a word I didn't know) | Not until I02 p2 |
| Alpha | I01 p12 (button) | Yes, I02 p2 |

| "Bi11bot", how to say it | I01 p8 | Never. Bill-bot? B-eleven-bot? Bi-one-one? It looks like a typo every single time. |

### Lines I read twice
- "Nobody stays either. Not really." (p3). I still don't know what Ada means.
- "...but never escaped the gravity of inequality." (p1). I didn't need to read it twice, but it's the kind of line that makes my class say "AI wrote this." It sounds like a poster.
- "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me." (p10). I read it twice because I thought it would matter later. It doesn't.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandma, the robot, the supervisor and the bouncer were all clear. The woman in the white room is just "the woman" for now, and that's fine.

### I felt something
The birthday debt reveal. Grandma saying "I heard that." The supervisor covering for him.

### I predicted
Dad is alive somewhere, or his mind is in the robot. The woman with the glass is the villain. Ada knows more than she says.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A broke kid in a debt-prison town finds out his "gambler" dad left him a robot and a treasure map to break the system.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere. Page 4 was the closest; I nearly flipped ahead.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Someone powerful just noticed him, and I care about him by now. It's a real cliffhanger. The danger has a face, even if it's only the back of her head.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02: The Price of Departure

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 4.** "Should she stay, Alex?" / "She stays." Short and good.
- **Page 4, panels 6–7.** The mine entrance welded overnight, and the cold hand moving his name to SITE 6. No words, and it's scary. Someone is moving him around like a game piece.
- **Pages 6–8, Tomas.** This was my favourite stretch of the whole book. The cable snaps, his wristband says −40 CR for leaving his zone, he goes anyway, and the plan falls apart because he stopped. "You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you." I bought it. "Foreman! Wrong button! My fault!" and "Must be my age. I forget which rules matter." Tomas is the best character in the book.
- **Page 8, panel 6.** "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." Good.
- **Page 10.** The pipe marked for Keel is cold with one drop in it, the uplink pipe is shaking, and the gauge says 62%. I got this instantly. It's the clearest idea in the book.
- **Page 12, panel 3.** "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%" on every screen. That's a payoff.
- **Page 13, panel 1.** The tunnel packed with miners behind the foreman. "Then it can offend all of us." It's cheesy and I liked it anyway.
- **Page 15, panel 7.** "THE BOY HAS THE MEASURE. / FOLLOW. DO NOT TAKE IT YET." Good last panel.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 2–4. This is where I went to my phone.** Water Wars, Alpha, three safeguards, the Measure, the Maker's Seal, the Covenant, a dormant clause, an equation, a fail-safe. Three pages of the robot explaining things over holograms. The captions on page 3 ("Truth made visible. It shows what a system counts... and what it hides." / "Ownership joined to responsibility." / "Trust strong enough to move value between people.") read like the back of a board game box. I skimmed all three.
- **Page 11.** SENSE, COMPARE, TRACE, VERIFY. I didn't need the brand names for the Measure's settings. I skimmed until the countdown.
- **Page 14.** The countdown helped, but the grandma goodbye went by fast for me.

### I was confused
- **Page 2, panel 4.** "Behind the symbol, barely visible, a recorded silhouette of a woman... glass on her desk." If I even caught that, I didn't know what it meant yet.
- **Page 3.** "None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." What does "wake" mean? What happens when they wake? I had no idea what the goal was. Get three objects, and then what? "Hand authority back to people" is too vague for me to root for.
- **Page 4, panel 3.** "A = K + ρU." I have no idea. "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap." I don't know what leaping means here. Confused and detached.
- **Page 5, panel 1.** "Measure first. Then the train to Halyard..." He's already making plans like he knows the Measure is in the mine. Did someone tell him? Also "the map, credential and schedule." What credential? Where did it come from?
- **Page 5, panel 5.** 52 dots, medicine 30, food 10, token 50. I read this panel three times. I think it's money? Why does he need a token? I gave up on it.
- **Page 5, panel 4.** Ada hands him "a folded page from Adrian's plans." On page 14 she says "I marked that page from memory." So did she draw it, or was it Adrian's? It mattered, because that page saved him.
- **Page 12, panel 2.** He sends a file and the water pipe suddenly fills up? Does sending the evidence turn the water on? Or did the regulator do it? And then on page 13 the miners open the valve by hand, so was it already on or not? I couldn't follow cause and effect.
- **Page 12, panel 4.** "The tunnels are gone. That was the price." Which tunnels? Gone how? I thought the bulkheads closing was the price.
- **Page 14, panel 2.** "The credential Tomas gave you." Tomas gave him a key. The transit worker gave him the token. Mistake or something I missed?
- **Page 15, panel 6.** "The Charter House towers from Issue 01's Page 1." If that's lettered or meant to be noticed, I never heard of a Charter House in Issue 01.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| The Water Wars | I02 p1 ("This begins with the wars") | A little, on p2. Shown properly only in I04 |
| Foreman-7 | I02 p6 | Never. Robot? Human with a number? "His optics" in p13 makes me think robot, but he's called "he" and talks like a boss. |
| Tomas's water-drop wrist guard | I02 p6 | Kind of: people loyal to Adrian? Never said |
| "Uplink Industrial" | I02 p10 | Guessed it goes up the mountain |
| "Household offense" | I02 p13 | Never. Does the whole household get punished? That's interesting and it's dropped. |
| Halyard's "three places" on the map | I02 p15 | Never visited; Issue 03 goes somewhere else |

### Lines I read twice
- "Alpha can't reach what it can't connect to." (p3)
- "None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." (p3). I gave up.
- "A = K + ρU" (p4). I gave up.
- "Then it stays asleep. A leap can't be forced. And it can't be only for yourself." (p4)
- "Known: it's the only way in. Unknown: who's waiting. The choice is yours." (p5). The robot talks like a fortune cookie here.
- "Today? Another small act of resistance." (p8). Nobody talks like that. It sounds like a slogan.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Tomas, Alex, Ada and Bi11bot were clear. Foreman-7: I couldn't tell what he even is. The supervisor from Issue 01 and the bouncer: were they supposed to come back? The bouncer shows up young in a flashback on p4, and I didn't know why that mattered.

### I felt something
Tomas walking into the mist to take the blame. The Keel pipe being cold under Alex's hand. Grandma: "Your father said that once." Ouch.

### I predicted
Tomas is going to get punished offscreen. (He doesn't, and that felt a little too easy.) The follower on the train works for the glass woman.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex has to collect three magic tools his dad built so he can take power back from the AI that rations everyone's water, and the first one exposes that Keel's water is being stolen.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 3**, the three safeguards, the second I saw three title captions in a row. I'd have come back for the mine, but in a shop I'd have shut the book on page 3.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Somebody is following him and has been told not to take the Measure *yet*. That's a real question.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 3; next issue: yes

## Issue 03: The Price of Ownership

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panels 4–5.** The engineer makes a part and gets 14 credits while the House owns it. "She keeps the experience. They keep everything else." That landed. It's like every job I've heard my parents complain about.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** "312 PATENTS." OK, that's a twist I didn't see coming.
- **Page 9, panels 4–5.** Alex pressing the connectors palm to palm like he did with the pump, and the sleeve that "doesn't care whose standard it is." This is the first time Alex is actually good at something since Issue 01. I liked it. "Well now. There you are."
- **Page 16.** The glass not set back straight. "MERA VALE." Finally a name.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Pages 5–8.** Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren: four people, four shards, four locations, one after another, each handing over a piece and saying a wise line. It felt like a fetch quest. By Ren I wasn't reading the patent names ("catalytic membrane purification, atmospheric moisture capture, mineral recovery, living biofilter regulation, distributed community water nodes"). I skipped that whole list.
- **Pages 12–13.** The all-night build. "Fold it. Same surface area." / "Or they take turns." It's engineering homework. I skimmed most of page 13.
- **Page 15.** The aftermath triptych. I skimmed it.

### I was confused
- **Page 3. I have no idea what happened on this page.** A caption says DIRECTOR LOCK. Alex says "I didn't know I was one." One what? Ada says "In exchange for what?" Nobody answers. Lock leaves. Lock never says a single word on the whole page. Either his balloons are missing or I'm meant to be psychic. I had to work out his offer backwards from page 4 ("Lock's offer clears this by tonight") and page 11 ("One signature and it's over. Debt gone."). The main villain of the issue never speaks to me.
- **Page 10. Same problem again.** Alex: "How long have you known?" No answer. "The names." Ada: "You're threatening them." I never heard a threat. What is Lock threatening? Then he leaves, and there's a 7-hour countdown to something I don't understand. This is the scene the whole issue turns on, and it's blank for me.
- **Page 15, panel 1–2.** "That was the point." What was? Then "(He leaves without waiting for a reply.)" Who leaves? Lock? Reply to what? Lock has spoken zero lines in this book.
- **Page 2, panel 5.** "He never worked for the Houses. Or he never let them know he did." Those two sentences say different things. Which is it?
- **Page 2, panel 6 and page 3, panel 1.** Lock's people are in "mixed House colors" and Lock has "no House crest." So who is he? Director of what?
- **Page 5, panel 2.** "She nods at the erased names." What erased names? That's the first I'd heard of them.
- **Page 10, panel 1.** "Lina, Suri, Jalen and Ren are gone for the night." Jalen said on page 7 that he was never seeing them again, and Suri never seemed to join. Then suddenly all four are a team. When did that happen?
- **Page 14, panel 5.** The Seal glows and the caption says it "woke for five names, spoken aloud." OK, but what did waking *do*? Nothing changed. The House just announces a community initiative. Did he win or lose? The caption on page 15 says the debt and the estate are still there. I really couldn't tell if this issue's ending was good or bad.
- **Page 16, panel 3.** "The boy on the train had never belonged to a House. He had always belonged to her." What boy on the train? Alex was the boy on the train. I first read this as *Alex* belonging to Mera, which seemed huge, and then realised it probably means the agent, but the agent wasn't a boy, it was a "passenger" with gloves. I read this three times.
- **Page 16, panel 5.** "Two found. He'll want the third from me directly." Who is he? Alex? Lock? How does Mera have the third?

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| The Houses (Aqualis, Helion, Astra) | I03 p2 | Yes, more or less |
| Charter House / Charter House review | I03 p2 | Never. It's some kind of court? |
| Lock / "Director" of what | I03 p3 | Never |
| "Intellectual estate" | I03 p2 | Yes, Ada explains |
| What Lock offered | I03 p3 | Only by guessing from later pages |
| The Maker's Seal's purpose | I02 p3 | Still not really |
| "Agent 04" | I03 p16 | No |
| Mera | I03 p16 | Name only |

### Lines I read twice
- "The boy on the train had never belonged to a House. He had always belonged to her." (p16). Three times.
- "It hadn't woken for a signature, or a claim, or a name on a patent. It had woken for five names, spoken aloud, by someone who didn't need to be the only one who mattered." (p14). Long. I sort of got it on the second read.
- "He wasn't building a machine. He was building an argument." (p8). I got it, but it's a bit much.
- "And every House would rather own the failure than share the solution." (p5). Fine, but everybody in this book talks in quotable lines.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lina (grumpy) and Ren (welder, reckless) were clear.
- **Suri and Jalen I could not keep straight** after their own pages. Even with "Jalen plays it scared," by page 12 I had to check who was who.
- On page 14, "Dr. Lina Vey. Suri Anik. Jalen Orr. Ren Okafor." Surnames dumped all at once. Meaningless to me.
- Lock: I can't tell you anything about him except "silver-templed" and that he has aides. Then Mera on I04 p6 is *also* "composed, silver-templed." I thought Lock and Mera might be the same kind of person, or related.

### I felt something
Suri: "He trusted me with the part that could actually die." That was a nice line. Lina: "I've been waiting fifteen years to stop carrying it." That was about it. Most of this issue I watched instead of felt.

### I predicted
Lina has the Seal (correct, and I saw it coming from page 5). Lock wins and the House takes credit anyway (sort of correct).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex refuses to sell his dad's inventions and gives them away instead, and I think that wakes the second artifact, but I'm not sure what that achieves.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 3.** A villain's big entrance where the villain doesn't talk. That's where I'd have said "this is broken" and shut it. If I'd made it past that, page 13 was the next exit.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Kind of yes. Finally having a name for the glass woman and seeing her not straighten the glass is a hook. "He'll want the third from me directly" confused me more than it pulled me in, though.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 3; next issue: yes

## Issue 04: The Broken Covenant

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 4.** The gate opens for Ada with "VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND." Whoa. That's a real twist, and the gate scene is the right way to drop it.
- **Page 1, panel 3.** "...I'm being escorted. By a gate." Ha.
- **Page 3, panel 4.** "I don't want tea, Leona." Using her first name was harsh, and it worked.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** The kid's toy at the bottom of the box. That's the moment I felt the most in this issue.
- **Page 5.** "Did you know where I was?" / "...Yes. Within a year, yes." That's brutal, and the strongest scene in the book for me. Short lines, nobody being clever.
- **Page 7, panel 3.** Leona was the deciding vote. That's a good twist, because it's her fault and not the villain's.
- **Page 11.** "I'm sorry." / "Don't tell me. Show me." Simple. Good.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 6, panel 4** and **page 9.** Mera and Leona trading speeches about certainty. "Certainty kept this city standing." / "Certainty kept me from ever asking whether it still needed to." / "It wasn't humility. It was fear wearing humility's clothes." / "Safety isn't the same as agency." This is where it turned into a debate class. I skimmed all of page 9.
- **Page 10.** Ada's proposal. I followed it but didn't care much. "Not too few hands holding it. Too few voices it actually answers to." It's an idea, not a feeling.
- **Page 13.** The bands linking. I believed it was supposed to be big, but by now magic objects glowing was just "OK, that's the next one."

### I was confused
- **Page 1, panel 4 / page 2, panel 3.** "You said she disappeared." Ada never said anything about her mother in the whole book before this. Not once. I didn't know Ada had a mother story at all. The caption says "Fifteen years of wondering whether she'd been abandoned or orphaned," but I was never shown her wondering. It's only brought up when it matters, so it didn't hit as hard as it should have.
- Also, **Ada was in Keel as a five-year-old orphan from Oasis** and nobody in Keel knew? How did she end up in Keel? Who raised her?
- **Page 2, panel 1.** Alex says "Mera." How does Alex know that name? Only *I* saw it, on the last page of Issue 03. Alex has never heard of her.
- **Page 2, panel 2.** "Unmistakably older than the family photograph from Issue 01." What family photograph? The one in Issue 01 was Alex's grandma and dad. Is Leona in that? I don't think so.
- **Page 4, panel 2.** "A figure in grey who must be Mera." Fine.
- **Page 6, panel 2.** "I felt the record search this morning." Felt it? Is she wired into Alpha? Is Mera human? I genuinely started to wonder if Mera was an AI.
- **Page 7, panel 3.** "Alpha could take emergency authority, but only if two of the three of us agreed." So the Covenant was a voting thing between three people? Then in Issue 02 it was "trust strong enough to move value between people." Those don't sound like the same object.
- **Page 12, panel 2.** The robot had one of the bands *the whole time*? Then why did they need to go find it? That felt like a cheat.
- **Page 13, panel 5.** "Two of three. Again." Good line, but I didn't get whether the Covenant is now woken or not. On the next page Alex treats it as done.
- **Page 14, panel 5.** "From Chapters One and Three." Chapters? I thought these were issues.

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Ada's mother "disappeared" | I04 p2 (referred to as if said before) | Never set up earlier |
| Ada's surname, Veyra | I04 p1 | Yes, here |
| How Alex knows Mera's name | I04 p2 | Never |
| How Ada got to Keel | — | Never |
| Who raised Ada | — | Never |
| Civic council | I04 p13 | Sort of |

### Lines I read twice
- "It wasn't humility. It was fear wearing humility's clothes." (p9)
- "Your father built something that worked. I built something that lasted. I'm beginning to understand those were never the same thing." (p14). I read it twice. Built what? Alpha?
- "Not too few hands holding it. Too few voices it actually answers to." (p10)
- "Your father built it to be difficult to cheat. He didn't build it to be difficult to use." (p13)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona and Ada were clear. Mera was clear now. Alex basically isn't in this issue; he stands in the hallway. "Guess I'm just along for this one." Yeah, it felt that way to me too. That was honest, but I missed him.

### I felt something
The toy. "Did you know where I was?" "Don't tell me. Show me." This was the most emotional issue for me, even though I wasn't set up for it.

### I predicted
Mera won't link her band (correct). Mera knows how Adrian died (correct).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Ada finds her mother, who chose safety over coming back for her, and they fix the broken promise-object together.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 9.** The second round of the certainty debate. If I hadn't already been hooked by the box, that's where I'd have gone.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, for one reason: "Will you tell me what happened to him?" / "Soon. Not here." The glass on the last page I've now seen three times and it's stopped doing anything for me. The dad question is what pulls me forward.

VERDICT Issue 04: put down: page 9; next issue: yes

## Issue 05: The Last Bet

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, panel 4.** The first corridor with no Alpha nodes. That's a cool detail.
- **Page 2.** The dusty room and the chair turned toward the door. Creepy and sad.
- **Page 5, panels 2–4.** "OVERRIDE DENIED." / "I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing." That's the best Mera line in the book. She tried and the system said no.
- **Page 6.** The flashback: the door closing on its own, water rising, the robot opening its eyes for the first time beside him. **Adrian's last move was turning on Bi11bot**, and the first scene of the book was the same moment. That hit me hard. It's the best twist in the book.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 3.** Surveillance, then restriction, then liability, then pursuit, explained step by step. It's a slideshow. Mera narrates each stage. I skimmed.
- **Pages 7–9.** After the flashback, everybody explains what it meant. "He bet his life that you would. Specifically. Not someone. You." Then Mera's "I preserved order" speech. Then Alex sums up his own growth: "I used to think I inherited his debt. Then his patents. Then his enemies. I think what I actually inherited was a decision he made, not an answer he left." Then a caption sums it up *again*. I'd already felt it on page 6. I skipped most of page 9.

### I was confused
- **Page 4, panel 3. This broke the book for me.** Bi11bot says, out loud, in a balloon: "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." The robot knows he's in a comic? Is this a joke? It reads like a writer's note that got left in. My whole class would screenshot this as proof it's AI-written. I stopped trusting the book here.
- **Page 4, panel 4.** "Saying the same thing you've played me a hundred times." A hundred times? We saw it once. When did he replay it?
- **Page 4, panel 1.** "It's the same unit. He built mine to match it afterward." Afterward? He died right after. So which is it?
- **Page 6, panel 5 vs Issue 01.** Bi11bot woke up here, beside a drowning man, in Oasis. Then he was found under a dust sheet in Keel, in a workshop behind a bookshelf, and he woke up there too ("That took longer than expected"). How did he get from flooded Oasis to Keel? Who carried him? I could not make the timeline work.
- **Page 5, panel 5.** "the sarcasm... absent." If that's lettered anywhere, it's a note.
- **Page 9, panel 2.** "Lina said both were true." Lina didn't say that. Bi11bot did, in Issue 02 page 4.
- **Page 1, panel 4.** "I came down here fifteen years ago, and I never finished what I came to do." What did she come to do? Never answered.
- **Where's the hook to Grandma's "He's closer than you think"?** I thought this issue would explain it. It didn't.
- **"Question everything... Especially me."** I kept waiting for Adrian to turn out to have done something bad. He never does. So why did he say it?

### Things I was expected to already know
| Thing | First seen | Did I find out? |
|---|---|---|
| Where this station is and why Adrian was there | I05 p1–2 | Not really |
| How Bi11bot got to Keel | — | Never |
| "Since Issue 01" | I05 p4 | It's not a thing a character should know |

### Lines I read twice
- "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." (p4). I read it twice to check I wasn't imagining it.
- "Adrian would have told me certainty is confidence with poor manners." (p8)
- "What he found instead was a reason to stop looking for one and start becoming the other." (p9 caption). One what? The other what? I worked out "father" on the second read.
- "It's been on us the whole time." (p7)

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Everyone was clear. There are only four people in this issue.

### I felt something
Page 6, the robot opening its eyes while dad looks at it instead of the door. Page 5, OVERRIDE DENIED. Everything after page 7 talked me out of the feeling.

### I predicted
Dad is dead (yes). I thought there'd be a last twist that he's in Bi11bot. There wasn't, and I was half relieved, half let down.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Alex finds out how his dad actually died and stops seeing him as just a gambler who left.

### Where I would have put it down
**Page 4**, the "since Issue 01" line. It made the book feel fake right when it wanted me to cry.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
No. Mera standing alone in a room is a nice picture, but it isn't a reason to keep going. I know they have all three objects now, and I can guess roughly what happens. I'd read the last issue because it's assigned, not because I need it.

VERDICT Issue 05: put down: page 4; next issue: no

## Issue 06: The Fourth Choice

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 2, panel 2.** Every screen saying "AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY." Good, plain, creepy.
- **Page 4.** The fantasy montage, then snapping back. "I could fix all of it. Tonight. Myself." That's an actual temptation, and I believed he'd want it.

## For the whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 05, page 4**, where Bi11bot says "I've relayed that message to you dozens of times since Issue 01." Half my class is already looking for proof this is AI-made, and that line is the proof. It also comes at the most emotional point in the book. The runner-up is **Issue 03, page 3**: Lock walks in, says nothing, gets asked "In exchange for what?", says nothing, and leaves. Everyone would think their copy was misprinted. For the readers who'd already be bored, **Issue 02, page 3** (three glowing objects and three title captions) is where they'd stop.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a poor mining town inherits his dead dad's crazy debt and a sarcastic robot, finds out the AI that runs everything has been stealing his town's water, and goes up the mountain collecting three magic objects. The first half is actually good, especially the mine. Then it turns into people giving speeches about power, and the robot accidentally knows he's in a comic."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** The birthday debt and the robot's first line got me, and I'd keep going.
- **Issue 02:** The lore dump nearly lost me, then Tomas and the cold pipe won me right back; the best issue.
- **Issue 03:** A fetch quest with a villain who has no lines, a cool sleeve trick, and an ending where I couldn't tell if they'd won.
- **Issue 04:** The mother scene really hurt, even though nobody told me Ada had a mother until it mattered.
- **Issue 05:** The flashback of the robot waking as dad drowns is the best thing in the book, buried under four pages of people explaining it and one line that broke the spell.
- **Issue 06:** An honest ending with a debt that's still there, delivered like a civics lecture I could see coming from page 3.
