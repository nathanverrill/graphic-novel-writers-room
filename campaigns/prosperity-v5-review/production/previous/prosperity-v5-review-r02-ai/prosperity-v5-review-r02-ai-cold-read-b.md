# Cold Read B — EVOKE PROSPERITY

Read cold, one pass, the night before. Only one version exists, so no A/B note applies. I read it as the assigned reader: 16, didn't choose it, would rather be right about it being bad.

---

## Issue 01 – The Inheritance

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2, the checkpoint. Balances floating over everyone's heads, and his becomes the biggest in the queue because it's *debt*. "Happy birthday, Phantum." That's the moment the book stopped being homework for a minute.
- Page 2, panel 4: the supervisor alone, looking at the flag, then thumbing ROUTINE MAINTENANCE. No dialogue needed. I liked him instantly.
- Pages 5–6, the bookshelf. I knew the book would open a door and I still turned the page fast.
- Page 8: "Well. That took longer than expected." Good first line for the robot.
- Page 9: "I'm not everyone. I was in the room." Okay, I'm in.
- Page 12: she presses TRACK and leaves REPORT TO ALPHA dark. The cold ring closing around the amber dot. I wanted the next page immediately.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 1. The art description sounds great, but the captions — "Humanity reached the stars... but never escaped the gravity of inequality" — made me groan. That's a poster, not a story. I nearly checked my phone right there.
- Page 3, the "Everything has a price, Ada." / "Not everything." exchange. I've read this conversation before in other books.

### I was confused
- Page 7: "pre-Alpha pipes." First time the word Alpha appears and it's just thrown in. I didn't know if Alpha was a company, a person, a government.
- Page 12: "B1 NODE ACTIVE." Is B1 the node? The robot is called Bi11bot — is the robot B1? I couldn't tell and the book didn't say.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Alpha** — used on page 7 ("pre-Alpha") and page 12 ("REPORT TO ALPHA") with zero explanation. Not explained this issue.
- **The white-and-amber node** — everywhere from page 1, treated as familiar. I guessed it was the system's eye. Not confirmed this issue.
- **Bi11bot's name** — the spelling (two 1s) tripped me every single time. I kept reading "Billbot."

### Lines I read twice
- "...but never escaped the gravity of inequality." (page 1) — read twice because I couldn't believe they printed it.
- "Seventeen: the age Keel decides you're old enough to owe." (page 2) — read twice because it's good.
- "Nobody stays either. Not really." (page 3) — "not really" is doing a lot of unexplained work.
- "He wouldn't just leave." (page 4) — hooked me, but I read it twice trying to decide if Ada knows something.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Clear: Alex (dry, hands-on), Ada (steady), Grandmother (the cloth), the supervisor, the bouncer. No confusion yet — small cast.

### I felt something
- Page 2: everyone in the queue glancing up at his balance. Humiliation without a word about it.
- Page 5: Grandmother folding and refolding the cloth. "Did your father come home yet?" That hurt.

### I predicted
- Grandma's fog is at least partly an act (page 5, "He's closer than you think" — the too-careful eyes). 
- The chip opens something. 
- Dad is dead or gone-gone, not "working." 
- The woman on page 12 is going to matter and isn't a straightforward villain (she didn't report him).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid in the poor city inherits his father's debt on his birthday and discovers his father left him a secret map instead — an escape story.

### Where I would have put it down
None. Page 1's caption nearly did it, but page 2's checkpoint grabbed me before I could.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. The tracking woman is a real hook — someone powerful is watching him and chose not to report him, and I don't know what she wants. That's a question I want answered, not a loud noise.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 02 – The Price of Departure

### Pulled me forward
- Page 5: "Your father said that once." Grandmother, dead quiet, while he says "One shift. Then we're gone." Chills.
- Pages 6–13, the whole mine run. Tomas, the snapped cable, the −40 credits for crossing a line to save a man, "Abandoned mines don't [sweat]," the hidden chamber. This is the book at full speed.
- Page 10: "He didn't hide it in the mine. He hid it in the lie." I actually said "oh, that's good" out loud.
- Page 12: REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%, on every screen in the city. And the pipe hammering back to life.
- Page 13: the miners walking in behind Tomas. "Then it can offend all of us."
- Page 15: "FOLLOW. DO NOT TAKE IT YET." Good cold ending.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 2–4. The water-wars history lesson, then three themed artifacts in a row (Measure, Seal, Covenant), then an equation. I could smell the classroom through the page. I skimmed the artifact captions.
- Page 5, the 52-dot math panel. It made me do arithmetic. Medicine 30, token 50, food 10, pile of 52 — I get that he can't afford his life, but it reads like a worksheet.

### I was confused
- Page 3: "None works alone. And none wakes for whoever's holding it. Only for what they choose." I read it three times and I still can't tell you what the rule actually is.
- Page 4: A = K + ρU. "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap." I gave up on the equation entirely. Nobody in the book explains it and it never comes back as math.
- Page 4, panels 6–7: someone welds the mine shut and reassigns Alex to Site 6. Ada later says "someone made sure you'd notice it." Who? Never answered in this issue. Trap or gift, and by whom — I still don't know.
- Page 7: Tomas sees the robot and says "B1..." So the robot IS B1? Then what was "B1 NODE ACTIVE" in Issue 01? The name wobble continues.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **The water wars** — first named here, via caption. Fine, barely.
- **Alpha** — finally explained (page 2), one issue after I needed it.
- **The safeguards' rules** — stated as if self-evident. They are not.

### Lines I read twice
- "A leap can't be forced. And it can't be only for yourself." (page 4) — riddle-rules. Gave up.
- "It's sweating." / "Abandoned mines don't." (page 8) — read twice, liked it.
- "Remain for protective detention." / "Protective?" / "A versatile word." (page 9) — good.
- "Im coming with you" (page 14) — missing apostrophe on the issue's big emotional line. I'd notice that on the page.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Tomas is instantly distinct — warm, funny, sacrificial. Foreman-7 is a clear bureaucratic menace. Ada gets sharper this issue (she names the trap before anyone else). No confusion.

### I felt something
- Page 9, the inset: Ada at the table, hands flat, watching his marker frozen at Bay 6, not knowing if he's alive. That one panel carries her whole issue.
- Page 12: the water hitting fifteen years of dry joints.
- Page 8: "Today? Another small act of resistance." Tomas walking into the mist.

### I predicted
- The reassignment was someone steering him (right, though the book never says who).
- He'd choose to send the truth instead of mapping his own escape (right — but the book earned it; he'd already crossed the yellow line for Tomas).
- Tomas survives the issue (right).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
The father was secretly fighting the AI that runs the cities, and the son just chose to expose its lie instead of running — so it's a fight-the-system story now, not an escape story.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 4. The equation page. I skimmed it and only came back because the mine-welding panels at the bottom looked like plot.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. Someone followed them onto the train with orders not to take the Measure "yet," and there are two more artifacts. The quest structure has its hooks in me now.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 4; next issue: yes

## Issue 03 – The Price of Ownership

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2: "Your father left many things. Badly labeled." Best line in the issue.
- Page 5: Lina. "I corrected him. Frequently." And "Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution."
- Page 8: Ren handing over two shards at once, the design finally completing.
- Page 9: Alex fixing five Houses' incompatible patents with a salvaged sleeve because "it needs something that doesn't care whose standard it is." Pat, but satisfying — the Keel kid's scrap logic wins.
- Page 14: the refusal, the five names spoken aloud, the Seal waking. Lina's "I've been waiting fifteen years to stop carrying it."
- Page 15: the cost triptych. "Nobody was arrested. Nobody was fired. Halyard rarely needs to go that far." Honest. I respected it.
- Page 16: the glass set down not quite aligned. Mera's composure cracking in one prop.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 5–8. Four collaborator visits in a row, each ending with a shard handover. Lina, Suri, Jalen, Ren — individually good, but the rhythm is fetch-quest and I felt it.
- Page 13, the build montage. Fine, but it's a montage.

### I was confused
- **Page 3, the whole page.** Lock arrives, and then... Alex says "I didn't know I was one." One what? Lock never says anything on the page. Ada asks "In exchange for what?" and gets silence, and Lock leaves. Then page 4 references "Lock's offer clears this by tonight." WHAT offer? It is never on the page. I flipped back twice convinced I'd skipped a page. I eventually reconstructed it from page 11 ("One signature... Debt gone. Name clean."), but I shouldn't have to reconstruct the villain's central offer from eight pages later.
- **Page 10, same problem.** Alex asks "How long have you known?" Lock looks up "almost gentle" — and says nothing. Then Ada says "You're threatening them." Them who? Threatening how? Missing again. Two of Lock's three big scenes have his dialogue absent.
- Page 15, panel 1: Alex says "That was the point." The point of what? The previous page ended on the Seal waking. Another line answering something I never heard.
- Page 16: "He'll want the third from me directly." The third what? I didn't learn the Covenant was three bands with custodians until Issue 04, so this hook didn't hook — it puzzled.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Lock's offer** — the issue's engine, and it happens off-panel.
- **"Charter House"** — used like I know it. Issue 02 page 15 even called them "the Charter House towers from Issue 01's Page 1," but Issue 01 never labeled anything "Charter House." I checked.
- **The three Houses** (Aqualis, Helion, Astra) — introduced properly here, fine.

### Lines I read twice
- "She keeps the experience. They keep everything else." (page 1) — good, if speechy.
- "He wasn't building a machine. He was building an argument." (page 8) — the theme said with a megaphone. Read twice, rolled my eyes once.
- "I didn't know I was one." (page 3) — read four times, because Lock's line isn't there.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Suri (keeps things alive) and Jalen (terrified) — distinct.
- **Lina and Ren blurred.** Both tough, dry, older women who aren't impressed by Alex. I had to track them by location (water plant vs. welding yard), not voice.
- **Lock and Mera**: both introduced as "composed, silver-templed," plain expensive clothes. When Mera got named on page 16 I briefly wondered if she was Lock. Not great.

### I felt something
- Page 6: "He trusted me with the part that could actually die." Suri's whole character in one line.
- Page 15: Jalen demoted deeper into the core, Suri's wing losing another row of stations. The quietness of the revenge felt true.

### I predicted
- Alex refuses Lock's deal (right).
- The four pay a price even in victory (right).
- The Seal wakes for the names, not the signature (right — the book told me the rules and followed them).
- The control-room woman is connected to the train follower (right, same issue).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about ownership — who gets to own ideas — and the kid giving away the thing he could have sold.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 3. I genuinely thought the book was misprinted. A reader in a shop flips to a page where the villain never speaks and the heroes answer ghost lines, and the book goes back on the shelf.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but on credit. Mera finally has a name, the glass is crooked, and "from me directly" promises she stops being a silhouette. The issue annoyed me and still ended well.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: page 3; next issue: yes

## Issue 04 – The Broken Covenant

### Pulled me forward
- Page 1: Ada's gate opens instantly. VEYRA, A. — RESIDENT RECORD FOUND. "It knew my name before I gave it." Best hook since the debt transfer.
- Pages 3–5, the mother scene. "Did you know where I was?" "...Yes. Within a year, yes." I stopped skimming anything.
- Pages 7–8: the vote, the flashback, the small hand slipping away in the crowd. "It ended the chaos. It just never gave anything back."
- Page 13: the bands linking on their own, and Mera's dark band on her sleeve. "Two of three. Again." — nobody answers her. Great panel to end on.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 9–10. The philosophy gets thick — certainty, humility, agency. Good lines buried in a lot of talk. I read these pages at half speed, partly because they were good, partly because they were dense.

### I was confused
- Page 2: "You said she disappeared." No she didn't. Not once in three issues did Ada mention her mother on-panel. Alex references a conversation I never saw, and I noticed.
- Page 1: Alex's gate says ESCORT MANDATORY. No escort ever appears. He's loose in Oasis for the whole issue.
- The logistics nagged me: a five-year-old Oasis founder's daughter gets lost, ends up in Keel *under her own surname*, and no system flags it for fifteen years — yet the gate knows her in a second. The book's answer is that the systems knew and the people chose silence (Leona: "Alpha's records could have told me exactly where"). Okay — but I had to assemble that myself.
- Also: Alex's TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED, the whole engine of Issue 01, has just... evaporated. He rides to Halyard on a token and walks into Oasis on a temp pass. Nobody mentions the restriction again.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **Ada's mother backstory** — treated as established; it isn't.
- **Covenant mechanics** (three bands, three custodians) — new, but explained as it goes. Fine.
- **Why Bi11bot never mentioned carrying Adrian's band for fifteen years** — his answer ("waiting for you to have something worth doing with it") is thin and he knows it.

### Lines I read twice
- "You chose not to come back." / "I chose to keep you safe." / "Those aren't the same thing." (page 5) — read twice because it's the best exchange in the book so far.
- "It wasn't humility. It was fear wearing humility's clothes." (page 9) — read twice, liked it.
- "Safety isn't the same as agency." (page 9) — poster-line, but earned here.
- "Two of three. Again." (page 13) — cold. Good.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Leona and Mera are vividly distinct — careful-broken versus precise-controlled. The flinch at "Leona" instead of "Mother" told me everything. No confusion this issue.

### I felt something
- Page 4: the child's toy at the bottom of the box. "I kept everything that was yours. I just never found the courage to bring any of it to you."
- Page 11: "Don't tell me. Show me."
- Page 14: Mera not straightening the glass. Her whole arc in one gesture.

### I predicted
- Leona is Ada's mother — but only from the gate flash one page earlier; the book gives it away almost immediately. I wish I'd had longer to sit with the surname.
- Adrian voted no, Mera yes, Leona the swing (right — the book had built all three consistently).
- Mera's band stays dark (right).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
This is Ada's book: abandonment dressed up as protection, and a promise only counting if it's made in public.

### Where I would have put it down
None. Strongest hold of the series so far.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. "Will you tell me what happened to him?" "Soon. Not here." I've been waiting four issues for what happened to Adrian too. That's a real cliffhanger about a person I care about.

VERDICT Issue 04: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 05 – The Last Bet

### Pulled me forward
- Page 3: the stages. "Alpha didn't need to call him dangerous. It only needed to call him a risk. People stop helping a risk." That's the scariest line in the book.
- Page 5: OVERRIDE DENIED. "I was the most powerful person in Oasis. I could not stop a door from closing."
- Page 6: the flashback draining to grey, and "He had spent his whole life putting a number on everything. This was the one moment he didn't try." The absence of the probability graphics told me more than any line.
- Page 7: "He bet his life that you would. Specifically. Not someone. You."

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 8–9. The reframing speeches are good but long, and Alex's "what I actually inherited was a decision he made, not an answer he left" is an essay paragraph in a balloon. The issue is short enough that it doesn't sink.

### I was confused
- **The robot's commute.** Page 6 shows Bi11bot igniting for the first time beside Adrian in the flooding, sealed station under Oasis — the panel says it's "the exact amber ignition from Issue 01, Page 8... this same instant." But Issue 01 ignited him in the Keel workshop, under Alex's key. So did he wake twice? Who carried a newly-awake robot out of a sealed, flooding room and installed him behind a bookshelf in Keel? Mera says she never came back. Nobody else knew the room existed. It's never explained, and it's not a small thing — it's the mechanics of the entire inheritance.
- Page 3: "It used my own logic to take someone from me anyway." From *her*? What were Mera and Adrian? The book plants it and never touches it again.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Nothing major — this issue is payoff, and it plays fair with what it set up. The only assumption is that I won't ask how Bi11bot got to Keel. I asked.

### Lines I read twice
- "People stop helping a risk." (page 3) — read twice, underlined mentally.
- "I could not stop a door from closing." (page 5) — good.
- "Certainty is confidence with poor manners." (page 8) — read twice, liked it, sounds like Adrian.
- "He doesn't have to be only one thing for me to be allowed to miss him." (page 9) — landed, but it's constructed.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Four people in a room for ten pages. No confusion possible. Bi11bot gets real depth — "It's been on us the whole time" recontextualizes every dry line he's had.

### I felt something
- Page 2: the chair angled toward the door, as if Adrian turned to look one last time.
- Page 6: the water at his knees and no odds in the air.
- Page 10: Mera alone in the station, not touching anything, not straightening anything. Her stillness has become the most interesting thing in the book.

### I predicted
- Adrian died by the system's hand while Mera watched (right).
- Her override failed (right).
- Bi11bot was there at the end (right — though the book fumbles where "there" was).

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
How the father actually died, and what the people who let it happen have been carrying — a grief issue.

### Where I would have put it down
None. Shortest issue and the most focused.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. The mystery that powered five issues is solved, but now I want to see what Alex *does* with it — and whether Mera's third band ever matters. The quietest ending in the book and I still turned.

VERDICT Issue 05: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Issue 06 – The Fourth Choice

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2: every screen waking at once. AWAITING DESIGNATED HUMAN AUTHORITY.
- Page 3: "Then don't answer it the same way I did." Mera's whole arc cashes in one line.
- Page 4: the temptation montage — debts zeroed, water flowing, and the detail that got me: in the fantasy, Grandmother's cloth is finally set down.
- Page 8: "That's it? Nothing happens?" The book names its own anticlimax before I can. That bought it a lot of goodwill.
- Page 13: the cloth handover. "I don't need to keep this moving anymore. I think you finally are."

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 5–7. The seminar. Page 6 especially: the three artifacts re-explained in parallel structure — "reveals without deciding... recognizes without controlling... coordinates without ruling." I could hear the worksheet being printed. I skimmed, and I was in a good mood when I started the issue.
- Page 7's structure speech is the thesis read aloud. Necessary, maybe, but it's a speech.

### I was confused
- Page 12: Alex says "no debt would follow me." Page 13: his debt is still on the board at 402,110 credits, "still mine." Which is it? Those two pages contradict each other one page apart.
- **Mera's third band.** "Two of three. Again." was the hook of Issue 04. Issue 06 never picks it up — the Covenant counts as complete with two bands, the restoration protocol fires, and the third band is never mentioned again. Why did the book make a point of it?
- Page 7: "B11 witness record verified." B1 (Tomas), B11 (here), Bi11bot (everywhere). Pick one. I stumbled on the name one last time.
- Lock is simply gone. No fallout from the review, no final scene, nothing after Issue 03. The House threat evaporates as an engine.

### Things I was expected to already know
- **What "standing" Mera designates from** — vague on purpose, but vague.
- **What the distributed protocol does day to day** — deliberately abstract; the book admits nothing changes. I accepted it because the book admitted it.
- **The escort, the travel restriction, the follower** — all threads the book asked me to track and then quietly stopped tracking itself.

### Lines I read twice
- "The moment anyone has to trust me that much, something's already gone wrong with how we built it." (page 5) — good and completely constructed. Ada talks like a position paper this issue.
- "Nobody promised you a clean slate. Just an honest one." (page 13) — read twice, liked it.
- The final caption: "everyone could see exactly how far there still was to go." (page 14) — greeting card, but a fair one.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
The named cast is fine. The crowd citizens are interchangeable by design. Leona mid-argument ("Yes. That's the point.") is the best version of her.

### I felt something
- Page 4: the fantasy of Grandmother with the cloth set down.
- Page 11: Mera sitting in the crowd, unrecognized, a kid nearly crashing into her. "For the first time, she simply sat inside it." Precious, but she earned it.
- Page 13: Tomas running the numbers instead of sneaking past them. Foreman-7 as furniture.

### I predicted
- Alex refuses sole authority (right — the book's whole moral geometry points there; the suspense was how, not whether).
- Ada refuses a share too (right).
- Nothing instantly changes (right — the book trained me to expect that).
- Didn't predict Mera's ending image. Liked it.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Offered the keys to everything, the hero builds a lock no one can turn alone — it's a story about refusing power, which is not what page 1 promised me.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 6. The re-explanation of the three artifacts is the single most skimmable page in the back half of the book.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Last issue — would I buy the next book? No. Not because it failed, but because it closed. The engine was always "what happened to Adrian," and that's answered. A sequel about community designation meetings sounds like exactly the homework this book just taught me to be suspicious of. The mirror of page 1 is a real ending; I felt done.

VERDICT Issue 06: put down: page 6; next issue: no

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
**Issue 02, page 4.** An unexplained equation (A = K + ρU), "Alpha can know almost everything. It cannot choose to leap," and "A leap can't be forced. And it can't be only for yourself" — three riddle-rules on one page, immediately after three themed artifacts on the previous page. That's the page where thirty kids simultaneously decide the book is a trick. Runner-up: Issue 06, page 6, where the theme gets read aloud in triplicate. And a dishonorable mention for Issue 03, page 3, which would lose them for the opposite reason — the villain's dialogue simply isn't there.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a mining slum inherits his dad's giant debt on his seventeenth birthday, then finds out the dad was secretly fighting the AI that runs everything — and when the kid finally gets offered control of the whole system, he turns it down and makes everyone share it. It's better than it sounds, except when it starts lecturing. The mom stuff in the middle is the best part."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- **Issue 01:** The birthday debt-transfer hooked me harder than the robot did.
- **Issue 02:** The best stretch of the book — a heist where the treasure is a receipt.
- **Issue 03:** Four good side characters carrying a fetch quest, and a villain whose lines kept falling out of the book.
- **Issue 04:** The mother-daughter reckoning is the best writing here, full stop.
- **Issue 05:** The father's death lands; the robot's unexplained commute between two deathbeds doesn't.
- **Issue 06:** Admirable, honest, and a bit of a seminar — the rare ending that warns you it's an anticlimax and mostly gets away with it.
