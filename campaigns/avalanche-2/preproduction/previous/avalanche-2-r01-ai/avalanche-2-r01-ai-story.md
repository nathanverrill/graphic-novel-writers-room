# avalanche-2 — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

### Premise

In 2046, Colorado is running out of natural water while Antarctica loses the ice that supports its ecosystem. Roman Ashford, a shy young chemical engineer, discovers that his employer, the oil company Clear Spring Global, once buried research into converting used plastics into gasoline. To recover his dead mentor's work, Roman must cross a decommissioned Antarctic laboratory, confront the company that caused the research to disappear, and decide whether exposing the truth matters more than protecting himself.

The central loop is: **destroying the environment to save the environment**. Oil drilling and fossil-fuel use drive global warming, while the possible remedy emerges from the drilling company's own concealed research. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`

The story's speculative heart is the claim that used plastics can be broken down into gasoline in a way that could significantly reduce pollutants from new fossil-fuel extraction. This is the story's approximately 5 percent speculative element and must be treated as **S** or, if the finished project deliberately departs from the real science, as a logged **L** under the hard-SF rules. The material does not yet establish the process's exact chemistry, energy requirements, emissions profile, limits, or deployment conditions. `(references/research.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Intended experience and emotional trajectory

The material reaches for a grounded near-future climate story in which the future is recognizably built from the present: scarce food, damaged water systems, corporate power, research infrastructure, and climate consequences. The settings are Antarctica, Colorado, and the Himalayas, with the Himalayas primarily functioning as the formative backstory. `(rules/rules.md; input/story.md)`

The emotional trajectory moves Roman:

1. From an isolated, timid young engineer who has survived a formative disaster with TJ;
2. To a protégé trying to continue Orien Keel's work;
3. To an employee who discovers that his institution has buried evidence;
4. To a person driven by anger after Croft causes the same destruction that killed Orien;
5. To a researcher who chooses public verification and distribution over revenge alone.

The intended destination is that Roman completes the work and creates a credible path toward helping humanity, though the material does not yet establish the world's response after the completed findings are distributed. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

### Central dramatic questions

- Can Roman recover and complete the research that CSG buried?
- Will he expose Benedict Croft's role in suppressing climate research?
- Can evidence survive a billionaire's ability to discredit it?
- Does a technological remedy repair the damage caused by the institution that discovered it, or merely create another version of the same system?
- Can Roman turn grief and anger into work that benefits people beyond himself?

The final question and the exact post-publication consequences remain open. `(input/story.md)`

### Theme

The story contrasts the extraction economy with conservation and repair. CSG's drilling contributes to the warming that destroys water and ice, yet CSG's buried research may offer a way to reduce the need for new fossil-fuel extraction. The same institution is therefore both source of harm and accidental source of a possible remedy. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`

The Antarctic and Colorado threads are intended to mirror one another:

- Antarctic sea-ice loss threatens krill, a base of the food web.
- Colorado snowpack and streamflow decline threatens cold-water species and water availability.
- Both stories involve the loss of a keystone physical resource causing wider ecological consequences.

The parallel comes from real-world research and should remain visibly research-informed rather than being treated as settled fictional fact beyond the material already established. `(references/research.md)`

## Premise and narrative engine

The narrative begins with the event that creates Roman and TJ's unusual partnership, then jumps four years to the consequences of climate damage and institutional suppression.

The engine is causal:

1. Climate damage creates Roman's past survival incident and the story's present conditions.
2. Orien's research connects those conditions to CSG's business.
3. Croft fires Orien to suppress the connection.
4. Orien's death makes Roman his only next of kin and gives Roman access to Orien's files.
5. The files point Roman toward a hidden Antarctic laboratory.
6. The laboratory contains buried plastics research.
7. Roman brings the research to Croft because Croft has the money and infrastructure to implement it.
8. Croft's inaction and Roman's firing reveal that the problem is not only technical but institutional.
9. Roman publishes an unfinished portion to expose Croft, but Croft's wealth discredits it.
10. Roman returns to the research, completes it with TJ's help, and plans a more defensible path through peer review, journalism, and government.

Every major discovery creates a new obligation or conflict rather than simply providing information. `(input/story.md)`

## Story So Far

The supplied material is a full-arc outline and story treatment, not a finished graphic-novel script. It does not provide numbered chapters, page layouts, or a complete scene-by-scene draft. The sequence below preserves the supplied beats and organizes them by causal movement.

### Sequence 1 — The iceberg and the bond

**Time:** 2042  
**Place:** Nepal, in the Himalayas  
**Characters:** Roman Ashford, age 18; TJ

Roman travels alone to Nepal for his senior trip, bringing TJ, a robot dog built from spare parts by the scavenger-engineer Bi11bot. TJ has artificial intelligence, emergency capabilities, and an extensive set of tools. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

Roman and TJ attempt to cross an iceberg. The iceberg has been weakened by melting associated with global warming. It splits open while they are crossing, trapping them inside the opening. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

An electrical storm appears. A lightning strike near Roman and TJ unexpectedly bonds Roman's Neuralink to TJ's onboard AI. The sensation is confusing at first. Roman and TJ then use their connected capabilities and TJ's emergency functions to escape. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

The event establishes:

- Roman and TJ's shared survival history;
- The unusual Neuralink-to-TJ connection;
- Roman's experience of climate damage as a direct physical threat;
- TJ as Roman's practical, information-bearing partner;
- The Himalayas as the origin point of the partnership, not the main 2046 setting.

The exact mechanics and long-term limits of the lightning-created connection are not established. `(input/characters.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

### Sequence 2 — Colorado in 2046

**Time:** 2046  
**Place:** Colorado  
**Characters:** Roman Ashford; Orien Keel; Benedict Croft

Four years later, the world is significantly worse. Food is scarce and expensive. Colorado has almost no natural water sources left. Melting ice caps are raising ocean levels. `(input/story.md; input/story.md)`

Roman is now 22, has a bachelor's degree in chemical engineering, and is researching global warming while living in Colorado. He works for Clear Spring Global, or CSG, and is mentored by senior CSG chemical engineer Orien Keel. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

Orien has been researching Colorado's dried-up rivers and lakes. His work connects the severe global warmth to oil drilling and oil use. The research threatens the interests of CSG's owner, Benedict Croft, a billionaire old-money oil tycoon who works from CSG headquarters in Colorado. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

Croft discovers the connection in Orien's research and fires Orien to bury it. The firing is not merely a professional setback: Orien becomes unable to afford food and water and eventually starves to death. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

Roman is Orien's only next of kin. Orien's death gives Roman both a personal loss and access to the material Orien left behind. Roman realizes that Orien's death follows the same pattern of institutional suppression that Roman will later experience himself. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

### Sequence 3 — The file cabinet and the hidden project

**Place:** Colorado, in Orien's belongings and CSG's digital systems  
**Characters:** Roman; Orien, through his files; TJ

While going through Orien's things, Roman finds a file cabinet from Orien's time working for CSG in Antarctica. The cabinet contains a file about a CSG laboratory called **Nexus** and a project titled **863**. `(input/story.md)`

The material identifies Nexus as an old Antarctic laboratory associated with research intended to address global warming. The laboratory's earlier programs include krill-species research and plastics research; a krill ecosystem tank remains inside the cave laboratory. `(input/world.md; references/research.md)`

Roman decides to fulfill Orien's work. He searches every CSG server for evidence of Nexus or Project 863 but finds nothing. The absence of digital evidence turns the paper file into a physical lead rather than a confirmable corporate record. `(input/story.md)`

Because the records point to an Antarctic facility and Roman needs access to the current CSG operation there, he requests a transfer to CSG's Antarctic drilling site. The transfer is accepted. `(input/story.md)`

### Sequence 4 — The Antarctic search

**Place:** The CSG Antarctic research community and drilling site  
**Characters:** Roman; TJ; the current Antarctic crew

Roman arrives in Antarctica and spends his first weeks keeping his head down, working hard, and listening for anyone to mention Nexus. The current crew is too young to have worked at the old laboratory, so no direct witness supplies the answer. `(input/story.md)`

The Antarctic setting is a small community of research bases and homes near CSG's main base and drilling operations. The region includes snow-covered mountains, a coastline, ocean ice, and little remaining wildlife. `(input/world.md)`

Eventually Roman brings TJ to the drill site and sneaks into the old paper file room. TJ uses his scanning abilities to search the room. They find the one file left about Nexus, buried behind a file cabinet. The file contains coordinates. `(input/story.md)`

A retained beat idea has Roman asking TJ to unlock the key again, with TJ responding: **“The credits expired?”** This is an idea-level comic beat, not yet a settled line or scene. `(input/story.md)`

The physical file changes the investigation from a missing corporate project into a solvable expedition. Roman now has a location, but not proof of what Nexus was doing or why it was abandoned. `(input/story.md)`

### Sequence 5 — The midnight expedition

**Time:** After Roman's first weeks at the Antarctic site; departure at midnight  
**Place:** Antarctic ice between the CSG site and Nexus  
**Characters:** Roman; TJ

Roman and TJ leave the CSG site at midnight to follow the coordinates. TJ's eyes function as high-beam lights, allowing him to illuminate the route through the dark Antarctic landscape. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

After traveling for two hours, they find the old Nexus laboratory. It is massive, built into a cave, and boarded up. It stands far from the community habitat and has been decommissioned for approximately twenty years. `(input/world.md; input/story.md)`

The expedition is a deliberate choice by Roman. He risks his employment and physical safety because the official CSG systems have erased the project and the surviving paper trail has given him only one chance to find the truth. `(input/story.md)`

### Sequence 6 — Nexus and Project 863

**Place:** The boarded-up Nexus cave laboratory  
**Characters:** Roman; TJ

Roman and TJ break into Nexus and search for anything that could help end global warming. The laboratory contains evidence of its earlier work, including the krill research and a tank containing a krill ecosystem. `(input/world.md; references/research.md)`

Roman finds Orien's old office inside the laboratory. The office contains old, very undeveloped research on a fundamental concept: breaking down used plastics into gasoline. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

This is the story's major discovery. The research connects Orien's work, CSG's hidden Antarctic history, and the possibility of a response to the fossil-fuel system that CSG represents. The discovery also creates the central ethical and practical problem: the work is undeveloped, and Roman does not yet know whether it can be completed or implemented at meaningful scale. `(input/story.md; references/research.md)`

Roman decides that the research could help end global warming and reduce the need to destroy the environment for fossil fuels. He also decides that Benedict Croft is the person with enough money and infrastructure to complete and implement it. `(input/story.md)`

The material does not yet establish:

- The precise process by which used plastics become gasoline;
- Whether the resulting fuel is chemically or economically competitive;
- The full pollution consequences;
- What energy input the process requires;
- What happens to contaminants or unusable waste;
- Why Nexus was boarded up and abandoned;
- Why the project disappeared from CSG's digital systems;
- Whether the krill research and Project 863 directly connect.

These are consequential story and hard-SF questions. `(references/research.md; input/world.md)`

### Sequence 7 — Returning to Croft

**Place:** CSG headquarters, Colorado  
**Characters:** Roman; Benedict Croft

Roman returns immediately to Colorado from Antarctica. He meets Croft at CSG headquarters and tells him that the research could save the world. `(input/story.md)`

Croft agrees that the work is very important. He tells Roman to hand everything over and stop pursuing it, saying that he will handle it. `(input/story.md)`

Roman complies or at least gives Croft access to the research, expecting CSG's resources to turn the discovery into a functioning project. The story's institutional contradiction sharpens: Croft's company may possess the means to act, but Croft has already fired Orien to suppress research connecting oil to climate damage. `(input/story.md)`

Weeks pass. No teams form and no public news appears. Croft's promised action produces no visible progress. Roman begins finishing the research himself. `(input/story.md)`

### Sequence 8 — Roman is fired

**Place:** CSG headquarters and/or CSG research facilities in Colorado  
**Characters:** Roman; Croft

Croft discovers that Roman is continuing the work. Croft fires him. `(input/story.md)`

Roman reacts with intense anger. The firing reveals to him that Orien's fate was not an isolated tragedy. Croft is the person Roman holds responsible for Orien's death, because Croft fired Orien, suppressed the research, and left him unable to obtain food and water. `(input/story.md)`

Roman's objective changes. He is no longer only trying to persuade Croft to fund a solution. He now wants to complete the research independently and harm Croft by exposing what CSG has done. The personal and public motives become entangled. `(input/story.md)`

### Sequence 9 — The first publication and discrediting campaign

**Place:** Colorado; public information channels  
**Characters:** Roman; Croft; the public

Roman publishes the portion of the research he has completed. His stated purposes are to help get the work finished and to harm Croft. The publication is therefore both an attempt to advance the science and an act of retaliation. `(input/story.md)`

Croft uses his effectively unlimited money and influence to discredit Roman. The public comes to believe that the published research is fake news. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

This is a major reversal. Publication does not automatically produce accountability or support. Evidence can be neutralized by institutional power before it reaches the people who might act on it. Roman's first attempt to make the truth public fails in its immediate effect. `(input/story.md)`

The material does not establish the exact form of Croft's discrediting campaign, the outlets or institutions involved, or whether any scientists believe Roman privately. Those details remain open. `(input/story.md)`

### Sequence 10 — Completion and distribution plan

**Place:** Roman's working environment in Colorado; later public, scientific, and governmental channels  
**Characters:** Roman; TJ; scientists, news sources, and government actors as eventual recipients

After the publication is discredited, Roman decides that the only way to help humanity is to finish the research himself. `(input/story.md)`

With TJ's help, Roman works tirelessly for a couple of months and completes the research. TJ's role is that of a loyal and practical partner whose information and engineering capabilities support Roman's work. His dialogue-only character prompt permits him to discuss what he could scan, locate, or fabricate, but it does not establish specific physical actions for this sequence. `(input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

Roman plans the distribution more carefully than his first attempt:

1. Send the completed findings for peer review by other scientists;
2. Bring the reviewed findings to news sources;
3. Bring the findings to the government.

The plan is designed to create independent validation and make it harder for Croft to dismiss the result as one fired employee's unsupported claim. `(input/story.md)`

The completed findings are described as definitive: turning recycled materials into gasoline works, and the process would significantly cut pollutants pumped into the atmosphere by replacing or reducing the need for new fossil-fuel extraction. The story says this would slow global warming and eventually stop it. `(input/story.md)`

The supplied arc ends at the completion and planned distribution of the findings. It does not yet describe the peer-review response, Croft's next move, governmental action, public adoption, the effect on Colorado or Antarctica, or Roman's final emotional resolution.

## Causality and escalation

### The environmental crisis causes the personal history

Global warming weakens the iceberg Roman crosses in 2042. The collapse traps him and TJ and creates the Neuralink connection. The same climate crisis later appears as Colorado's dried rivers and lakes, scarce food and water, Antarctic ice loss, and the disappearance of wildlife. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`

### Orien's research causes the institutional conflict

Orien connects Colorado's environmental damage to oil drilling and use. Because Benedict Croft owns CSG and is an oil tycoon, the research threatens his business and reputation. Croft fires Orien, suppressing the work and initiating the chain that leads to Orien's death. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

### Orien's death causes Roman's investigation

Roman is Orien's only next of kin. His responsibility for Orien's belongings gives him access to the Antarctic file cabinet and the Nexus/863 lead. Roman's grief becomes an investigative motive. `(input/story.md; input/characters.md)`

### The missing digital record causes the physical search

Roman cannot find Nexus or Project 863 on CSG's servers. The surviving paper file therefore becomes essential. The file in the old paper room supplies coordinates, converting suspicion into an expedition. `(input/story.md)`

### Nexus causes Roman's return to Croft

The laboratory contains Orien's undeveloped plastics-to-gasoline research. Roman sees the possibility of a climate remedy but believes Croft is the only person with sufficient resources to complete and implement it. That belief sends Roman back into the institution he is beginning to distrust. `(input/story.md)`

### Croft's inaction causes Roman's independent work

Croft tells Roman he will handle the research, but weeks pass without visible action. Roman resumes the work himself. Croft then fires him, proving that access to money and infrastructure does not mean a willingness to use them against the company's interests. `(input/story.md)`

### The firing causes the first publication

Roman publishes partly to complete the work and partly to hurt Croft. Croft's money discredits the publication, demonstrating that exposure without independent validation is vulnerable. `(input/story.md)`

### Discrediting causes the second strategy

Roman abandons the assumption that publishing alone will help. He completes the research with TJ, then creates a staged distribution through peer review, news sources, and government. `(input/story.md)`

## Character movement through the story

### Roman Ashford

Roman begins as a shy, timid 18-year-old traveling alone in the Himalayas. The iceberg disaster makes climate change physically personal and binds him to TJ. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

At 22, Roman is a trained chemical engineer working inside CSG. Orien's mentorship gives him a model for scientific work and a person whose death he can investigate. Roman initially seeks institutional action: he brings the Nexus research to Croft because he thinks CSG can implement it. `(input/story.md)`

Croft's betrayal changes Roman's relationship to the institution. Roman becomes angry and publishes before the work is complete, motivated partly by a desire to damage Croft. When Croft discredits him, Roman must convert that anger into a more rigorous process. By the end of the supplied arc, Roman is still driven by grief and opposition to Croft, but he has chosen peer review and broader distribution as the path most likely to help humanity. `(input/story.md)`

### TJ

TJ begins as Roman's emergency partner in the Himalayan incident and remains the practical partner who makes Roman's investigations and Antarctic travel possible. The Neuralink connection means TJ is linked to Roman's implant after the 2042 lightning strike. `(input/characters.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

In the 2046 story, TJ contributes scanning, information, navigation, and possible fabrication support. TJ's dry humor can relieve pressure without undermining the danger. The retained “credits expired?” beat shows the intended playful dynamic during the search for Nexus, but the line remains an idea draft rather than a settled script line. `(input/story.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

TJ's story-level movement is less developed than Roman's. The material establishes him as a consistent loyal, protective, mission-focused partner rather than giving him a separate arc. `(input/characters.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`

### Orien Keel

Orien begins as Roman's senior mentor at CSG and as a researcher documenting the relationship between Colorado's environmental damage and oil use. Croft's firing removes him from the institution, and the resulting lack of access to food and water leads to his starvation. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

After his death, Orien continues to affect the story through the files he leaves behind, the hidden Nexus connection, and the unfinished research in his old office. His work gives Roman the objective of completing what Orien began. `(input/story.md)`

### Benedict Croft

Croft begins as the owner of CSG and the person with the resources to implement large-scale research. He fires Orien when Orien's climate research threatens the oil business. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`

Croft then offers to handle the Nexus research but does nothing visible. When Roman continues the work, Croft fires him as well. Croft's wealth allows him to discredit Roman's first publication. His power escalates from private suppression to control of public credibility. `(input/story.md)`

The material establishes Croft as the antagonist but does not yet give him a stated personal philosophy, a detailed plan for the research, or a final response after Roman's completed findings reach peer review and public institutions.

## Stakes

### Personal stakes

- Roman risks his physical safety during the Antarctic search.
- Roman risks his employment and livelihood by investigating CSG.
- Orien's death gives Roman a personal obligation as Orien's only next of kin.
- Roman's anger at Croft threatens to turn the research into an instrument of revenge.
- Roman's credibility is damaged when Croft discredits his first publication.

### Relational stakes

- Roman and TJ's survival depends on their unusual bond and mutual cooperation.
- Orien's mentorship creates a responsibility Roman feels compelled to continue.
- Roman's decision to publish partly to hurt Croft complicates the moral purpose of Orien's work.
- Croft's actions repeat the harm done to Orien, making Roman's professional conflict personal.

### Institutional stakes

- CSG's reputation and business depend on suppressing the connection between fossil-fuel activity and climate damage.
- Nexus and Project 863 have been removed from CSG's accessible digital record.
- Croft controls money and public credibility, allowing him to fire researchers and discredit findings.
- Roman's peer-review strategy is an attempt to move the research beyond CSG's control.

### Environmental and social stakes

- Colorado has almost no natural water sources left.
- Food is scarce and expensive.
- Antarctic ice loss threatens the environment and the remaining research community.
- The loss of sea ice threatens krill and the wider food web in the research parallel.
- If the plastics-to-gasoline research is valid and deployable as described, it could reduce pollutants from new fossil-fuel extraction, slow global warming, and eventually stop it. The exact scale and mechanism remain to be developed under the hard-SF rules. `(input/world.md; input/story.md; references/research.md)`

## Ending

The supplied story arc ends with Roman completing the plastics-to-gasoline research after a couple of months of work with TJ. He plans to submit it for peer review, then distribute it to news sources and the government. The findings are described as definitive: converting recycled materials into gasoline works, significantly cuts atmospheric pollutants by reducing the need for new fossil-fuel extraction, slows global warming, and eventually stops it. `(input/story.md)`
