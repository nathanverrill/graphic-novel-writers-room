# Avalanche-2 — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- The story is set in **2046**. Its principal settings are **Antarctica** and **Colorado**, with the Himalayas appearing only in the protagonist's backstory. The intended balance is approximately **80% familiar present-day reality, 15% emerging technology or conditions made ordinary, and 5% genuine change.** `(rules/rules.md; input/story.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- The world must be **hard science fiction**. Invented technologies, institutions, economic conditions, environmental changes and physical mechanisms must carry the labels **T**, **EG**, **S**, **L** or **Cut**, and must remain internally consistent. `(rules/rules.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- A central world tension is the loop of **destroying the environment to save the environment**: oil drilling and fossil-fuel use drive warming, while a waste-to-value process connected to an oil company may offer a way to reduce further extraction. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`
- A waste-to-value organization in Antarctica aids conservation in Colorado. The material identifies **Clear Spring Global (CSG)** as the company whose Antarctic research and Colorado operations are connected. `(rules/rules.md; input/story.md; input/world.md)`

## Overview

This is a near-future Earth in which most buildings, institutions, habits and technologies remain recognizable, but climate damage has become difficult to ignore in ordinary life. Colorado has lost most of its natural water sources, food is scarce and expensive, and the melting ice caps are raising ocean levels. Antarctica is still a working research frontier, but its ice, ecosystems and remaining wildlife are under severe pressure. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`

The major speculative development is not a wholly transformed future society but a familiar world under intensified environmental and corporate pressure. The setting retains research bases, company headquarters, paper records, file cabinets, food markets, laboratories and employment hierarchies alongside neural implants, artificial intelligence and robotic fabrication. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; input/characters.md; input/world.md; input/story.md)`

### Antarctica

- Antarctica contains a **snow-covered mountain range on a coastline**, with ice extending into the ocean. `(input/world.md)`

- Nexus contains or formerly contained research programs in **krill species** and **plastics**. Project 863 began as a combined program: krill decline supplied the ecological evidence that motivated the plastics-to-fuel work, while the two research tracks remained technically separate. The krill tank therefore has thematic and institutional importance but does not produce the fuel. `(input/world.md; rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`

- The CSG Antarctic operation includes a current drilling site and an older paper-file archive. `(input/story.md)`

## History and Environmental Change

### Climate pressure

- By 2046, global warming has produced severe and visible environmental consequences: melting ice caps, rising oceans, loss of Antarctic wildlife, dried Colorado rivers and lakes, and scarce or expensive food. `(input/story.md; input/world.md)`
- The story connects severe global warming to **oil drilling and oil use**. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`
- CSG's existing business therefore occupies a contradiction: the company conducts or owns oil-related drilling while also possessing research intended to address the environmental damage associated with that industry. `(input/world.md; input/story.md)`
- The Antarctic ice environment is not only scenic or remote infrastructure; its decline threatens ecological systems, including krill and the food web that depends on them. **Real-world reporting:** research supplied by the showrunner describes Antarctic sea-ice decline as a threat to krill and identifies krill as a base of the food web. This is true of the real world and is not automatically a book event. `(references/research.md)`
- **Research-informed proposal:** Antarctic ecological decline could be shown through reduced or disrupted krill habitat around the research community, making the surviving krill tank at Nexus a concrete contrast between managed and collapsing ecosystems. `(references/research.md; input/world.md)`

### Colorado water and ecology

- Colorado's drying rivers and lakes are part of the everyday environmental crisis, not a distant forecast. `(input/world.md; input/story.md)`
- **Real-world reporting:** USGS research supplied by the showrunner projects approximately **52% mean summer streamflow declines** and physical habitat loss for Greenback Cutthroat Trout under future climate scenarios. This is real-world research, not settled book canon. `(references/research.md)`
- **Research-informed proposal:** Colorado scenes could use reduced snowpack, earlier runoff, shrinking streams and stressed cold-water fish habitat as visible evidence of the wider crisis, rather than relying only on dialogue about global warming. `(references/research.md)`

### The 2042 incident

- In 2042, a lightning strike unexpectedly bonds a Neuralink implant to an artificial intelligence system in a robot dog. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`
- **EG:** Neuralink implants are common enough in 2042 that the material states that a majority of people have them. The story does not establish the exact percentage, legal status, manufacturer, medical risks, or standard capabilities of these implants. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`
- The lightning-mediated bonding is not established as an ordinary or repeatable procedure. Its mechanism remains an unusual project-world event. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md)`
- The event creates a persistent encrypted neural-comms bridge between Roman's implant and TJ's processor. It carries short thoughts, alerts and selected sensory data at close range, but not unrestricted mind-reading or unlimited remote access. The connection can drop under shielding, interference, distance or power loss, and prolonged high-bandwidth use causes Roman pain, confusion or neurological fatigue. `(rules/decisions.md; characters.md; story.md)`

## Institutions and Corporate Power

### Clear Spring Global

- **Clear Spring Global (CSG)** is a company with a headquarters in Colorado, a Colorado research or drilling operation, and an Antarctic research and drilling presence. `(input/characters.md; input/world.md; input/story.md)`
- CSG is owned by **Benedict Croft**, an old-money oil billionaire. `(input/characters.md)`
- CSG's corporate interests include oil drilling and use, while its buried or abandoned Antarctic research includes work intended to address global warming and convert selected waste plastics into gasoline-range hydrocarbons. `(input/story.md; input/world.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- CSG maintains or once maintained an Antarctic laboratory called **Nexus**, with a combined program called **Project 863**. `(input/story.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- By 2046, the current Antarctic crew is young enough that none of its members is known to have worked at Nexus. `(input/story.md)`
- CSG's older institutional memory is partly preserved in paper files. The current digital servers contain no evidence of Nexus or Project 863 because Croft ordered the records suppressed; an old paper archive at the drilling site still contains a file with coordinates leading to the laboratory. `(input/story.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- CSG operates under a national Antarctic research agreement and leases or manages its own facilities under overlapping government and treaty permissions. CSG owns the equipment and records at Nexus, while public authorities retain emergency and environmental oversight. Roman's unauthorized search is trespass under CSG rules, though enforcement is complicated by the facility's abandonment. `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`
- CSG can fire researchers whose findings threaten the company's interests, suppress or bury research, control access to company sites and records, and use its financial power to discredit inconvenient findings. `(input/characters.md; input/story.md)`
- CSG personnel in Antarctica send encrypted status messages to Croft's private operations office in Colorado. They report environmental conditions, research activity and unauthorized access through a company-controlled satellite link, making the messages part of Croft's surveillance and suppression system. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Nexus laboratory

- Nexus is a CSG laboratory built into a cave in Antarctica. It is massive, boarded up and far from the active research community. `(input/world.md; input/art-direction.md)`
- The laboratory was decommissioned approximately twenty years before the main story for ordinary budget and logistics reasons. It was not fully removed because of the cost and remoteness of the site. Croft later suppressed its digital records after recognizing the threat posed by the plastics research. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Its programs included technically separate krill research and plastics-to-fuel research under the combined Project 863 program. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Nexus includes a krill ecosystem tank that remains alive but degraded under isolated emergency power. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Orien Keel's old office remains inside Nexus and contains incomplete but reproducible Project 863 research on converting selected waste plastics into gasoline-range hydrocarbons. `(characters.md; story.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- The facility's physical security, air supply, heating and structural integrity are not otherwise established. Its emergency power includes the system sustaining the krill tank. `(input/world.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### TJ's robotic systems

- TJ is an artificial-intelligence robot dog assembled from spare parts by a scavenger-engineer known as **Bi11bot**, then released into the world. Bi11bot remains backstory only and does not enter the 2046 investigation. `(input/characters.md; input/tj-ai-agent.md; rules/decisions.md)`

- TJ has an internal 3D-printing fabricator in the chassis. It uses sorted feedstock, works slowly, produces small tools and replacement parts rather than large structures, and cannot reproduce complex electronics. `(input/characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- TJ carries rechargeable batteries and can supplement them from CSG or field equipment. Cold reduces capacity, and long-range travel requires rationing. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- TJ's eyes double as high-beam lights. His lights and emergency systems consume significant power. `(input/characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- TJ's emergency capabilities contributed to the 2042 escape but do not make him a consequence-free solution to later obstacles. `(input/characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- TJ's physical actions are shown silently in the art while spoken explanation remains in dialogue. He may say, “Give me a second, I'm running a scan now,” while the panel shows scan lights, a material readout or fabrication. `(rules/decisions.md; tj-ai-agent.md)`

### Plastic-to-gasoline process

- Project 863 is a **catalytic pyrolysis process** that converts selected waste plastics into gasoline-range hydrocarbons. **S, Speculation.** `(rules/decisions.md; references/research.md)`
- It requires substantial external heat and electricity. It cannot accept all plastics, and its feedstock must be selected and controlled rather than treated as undifferentiated waste. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The process produces contaminated residues that must be isolated and managed. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- It is climate-positive only when powered by low-carbon energy and paired with strict controls on the resulting fuel's use. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- It can reduce demand for new petroleum extraction, but it does not end fossil-fuel use or stop global warming by itself. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- Orien established the link between environmental damage and the need for a waste-to-fuel process, developed initial laboratory protocols and left a reproducible but incomplete method. Roman must supply the missing chemistry, testing and scale analysis. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The process is therefore not an unlimited or automatic climate solution. Its energy demand, feedstock limits, contaminated residues and fuel-use controls remain consequential deployment constraints. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- The material does not establish commercial-scale economics, exact yields, the full emissions profile, or the infrastructure required for deployment beyond these settled limits. `(references/research.md; rules/decisions.md)`
