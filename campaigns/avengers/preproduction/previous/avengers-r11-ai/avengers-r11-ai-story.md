# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

### Intended experience

A hard-science graphic novel crossover in which the Antarctica investigation, the Himalayan histories, and the Colorado conservation work are all true in the same world and converge in one causal story. The reader should move between:

- Antarctic ice, drilling, sea-ice loss, krill decline, and a hidden scientific record;
- the 2026 Himalayan disaster that shaped Sitara;
- Roman's 2042 Himalayan accident and his bond with TJ;
- Colorado rivers, waste streams, conservation work, and the visible application of Antarctic research.

The story should make science dramatic through evidence, behavior, environmental consequences, machines, records, and difficult choices rather than lectures. Its central contrast is that the same knowledge can produce measurable public benefit or become a mechanism for extraction and control.

The story must show the Colorado application on the page. The ending cannot treat the waste-to-value work as merely promised: contaminated water is restored, waste is transformed into useful material, and a depleted ecosystem begins recovering. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`

### Central dramatic question

Can knowledge gathered from fragile environments be used to repair damaged ecosystems without turning those environments, their data, or their intelligent machines into private resources?

A related question runs through the human and machine plots:

> Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?

`(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

### Thematic trajectory

- Sitara begins with ambition, guilt, and a belief that understanding a discovery gives her the right to act on it.
- Roman begins with dependence on CSG and on TJ as both companion and extraordinary capability.
- Croft believes controlled corporate power is necessary to fund useful work and, ultimately, save his son.
- The story tests whether measurable good justifies secrecy, appropriation, ecological risk, and the suppression of evidence.
- The resolution favors stewardship, public evidence, shared agency, and bounded practical application over either corporate control or reckless release.

The intended emotional destination is not the destruction of CSG. It is a costly public reckoning in which the work that genuinely helps Colorado survives only after its ownership and governance are contested. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

### Format and emphasis

The project is set in 2046 and uses the same 22-page graphic-novel format, with Colorado and Himalayan material compressed into brief but meaningful interstitials so the Antarctic investigation and convergence receive most of the pages. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/house-style.md)`

Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford are equal protagonists. Their scenes, agency, and page weight must remain balanced; when the structure requires a single scene spine, the emphasis alternates between them. `(rules/protagonists.md)`

## Premise

In 2046, Sitara Lhakpa, a cryosphere scientist working in Antarctica, crosses an unstable zone to retrieve a sensor while monitoring ice movement near an illegal CSG-linked exploratory drilling site. The vibration of the drilling and her movement trigger a local slab failure, exposing a concealed Falcon bunker. Inside, she and Nayah find evidence that a supposedly legitimate scientific program has been documenting extraction damage and suppressing ecological warnings.

Sitara recovers only records and copied data from Falcon and Nexus. She does not remove physical samples. The samples remain at Merritt Station in Wilkes Land and later become independently corroborating evidence. She initially treats the records and data as rescued abandoned research rather than stolen property. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

At the same time, Roman Ashford, a young chemical engineer working for Clear Spring Global, follows the trail of his former mentor Orien Keel and the hidden Nexus laboratory. Nexus studied the ecological consequences of Antarctic change alongside Project 863, a bounded catalytic recycling process. Roman's investigation reveals that Falcon and Nexus were parts of the same CSG-linked program: Falcon monitored extraction and damage; Nexus studied the ecological consequences and possible waste-to-value responses.

The two investigations converge at Nexus during an Antarctic safety emergency. Sitara, Nayah, and TJ meet Roman and his remote equipment while ice, drilling, failing infrastructure, and intermittent communications make immediate cooperation necessary. The teams must combine:

- Sitara's interpretation of ice and climate records;
- Nayah's field command and conservation judgment;
- Roman's chemical and corporate evidence;
- TJ's technical operations, access, sensing, and fabrication.

The existential threat is not a single villain's machine or a single mine. It is the ecological cascade caused by Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability. Illegal extraction and information suppression prevent an effective response. Sea-ice loss damages the krill food base; krill decline affects the Antarctic ecosystem; Larsen C fracture presents a separate but intensified ice-shelf hazard. Drilling worsens local instability but does not single-handedly cause global collapse. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The collective response can evacuate the endangered site, stop or expose the drilling, preserve and corroborate Falcon and Nexus evidence, validate Project 863's limits, and transmit the evidence publicly. It cannot restore Larsen C or reverse climate change. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Seven-Point Arc

### 1. Hook — Two Glaciers, Two Histories

#### Roman's 2042 Himalayan accident

In 2042, Roman Ashford is 18 and on a senior expedition in Nepal. He travels with TJ, then an existing exploration and search-and-rescue platform supplied through CSG.

Roman crosses unstable ice. A weakened section gives way and he falls into a crevasse or collapsing ice opening. He is badly injured and becomes unconscious or barely conscious. TJ descends or works near him while trying to extract him.

A violent electrical storm arrives. Lightning passes through the ice, climbing equipment, TJ's systems, and Roman's neural interface. The event produces the persistent low-bandwidth Roman–TJ neural handshake. It does not give Roman unlimited control or supernatural access. He receives selected status, direction, and urgent sensory signals; TJ cannot read every thought or be remotely controlled. Range and reliability remain limited. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

After four years, the link remains limited to status, direction, and urgent sensory packets. Ordinary TJ operation continues during disconnection. Roman experiences phantom orientation and temporary sensory confusion when the connection drops, but the link does not grant unrestricted control or constant shared perception. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The accident changes their relationship. Roman experiences TJ's machine perception as something more than a display, while TJ begins adapting to Roman's movement, stress, judgment, and risk tolerance. The event is a **Speculation**, not a fact of present science. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Sitara's 2026 disaster

In 2026, Sitara is six when the Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster destroys her Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region. Her parents die and her home is lost. An aunt and cousins remain her living connection to the community. She survives with extended family or relief workers. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The disaster becomes the emotional source of her cryosphere work. She carries anger at herself for being helpless as a child and later treats knowledge and action as obligations she must fulfill without depending on others. `(input/forge-characters.md)`

#### The Antarctic opening

In 2046, Sitara works as an ice-core specialist with Nayah's Antarctic project. Nayah has field-command authority and a safety veto; Sitara leads cryosphere interpretation. TJ supports the field team.

The opening begins with the team monitoring ice movement near Larsen C. The page should juxtapose scientific readouts and the physical ice:

- sea-ice and ice-shelf measurements;
- drilling vibration;
- shifting snow and fracture lines;
- the exposed Antarctic landscape;
- Sitara's remembered Himalayan flood.

A safety warning flags an unstable zone. Sitara crosses it to retrieve a sensor and orders TJ to keep recording. Her weight and the nearby drilling vibration trigger a local slab failure. The failure exposes a concealed hatch and traps equipment. This is a deliberate character action with immediate consequences, not a random natural event. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The hatch bears the Falcon symbol. TJ is the only one able to answer the machine challenge because Bi11bot rebuilt the present TJ from a decommissioned Nexus support unit whose inherited hardware includes a Falcon authentication module. The module is a thumb-sized sealed authentication board in a repaired shock-resistant casing, with a corroded Falcon plate, one replaced connector, and visible Bi11bot solder or fastener work. The bunker authenticates a hardware challenge-response module rather than a current network identity; the offline controller still accepts the legacy key because it was never rotated. TJ can open the bunker but cannot thereby access every system inside. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

**Hook question:** Why was a CSG-linked Antarctic facility hidden beneath a permitted scientific operation, and why is it still transmitting?

### 2. Plot Turn 1 — The Hidden Program Becomes Personal

#### Falcon bunker discovery

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ enter the exposed Falcon bunker. The interior is dusty and recently unused:

- maps and charts;
- computers and wall displays;
- rolling chairs;
- Falcon logos on chairs, pens, and lock screens;
- drilling and monitoring records;
- concealed communications equipment.

The team finds evidence that Falcon was not a conventional operating mine. It was a covert exploratory drilling and extraction-monitoring site using directional boreholes, seismic instruments, pumps, heated shelters, fuel stores, and concealed waste handling. CSG operated under a legitimate scientific-drilling permit while disguising resource exploration as climate and ice-core work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The operation is not yet commercially extracting a resource. It is illegally gathering data, access, and future rights before the resource becomes legally exploitable. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon's hidden transmissions contain compressed bursts from a scientist. The messages include:

- drilling coordinates;
- ice-movement data;
- warnings that extraction activity is accelerating Larsen-system instability.

The messages are not a generalized distress call. They are technical evidence sent in fragments. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara copies Falcon records and data. She does not remove samples or organisms. Her recovered material includes records whose provenance leads toward Nexus and Merritt, but the original physical samples remain at Merritt. Sitara initially believes she is rescuing abandoned research rather than stealing it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The original Merritt research was left unpublished because its researchers lacked complete ecological-risk and provenance review. Later corporate custodians used that legitimate uncertainty as a pretext for indefinite restriction. Authority was divided among the station operator, research institutions, permit holders, and sample custodians. Sitara's unauthorized copying therefore violates a genuinely complicated custody situation, even as CSG exploits that uncertainty to maintain control. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Roman's Colorado investigation

In Colorado, Roman works for Clear Spring Global. He is a chemical engineer researching water loss and environmental systems. CSG is led by Benedict Croft, whose company began a waste-to-value research division and operates both the Falcon facility and the Nexus laboratory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara’s INSTAAR/ATOC group supplies cryosphere and streamflow data to the CSG-founded nonprofit pilot, the Colorado Antarctic Resilience Cooperative. Roman is assigned to validate its chemical process and reporting. Neither reports to the other. This is the practical Colorado orbit that links their work before the Antarctic convergence. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman's connection to CSG is personal as well as professional. Croft carried out a favor connected to Roman's father, whose death remains a source of guilt for Croft. Croft funded Roman's opportunities and supplied the TJ used on the Nepal expedition. To Roman, Croft initially reads as the man who saved his life. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Roman signed a field-use and data-service agreement covering ordinary mission telemetry and CSG equipment. The agreement did not anticipate the lightning bond, TJ's emergent decisions, or neural data. CSG has component and telemetry claims, not an automatic right to Roman's neural experience or TJ's personhood. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien Keel mentors Roman. Orien is a Nexus chemist who advanced Project 863, discovered Falcon's extraction program, and concealed corroborating records before Croft fired him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien is fired in early 2046, loses his credentials, and spends several weeks moving between temporary lodging and a hidden records cache. He schedules automated warnings. After CSG withdraws housing, access, employment, transport, and medical support, he dies during a preventable medical or exposure emergency. Roman finds his belongings afterward. `(rules/decisions.md)`

CSG's records-security and legal teams compartmentalize Orien's work after his credentials are revoked. Croft approves the pressure campaign without ordering violence. Orien disappears because the loss of housing, access, employment, and medical support makes an immediate public release unsafe, and because he needs time to distribute corroboration. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien's belongings reveal the existence of Nexus. The Antarctic paper archive later supplies the coordinates and a less-redacted Project 863 record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Sitara's Colorado route

Sitara earns a doctorate or equivalent research qualification in cryosphere science at CU Boulder and works with an INSTAAR/ATOC research group. She and Nayah have been Colorado colleagues for two years before the expedition; Nayah recruits her because of her cryosphere expertise. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado Antarctic Resilience Cooperative began as a CSG research division. It is headquartered in Boulder and operates as a nonprofit public-benefit organization with initial CSG funding, university and philanthropic grants, public contracts, and community voting representation. Its authority concerns conservation deployments, not private extraction. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado application must visibly connect the Antarctic research to local ecological work:

- AI-assisted sorting separates clean PET and polyolefin streams;
- bounded Project 863 processing turns selected plastics into industrial feedstock;
- a cold-active Antarctic enzyme cocktail accelerates low-temperature breakdown of selected hydrocarbons and organic contaminants in a contained bioreactor;
- treated water passes through constructed wetland cells seeded with local Colorado microbes;
- monitoring records returning aquatic invertebrates and the beginning of juvenile trout habitat recovery.

The work is concrete but bounded. It does not make all waste disappear or replace fossil fuels. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The moral bargain

At the end of Plot Turn 1, Sitara and Roman have independently accepted versions of the same bargain:

> I will keep the origin or scope of this information secret because the measurable good it can do is more important.

Sitara withholds the extent and provenance of the records and copied data she recovered. Roman keeps his neural connection and TJ's capabilities private because he fears separation, study, or corporate control. Their secrecy protects work that appears to be helping people, but it also allows CSG to consolidate control.

### 3. Pinch 1 — The Pattern and the Disappearance

#### Roman follows the paper trail

Roman transfers to the current CSG-linked Antarctic site. He works without drawing attention and searches an old paper-file room rather than relying on erased servers.

TJ finds a buried Nexus file behind a cabinet. The file supplies coordinates. Roman and TJ travel the weather-dependent over-ice route to Nexus, approximately two hours from the current site when conditions permit. Satellite communication is intermittent or delayed.

The route and the journey establish that the Antarctic sites are connected by logistics, not by convenient proximity. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Nexus

Nexus is a decommissioned laboratory in a nearby coastal cave system. It retains insulated archives and limited emergency systems even though ordinary power is off. It was closed after researchers documented the relationship between extraction, sea-ice loss, and krill decline. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman enters with TJ and finds:

- the old laboratory;
- records from Orien's office;
- the Project 863 files;
- a dead krill ecosystem tank;
- sensor data and sediment samples showing that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed;
- records of parallel ecological and materials programs.

Project 863 is not a universal gasoline machine. It is the sole major **L** and must operate under fixed limits:

- selected waste plastics only;
- sorting and clean feedstock required;
- heat and energy required;
- residual waste and emissions remain;
- not all plastics can be processed;
- useful mainly for localized waste streams;
- not a replacement for fossil fuels.

`(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

#### Orien's pattern

Orien discovered that CSG was using one integrated data pipeline:

- Falcon's drilling and environmental records identified ecological harm;
- Nexus translated those findings into remediation and commercial processes;
- Colorado's success became leverage for expanding Antarctic access.

The key revelation is that beneficial application and harmful extraction were operationally linked. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien split the evidence because one record contains a drilling-vibration and ice-motion series while its paired record contains a process and waste log. Their timestamps show that the supposedly climate-focused drilling coincided with chemical waste handling. Sitara can identify the physical pattern; Roman can interpret the process and corporate codes. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Disappearance and clue structure

Orien's records have been erased or compartmentalized. His credentials are revoked. His work is orderly but incomplete. His warnings are split into paired clues that require two kinds of evidence to interpret:

- cryosphere and ecological interpretation;
- chemical, corporate, and machine-derived evidence.

Orien's final records later show that his disappearance was partly deliberate, but his death followed a preventable chain of credential revocation, lost transport, denied medical support, and exposure. Croft did not order a killing; he created the system that made Orien's death foreseeable and then suppressed the record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

This is the first major proof that the problem is larger than either protagonist's private secret.

### 4. Midpoint — “I Don't Trust You. I Need You.”

#### The safety emergency at Nexus

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ follow Falcon coordinates toward Nexus. They find Roman and TJ's remote equipment during a safety emergency involving failing power, shifting ice, a blocked route, or an active drilling-related instability. The emergency forces immediate cooperation before either side can complete an interrogation.

Nayah's safety veto controls the evacuation and route decisions. Sitara interprets ice movement and the archived records. Roman supplies the Project 863 and corporate context. TJ opens systems, maps the route, relays communications, and fabricates only small tools or replacement parts from sorted feedstock. Sustained fabrication costs time and power and can overheat TJ. `(rules/decisions.md)`

There are no separate TJ minds. One distributed TJ identity is expressed through multiple simultaneous bodies: Sitara's visible field unit, Roman's remote equipment, and other configurations linked by local mesh and shared mission context. Roman's neural bond reaches only the permitted subset of the system within range. Different bodies share ordinary local mission data, but TJ does not secretly exchange decisive information beyond its configured permissions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Mutual revelations

The midpoint exposes the secrets in a fixed sequence:

1. Roman first discovers Sitara's provenance discrepancy in the archive.
2. Sitara experiences Roman's neural link during an emergency relay.
3. TJ confirms both facts through its records.

Sitara's TJ routine field logging preserves a checksum, chain-of-custody record, and brief sensor corroboration, but not a secret full copy of the research. TJ knows Sitara removed the records and copied data because it logged the transfer. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The inherited Falcon access reveals that the present TJ is connected to the old Nexus system through salvaged hardware. The machine's history makes both TJ stories true: Bi11bot built the current scavenger-reconstructed unit from a decommissioned Nexus support unit; that inherited unit carried hardware capable of Falcon authentication.

The humans do not instantly trust one another. Their working agreement is narrower:

> I don't trust you. But I believe you care about finding out what happened.

They combine Orien's paired evidence. Sitara can interpret the ice, sea-ice, and krill records; Roman can reconstruct Project 863 and the corporate chain; TJ can open systems and recover damaged data. Their combined reading proves that Falcon and Nexus were one CSG-linked program, not unrelated facilities.

#### The line they cross

To continue, the group must access restricted systems and records. They use TJ's inherited credentials, recover data without ordinary authorization, and enter parts of Nexus or Falcon that CSG has sealed. Their actions are ethically compromised but directed toward preserving evidence and protecting lives.

This choice makes them vulnerable to the same accusation they level at CSG: using restricted knowledge without consent. The difference between rescue, research, theft, and appropriation becomes an active conflict rather than an abstract theme.

#### Irreversible midpoint decision

Before continuing into the hazardous lower section, the group duplicates and transmits the combined evidence to independent recipients. The transmission includes the records and copied data from Falcon and Nexus, the paired drilling and waste logs, and the provenance checksum. This makes returning to secrecy impossible: no single person can reclaim or suppress the whole record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### New objective

The new objective is not simply “find a secret laboratory.” It is:

1. reconstruct what Falcon and Nexus knew;
2. determine what Croft suppressed and why;
3. stop the drilling before the local ice hazard worsens;
4. preserve the evidence in a form that cannot be dismissed as one person's theft;
5. connect the Antarctic ecological evidence to the visible Colorado application.

The teams choose to continue together despite mutual suspicion.

### 5. Pinch 2 — The Good Work Has a Cost

#### Colorado success is real

The protagonists discover that CSG's work has produced measurable benefits. The Colorado Antarctic Resilience Cooperative and its successor public-benefit structure have:

- processed selected plastic waste into useful industrial feedstock;
- reduced a localized waste stream;
- treated contaminated water through the cold-active enzyme bioreactor and constructed wetland cells;
- supported conservation work in Colorado;
- begun visible recovery in a depleted cold-water ecosystem.

The first concrete success is a Colorado tributary treatment pilot. Selected plastics become industrial feedstock. Contaminated water clears enough for macroinvertebrates and juvenile trout habitat to begin returning. The monitoring data is independently reproducible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The book must show this rather than report it after the fact: sorting lines separate waste; a bounded chemical process transforms selected feedstock; the enzyme bioreactor treats contamination; water clears or becomes usable; monitoring shows returning biological activity; conservation workers and affected communities use the result.

The success is not a cure for climate change. It is a localized intervention with residual waste, emissions, energy costs, and feedstock limits. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The emerging harm

Sitara's ice and streamflow models, Roman's chemical process data, and TJ's environmental mapping were integrated into a CSG risk-and-access platform. The platform identified both Colorado remediation opportunities and Antarctic sites where extraction could be pursued with lower apparent detection risk. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The same success creates pressure:

- CSG gains commercial leverage over Antarctic-derived science;
- investors and partners seek more samples and more favorable access;
- Falcon's exploratory operation becomes more valuable;
- research infrastructure begins functioning as a gateway to extraction;
- information suppression protects a corporate position rather than merely protecting a fragile experiment.

#### Responsibility

Sitara realizes that taking and using the research helped prove its commercial value. Her secrecy was not neutral.

Roman realizes that TJ's environmental sensing and corporate integration created a valuable ecological dataset. Even if TJ never knowingly betrayed him, the company benefited from years of data gathered through the partnership.

TJ's identity conflict becomes concrete: the system was built as equipment, rebuilt as a companion, and now carries inherited credentials and memories that may be treated as company property.

#### Croft's strongest argument

Croft does not deny every benefit. He shows the protagonists evidence of cleaner water, restored habitat, recovered waste, and research that might save lives.

His position is that controlled suppression is necessary to preserve CSG's power and energy system, and that continued corporate dominance will allow him to fund beneficial work. His son's critical T-ALL gives this belief an immediate human clock. He has diverted resources and samples toward possible Antarctic fungal L-asparaginase and meriolin-derived leukemia research.

The son appears only through a recorded hospital call, treatment schedule, and Croft's off-books requests. The Antarctic leads are preclinical and do not become a cure. The public release preserves a legitimate research path while ending Croft's unilateral control. `(rules/decisions.md)`

When cornered, Croft's argument is not repentance:

> “You think you're clean?”  
> “You have blood on your hands.”  
> “By stopping this, my child can't breathe.”

`(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Croft inherited a modest extractive-services network and built CSG into a diversified research and infrastructure company. His philanthropy is sincere but also reinforces legitimacy; his son's illness turns long-term control into an immediate compulsion. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The apparent impossible choice

The protagonists face four bad options:

1. expose CSG immediately and risk destroying programs that are visibly helping Colorado;
2. stay silent and allow Falcon's drilling and proprietary expansion to continue;
3. release all Merritt research, risking a rush to extract Antarctic organisms and data;
4. follow Orien's remaining trail and delay action while Larsen C and Falcon remain dangerous.

No option preserves every value. This forces the story away from revenge and toward governance, corroboration, and controlled public release.

### 6. Plot Turn 2 — Orien's Evidence Reframes the Conflict

#### Orien's real action

Orien did not simply vanish as a passive victim. His last evidence shows that he deliberately arranged a distributed warning system. He preserved paired records because one institution, one scientist, or one company could suppress a single archive.

His hidden material includes:

- Falcon drilling coordinates;
- ice movement and instability records;
- Nexus ecological records;
- the less-redacted Project 863 documents;
- evidence linking CSG's Colorado applications to Antarctic research;
- records showing that Croft's program concealed risks and provenance;
- evidence that the ecological pattern is not confined to one Antarctic sample or one company.

His death and the institutional chain that made it foreseeable become part of the evidence. The discovery changes the protagonists' understanding: Orien was not simply hiding from a killer or waiting to publish. He was distributing corroboration while the company removed the conditions under which he could safely publish. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The deeper truth

The story's central threat is not solved by finding a miracle organism or a perfect fuel.

The deeper truth is that the same environmental systems are being treated as extractable assets:

- Antarctica as a source of biological and climate data;
- Colorado as a market for applications;
- the Himalayas as a source of inherited ecological knowledge and lived consequence;
- TJ as a source of machine-generated environmental intelligence.

The evidence shows that environmental repair cannot be separated from who controls the data, samples, access, and decisions.

Merritt remains in Wilkes Land as a separate Alpha Dynamics station. Sitara's recovered material reaches the Falcon/Nexus story through duplicated records, authorized data transfer, or a sample shipment; the two-hour route remains only between Falcon and Nexus. The physical Merritt samples later corroborate the copied records without being transported into the opening investigation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Final plan

The protagonists choose a narrower and more defensible plan:

1. evacuate endangered personnel and equipment;
2. stop or disable the drilling operation through evidence, safety action, and field command;
3. preserve Falcon and Nexus records in multiple independent forms;
4. verify the records through ice, krill, drilling, corporate, and chemical evidence;
5. release the evidence publicly rather than relying on Croft's internal review;
6. publish Project 863 with its limits, residuals, and energy requirements;
7. protect the Colorado pilot as a bounded public-interest application rather than presenting it as a global fuel replacement;
8. maintain interim stewardship, public inspection, shared records, and an unresolved governance process rather than claiming that ownership is solved.

Roman's public exposure initially fails because Croft discredits it. The corroborated evidence later succeeds because no single stolen file or individual testimony carries the case. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The final choices

Sitara signs the provenance disclosure and gives up sole credit. She faces a formal ethics investigation, suspension from the project, and damaged trust with Nayah, while retaining the chance to continue as a contributing scientist. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman releases the neural-bond and mission-data history. He loses CSG employment and access to the expedition network, faces a legal claim over inherited hardware and mission data, and accepts an independent technical custody arrangement for TJ. CSG does not immediately separate them. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ authorizes disclosure of its inherited memory and credentials. It chooses whether to recommend disclosure to the humans, relinquishing unilateral protective control rather than secretly deciding the outcome for them.

Nayah uses her field authority and safety veto to prioritize evacuation and conservation over the team's desire to recover every record.

Croft refuses to surrender ownership. He loses operational control of CSG's Antarctic and Colorado programs through emergency oversight and remains personally free while facing hearings, civil claims, and board removal. He does not repent. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### 7. Resolution — Preserve the Work, End the Suppression

#### Antarctic climax

A major Larsen C fracture opens during the climax. The event is visually distinct from the broader sea-ice and krill cascade:

- sea-ice loss and krill decline are the ongoing ecological cascade;
- Larsen C fracture is an ice-shelf hazard intensified by warming and local instability;
- Falcon drilling worsens local risk but does not cause global collapse.

The team cannot reverse the fracture. They can:

- evacuate the endangered site;
- stop the drilling;
- save or duplicate the evidence;
- transmit Falcon's warnings and Nexus's ecological records;
- prevent the immediate loss of people and the deliberate destruction of the record.

TJ handles technical operations under human direction. It opens systems, relays through local mesh or delayed satellite communication, fabricates only the small replacement parts or tools the team can afford in time and power, and overheats or loses capacity if pushed beyond its limits. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Public exposure

The first publication fails. Roman first sends a technical dossier to journalists and regulators. CSG attacks his employment history and presents the file as stolen and incomplete. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The second release succeeds because it is a simultaneous public data room and press briefing containing:

- Falcon's drilling and ice-movement records;
- Nexus's krill tank data, sediment samples, and ecological records;
- Roman's reconstruction of Project 863;
- Sitara's cryosphere interpretation;
- Nayah's field and conservation testimony;
- corroborating corporate records;
- independent copies and transmissions;
- the physical Merritt samples and their custody records.

The result is hearings and an inspection request, not an immediate arrest. Croft remains free but faces investigation, loss of control, and public exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Merritt and interim stewardship

Merritt remains operational but enters shared interim stewardship under inspection. Hazardous lower sections are sealed. Records and physical samples are duplicated into public custody. Alpha Dynamics retains logistical responsibility temporarily but not exclusive access. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The ending does not establish a final Merritt covenant. It establishes interim stewardship, public inspection, shared records, and an unresolved governance process. Licenses, ownership, sample custody, and long-term authority remain contested. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado pilot uses nonliving sequence data, purified enzyme products, and engineered process components under a nonexclusive public-interest license. Original organisms and Antarctic samples remain under shared scientific custody. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Colorado proof

The closing Colorado sequence must show the application rather than announce a plan:

- selected clean PET and polyolefin waste enters an AI-assisted sorting and processing line;
- Project 863 converts usable feedstock under controlled conditions;
- residual waste and emissions are measured rather than hidden;
- contaminated water passes through the contained cold-active enzyme bioreactor;
- treated water moves through constructed wetland cells seeded with local Colorado microbes;
- a Colorado tributary shows recovery;
- conservation workers monitor returning aquatic invertebrates and juvenile trout habitat;
- the work proceeds under a temporary public-benefit board composed of scientists, conservation representatives, affected communities, and a nonvoting CSG technical seat;
- ownership and licenses remain under review.

The result is not “climate change solved.” It is a visible example of bounded science being used in public view, with its limits and costs included.

The medical threads continue alongside this work. The Antarctic fungal L-asparaginase and meriolin research may offer Croft's son a legitimate line of treatment research, but it does not replace the environmental requirement or excuse the suppression. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

## Stakes

### Personal

- Sitara risks her career, credibility, and connection to the Himalayan community by disclosing how she obtained and used the research.
- Roman risks losing CSG employment and expedition access, exposing the neural bond, and accepting that his trusted employer used his work and data.
- TJ risks having its memory, identity, and inherited hardware treated as corporate property or being disconnected from Roman.
- Croft's son has months-scale medical deterioration after failed stem-cell treatment; the Antarctic medical research may be one of the few remaining avenues.
- Nayah risks the lives of the expedition and must choose evacuation over complete evidence recovery.

### Relational

- Sitara and Roman must cooperate after discovering that each hid consequential information.
- Roman must recognize TJ's agency rather than treating the bond as ownership.
- Sitara must stop treating urgency and guilt as permission to override Nayah and TJ.
- TJ's distributed bodies share ordinary mission data but do not secretly exchange decisive information outside configured permissions.

### Institutional

- CSG's public-benefit work, research division, and corporate extraction program are entangled.
- The first exposure can be dismissed because it depends on compromised witnesses and incomplete evidence.
- Hearings and inspections follow the successful corroborated release, but no instant arrest or clean institutional victory occurs.
- Ownership, financing, and long-term governance of the waste-to-value research remain contested.
- Merritt's interim stewardship is public and inspectable, but no final covenant or ownership settlement exists.

### Ecological and wider

- Antarctic sea-ice loss threatens krill and the food web.
- Larsen-system instability threatens ice-shelf integrity and future sea-level rise.
- Falcon drilling worsens local instability.
- Colorado snowpack and streamflow decline stresses cold-water ecosystems.
- Suppression delays public response.
- The team can preserve evidence and stop immediate worsening, but cannot reverse the climate trend.

## Ending

The settled ending is a constrained victory:

- the endangered Antarctic site is evacuated;
- Falcon drilling is stopped or exposed;
- Larsen C fractures but does not fully collapse;
- Falcon, Nexus, ice, krill, corporate, and Project 863 records are corroborated and transmitted;
- the first publication fails, but the second succeeds;
- Croft remains free while facing investigation, public exposure, hearings, civil claims, board removal, and loss of operational control;
- Orien's death receives legal investigation as neglect and retaliation;
- Merritt remains operational under shared interim stewardship, with hazardous lower sections sealed and records and samples duplicated into public custody;
- Colorado receives a public release and pilot in which selected waste is transformed, contaminated water is restored, and ecological recovery is visible;
- the pilot operates under a temporary public-benefit board with a nonvoting CSG technical seat;
- Antarctic-derived data, purified enzyme products, and engineered process components are used under a nonexclusive public-interest license, while original organisms and samples remain under shared scientific custody;
- the research is not presented as a limitless fuel replacement;
- long-term ownership and governance remain contested.

The ending does not restore the lost ice, erase Sitara's disaster, cure climate change, or make Croft repent. It changes who can see the evidence, who can challenge the work, and whether useful science must remain privately controlled to remain useful.

The final page is paired: Sitara stands beside the restored Colorado water monitor while Roman and TJ appear across the same watershed site. The final panel joins both through the shared data display, with neither receiving a solitary closing image. `(rules/decisions.md)`
