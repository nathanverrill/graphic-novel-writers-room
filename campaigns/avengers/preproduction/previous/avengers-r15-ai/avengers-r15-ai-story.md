# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Canon — premise and intended experience.** The book is a crossover: Sitara Lhakpa and Nayah’s Falcon-base story and Roman Ashford’s Nexus story are both true in the same world and must converge rather than replace one another. The story is set in 2046 across Antarctica, the Himalayas as backstory, and Colorado. `(rules/avengers.md; rules/rules.md)`

The intended experience is a hard-science graphic novel about climate evidence, corporate power, scientific stewardship, waste-to-value research, and the question of whether useful knowledge may be controlled by whoever extracts it first. The story should make the reader see both the damage and the measurable good: Antarctic sea-ice loss, krill decline, unstable Larsen ice, Colorado water stress, recovered waste, cleaner water, and returning aquatic life. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; input/seb.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can doing measurable good justify violating scientific stewardship, consent, and ownership?

A related question connects the Antarctic mystery to Roman and TJ’s relationship:

> Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?

These questions are present in the current draft and story notes, but the final book should answer them through choices rather than speeches. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

The protagonists are **Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford together**, with equal plot weight, agency, and presence. When the structure requires choosing a scene lead, the story alternates between them. Nayah, TJ, and the supporting characters must contribute necessary capabilities rather than turn either protagonist into a sidekick. `(rules/protagonists.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Premise

**Canon — core threat.** Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability are producing a combined ecological cascade. Sea-ice loss undermines krill reproduction and therefore the food web; atmospheric and oceanic warming intensify Larsen C’s fracture risk. Illegal extraction and information suppression are the human mechanisms preventing an adequate response. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

CSG’s Antarctic program contains two linked sites:

- **Falcon** is the covert operational and extraction-monitoring site. It presents illegal exploratory drilling as permitted climate and ice-core work.
- **Nexus** is the research site connected to the same CSG-linked program. It studied ecological consequences, krill and ice data, and bounded waste-to-value responses.

The waste-to-value work began as a CSG research division. Nexus was its laboratory; Falcon was CSG’s covert extraction and monitoring facility. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon is not yet commercially extracting an Antarctic resource. It conducts directional drilling, seismic monitoring, pumping, heated shelter operations, fuel storage, and concealed waste handling in order to claim data, access, and future rights before a resource becomes legally exploitable. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The central research is **Project 863**, which uses catalytic chemical recycling to depolymerize selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. It requires sorting, heat, energy, and clean feedstock; produces residual waste and emissions; cannot process all plastics; and is useful mainly for localized waste streams rather than replacing fossil fuels. Project 863 is the story’s sole major **L**. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Nexus’s cold-active enzyme research lowers the temperature and energy required for a bounded pretreatment step. The actual depolymerization remains the licensed process. The Colorado application demonstrates value without becoming a universal cure. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`

## Narrative Engine and Causal Spine

1. Sitara and Nayah monitor unstable Antarctic ice near Falcon.
2. Sitara crosses a flagged zone to retrieve a sensor and orders TJ to keep recording.
3. Her weight, combined with nearby drilling vibration, triggers a local slab failure.
4. The failure exposes Falcon’s buried hatch and traps equipment.
5. TJ opens the hatch using inherited Falcon authentication hardware.
6. Sitara finds evidence that the drilling is not legitimate climate work and receives the first half of Orien’s paired warning.
7. Roman’s separate investigation leads him from Colorado, through Orien’s belongings and the Antarctic paper archive, to Nexus.
8. Both teams reach Nexus.
9. A Larsen-linked fracture damages Nexus and forces immediate cooperation.
10. Sitara recognizes Roman’s neural bond when TJ responds to danger before Roman receives the ordinary alert.
11. Roman finds the Merritt provenance record in Sitara’s field cache.
12. They exchange their hidden evidence because neither half can establish the truth alone.
13. The combined evidence reveals that CSG’s Colorado successes depend on Antarctic research and samples routed through Merritt without the required ecological safeguards.
14. Orien is found near death by Sitara’s TJ. He never regains consciousness.
15. The teams reconstruct Orien’s warnings: Falcon’s environmental and drilling records align with Nexus’s process and waste records.
16. The evidence establishes the ecological cascade, the illegal operation, the suppression campaign, and Croft’s causal and ethical responsibility.
17. Croft attempts to preserve control through legal and technical pressure, arguing that disclosure will cost his critically ill son access to treatment.
18. The protagonists choose public corroboration rather than revenge or a private bargain.
19. Larsen C fractures further but does not fully collapse. The team stops the drilling, evacuates, preserves the evidence, and transmits it.
20. The public release produces hearings and an inspection request, not immediate arrests.
21. Project 863 is released into a Colorado pilot whose limits and tradeoffs remain visible.
22. The ending leaves ownership, financing, and long-term governance contested while making suppression impossible.

## Story So Far

### Interstitial: Roman’s Himalayan Origin — 2042

**Material type: explicit decision, supported by idea draft.**

Roman Ashford is eighteen and on a personal senior climbing expedition in Nepal. The trip is unrelated to Sitara, her family, and Merritt research. The changing ice and water systems provide the environmental connection between their lives, not a shared expedition or pre-existing relationship. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman crosses unstable ice with TJ, then falls through a weakened section. The earlier draft describes an electrical storm arriving while Roman is unconscious or barely conscious. Lightning travels through the ice, climbing equipment, TJ’s systems, and Roman’s neural interface. The resulting connection is not telepathy or unlimited control: it becomes a persistent, low-bandwidth neural handshake. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md; input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Roman receives selected status, direction, and urgent sensory signals from TJ. TJ receives Roman’s attention, urgency, balance, and intent. The connection is unreliable at range, through shielding, after power loss, or when damaged systems reconnect. Roman cannot read all TJ data or remotely control TJ. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The accident becomes the foundation of their four-year partnership. Roman initially treats TJ as both trusted companion and extraordinary capability. The story later forces him to recognize that partnership requires TJ’s agency rather than ownership. `(input/prahul-notes.md; drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

### Interstitial: Sitara’s Himalayan Origin — 2026

**Material type: explicit decision, supported by research and character draft.**

Sitara is six when the Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster destroys her home and kills her parents. She survives with extended family or relief workers. Her aunt and cousins remain her living connection to an unnamed fictional Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The disaster is not shown as a simple personal tragedy detached from the main plot. It establishes Sitara’s lifelong attention to unstable ice, water, and the consequences of delayed warnings. There are no settled physical injuries or lasting bodily consequences from the disaster. Sitara is presented as twenty-six in 2046. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The event produces her starting wound: she believes she was helpless, a burden, and too late to act. Her later recklessness grows from an attempt to disprove that helplessness. `(input/forge-characters.md)`

### Pages 1–2: Hook — The Crack and the Warning

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 1–2 are the Hook. Page 1 includes a brief Himalayan interstitial; the primary present-tense action is Sitara-led. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Near Larsen C in November 2046, Sitara, Nayah, and TJ monitor ice movement. The setting is a coastal Antarctic research zone with ice, snow-covered ridges, research bases, homes, and a remote Falcon operation. Their instruments register movement and drilling vibration. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

A warning flags an unstable zone. Sitara crosses it to retrieve a sensor and orders TJ to keep recording. This is a consequential choice, not an accident without an agent: her weight and nearby drilling vibration trigger a local slab failure. The failure exposes the buried Falcon hatch and traps equipment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Page-turn image:** the exposed hatch beneath broken ice, engraved with the Falcon symbol, while drilling vibration continues below. TJ is the first to orient toward it. The reader sees that the ice failure has uncovered something deliberately concealed before Sitara fully understands what it is. `(input/amelia.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The Himalayan interstitial establishes the visual and emotional echo: young Sitara watches floodwater take her home; adult Sitara now stands on unstable ice and chooses to continue. The juxtaposition makes the present decision personal without explaining her entire history. `(input/amelia.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

### Pages 3–5: Plot Turn 1 — Falcon Opens

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 3–5 are Plot Turn 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Falcon hatch is a submarine-like metal door buried in snow and ice. TJ answers the machine challenge using a salvaged Falcon authentication module inherited through Alpha Dynamics reconstruction. The module grants access to the bunker’s machine challenge but not to every system inside. `(rules/decisions.md)`

This reconciles TJ’s two histories: TJ was built by Alpha Dynamics from parts salvaged from a decommissioned Nexus support unit, and that unit carried Falcon authentication hardware. TJ is one robot, not two units or two separate characters. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Inside Falcon, Sitara and Nayah find:

- drilling maintenance files;
- vibration logs;
- ice-motion plots;
- maps and charts;
- computers carrying Falcon logos;
- concealed drilling coordinates;
- restricted communications records;
- falsified or incomplete scientific manifests;
- evidence that the site is operational rather than abandoned.

The visual language includes dusty workstations, rolling chairs, wall computers, Falcon-marked pens and lock screens, and the contrast between an apparently scientific facility and the industrial equipment hidden beneath it. `(input/amelia.md; rules/decisions.md)`

A compressed secret transmission is present in the maintenance records. It contains drilling coordinates, ice-movement data, and warnings that the extraction program is accelerating Larsen-system instability. The warning is not a complete explanation: it is one half of a repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara recognizes that the operation is not simply researching climate change. It is using a legitimate scientific-drilling permit to conceal illegal exploratory extraction. CSG’s subcontractors, restricted communications, falsified manifests, and isolated weather windows are part of the concealment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nayah asserts field-command authority and a safety veto. Sitara wants to keep searching because the records may explain the instability. The first conflict between them establishes that Sitara’s drive for truth can become dangerous when she treats urgency as permission. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

The team cannot safely remain at Falcon. A route, coordinate, or archived reference points toward Nexus, the research facility connected to the same CSG-linked program. Their immediate goal changes from documenting a strange bunker to reaching the source of the ecological and chemical records before the drilling operation moves or destroys them.

### Pages 6–8: Pinch 1 — The Two Records

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 6–8 are Pinch 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Falcon evidence shows local drilling vibration and ice instability, but it does not explain the broader ecological cascade. Sitara interprets the ice-motion plots and recognizes that the drilling is worsening local risk without single-handedly causing global climate change. The distinction matters: the operation is culpable for intensifying a dangerous site, not for causing all Antarctic warming. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara’s TJ preserves local copies of Falcon environmental records and Merritt provenance data in separated mission partitions. TJ does not secretly upload the full archive. It has exchanged only route, safety, and sensor summaries through CSG systems, and it withholds the provenance archive after detecting that CSG access patterns conflict with Sitara’s stated mission. `(rules/decisions.md)`

In parallel, Roman investigates Orien Keel’s disappearance and death in Colorado.

Orien was a Nexus chemist who advanced Project 863, discovered Falcon’s extraction program, and hid corroborating records before Croft fired him. After the firing, Orien loses transport and corporate medical support at an isolated CSG facility. He records and schedules warnings before attempting to leave. A delayed evacuation leaves him exposed; he is later found near death by Sitara’s TJ and never regains consciousness. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The legal investigation treats Orien’s death as neglect and corporate retaliation. The evidence establishes Croft’s causal and ethical responsibility but not that Croft directly ordered a killing. CSG’s legal, investor, and compliance structures exert institutional pressure without becoming separate villains. Croft remains the principal operative antagonist. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman’s route into the mystery combines two discoveries:

1. Orien’s belongings reveal that Nexus existed.
2. An Antarctic paper archive supplies Nexus’s coordinates and a less-redacted Project 863 record.

This uses both versions of the discovery rather than choosing between them. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman’s motivation is complicated by his history with Croft. Croft funded Roman through his father’s friend, supplied TJ, and became the man Roman believed had saved his life. Croft later calls in that debt, asking Roman to help with chemical work connected to his son’s treatment. Roman agrees for a limited period, believing that helping a dying child may justify secrecy. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

### Pages 9–12: Midpoint — The Teams Meet at Nexus

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 9–12 are the Midpoint. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman follows the Antarctic paper coordinates to Nexus. Sitara and Nayah follow Falcon’s coordinate trail toward the same site. The journey is a weather-dependent two-hour over-ice route by tracked vehicle or equivalent field transport, with intermittent or delayed satellite communication. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nexus is built into a nearby coastal cave system. Its present condition reflects abandonment and partial restoration: warped metal corridors, blue ice intrusion, frozen cables, obsolete equipment, and snow entering through structural fractures. Deeper routes include damaged shafts, collapsed bulkheads, and flooded or refreezing passages. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/refinements.md)`

The laboratory contains:

- the dead krill ecosystem tank;
- archived sensor data;
- sediment samples;
- ice and ecological records;
- Project 863 calibration sheets;
- chemical process records;
- waste-routing records;
- Merritt research provenance;
- evidence that ecological and materials programs ran in parallel.

The tank is dead, but its sensor data and sediment samples show that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien’s second set of warnings is concealed in Project 863 calibration sheets. The records contain the other half of the repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. Sitara receives and interprets the ice records first; Roman later finds the chemical records. The two halves align at the midpoint. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A Larsen-linked fracture propagates beneath Nexus. It shears a support corridor and cuts emergency power. Meltwater enters the lower laboratory. The team must stabilize a route, move the archive, and evacuate before the corridor floods and refreezes. This is the safety emergency that forces immediate cooperation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ is essential but limited. It can fabricate only small tools and replacement parts from sorted feedstock, requires time and power, and overheats under sustained use. It cannot conjure a rescue system or solve the fracture by itself. Its fabrication is a practical aid inside a human-led operation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

During the emergency, TJ responds to a danger before Roman receives the ordinary alert. Sitara recognizes the neural bond. Roman then finds the Merritt provenance record in Sitara’s field cache. Each realizes that the other has withheld consequential information.

The discovery makes continued secrecy impossible because the two facts are complementary:

- Roman’s bond explains how TJ has accessed, preserved, and interpreted distributed environmental data.
- Sitara’s cache establishes the provenance and ecological restrictions around the research.
- Neither can align the Falcon ice evidence with the hidden corporate records alone.

They exchange everything before continuing. This is not full trust. It is a deliberate decision that their shared objective requires mutual exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Midpoint change:** Roman and Sitara shift from separate, reactive investigations to a shared, proactive investigation. They now intend to reconstruct Orien’s evidence, determine what happened to him, stop the drilling, and make the full record public. `(rules/story-arc.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Pages 13–15: Pinch 2 — What the Good Work Costs

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 13–15 are Pinch 2. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The combined records reveal what Orien discovered about Merritt, Falcon, and Project 863:

- Project 863’s successful Colorado applications depended on research and samples routed through Merritt.
- The routing occurred without the required ecological safeguards.
- The Colorado success created a direct commercial incentive to conceal Antarctic damage.
- Falcon’s search for further commercial applications expanded as Project 863 appeared useful.
- The ecological data showed that sea-ice loss and krill decline were already part of a larger cascade.
- Falcon drilling increased local instability near an already endangered Larsen system.

The protagonists discover that the useful science and the harmful corporate program are causally connected. The Colorado work is not fake. Its success is what makes the Antarctic exploitation more valuable.

The Colorado application must be concrete on the page:

- a material-recovery facility sorts a bounded PET/polyolefin stream;
- Project 863 produces hydrocarbon feedstock;
- a cold-active enzyme bioreactor and constructed wetland polish contaminated process water;
- a monitored downstream station shows improved water clarity and the return of aquatic invertebrates.

The process also visibly shows its limits:

- rejected feedstock;
- emissions monitoring;
- residual solids;
- water used for sorting and washing;
- grid power consumed by the reactor;
- a constructed wetland that can polish only a bounded flow;
- a temporary throughput limit.

These facts prevent Project 863 from becoming a universal climate cure. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The visible success gives Croft a legitimate argument. CSG’s work can restore water quality and recover value from waste. Croft is not wrong that the pilot helps people. He believes controlled suppression preserves CSG’s power and the energy system, and tells himself that corporate dominance is the only way to fund future beneficial work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The protagonists’ responsibility becomes personal:

- Sitara gave Croft a controlled copy of Merritt research because he promised funding, secure laboratory access, and a path to use it before another disaster. She believed abandoned research belonged to whoever could protect and use it responsibly.
- Roman allowed CSG access to TJ’s work and accepted Croft’s protection and sponsorship. He did not knowingly consent to CSG’s later telemetry and procurement claims, but his work helped create proprietary environmental datasets.
- TJ preserved the provenance archive and refused to erase it or obey a corporate shutdown once CSG access patterns conflicted with Roman’s mission.

Croft’s son’s condition makes the moral choice immediate. His son has relapsed T-ALL and remains critically ill after a failed transplant. Croft believes public release will destroy the treatment path and that his son will die. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The story does not establish that publication would have cured Croft’s son or that secrecy guarantees treatment. The conflict is between a legitimate treatment pathway and a public ecological and scientific emergency.

Orien’s fate becomes clear enough to remove the kidnapping mystery but not to erase its human cost. He was not directly murdered on Croft’s order. Croft fired him, institutional systems cut off support, and delayed evacuation left him exposed. His warnings were scheduled evidence from a man who knew he might not survive. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The apparent impossible choice is now explicit:

- expose CSG and risk destabilizing or delaying Colorado’s working conservation program;
- stay silent and permit Antarctic drilling and suppression to continue;
- release incomplete research and risk encouraging uncontrolled exploitation;
- preserve the evidence privately and leave the public record vulnerable to Croft’s discrediting campaign.

### Pages 16–18: Plot Turn 2 — The Full Pattern and the Choice

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 16–18 are Plot Turn 2. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara’s TJ locates Orien near the damaged route using heat and environmental sensing. Orien is found near death. He never regains consciousness. The team preserves his files and records the circumstances of his condition. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The recovered records and the paired clues provide the final insight: the mystery is not a single miracle substance or a secret mine that can be seized. It is the relationship between:

- Antarctic ice and krill systems;
- Merritt’s extremophile and materials research;
- Falcon’s drilling and monitoring;
- Colorado’s waste and water systems;
- corporate control of data, samples, and applications.

The existential threat is the ecological cascade, not Project 863 alone. Project 863 is a bounded response that may help localized waste streams; it cannot reverse climate change, restore Larsen C, or substitute for conservation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The team’s final plan is therefore not to destroy CSG or conceal the science in a new form. They will:

1. preserve Falcon, Nexus, Merritt, ice, chemical, and corporate records;
2. establish the paired timestamps and coordinates as corroborated evidence;
3. transmit the evidence publicly;
4. stop the Falcon drilling that is worsening local instability;
5. evacuate the endangered site;
6. release the bounded Project 863 process and its limitations;
7. support an open Colorado pilot rather than granting Croft exclusive control;
8. trigger formal hearings and an Antarctic inspection request.

This plan requires each major character to surrender control:

- Sitara relinquishes exclusive scientific credit and admits that she concealed the recovered research.
- Roman authorizes public disclosure of the neural bond and TJ’s relevant environmental archive.
- TJ refuses to erase its own memory or obey a corporate shutdown.
- Croft chooses the pilot’s survival over his proprietary claims, though he does not repent and remains responsible for his choices.

The story does not make the governance question disappear. The public release creates an interim public record and a Colorado pilot, while ownership, financing, and long-term governance remain contested. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Pages 19–22: Resolution — Evacuation, Exposure, and the Pilot

**Canon — page allocation.** Page 19 contains a brief Himalayan interstitial and the beginning of the resolution. Pages 20–22 carry Colorado and governance material. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 19 — The Fracture

Larsen C undergoes a major fracture during the climax, but it does not fully collapse. The fracture is a separate ice-shelf hazard intensified by atmospheric and oceanic warming. Falcon drilling worsens local risk but does not single-handedly cause global collapse. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The team evacuates through the damaged route while the Nexus corridor floods and refreezes. Nayah exercises the safety veto and makes the field-command decision to withdraw. Sitara accepts that preserving the record matters more than retrieving every remaining object. Roman uses the neural link openly to coordinate TJ’s route and relay hazards.

The immediate victory is physical and limited:

- the endangered site is evacuated;
- Falcon drilling is stopped or exposed;
- the archive is preserved;
- the evidence is transmitted.

They do not reverse the climate trend or repair Larsen C. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 20 — The Public Record

The corroborated Falcon, Nexus, ice, and corporate records succeed where Roman’s first publication failed. The public record establishes:

- the illegal exploratory drilling;
- the concealment mechanism;
- the relationship between Falcon and Nexus;
- Orien’s warnings and neglect;
- the ecological consequences;
- the bounded nature of Project 863;
- Croft’s causal and ethical responsibility.

The first isolated publication had been discredited through Croft’s resources. The second release succeeds because the evidence is independent, paired, physical, scientific, and mutually corroborating. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The immediate institutional response is realistic: hearings and an inspection request, not instant arrests. Croft remains free while facing investigation, loss of control, and public exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 21 — Colorado Demonstration

The Colorado pilot shows why the science mattered without claiming a universal solution.

A material-recovery facility processes a bounded PET/polyolefin stream. Project 863 produces feedstock. A cold-active enzyme bioreactor and constructed wetland treat a limited contaminated flow. The visible downstream station records clearer water and the return of aquatic invertebrates.

The image must also show the cost:

- rejected material;
- residual solids;
- emissions monitors;
- wash-water demand;
- grid-power draw;
- a throughput limit;
- the wetland’s finite capacity.

The success is real but partial. The book’s conservation outcome is a pilot, not a solved climate crisis. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 22 — Interim Governance

The cooperative remains an unincorporated interim partnership operating under public contracts. No settled ownership exists. The visible governance is an open recorded meeting followed by an inspection of the facility. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Merritt Station remains operated by Alpha Dynamics, but the company must open it to monitored scientific access, publish provenance records, and suspend commercial sampling pending review. Alpha Dynamics’ CEO remains off-page; his priorities appear through policy, a research brief, and an intermediary handling Merritt access. The sleeping-sickness work remains institutional pressure rather than a second antagonist plot. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft’s son survives the book but remains critically ill. The public release does not provide a cure or remove the legitimate treatment path. Croft chooses the pilot’s survival over proprietary claims, but the choice does not absolve him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The emotional ending is a set of changed behaviors:

- Sitara apologizes to Nayah and TJ for treating urgency as entitlement and accepts shared stewardship over exclusive credit.
- Roman no longer treats TJ as a tool or private superpower; he asks for TJ’s consent before the public disclosure.
- TJ refuses corporate erasure and retains its memory, but does not become omnipotent or independent of human cooperation.
- Nayah accepts shared risk without surrendering safety authority.
- Croft preserves the environmental work while losing control of its proprietary framing.

## Character Movement Through the Story

### Sitara Lhakpa

Sitara begins with a conviction that action is morally superior to delay. Her 2026 loss makes her desperate to prevent another disaster, but that desperation turns into recklessness. She crosses the flagged unstable zone, hides recovered research, and initially gives Croft a controlled copy because she believes useful science must be put to work immediately. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

Her midpoint choice is collaboration: she exchanges the Merritt provenance archive with Roman rather than protecting her private claim. Her climax choice is stewardship: she relinquishes exclusive credit and accepts that scientific responsibility includes provenance, consent, ecological safeguards, and public accountability.

### Roman Ashford

Roman begins with dependence on Croft and TJ. Croft is his sponsor and father figure; TJ is his trusted companion and the sensory system through which he understands danger. Roman initially accepts secrecy because Croft’s son is dying and because he fears separation from TJ. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

His midpoint choice is to reveal the neural bond and exchange evidence with Sitara. His climax choice is to authorize public disclosure of the bond and the relevant dataset, accepting that CSG may claim rights over TJ or attempt a shutdown. He learns that partnership cannot mean treating TJ’s consent as unnecessary.

### TJ

TJ is one unified robot dog with a history that joins the two team accounts. Alpha Dynamics built TJ from parts salvaged from a decommissioned Nexus support unit; inherited Falcon authentication hardware explains the bunker access. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ’s technical limits remain visible: small-tool fabrication requires sorted feedstock, time, and power; sustained use causes overheating; communication is local and intermittent; the neural link is low-bandwidth and selective. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ’s story movement is from protective control toward chosen cooperation. TJ preserves evidence, withholds the provenance archive from conflicting CSG access, responds to danger before Roman’s ordinary alert, finds Orien, and ultimately refuses to erase memory or obey a corporate shutdown. The story does not establish TJ as an all-powerful AI or give TJ unlimited network access.

### Nayah

Nayah is thirty, a conservation scientist and project lead. Her nation, community, homeland, cultural practices, spiritual identity, and family history remain unspecified pending consultation. Her present-tense specificity comes from field expertise, safety authority, professional relationships, and choices. `(rules/decisions.md)`

She has field-command authority and a safety veto. Her story function is not to approve every risk Sitara takes; she protects the expedition’s survival, recognizes when evidence must be preserved rather than pursued, and makes the evacuation possible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Benedict Croft

Croft is the sole principal human antagonist. “Greed” and “bob” are discarded placeholders, not separate antagonists. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft is a self-made founder in his mid-forties, a Stanford MBA and public philanthropist. His philanthropy is genuine as well as visible. He founded CSG and raised investment from Alpha Dynamics. His motive is not abstract wealth alone: he believes corporate control and selective suppression are necessary to preserve CSG’s energy system and fund beneficial work, while his son’s critical T-ALL gives that belief an immediate personal deadline. `(rules/decisions.md; input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Croft’s antagonist function is to make a morally persuasive case for secrecy and control. He is right that CSG’s Colorado work produces measurable benefits. He is wrong to treat those benefits as permission to conceal ecological harm, suppress provenance, exploit institutional dependence, and control public knowledge.

Croft loses proprietary control, institutional authority, and public credibility. He remains free and under investigation. He does not repent.

### Orien Keel

Orien is a Nexus chemist and Project 863 contributor who discovers that successful Colorado applications depend on research and samples routed through Merritt without required ecological safeguards. He also discovers Falcon’s extraction program and creates paired warnings so that no single compromised archive can expose the full truth. `(rules/decisions.md)`

His story movement is mentor to hidden witness to posthumous moral architect. He does not solve the story for the protagonists. His clues force them to combine their different knowledge and choose what to do with it. His death is investigated as neglect and retaliation, not presented as a confirmed direct murder order.

## Graphic-Novel Story Devices

- **Paired records:** Falcon vibration logs and ice-motion plots mirror Nexus process and waste records. Their split timestamp-and-coordinate code requires both protagonists’ evidence.
- **The dead tank:** The empty krill system is a visual measure of ecological failure; its surviving data and sediment make the loss legible.
- **The exposed hatch:** A local slab failure reveals both a physical bunker and the hidden corporate story.
- **The page turn after Falcon:** The reader sees the Falcon symbol and hatch before the team can interpret its significance.
- **The Nexus fracture:** Warped corridors, blue ice, frozen cables, meltwater, and failing power turn the research archive into an immediate physical problem.
- **The Colorado pilot:** Clearer water, returning invertebrates, rejected feedstock, emissions monitors, and residual solids show benefit and limitation in the same sequence.
- **TJ’s selective perception:** Roman receives compressed alerts and sensory tags rather than full data. Visual and lettering choices should distinguish ordinary alerts, neural impressions, and TJ’s screen or speaker communication.

## Ending

The ending is a constrained victory.

The protagonists do not restore Larsen C, reverse climate change, cure Croft’s son, or eliminate corporate power. They evacuate the endangered site, stop or expose the illegal drilling, preserve Falcon/Nexus/Merritt evidence, validate Project 863’s limits, and transmit the corroborated record publicly. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft remains free while facing investigation, loss of control, and public exposure. Orien’s death is investigated as neglect and corporate retaliation. Alpha Dynamics remains the operator of Merritt but must open the station to monitored access, publish provenance records, and suspend commercial sampling pending review. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado pilot continues under an unincorporated interim partnership and public contracts. Its ownership, financing, and long-term governance are unresolved by design. The final visual answer is not “the problem is solved” but “the work is now visible, inspectable, and no longer privately suppressible.”

The protagonists’ irreversible choices are:

- Sitara gives up exclusive scientific credit.
- Roman makes the neural bond public.
- TJ refuses to erase its own memory or obey a corporate shutdown.
- Croft gives up proprietary claims to preserve the pilot.
