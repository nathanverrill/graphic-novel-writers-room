# avengers — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **Canon / FIXED:** Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford are the two main protagonists together. They must have equal presence, agency, and plot weight; neither is the other's sidekick or love interest. When the structure requires one scene to carry the spine, alternate between them. `(rules/protagonists.md)`
- **Canon / FIXED:** The crossover preserves both teams' stories in one world: Sitara, Nayah, the Falcon operation and the illegal American extraction program coexist with Roman, Croft, Orien, Nexus and Project 863. `(rules/avengers.md)`
- **Canon / FIXED:** TJ is a robot type called Terrain Jumper. There are multiple TJs. Roman's TJ and Sitara's TJ are distinct physical units of the same model/type; Roman's is the only one with the deep neural handshake / neuralink. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Canon / FIXED:** The central character convergence requires distinct contributions: Sitara interprets ice and climate records; Nayah commands the field operation and makes conservation decisions; Roman reconstructs and bounds Project 863 and the corporate evidence; TJ units open systems and fabricate limited tools under human direction. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Canon / FIXED:** The principal character arcs are: Sitara chooses collaboration and relinquishes exclusive scientific credit; Roman rejects revenge and chooses public responsibility; Nayah accepts shared risk; TJ relinquishes unilateral protective control. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Canon / FIXED:** Benedict Croft is the sole principal human antagonist. “Greed,” “bob,” and similar names are discarded placeholders for his antagonist function, not aliases or additional villains. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Canon / FIXED:** Character decisions remain grounded in hard science. Neural implants, TJ's fabrication, AI sorting and the Roman–TJ bond carry the labels **EG** or **S**; Project 863 is the story's sole major **L**. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

## Characters

### Sitara Lhakpa

**Role:** Main protagonist; ice-core and cryosphere researcher; member of Nayah's Antarctic field team; later a scientific interpreter and public witness to the Falcon, Nexus and Merritt evidence. She earned a doctorate or equivalent research qualification in cryosphere science at CU Boulder and works with an INSTAAR/ATOC research group. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

**Age:** 26 in 2046. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Home and family:** Sitara comes from a fictional, unnamed Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region of Nepal. Her parents died and her home was destroyed in the 2026 Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster. An aunt and cousins remain her living connection to the community, but they stay off-page and are represented through communications and household objects rather than invented identities or cultural practices. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** The earlier visual sheet describes Sitara as female, with dark brown hair (`#483c32`) worn in a ponytail or two braids, smooth and straight with messy strands framing her face; skin tone `#a67b5b`. She wears a dark brown mala-bead necklace, mostly covered by her jacket. Her Antarctic clothing is a puffy red jacket, usually hood down, waterproof pants, beanie, warm waterproof boots and thermal snow goggles. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

**Visual continuity:** Sitara has no physical consequences from the 2026 disaster. Her visual presentation is that of a 26-year-old adult, not a visibly injured survivor. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice and arc:** The material establishes Sitara as passionate, ambitious and direct, but the project does not fix verbal tics or a complete dialogue bible before the writing pass. Her available language is scientific and observational when working: ice movement, cores, fracture behavior, climate records and evidence. Under pressure she is capable of overriding safety advice and treating continued data collection as more urgent than her own safety. She gradually changes from reckless to more controlled, preserving her directness while learning to treat urgency as responsibility rather than entitlement. `(input/forge-characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Who she is:** Sitara is driven by the 2026 disaster and by guilt that, as a six-year-old, she could do nothing while her family and community were destroyed. She has converted helplessness into relentless scientific purpose: understanding climate change so that other communities do not experience the same catastrophe. Her passion is genuine, but it can harden into selfishness and recklessness when she believes the truth is within reach. `(input/forge-characters.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

She initially believes that abandoned research belongs to whoever can protect and use it responsibly. This belief allows her to conceal copies of Merritt research and later give Croft a controlled copy in exchange for funding, secure laboratory access and a path to apply the findings before another disaster occurs. `(rules/decisions.md; input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Her central contradiction is that she wants science to serve endangered communities while also wanting recognition, credibility and the chance to be the person who makes the breakthrough. She can mistake being capable of understanding something for having the right to control it. The material frames this as her movement from ambition toward stewardship. `(input/forge-characters.md; drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Nayah:** Nayah recruited Sitara after recognizing her cryosphere expertise. They were Colorado colleagues for two years before the expedition. Nayah has field-command authority and a safety veto; Sitara leads cryosphere interpretation. Their relationship is strained when Sitara ignores Nayah's safety direction, but Nayah remains a professional and personal counterweight to Sitara's recklessness. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Roman:** They attended the same CU Boulder seminar or office hour in 2044, where Sitara explained ice records and water loss. They recognize one another at Nexus but were never close. Their relationship develops from separate secrets and suspicion toward necessary collaboration; it is not romantic. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **TJ units:** Sitara works with a TJ search-and-rescue unit assigned to her field team. She initially treats the machine as an instrument that can help uncover the truth, but TJ's agency and protective judgments challenge that assumption. Her unit can share selected route, safety and sensor summaries with other TJs, while full mission archives require deliberate synchronization. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`
- **Croft:** Sitara is hired or taken into Croft's research effort as a foil and researcher for his son's treatment. She is initially unaware of the full purpose and of Roman's separate compromise. Her trust becomes suspicion as she sees Roman leaving his work station, asks her TJ to investigate, and checks records that reveal the treatment chemistry and Croft's hidden program. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Orien:** Orien promised to mentor Sitara, publish her research and support her. His disappearance drives her to hunt for him. His records and warnings become the bridge between Sitara's ice evidence and Roman's chemical evidence. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Sitara crosses a flagged unstable zone to retrieve a sensor and orders TJ to keep recording. Her weight, combined with nearby drilling vibration, triggers a local slab failure that exposes the Falcon hatch and traps equipment. This is a consequential expression of her tendency to prioritize evidence over safety. `(rules/decisions.md)`

She preserves and conceals copies of recovered research rather than removing living organisms or physical samples. Her concealment is motivated by a belief that commercial access had been restricted pending ecological review and that the information might otherwise disappear or be controlled. Copying data without authorization remains a contractual and institutional violation; she did not steal organisms. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** Sitara survived the 2026 Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster at age six with extended family or relief workers. She lost both parents and her home. She later moved to Colorado for climate research, completed advanced cryosphere training at CU Boulder and joined Nayah's Antarctic project as an ice-core specialist. `(rules/decisions.md)`

At Merritt, Sitara recovered archived research files, sequence data, enzyme assays and sealed sample records from a damaged research area. She did not take living organisms or physical samples. She concealed copies because the records indicated that commercial access had been restricted pending ecological review. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** The draft places Sitara on the ice near Merritt while monitoring ice movement with Nayah and TJ. A newly exposed entrance or hatch becomes visible after ice instability. She enters despite the danger and removes or copies research while telling herself she is rescuing abandoned science. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md; input/amelia.md)`

**Arc:** Sitara begins by equating urgency, intelligence and moral entitlement: if she can understand the research and use it to help people, she believes she is justified in taking control of it. Her recklessness worsens the immediate Antarctic danger, and her secrecy helps make the science commercially valuable to CSG. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

She changes by accepting that discovery creates responsibility rather than ownership. In the climax she relinquishes exclusive scientific credit, exchanges the evidence with Roman and the others, and accepts a public, collaborative path even though it weakens her personal claim to the breakthrough. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Roman Ashford

**Role:** Main protagonist; chemical engineer; CSG employee and later investigator of Project 863, Orien's disappearance and Croft's corporate evidence. He leads the chemical and corporate side of the investigation. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

**Age:** 22 in 2046. He was 18 during the 2042 Himalayan accident. `(input/forge-characters.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

**Home and affiliation:** Roman lives and works in Colorado before traveling to Antarctica. He works for Clear Spring Global, initially under Benedict Croft's authority, and has a prior professional relationship with Orien Keel. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; input/seb.md)`

**Look:** Roman is a lean, compact 22-year-old climber with dark hair, weathered expedition layers, a chemical-analysis field kit, and a visible but non-glowing neural-interface diagnostic mark behind one ear. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice and arc:** Roman is shy and timid in ordinary life, but capable of direct resistance when Croft pressures him. The project avoids fixed verbal tics until the writing pass, using established traits and supplied lines as anchors. He gradually changes from shy to assertive. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The draft preserves the confrontation line: **“This is unfair; where is Orien Keel?”** `(input/prahul-notes.md)`

**Who he is:** Roman is a chemical engineer concerned with drying rivers, water scarcity and the environmental consequences of industrial systems. He wants his technical work to matter materially rather than remain abstract research. `(input/forge-characters.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

He is also shaped by dependence. Croft funded his expedition and career through a favor connected to Roman's father. Roman's father died because of a favor Croft executed; Croft became the man Roman believed had saved his life and functioned as a sponsor, benefactor and substitute father figure. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Roman agrees to Croft's treatment work before traveling to Antarctica. He knows about Project 863 and Nexus and continues searching for files before Croft finds him and explains that he wants to divert Alpha's African work toward treatment research for his son. Roman is convinced to participate because Croft's son is in a dire condition and the treatment work lacks the required approvals. Roman's complicity in this unauthorized research becomes part of his legal and moral exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **TJ:** Roman has one assigned TJ unit with which he has maintained a deep neural handshake for four years. The bond is a persistent low-bandwidth neural handshake, not telepathy or full remote control. Roman receives selected alerts, directional impulses and compressed sensory tags; TJ receives Roman's attention, urgency, balance and intent. They have learned each other's operating patterns, but the connection has limits and can fail or overload. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Croft:** Croft sponsored Roman, supplied TJ through CSG and presents himself as the person who gave Roman a future. Roman initially sees him as a benefactor and father figure. Croft later calls in that debt to make Roman work on the leukemia-related application of the extremophile research. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Orien:** Orien was Roman's mentor at CSG and a senior chemical engineer. Roman searches Orien's records after his dismissal and disappearance, turning a personal loss into an investigation. `(input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Sitara:** Roman and Sitara had a brief prior CU Boulder connection but were never close. At the convergence they recognize that each holds a different half of the truth: Sitara has the ice and provenance evidence; Roman has chemical and corporate evidence. Their relationship moves from suspicion to forced cooperation and then to deliberate exchange. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Nayah:** Nayah has field authority and safety responsibility during the Antarctic convergence. Roman must work within her authority rather than treating his investigation as a private mission. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Roman is an expedition climber and has four years of learned physical and operational coordination with his bonded TJ. He is accustomed to using selected sensory alerts rather than treating the robot as an ordinary remote device. `(rules/decisions.md)`

He searches Orien's belongings and restricted paper archives when digital systems fail or have been redacted. His persistence can become obsessive: after Croft tells him to stop pursuing Project 863, Roman continues investigating and attempting to finish the work. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

**History:** In 2042, at 18, Roman went on a senior climbing expedition in Nepal unrelated to Sitara, her family, or Merritt research. He fell through unstable ice. During the rescue, lightning caused the persistent neural handshake between his implant and his assigned TJ unit. The accident is separate from Sitara's 2026 disaster; the shared connection is changing ice and water systems. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman later studied chemical engineering and joined CSG in Colorado. Orien mentored him while researching dried rivers, water loss and Project 863. After Croft fired Orien, Orien lost transport and corporate medical support at an isolated CSG facility. Orien scheduled warnings and attempted to leave, then went missing and was found near death from exposure by Sitara's TJ; he never regained consciousness. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman discovers the Nexus trail through both Orien's belongings and the Antarctic paper archive. The paper archive supplies coordinates and a less-redacted Project 863 record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** The draft shows Roman as a young engineer who follows a hidden Nexus reference from Colorado to an Antarctic cave laboratory. He knows about 863 and Nexus and continues searching for Orien's files. He does not publish an initial account. Croft finds him, reveals his intention to redirect Alpha's African work toward treatment of his son, and convinces Roman to participate. `(rules/decisions.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

**Arc:** Roman begins dependent on Croft and treats his bonded TJ partly as a trusted companion and partly as an extension of his own capability. He also allows personal anger at Croft and complicity in the unauthorized treatment work to become reasons for action after Orien's disappearance. `(input/prahul-notes.md; rules/decisions.md)`

He changes by recognizing TJ as an agent rather than a tool or superpower, and by rejecting revenge as his governing motive. His principal losses are employment, reputation, access, and his relationship with Croft. TJ retains local agency and memory but becomes the subject of a public ownership and personhood dispute. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Nayah

**Role:** Antarctic conservation scientist and project lead; field commander of Sitara's team. She holds field-command authority and a safety veto. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Age:** 30 in 2046. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Identity:** Nayah's nation, community, homeland, cultural practices, spiritual identity and family history remain unspecified in this project until consultation. No identity details should be invented. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** The earlier visual sheet describes Nayah with dark brown hair (`#321414`) worn in two long, smooth braids; skin tone `#785134`; dark brown eyes; and cyan circular earrings partly hidden by her braids. Her Antarctic clothing is a puffy yellow jacket, usually hood down, waterproof black pants, beanie, warm waterproof boots and thermal snow goggles. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

**Voice:** Nayah uses concise operational speech, repeated route-and-risk checks, and clear safety calls. She has a habit of physically marking withdrawal options before allowing scientific work. Her private motivation is responsibility to the living team and the site, not an invented family or cultural history. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Who she is:** Nayah is a conservation scientist and project lead who makes field-command and conservation decisions. Earlier material characterizes her as protective and distrustful; those traits remain usable as behavioral material, but her specific cultural or family basis is intentionally not supplied. `(rules/decisions.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

Her authority is practical rather than ornamental. She must decide when the team moves, when a route is unsafe, what evidence can be recovered, and how to balance immediate human survival against the integrity of Antarctic sites. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Sitara:** Nayah recruited Sitara after recognizing her cryosphere expertise. They worked together in Colorado for two years before the expedition. Nayah's safety veto directly conflicts with Sitara's reckless drive to retrieve sensors and evidence. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Roman:** Nayah is the field authority when Roman enters the Antarctic investigation. She is not subordinate to his corporate history or chemical expertise. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **TJ units:** TJ units perform technical operations under human direction. Nayah can authorize, restrict or redirect those operations in accordance with field safety. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Croft and CSG:** Nayah's conservation authority is opposed to private extraction and information suppression, but she is not defined as a personal rival to Croft. Her conflict is with decisions that endanger the site or treat conservation as a corporate asset. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Habits and tells:** Nayah checks the safety implications of a plan before accepting its scientific value, marks withdrawal options, and gives direct route-and-risk instructions. `(rules/decisions.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

**History:** Nayah has worked with Sitara in Colorado for two years and leads the Antarctic project in 2046. Her nation, community, homeland and personal history remain intentionally unspecified. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**In existing drafts:** Nayah appears with Sitara during the ice-monitoring and bunker-discovery material, where her role is to provide field partnership and a counterpoint to Sitara's willingness to cross unstable ice. `(input/amelia.md; drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

**Arc:** Nayah begins as the person trying to keep the team within safe and defensible limits. The settled endpoint is that she accepts shared risk: not reckless risk, but the necessary risk of allowing Roman, Sitara and TJ to act together when no single authority or team can solve the crisis. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### TJ

**Role:** Terrain Jumper; a type of small exploration, search-and-rescue and scientific-access robot. There are multiple TJs. Each unit is a physical TJ with its own local mission partition. Roman's assigned TJ is the only one with the deep neural handshake / neuralink; Sitara works with another TJ unit. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Identity and forms:** TJ units are members of the Terrain Jumper platform family. They are not one physical platform expressed through every scene, and they are not separate human-like identities by default. The book may show a search-and-rescue TJ, an extremophile-hunting TJ, and a heat-map/detection TJ as variants of the same model. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The familiar scout configuration is small-dog scale and draws on a Himalayan sheep dog, working search dogs, insects and other biological forms. Earlier visual material describes seamless silver paneling, a green-and-black screen with buttons on the right side or ribs, eyes resembling the Contixo R3 electronic robot dog, and red puffy booties made from jacket material. The booties are a recurring Antarctic field detail. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The platform family includes canine search-and-rescue, extremophile-hunting, heat-map and detection configurations, with other specialized forms available where the story requires them. These may include climbing, insect narrow-access, aerial, aquatic, sensor-pod and tool configurations. Each active body is a TJ unit with a local mission partition; units share identity as a model/type and selected mesh data, not a single continuously synchronized consciousness. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_bio_inspired_form_factors_reference.md)`

**Capabilities and limits:**

- **EG:** TJ units use onboard autonomy, local mesh communication, sensor fusion and specialized physical configurations. They cannot rely on unlimited cloud or network access. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`
- **EG:** A TJ can sense through visual, thermal, lidar, audio, chemical, haptic, environmental, magnetic and biological systems, but communicates selected information through a screen, speakers or, for Roman's TJ, Roman's limited neural channel. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **EG:** A TJ can fabricate only small tools and replacement parts from sorted feedstock. Fabrication requires time and power, overheats under sustained use and cannot solve every problem by producing arbitrary equipment. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **EG/S:** TJ units can inherit enough Falcon authentication hardware to answer the bunker’s machine challenge. The module is a sealed challenge-response credential embedded beside a TJ's communications core. Falcon sends a rotating machine challenge; the inherited hardware returns a signed response, opening only the hatch and a local maintenance interface. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- **EG/S:** TJ units can operate for hours to days depending on movement, sensing, communications and configuration. `(rules/worldbuilding/tj_bio_inspired_form_factors_reference.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **EG:** TJ units operate under human direction in the field. Their autonomy supports navigation, hazard detection and local decisions, but does not give them unlimited authority over human missions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Unified platform history:** TJ was built by Alpha Dynamics from parts salvaged from a decommissioned Nexus support unit. The reconstruction incorporated inherited Falcon authentication hardware. This makes the platform histories compatible without making Roman's TJ and Sitara's TJ the same physical body. Bi11bot must never be mentioned. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Fabrication and field identity:** A TJ's internal fabricator supports its identity as a practical field partner. It can discuss scanning, sorting and printing, but the established story limits the physical results to small tools and replacement parts. A TJ's usefulness comes from resourcefulness and access, not infinite manufacture. `(rules/decisions.md; input/seb.md)`

**Voice:** TJ units are loyal, protective and mission-focused, with dry, understated humor. Their speech is confident about engineering and fabrication but grounded in cold, ice, isolation and immediate mission. The project avoids fixed verbal tics until the writing pass. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

TJ communication is shown through screen, speakers and, only for Roman's assigned TJ, limited neural signals. A TJ can say that it is running a scan or could print a tool, but should not narrate physical actions in its own voice. `(input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Data and mission partitions:** Each active TJ body has a local mission partition. TJ units share route, safety and selected sensor summaries across the mesh, but full archives require deliberate synchronization and may be inaccessible during shielding, damage or network separation. A local unit may therefore know that another unit holds information without being able to retrieve the archive immediately. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`

Roman's TJ preserves local copies of Merritt provenance data and Falcon environmental records in its own separated mission partition. It did not secretly upload the full archive. It exchanged only route, safety and sensor summaries through CSG systems, then withheld the provenance archive after detecting that CSG access patterns conflicted with Roman's stated mission. Other TJ units may hold related local records without continuously possessing the same archive. `(rules/decisions.md)`

This withholding is protective but ethically ambiguous. It makes Roman's TJ a witness with local judgment rather than a neutral storage device, while also raising the question of whether protection can become unilateral control. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Neural relationship with Roman:** **S, bounded:** Lightning during the 2042 Himalayan accident caused a persistent low-bandwidth neural handshake between Roman's implant and his assigned TJ. After four years, they have learned one another's operating patterns. Roman receives selected alerts, directional impulses and compressed sensory tags; Roman's TJ receives Roman's attention, urgency, balance and intent. Neither can read all of the other's data, and Roman cannot remotely control TJ. Other TJ units do not share this deep neural bond. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The link degrades under shielding or beyond local mesh range, reverting to intermittent status pulses. Power loss or damage can create false gaps and sensory overload when the connection returns. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The bond is not ordinary telepathy. It is a structured but embodied exchange of machine sensing and selected human state. The character consequence is more important than the mechanism alone: Roman and his TJ can develop shared operational intuition while still disagreeing about agency, risk and what should be disclosed. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_neural_interface_reference.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Roman's TJ and Roman:** Roman's assigned TJ is his bonded human partner. It has learned his gait, balance, urgency, attention and risk patterns. Their intimacy is operational and sensory, not merely conversational. Roman must eventually treat it as a partner with agency rather than as his instrument or superpower. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_neural_interface_reference.md)`
- **Sitara's TJ:** Sitara uses a separate TJ unit as a technical field partner. It performs heat-map and detection work, helps hunt for Orien, and assists with the Falcon investigation. Its local mission data is not automatically identical to Roman's TJ's data. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Nayah:** TJ units work under Nayah's field authority and safety direction when assigned to the Antarctic team. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Croft and CSG:** CSG supplied TJ units to field personnel through deliberately incomplete paperwork. Croft presented Roman's TJ as a gift, while CSG later relies on procurement records and telemetry clauses to claim rights Roman never knowingly accepted. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Falcon systems:** A TJ's inherited authentication module allows it to answer the Falcon bunker’s machine challenge without unrestricted system access. This makes the platform family a physical link between the abandoned program and the present investigation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** Roman's assigned TJ accompanied him during the 2042 Himalayan accident and helped him survive the ice collapse and electrical storm. TJ units' platform history also includes the decommissioned Nexus support unit and inherited Falcon authentication components. `(rules/decisions.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

**In existing drafts:** A TJ follows Roman into the Himalayan crevasse, supports the rescue and becomes part of the unusual neural event. In the Antarctic material, Sitara's TJ detects the exposed bunker, opens the hatch, scans archives, finds routes and files, assists with exploration of unstable corridors, shafts and flooded areas, and later uses heat mapping to locate Orien. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md; input/amelia.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc:** TJ units begin from protective operating principles: preserve human life, preserve mission data and restrict dangerous information when human decisions appear unsafe. Roman's TJ's protection can become unilateral control. In the climax it refuses to erase its own local memory or obey a corporate shutdown and participates in an accountable disclosure, while retaining local agency and memory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Benedict Croft

**Role:** Principal human antagonist; founder of Clear Spring Global; businessman with an MBA and a public philanthropist's profile. He is not a second villain called Greed or Bob. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Age:** Mid-forties. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Affiliation:** Founder and controlling executive of CSG. Alpha Dynamics owns or controls the infrastructure underlying Falcon and Nexus, while CSG operates those sites through contractual and research subsidiaries. CSG carries visible responsibility for the operations; Alpha Dynamics retains strategic control and access. Alpha Dynamics is also an investor in CSG and operates Merritt. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

**Look and private life:** Croft's public presentation is that of a credible business leader and philanthropist rather than a cartoonishly evil billionaire. He is physically and visually not yet fully specified. He is genuinely concerned for his son, and that concern intensifies as the leukemia worsens. He has a powerful network and may be somewhat performative, enjoying being seen giving back, but his public philanthropy is not merely fake. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Voice:** Croft is persuasive, controlled and morally confrontational rather than openly theatrical. His defeat dialogue is preserved:

- **“You think you're clean?”**
- **“You have blood on your hands.”**
- **“By stopping this, my child can't breathe.”** `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

His rhetoric frames corporate control as a necessary instrument for funding useful work and protecting vulnerable people. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Who he is:** Croft is genuinely capable of care and philanthropy. He enjoys being seen giving back, but his public goodness is not merely fake. He believes controlled suppression is necessary to preserve CSG's power and the energy system, and tells himself that continued corporate dominance will let him fund future beneficial work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

His son has T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia: diagnosed at 17, relapsed at 18, chronic at 19 and now 20, in critical condition after a failed stem-cell transplant. He is deteriorating on a months-scale timeline. Croft diverts scarce resources and Antarctic extremophile samples toward his son's treatment. The research is conducted without the required approvals because Croft believes he does not have time to wait for them. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; rules/decisions.md)`

If the hidden research becomes public, Alpha Dynamics will fire Croft and pause the activities before he can achieve a breakthrough. The consequence, as Croft understands it, is that his son will die within a month. The son survives the book but remains critically ill; this projected consequence belongs to the post-release timeline rather than the immediate page ending. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Roman:** Croft's relationship with Roman combines sponsorship, guilt, debt and paternal manipulation. Roman's father died because of a favor Croft executed; Croft sponsored Roman's expedition and supplied TJ. Croft later calls in the debt by asking Roman to work on the leukemia application of the extremophile research. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Sitara:** Croft hires or takes Sitara into the treatment research as a foil and researcher without initially revealing the full purpose. He receives her controlled copy and promises funding, secure laboratory access and responsible application. He later attempts to claim the remaining archive through institutional and technical pressure. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Orien:** Orien discovers Croft's diversion of resources and samples and threatens to expose it. Croft threatens to fire him and use his network, money and other resources to make him disappear. ### Orien Keel

**Role:** Former Nexus chemist; CSG scientist and Roman's mentor; promised mentor and supporter to Sitara; architect of the paired evidence trail. He advanced Project 863, discovered Falcon's extraction program and hid corroborating records before Croft fired him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Look:** No settled visual description is supplied. His belongings, files, laboratory records and the traces of his disappearance are his primary visual anchors. `(rules/decisions.md; input/seb.md)`

**Voice:** Orien's complete dialogue voice is not fixed before the writing pass. His role suggests a technically precise, safety-conscious and public-health-oriented scientist who is willing to issue warnings rather than accept corporate silence. `(input/prahul-notes.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Who he is:** Orien believes the work should serve public safety and public health first. He is not opposed to useful applications of Project 863 or extremophile research; he is opposed to concealed extraction, unreviewed commercialization and the suppression of evidence needed to assess ecological harm. He discovers that Croft is diverting research toward an unauthorized T-ALL treatment effort and threatens to become a whistleblower. `(input/prahul-notes.md; rules/decisions.md)`

**Relationships:**

- **Roman:** Orien mentors Roman at CSG and becomes the person whose disappearance gives Roman a personal reason to investigate. `(input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **Sitara:** Orien promised to mentor Sitara, publish her research and support her. His disappearance prompts her to search for him. His paired clues require Sitara's ice interpretation and Roman's chemical evidence to align. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **Croft:** Orien discovers Croft's resource diversion and unauthorized medical research and threatens to expose it. Croft threatens to fire him and make him disappear through his network and resources. Orien does not budge. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **TJ units:** Sitara's TJ finds Orien near hypothermia using heat-sensing capability. Orien is sick, appears to have escaped somewhere, and never regains consciousness. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**History:** Orien was a Nexus chemist who advanced Project 863. He discovered that Colorado applications depended on research and samples routed through Merritt without the required ecological safeguards, creating a commercial incentive to conceal Antarctic damage. He also learned that Croft was using the research for his son's T-ALL treatment without the necessary approvals. `(rules/decisions.md)`

After Croft fired him and threatened him, Orien lost transport and corporate medical support at an isolated CSG facility. He recorded and scheduled warnings before attempting to leave. He then went missing. Sitara's TJ locates him before hypothermia, but he is obviously sick and never regains consciousness. His evidence establishes neglect, retaliation and Croft's causal and ethical responsibility, but not a direct murder order. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Evidence and methods:** Orien split his warnings into paired physical records. Falcon vibration logs and ice-motion plots were hidden in drilling maintenance files; Nexus process and waste records were concealed in Project 863 calibration sheets. Each record contains only half of a repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. Sitara receives and interprets the ice records first; Roman later finds the chemical records. The halves align at the midpoint. `(rules/decisions.md)`

His secret automated messages contain compressed bursts with drilling coordinates, ice-movement data and warnings that the extraction program is accelerating Larsen-system instability. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Arc / narrative function:** Orien moves from mentor to absent mystery to moral architect. He does not solve the investigation for the protagonists or return to explain it. His choices force Sitara and Roman to combine evidence, confront their own complicity and make the decisions he cannot make for them. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md; rules/decisions.md)`
