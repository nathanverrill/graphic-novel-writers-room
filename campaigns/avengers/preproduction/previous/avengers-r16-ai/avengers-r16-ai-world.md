# avengers — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- **FIXED.** The book is set in **2046** and takes place across **Antarctica, the Himalayas, and Colorado**. Both team storylines are true in the same world. `(rules/rules.md; rules/avengers.md)`
- **FIXED.** The central ecological threat is the combined cascade caused by Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability. Illegal extraction and suppression of information prevent an adequate response. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED.** A waste-to-value organization applies Antarctic research to conservation in Colorado. The Colorado application must appear on the page through concrete, measurable outcomes rather than remaining an aspiration or backstory. `(rules/rules.md; rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`
- **FIXED.** The story remains hard science fiction. Project 863 is the sole major **L**; other speculative elements remain labelled **EG** or **S**. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED.** The world is mostly recognizable 2046 infrastructure and institutions, with emerging technologies made ordinary and a very small number of genuinely speculative elements. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

## Overview

This is a near-future world in which climate damage is no longer an abstract forecast. Antarctic sea ice is declining, krill habitat is being disrupted, glaciers and ice shelves are becoming more dangerous, and Colorado’s reduced snowpack is stressing rivers and cold-water species. Food and water are more expensive and less reliable than they were in the early 2040s. Research stations, corporations, universities, governments, and conservation groups compete and collaborate around the same shrinking environmental margins. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

Useful technologies exist, but they do not erase scarcity. Autonomous robots can enter spaces that humans cannot, neural interfaces can exchange selected sensory information, and cold-active biological processes can make some industrial chemistry more efficient. None of these systems is unlimited, instantaneous, or consequence-free. They require power, maintenance, authorization, skilled operators, communications infrastructure, and physical access. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_neural_interface_reference.md)`

The central institutional conflict is not between science and a cartoonishly evil corporation. It is between public ecological need, open research, commercial control, personal medical urgency, and the ability of powerful organizations to decide which risks other people must bear.

## Time and Historical Conditions

### 2042: The Himalayan accident

**FIXED.** In 2042, Roman Ashford is caught in a Himalayan ice accident. A lightning strike produces the persistent Roman–TJ neural handshake. The accident is separate from Sitara’s 2026 disaster; the shared connection is changing ice and water systems. `(rules/decisions.md; input/seb.md; drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

The original draft describes Roman crossing unstable glacier ice in Nepal, falling into a break, and being rescued with TJ during an electrical storm. The final mechanism is not ordinary telepathy: the lightning produces a persistent, low-bandwidth neural connection with limited range and reliability. `(input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The neural bond is unique to Roman among the TJs in this story. Roman’s TJ is one unit of the Terrain Jumper type, but it is the only one with the deep neural handshake/neuralink. Other TJs, including the TJ assigned to Sitara, communicate through ordinary local mesh, screen, speaker, and mission interfaces. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### 2026: The Himalayan disaster

**FIXED.** Sitara’s 2026 disaster is the Lirung–Lhende Khola event. She is six years old, survives with extended family or relief workers, and loses her parents and home. There are no lasting physical injuries or visible physical consequences for her. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The supplied real-world reporting describes a rock–ice avalanche, debris flow, flash flooding, damage along a 72-kilometre river stretch, destruction around Gyirong Port, and large-scale death, disappearance, and displacement. This research grounds the fictional event but is not itself a separate book event unless the story uses it. `(input/amelia.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### 2046: The story world

**FIXED.** By 2046, environmental instability has become a daily logistical and economic fact. Climate research, conservation, industrial chemistry, robotics, and proprietary data are tied together through corporate and academic systems. `(rules/rules.md; input/seb.md)`

Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B in 2002. Larsen C remains a major ice-shelf hazard. A major fracture opens during the book, but Larsen C does not fully collapse. The teams can stop local drilling and expose evidence; they cannot reverse the climate trend or restore the ice shelf. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

## Geography and Major Locations

### The Antarctic Peninsula and Larsen region

The Antarctic setting is a coastal, mountainous ice environment with floating sea ice, glaciers, research bases, tracked routes, drilling infrastructure, caves, and dangerous fractures. Human bases and homes occupy or border an accessible coastal research zone on the Antarctic Peninsula. Falcon is inland; Nexus is a nearby coastal cave laboratory. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The environment includes:

- Snow-covered mountain ranges descending toward a coast.
- Sea ice and open water.
- Glaciers, crevasses, fracture zones, and unstable slabs.
- A small community of research bases and homes.
- Permanent or semi-permanent scientific infrastructure mixed with temporary field camps.
- Sparse wildlife, appearing only where habitat and season support it.
- Weather windows that determine whether vehicles, aircraft, and field teams can move.
- Satellite communications that may be delayed or intermittent. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Scientists adapt to unstable ice by relocating camps, using airborne surveys, avoiding crevasse zones, and monitoring movement in real time. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### Falcon

**FIXED.** Falcon is a covert exploratory drilling and extraction-monitoring site operated under a legitimate scientific-drilling permit. It is not a conventional commercial mine and is not yet extracting a commercial resource. Its permit authorizes scientific drilling and environmental monitoring only. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Falcon uses directional boreholes, seismic equipment, pumps, heated shelters, fuel stores, and concealed waste-handling systems to survey sub-ice resources and record environmental effects. The violations are the concealed resource survey, unauthorized directional boreholes, falsified manifests, concealed waste, and preparation of future extraction rights. The story does not call this activity lawful mining. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon presents resource exploration as climate and ice-core work. Its concealment system includes subcontractors, falsified manifests, restricted communications, and carefully chosen weather windows that reduce the chance of inspection. The operation is illegal because it uses a scientific permit to establish data, access, and future rights before any resource becomes legally exploitable. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon’s operational records include drilling vibration logs and ice-motion plots. These records also contain one half of Orien’s repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Nexus

**FIXED.** Nexus is a decommissioned research laboratory built into a coastal Antarctic cave system. It was part of the same broader infrastructure controlled by Alpha Dynamics and operated through CSG’s contractual and research subsidiaries. CSG carries the visible operational responsibility; Alpha Dynamics retains strategic control and access. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

Nexus was the scientific site connected to Falcon’s operational and extraction-monitoring work. It studied ecological consequences, krill and ice data, cold-active enzymes, extremophile research, Project 863 materials, waste, and process-water systems. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nexus ran parallel programs:

- Antarctic ecological research.
- Sea-ice, krill, and food-web research.
- Cold-active enzyme and extremophile research.
- Project 863 materials research.
- Waste and process-water studies.
- Records and sample storage.

Nexus was closed after its researchers documented connections between extraction, sea-ice loss, and krill decline. By 2046 its main power is off, but insulated archives, emergency systems, and surviving sensor equipment remain. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The physical laboratory contains:

- A cave entrance and sealed or damaged access routes.
- Warped metal corridors.
- Blue ice intruding through structural fractures.
- Frozen cables.
- Obsolete equipment.
- Snow entering through breaks in the structure.
- Flooded lower areas.
- A support corridor vulnerable to ice movement.
- Archives, calibration sheets, process records, and ecological sensor data.
- A dead krill ecosystem tank whose sensors and sediment samples survive. `(rules/refinements.md; rules/decisions.md)`

A two-hour journey connects Falcon and Nexus by a weather-dependent over-ice route. Tracked vehicles and occasional aircraft serve the broader Antarctic network. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Merritt Station

**FIXED.** Merritt Station is a separate Alpha Dynamics-operated site in the Wilkes Land region of East Antarctica. Alpha Dynamics owns or controls the infrastructure underlying Falcon and Nexus as well as Merritt’s access systems, while CSG operates Falcon and Nexus through contractual and research subsidiaries. CSG carries visible responsibility for site operations; Alpha Dynamics retains strategic control and access. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

Merritt is a restored and partially restored research, logistics, and remediation hub, not the same facility as Falcon or Nexus. Alpha Dynamics restored and reactivated portions of it between 2042 and 2046. The site now combines:

- Scientific laboratories.
- Logistics and staging infrastructure.
- Environmental remediation facilities.
- Robotics and autonomous-system testing.
- Cold storage and archives.
- Access to ice cores and subglacial environments.
- Legacy research areas.
- Restricted access controlled by Alpha Dynamics. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_merritt_station_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md)`

Merritt’s original surface is buried in places beneath accumulated snow and ice. The current surface may be **3–20 metres higher** than the original station surface. The complex includes partially collapsed habitation areas, fractured passageways, flooded sections, shafts, a deep science wing, and possible subglacial access. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md)`

Restoration proceeds in stages:

1. Surface entry and stability assessment.
2. Structural mapping using robotics, radar, and sensors.
3. Subsurface exploration through fractured corridors, shafts, and flooded areas.
4. Carefully controlled access to deeper subglacial environments.

Teams may rappel through damaged shafts toward sealed lower sections. TJ units identify narrow routes around collapsed bulkheads and ice-blocked access points. `(rules/refinements.md)`

Immediately after exposure, Alpha Dynamics opens only the damaged archive and surface facilities. Deeper subglacial areas remain closed pending structural and environmental assessment. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Merritt remains both a remediation problem and a scientific time capsule. It contains lost research, unpublished records, legacy instruments, ice cores, sealed sample records, and biological or geological data. The site’s commercial value lies largely in early access to knowledge and applications rather than legal ownership of Antarctic organisms. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md)`

### The Himalayas

The Himalayas function as a high-altitude environment shaped by unstable glaciers, flash floods, difficult travel, local knowledge, and communities living with changing water systems.

The project keeps Nayah’s nation, community, homeland, cultural practices, spiritual identity, and family history unspecified. Sitara’s fictional Thakali settlement also remains unnamed pending consultation. Her aunt and cousins are living but off-page, represented through communications and household objects rather than invented identities or practices. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Himalayan setting is not a second Antarctic base. It is a place where inherited and local environmental knowledge exists alongside scientific expedition technology, and where ice instability has already altered lives before the main story begins.

### Colorado

Colorado is the visible application site for the Antarctic research.

The pilot occupies a stressed tributary connected to a CU Boulder/INSTAAR monitoring program. Its facilities include:

- A material-recovery facility.
- Optical and AI-assisted sorting equipment.
- A bounded PET/polyolefin feedstock line.
- Project 863 process equipment.
- A cold-active enzyme bioreactor.
- Emissions monitoring.
- Residual-solids handling.
- A constructed wetland for polishing contaminated process water.
- Downstream monitoring stations. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`

The first visible success is improved water clarity and the return of aquatic invertebrates at a monitored downstream station. The system also produces useful feedstock from selected waste. It does not restore every river, replace fossil fuels, or remove the need for ordinary treatment and conservation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The required visual treatment is:

1. A split-screen of rejected material and recovered feedstock.
2. A monitoring wall showing water and power metrics.
3. A downstream invertebrate survey beside a finite-throughput warning. `(story.md; world.md)`

## Environmental Systems

### Antarctic sea ice and krill

Sea ice is an ecological infrastructure, not merely scenery. Its loss affects krill habitat and therefore the food web that depends on krill. The krill decline is part of the larger cascade rather than a separate monster or single-cause disaster. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The Nexus tank is dead. Its surviving sensor data and sediment samples indicate that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Larsen-system instability

Sea-ice loss and krill decline are ecological cascade elements. Larsen C fracture is a related but distinct ice-shelf hazard intensified by atmospheric and oceanic warming. Illegal drilling worsens local instability and risk, but does not single-handedly cause global ice-shelf collapse or global climate change. `(rules/decisions.md)`

During the climax, a Larsen-linked fracture propagates beneath Nexus, shearing a support corridor and cutting emergency power. Meltwater enters the lower laboratory. The team must stabilize a route, move the archive, and evacuate before the corridor floods and refreezes. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Colorado water and ecosystems

Colorado’s reduced snowpack and streamflow stress cold-water species, including trout. The research supplied to the project cites projected mean summer streamflow declines of approximately **52%** and associated loss of Greenback Cutthroat Trout habitat under future climate scenarios. This is real-world reporting used to ground the fictional Colorado crisis. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The book’s Colorado pilot demonstrates bounded improvement rather than miraculous reversal:

- Sorting and washing consume water.
- The reactor consumes grid power.
- The constructed wetland can treat only a bounded flow.
- Rejected feedstock remains.
- Emissions must be monitored.
- Residual solids remain.
- Throughput is temporarily limited. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Governance and Law

### Antarctic governance

The Antarctic Treaty system and Madrid Protocol shape access, permits, environmental assessment, scientific activity, waste handling, and restrictions on commercialization. The project treats the organisms and associated samples under a provisional non-commercialization restriction until independent ecological assessment is complete. No single person made that decision; it arose from joint Antarctic research and environmental review within the Treaty, Madrid Protocol, and related environmental-agreement framework. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The restriction is also implemented as a project-specific permit and environmental-review condition. It limits commercial use and sample transfer. Copying data without authorization remains a contractual and institutional violation rather than theft of organisms. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Roman’s concealed T-ALL research is separate from ordinary ecological research. Required safety and ethics approvals were not obtained because Croft believes the approval process is too slow for his son’s deteriorating condition. Testing or treatment research conducted without those approvals is illegal and unethical, and is carried out in hiding by Croft and Roman. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha Dynamics and CSG exploit the difference between formal compliance and practical control. A company can obey the visible permit system while gaining disproportionate influence through logistics, infrastructure, data access, restricted communications, and first access to discoveries.

### Inspection and enforcement

CSG’s Falcon operation uses a legitimate scientific-drilling permit as cover. The permit authorizes scientific drilling and environmental monitoring only. Its concealment depends on the practical limitations of inspection in an isolated, weather-dependent environment. After exposure, publication produces hearings and an inspection request rather than an immediate arrest. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The realistic institutional victory is not instant punishment. It is the creation of a public record that makes suppression impossible, followed by investigation and inspection.

### Colorado governance

The waste-to-value organization began as a CSG research division. It is now a nonprofit public-benefit corporation funded initially by CSG and governed by scientists, conservation representatives, and affected communities. It has authority over conservation deployments but not private extraction. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado pilot ends under an **unincorporated interim partnership** operating through public contracts. Ownership, long-term financing, and permanent governance remain contested. The inspection comes first; afterward, a public meeting reads the interim contract aloud while participants visibly disagree over ownership and financing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Institutions and Corporate Power

### Clear Spring Global

Clear Spring Global, or CSG, began as a corporate organization involved in energy, research, and environmental systems. Its waste-to-value work began as a CSG research division. `(rules/decisions.md)`

CSG operates Falcon and Nexus through contractual and research subsidiaries under infrastructure controlled or owned by Alpha Dynamics. CSG carries the visible responsibility for the sites and their records. Alpha Dynamics retains strategic control, infrastructure leverage, and access. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

The CSG-linked Antarctic program is divided across facilities:

- **Falcon:** covert exploratory drilling and extraction monitoring.
- **Nexus:** ecological and materials research.
- **Colorado:** application and conservation pilot.
- **Merritt access:** controlled by Alpha Dynamics, with CSG receiving preferred access through the investment and subsidiary relationship. `(rules/decisions.md)`

CSG’s incentives are mixed:

- It can produce real environmental benefits.
- It wants to maintain control over valuable research and data.
- It uses corporate legal, investor, and compliance structures to apply pressure.
- It has a commercial incentive to suppress evidence that could halt extraction or expose ecological harm.
- It can present harmful choices as necessary to preserve energy systems and fund future beneficial work.

Roman’s complicity in Croft’s unauthorized T-ALL treatment research creates his principal legal and regulatory exposure. He agreed to Croft’s treatment work before traveling to Antarctica and helped continue research conducted without required approvals. This is the basis for any violation attributed to Roman, rather than an unexplained CSG power to control him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft remains the principal operative antagonist. CSG’s structures create the conditions and visible responsibility, but they do not become separate human villains. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alpha Dynamics

Alpha Dynamics is a privately held global conglomerate headquartered in Luxembourg and controlled by a secretive founder and CEO. It operates and invests on every continent across resources, energy, infrastructure, remediation, defense, robotics, artificial intelligence, space systems, biotechnology, advanced materials, finance, real estate, and logistics. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

Its operating model is built around **capability, access, and advantage**:

1. Identify difficult or underutilized opportunities.
2. Build, buy, finance, or partner.
3. Deploy capital, technology, logistics, and management.
4. Monetize products, services, data, licensing, infrastructure, or strategic access.
5. Scale through partnerships, acquisitions, and geographic expansion.

Alpha Dynamics is not fundamentally an environmental or humanitarian organization. It follows conventional profit motives and is willing to participate in mining, fossil fuels, defense, resource extraction, and large infrastructure where commercially attractive and legally viable. It can also fund remediation, biotechnology, climate technology, and conservation when those produce strategic or commercial value. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

Alpha Dynamics owns or controls the infrastructure underlying Falcon and Nexus. CSG operates those sites through contractual and research subsidiaries and carries their visible responsibility. Alpha Dynamics remains an investor in CSG, operates Merritt, controls Merritt access, and retains strategic access to Falcon, Nexus, and their research. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

The CEO remains off-page. He is Congolese, a former child soldier from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and his identity and history are not public in-world. His priorities appear through Alpha Dynamics policy, a research brief, and an intermediary who handles Merritt access. His interest in Antarctic extremophile research is connected to African sleeping sickness, or trypanosomiasis, which climate change is worsening in the DRC. The medical thread creates institutional pressure but does not become a second antagonist plot. `(rules/refinements.md)`

### Merritt Station’s access economy

Alpha Dynamics benefits from restoring Merritt because the station gives it:

- Early visibility into discoveries.
- Access to scientific data and samples.
- Control over logistics and infrastructure.
- A platform for robotics and AI.
- A test environment for extreme-environment systems.
- Relationships with universities and research institutions.
- First-mover opportunities in off-continent applications.

This is a form of power that may remain technically legal. The company does not need to own an Antarctic organism to profit from being first to see, understand, test, and commercialize its applications. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_merritt_station_reference.md)`

After exposure, Alpha Dynamics opens only the damaged archive and surface facilities immediately. Deeper subglacial access remains closed pending structural and environmental assessment. Commercial sampling is suspended pending review, and provenance records must be published. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### CSG and Alpha Dynamics as competing institutional purposes

The endgame is not simply heroes against a lone corporation. Alpha Dynamics and Croft’s CSG-linked interests represent competing purposes around the same science.

- Alpha Dynamics seeks commercially viable applications, including medical and industrial uses.
- Croft seeks to preserve CSG’s power, energy position, research control, and his son’s treatment path.
- Orien treats the work as public safety and public health.
- The conservation organization needs open enough access to validate and deploy bounded environmental applications.

These institutions may produce real benefits while still creating systems of dependency and exclusion.

## Economics, Labour, and Ownership

### Resource extraction

Falcon is conducting illegal exploratory drilling rather than commercial extraction. The purpose is to claim data, access, and future rights before the resource becomes legally exploitable. Its infrastructure and concealment practices resemble a scientific operation because that appearance is what allows it to operate. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The operation demonstrates a central economic fact of the world: information about future resources can be valuable before the resources themselves can legally or technically be extracted.

### Scientific labour

Antarctic research depends on specialized labour:

- Cryosphere scientists.
- Chemical engineers.
- Ecologists.
- Field-command and safety personnel.
- Robotics operators.
- Data analysts.
- Logistics crews.
- Maintenance workers.
- Communications and inspection staff.

Work is constrained by weather, transport windows, limited shelter, power, and the physical danger of ice movement. The people who control transport, safe routes, archives, and communications can exert power over the scientific work even when they do not own the underlying knowledge.

### Corporate ownership and data

The paperwork accompanying TJ is deliberately incomplete. Croft presents one TJ as a gift, while CSG later relies on procurement records and telemetry clauses to claim rights that Roman never knowingly accepted. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ is a type of robot: **Terrain Jumper**, designed for exploration, search, and rescue. There are many TJs. Roman’s TJ and Sitara’s TJ are separate physical units of the same model/type, not one body appearing in two places. Roman’s TJ is distinguished by its deep neural handshake with Roman. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Each active TJ body has a local mission partition. TJ units share route data, safety information, and selected sensor summaries across the mesh. Full archives require deliberate synchronization and may be inaccessible during shielding, damage, or network separation. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`

This creates a world in which ownership disputes concern not only physical machines but also:

- Sensor records.
- Environmental maps.
- Mission memory.
- Research provenance.
- Neural-interface telemetry.
- Data collected by autonomous systems.
- Commercial applications derived from public or restricted science.

### Medical pressure

Croft’s son has T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia. He was diagnosed at seventeen, relapsed at eighteen, became chronically ill at nineteen, and is critically ill at twenty after a failed stem-cell transplant. His condition deteriorates on a months-scale timeline. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

The possible medical leads include Antarctic fungal L-asparaginase and meriolin derivatives. These are grounded in real research directions but are not a cure in the book. Croft’s unauthorized research lacks required approvals and is conducted in hiding. `(rules/decisions.md; input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

If the hidden research becomes public, Alpha Dynamics fires Croft and pauses the activities before he can achieve a breakthrough. Within the story’s assumed timeline, the interruption causes his son to die within a month. The son’s death is the consequence Croft fears and ultimately accepts when he chooses the Colorado pilot’s survival over proprietary control. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft’s personal stakes therefore interact with institutional power. Preserving the public release costs him the treatment path he believes could save his son. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Technology

### Hard-science limits

The world’s technology follows the project’s hard-SF rules:

- Most infrastructure is recognizable and retrofitted.
- Emerging systems are common enough to be practical rather than magical.
- The few genuinely new systems carry explicit labels.
- A device cannot solve a problem merely because the scene needs it to.
- Power, heat, materials, data, communications, access, and maintenance remain constraints. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Project 863

**Label: L — sole major story license.**

Project 863 uses catalytic chemical recycling to depolymerize selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. It requires:

- Sorting.
- Heat.
- Energy.
- Clean, bounded feedstock.
- Controlled process conditions.

It:

- Cannot process all plastics.
- Produces residual waste.
- Produces emissions that must be monitored.
- Does not eliminate the need for sorting, washing, transport, or energy.
- Does not replace fossil fuels globally.
- Is most useful for localized waste streams.
- May produce feedstock rather than an automatically usable finished fuel. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The license exists to support the story’s waste-to-value premise. Its surrounding chemistry, logistics, waste handling, and environmental tradeoffs remain grounded.

Nexus’s Project 863 records are hidden in calibration sheets. Those records form one half of Orien’s repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Cold-active enzymes

**Label: EG.**

Antarctic extremophile research provides cold-active enzyme systems that lower the temperature and energy needed for a bounded Project 863 pretreatment. The actual depolymerization remains the licensed process. The enzymes do not make arbitrary waste disappear and do not remove the need for sorting, power, water, process controls, or residual handling. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Possible research applications include:

- Low-temperature recycling.
- Water treatment.
- Bioremediation.
- Cold-environment industrial chemistry.
- Medical research.
- Environmental monitoring.

### Terrain Jumper robots

**Label: EG, with the Roman neural bond labelled S.**

TJ means **Terrain Jumper**. It is a type of exploration, search-and-rescue, and reconnaissance robot. There are many TJs rather than one distributed individual appearing through every body.

The project uses multiple variants of the same model/type, including:

- A search-and-rescue unit.
- An extremophile-hunting or scientific-sensing unit.
- A heat-map and detection unit.
- Other specialized configurations where needed.

The familiar canine scout is a small-dog-scale visual anchor, but the variants are separate bodies of the same TJ type. Roman’s TJ is the only one with the deep neural handshake/neuralink. Sitara’s TJ and other TJs do not share that bond. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**FIXED specifications and limits:**

- Full scout configuration is approximately **249 grams**.
- The small scale is deliberate and associated with a **250-gram legal limit for technology transport** under certain situations.
- TJ must never be visualized as a large stereotypical robot dog.
- The complete scout remains at small-dog scale despite its extremely low mass.
- The platform uses ultra-light materials and low-power systems.
- Mission endurance is measured in hours to days depending on movement, sensors, communications, and environmental conditions.
- TJ can fabricate only small tools and replacement parts from sorted feedstock.
- Fabrication requires time and power.
- Sustained fabrication overheats the unit.
- TJ has no unlimited network access.
- TJ communicates through a screen, speakers, or, for Roman’s bonded unit, Roman’s limited neural channel. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ variants may include:

- Canine scout form.
- Feline or climbing form.
- Insect narrow-access form.
- Avian aerial form.
- Aquatic under-ice form.
- Sensor pods.
- Tool units.
- Heat-map and detection packages.
- Extremophile-sensing packages.

The variants share the Terrain Jumper design language and model identity, but each body has its own local mission partition and sensor package. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_bio_inspired_form_factors_reference.md)`

### TJ data architecture

Each active TJ body maintains a local mission partition containing the data needed for its assigned task, including local maps, sensor records, mission instructions, and relevant archives.

Across the mesh, TJs share:

- Routes.
- Safety alerts.
- Selected sensor summaries.
- Limited mission status.
- Communications-relay information.

Full archives require deliberate synchronization. They may be inaccessible when a unit is:

- Shielded.
- Damaged.
- Out of local mesh range.
- Power-limited.
- Physically separated from the other units.
- Operating under a different mission partition.

A TJ unit can therefore know that another unit has information without being able to retrieve the full archive. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`

Roman’s bonded TJ preserves local copies of Merritt provenance data and Falcon environmental records in its own mission partition. Sitara’s TJ has its own local data associated with search, heat mapping, and field detection. No TJ secretly uploads the full archive. The units exchange only selected summaries through CSG systems. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### TJ’s inherited Falcon authentication module

**Label: EG.**

The module is a sealed challenge-response credential embedded beside TJ’s communications core. Falcon sends a rotating machine challenge; the inherited hardware returns a signed response. The response opens only the hatch and a local maintenance interface. It does not grant access to every internal system. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The module is inherited from Alpha Dynamics’ reconstruction of the Terrain Jumper hardware and connects TJ’s history to the Falcon and Nexus infrastructure. Its physical appearance is compact, sealed, industrial, and worn, with protected connectors beside the communications core.

### Roman–TJ neural interface

**Label: S — the persistent bond.**

The lightning accident creates a low-bandwidth neural handshake between Roman and his TJ. The connection is not full telepathy, total sensory fusion, or remote control.

After four years:

- Roman receives selected alerts, directional impulses, urgent sensory signals, and compressed sensory tags.
- TJ receives Roman’s attention, urgency, balance, intent, and selected embodied cues.
- Roman cannot read all of TJ’s data.
- Roman cannot remotely control TJ.
- TJ cannot directly control Roman.
- Shielding or distance beyond local mesh range reduces the connection to intermittent status pulses.
- Power loss or physical damage can create false gaps.
- When the connection returns after a gap, sensory overload is possible.
- Roman and his TJ have learned each other’s operating patterns. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The neural interface is more than a display. It allows selected machine-derived sensory information to reach Roman and selected human state information to reach TJ. However, the bond remains constrained by bandwidth, range, reliability, shielding, power, and physical damage. `(rules/worldbuilding/tj_neural_interface_reference.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Other neural-interface technology

**Label: EG.**

Neural implants are common enough in the 2042–2046 setting to be part of Roman’s life, but the specific Roman–TJ bond is not a normal consumer feature. The electrical event produces an unusual persistent handshake rather than a standard supported interface. `(input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Transport

Falcon and Nexus are linked by tracked vehicles and occasional aircraft. The route takes approximately two hours when weather permits. Weather can delay or interrupt transport. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Antarctic bases depend on:

- Tracked vehicles.
- Aircraft.

- Heated shelters.

### Energy and heating

Field structures use heated shelters and internal heating systems to keep people, instruments, batteries, and process equipment within operating ranges. Energy is limited and must be allocated among life support, communications, scientific instruments, vehicles, robotics, and emergency systems.

Nexus retains limited emergency systems after its main power is shut down. The climax’s fracture cuts emergency power, making archive movement and evacuation time-sensitive. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Communications

Satellite communications are delayed or intermittent in the Antarctic field environment. Local mesh networks allow TJ units and sensor nodes to relay information when direct links or conventional infrastructure fail. Neural communication is also degraded by distance and shielding. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`

### Water and waste

Antarctic operations must transport, store, treat, and remove water and waste under environmental restrictions. Falcon uses concealed waste-handling systems. Nexus and Merritt contain legacy waste, damaged infrastructure, and contamination risks.

The Colorado pilot makes waste handling visible rather than magical:

- Feedstock is sorted.
- Some material is rejected.
- Washing consumes water.
- Process water is treated through a bounded constructed wetland.
- Residual solids remain.
- Emissions are monitored.
- Throughput is limited. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Housing and field life

Antarctic researchers live in heated base modules, field shelters, and transport-linked habitats. Protective clothing includes insulated layers, waterproof boots, goggles, gloves, and equipment designed for snow, ice, wind, and meltwater.

The supplied visual direction includes booties for ice exposure, internal heating, puffy expedition jackets, waterproof trousers, beanies, warm boots, and thermal snow goggles. These details are production material rather than universal legal requirements. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`
