## Overview

In 2046, climate disruption is no longer an abstract future threat. Antarctic ice, krill, Himalayan glaciers, Colorado snowpack, rivers, food supplies, and water access are all under pressure. The world still contains familiar roads, laboratories, universities, companies, paper files, research bases, and corporate hierarchies, but those systems operate inside a more unstable climate and a more unequal economy. `(rules/rules.md; input/seb.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

The main world connection is a **waste-to-value system**: Antarctic research and recovered materials are linked to conservation work in Colorado. The project’s intended loop is deliberately uncomfortable: technology and industry that have damaged the environment are redirected toward recovering materials and protecting ecosystems. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The Antarctic and Colorado crises have a shared structure. In Antarctica, declining sea ice threatens krill, a foundation of the food web. In Colorado, declining snowpack and streamflow threaten cold-water species such as greenback cutthroat trout. In both places, the loss of a keystone physical resource spreads through the rest of the ecosystem. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

## Time and History

### The 2026 Himalayan disaster

**Established project material.** A massive rock–ice avalanche fell from the Lirung massif in the Himalayas, became a fast-moving debris flow, and entered the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system before reaching the Nepal–Tibet border at Gyirong Port. It produced a wall of water, mud, and rock and destroyed infrastructure along a **72-kilometre stretch** of river. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The reported consequences include:

- **1,451+ deaths in Nepal**;
- **43+ deaths in Tibet**;
- **6,264+ people missing** across Nepal and Tibet;
- destruction of border facilities at Gyirong Port;
- destruction of houses, roads, bridges, and power projects;
- bodies carried as far as **240 kilometres downstream into India**;
- hundreds of missing foreign nationals, including Americans, Indians, British, Australians, Ukrainians, and others. `(input/amelia.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The disaster remains part of the world’s climate memory and is connected to the Himalayan background of the book. `(input/forge-characters.md; input/seb.md)`

### 2042 and the neural-link incident

**Idea-draft account, not settled canon.** In 2042, an eighteen-year-old Roman Ashford travelled in Nepal and crossed a glacier or iceberg with TJ. The ice split beneath him. During the rescue, a thunderstorm and a nearby lightning strike caused Roman’s neural implant to connect with TJ’s onboard systems. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The account says that neural implants were common among people in 2042, but the precise prevalence, medical basis, and mechanism of the lightning-mediated connection remain undefined. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Earlier Antarctic research

An underground or cave-based research facility called **Nexus** was decommissioned approximately twenty years before the main 2046 events, according to the idea draft. Its former programs included krill research and plastics research. The facility contained a krill ecosystem tank and research connected to project **863**. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

A separate abandoned Antarctic facility or bunker bears a **Falcon** symbol. It contains maps, charts, computers, drilling information, and evidence concerning who is conducting the drilling. It was dusty and had apparently gone unused for some time. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The relationship between Nexus, the Falcon facility, the current drilling site, and the secret messages from Antarctica is not yet established. `## Open`

## Geography and Major Settings

### Antarctica

The primary Antarctic setting is the **Larsen region**, especially the area around **Larsen C Ice Shelf**. The visual environment includes a snow-covered mountain range, coastline, floating sea ice, glaciers, and an icy ocean. The region contains a small community of research bases and homes, a current research and drilling base, an abandoned facility, and a cave laboratory. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The Antarctic season shown is **November**, from late morning into early afternoon. The sun remains above the horizon, though clouds can cover it. The expected weather is an icy blue sky with clouds and light wind. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

Antarctic field operations must account for unstable ice. Researchers relocate camps, use airborne surveys, avoid crevasse zones, and monitor ice movement in real time. Personnel wear insulated mission clothing, warm waterproof boots, and equipment adapted to ice travel. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The current base includes living quarters and laboratory or drilling infrastructure. A separate drilling site is associated with the illegal mining operation. The exact relationship between research, drilling, mining, and conservation is unresolved. `## Open`

### The Larsen Ice Shelf

Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B collapsed in 2002. Larsen C is described in the project material as thinning, fragile, and vulnerable; Larsen D is also identified as a subsection of the region. Basin geology affects ice-shelf stability. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not book canon.** Research gathered for the project describes Föhn-induced melting over Larsen C and examines how atmospheric-river shape and landfall affect melt patterns. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

**Research-informed proposal.** A major Larsen C structural failure in or around 2046 could remove the floating ice shelf’s buttressing effect, allowing land-based glaciers behind it to accelerate and adding several millimetres to global sea-level rise. The material calls this the **“cork out of the bottle” effect**. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Whether this failure occurs in the book, and whether it is the shared existential threat, remains undecided. `## Open`

### The Himalayas

The Himalayas function as both a physical setting and a source of family, cultural, and scientific history. The material places glacier travel, high-altitude hiking, the 2026 disaster, and the neural-link incident in Nepal. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Colorado

Colorado is a research, corporate, and conservation setting. The material includes the Rocky Mountains, drying rivers and lakes, declining snowpack, trout habitat, university research, corporate headquarters, research laboratories, drilling sites, and AI-driven recycling infrastructure. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not book canon.** A USGS study gathered for the project projects approximately **52% mean summer streamflow decline** and physical habitat loss for greenback cutthroat trout under future climate scenarios. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

Colorado’s conservation work is intended to connect to Antarctic waste recovery and research. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md)`

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

The book’s environmental baseline is a world of climate-driven instability:

- Antarctic ice shelves and sea ice are melting or becoming structurally unsafe;
- krill habitat and populations are threatened by sea-ice decline;
- Himalayan glaciers and ice routes are dangerous and changing;
- Colorado snowpack and natural water sources are declining;
- Colorado rivers and lakes are drying out;
- cold-water trout habitat is shrinking;
- food supplies are low and food prices are high;
- sea levels are rising as land-based ice becomes less restrained. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/forge-characters.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The project repeatedly uses ice, snow, and water as physical systems rather than abstract symbols. A crack, collapse, flood, dried river, disappearing snowpack, or damaged ecosystem must have material consequences for travel, work, food, research, and survival. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md)`

## Economics, Labour, and Ownership

The 2046 economy is under pressure from food scarcity, high food prices, declining water access, climate damage, and unequal control of technical resources. `(input/seb.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

CSG employs chemical engineers and operates across research and extraction. Its seniority structure includes a company owner, senior employees, and younger researchers. Corporate employment can determine access to laboratories, archives, travel assignments, research data, food, and water. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The draft presents a worker whose employment loss leaves him unable to afford food and water. This is an idea-draft event, but it establishes the intended possibility that corporate power and basic-resource access are closely connected. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Recycling and waste sorting involve significant labour and technical infrastructure. AI-guided optical sorting and air-jet systems can separate metals and other recyclables, but the material does not establish who owns the recovered material or who benefits financially. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### Real-world reporting

AMP Robotics, based in Louisville, Colorado, is cited as a real-world example of AI-guided sorting systems. The gathered material describes optical sorting and automated air jets separating ferrous metals, non-ferrous metals, and other recyclables, with reported efficiency of up to 90% in a next-generation Commerce City facility. This is research about the real world, not automatically a 2046 story fact. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

## Technology

### Hard-science baseline

The world follows an **80/15/5** structure:

- **80% Truth:** familiar present-day systems, institutions, materials, and habits;
- **15% Educated Guess:** emerging technologies made common, cheaper, more rugged, or more integrated;
- **5% Speculation:** a small number of genuinely new elements. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The future should look retrofitted rather than replaced: current buildings, vehicles, paper records, old bunkers, institutional logos, ordinary clothing, and legacy infrastructure remain alongside newer systems. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### TJ

TJ is a single robot dog whose history must reconcile two descriptions:

1. a Himalayan sheep-dog-shaped robot with seamless silver paneling, a green-and-black screen, side buttons, expressive electronic eyes, and red insulated booties;
2. a scavenger-built machine assembled from spare parts by an engineer called **Bi11bot**, equipped for material scanning and fabrication, and bonded to Roman through the lightning incident. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/avengers.md)`

The following capabilities are established in the supplied material:

- scanning surroundings for usable raw materials, including scrap metal, salvaged plastics, and ice-compatible alloys;
- an internal three-dimensional printing fabricator;
- printing tools, parts, or equipment from gathered metals and plastics;
- high-beam lights in the eyes;
- emergency and survival functions;
- communication through a screen and spoken dialogue. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; input/amelia.md)`

The detailed character prompt restricts TJ’s presentation to dialogue in its chat/comms mode: TJ may say that it is scanning or could print something, but the prompt forbids narrated physical actions in that mode. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The relationship between TJ’s physical body, its chat interface, its onboard AI, and Roman’s neural implant is not fully defined. The lightning connection is an intentional speculative element unless a different mechanism is approved. `## Open`

### Plastics-to-fuel research

Nexus project 863 includes an unfinished concept for breaking down used plastics into gasoline or another usable fuel. The draft presents it as a possible way to reduce dependence on fossil-fuel extraction and lower atmospheric pollution. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

**L candidate.** The material explicitly identifies this as the story’s speculative heart: a process that can transform used plastics into a useful fuel at meaningful scale. The nearest real-world basis is chemical recycling and waste-to-fuel research, but the project has not established the chemistry, energy balance, feedstock limits, emissions profile, catalysts, by-products, or deployment scale. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Under the hard-SF rules, this process cannot automatically “end global warming.” It must have fixed rules, limits, costs, waste streams, and consequences once the Director approves its exact form. `## Open`
