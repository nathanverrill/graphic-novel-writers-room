## Overview

In 2046, climate disruption is no longer an abstract future threat. Antarctic sea ice and ice shelves are under pressure, krill habitat is declining, Himalayan glaciers and ice routes are changing, and Colorado snowpack, rivers, lakes, and cold-water habitats are deteriorating. The world still contains familiar roads, laboratories, universities, paper files, research bases, companies, corporate hierarchies, and ordinary field equipment, but those systems operate inside a more unstable climate and a more unequal economy. `(rules/rules.md; input/seb.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

The central ecological threat is the combined cascade caused by **Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability**. Sea-ice loss threatens krill and the food web that depends on them. Atmospheric and oceanic warming intensify Larsen C’s structural hazard. The illegal extraction program and the suppression of evidence are human mechanisms that prevent an adequate response; drilling worsens local instability but does not single-handedly cause global climate collapse. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The main world connection is a waste-to-value system: Antarctic research and recovered materials are linked to conservation work in Colorado. The project’s intended loop is deliberately uncomfortable: technology and industry that have damaged the environment are redirected toward recovering materials and protecting ecosystems. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

## Time and History

### The 2026 Himalayan disaster

The Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster is Sitara Lhakpa’s defining childhood event. In 2026, when Sitara was six, a massive rock-and-ice avalanche from the Lirung massif became a fast-moving debris flow, entered the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system, and reached the Nepal–Tibet border at Gyirong Port. It destroyed Sitara’s home and killed her parents. Sitara survived with extended family or relief workers. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The reported disaster consequences include:

- **1,451+ deaths in Nepal**;
- **43+ deaths in Tibet**;
- **6,264+ people missing** across Nepal and Tibet;
- destruction of border facilities at Gyirong Port;
- destruction of houses, roads, bridges, and power projects;
- bodies carried as far as **240 kilometres downstream into India**;
- hundreds of missing foreign nationals. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Sitara’s hometown is a fictional Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region. Her aunt and cousins remain her living connection to the community. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### 2042 and the neural-link incident

**S / L boundary.** In 2042, an eighteen-year-old Roman Ashford travelled to Nepal on a personal trip with TJ. While Roman crossed an ice formation, the ice split beneath him and he fell into a fracture. During a nearby electrical storm, lightning caused a persistent low-bandwidth neural handshake between Roman’s neural implant and TJ’s onboard systems. `(rules/decisions.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman receives status, direction, and urgent sensory signals from TJ, but cannot read all of TJ’s data or remotely control TJ. The connection has limited range and reliability. It is not unrestricted telepathy. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman’s 2042 accident is separate from Sitara’s 2026 disaster. Both occurred in Nepal and are connected by the changing ice conditions of the Himalayas, not by a shared event. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Earlier Antarctic research

Nexus was a CSG-linked scientific laboratory associated with parallel ecological and materials programs. It studied Antarctic krill systems and developed Project 863, a waste-to-value process for selected waste plastics. Nexus was closed after researchers documented the connection between extraction, sea-ice loss, and krill decline. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon was the operational and extraction-monitoring site belonging to the same CSG-linked program. It documented drilling, local damage, ice movement, and the consequences of the extraction operation. Nexus studied the ecological consequences and possible waste-to-value responses. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nexus was decommissioned approximately twenty years before the main 2046 events. Its power is off, but insulated paper archives and limited emergency systems remain. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Geography and Major Settings

### Antarctica

The primary Antarctic setting is the Larsen region, especially the area around **Larsen C Ice Shelf**. Human bases and homes sit on or near the Antarctic Peninsula’s accessible coastal research zone. Falcon lies inland as a covert exploratory drilling and monitoring site. Nexus occupies a nearby coastal cave system. Wildlife appears only where habitat and season support it. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The visual environment includes snow-covered mountains, coastline, floating sea ice, glaciers, an icy ocean, exposed rock, research bases, homes, drilling infrastructure, abandoned facilities, and cave laboratories. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The Antarctic season shown is **November**, from late morning into early afternoon. The sun remains above the horizon, though clouds can cover it. Expected conditions include an icy blue sky, clouds, and light wind. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

Field operations account for unstable ice. Researchers relocate camps, use airborne surveys, avoid crevasse zones, and monitor ice movement in real time. Personnel use insulated mission clothing, warm waterproof boots, and equipment adapted to ice travel. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### The Larsen Ice Shelf

Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B collapsed in 2002. Larsen C is thinning, fragile, and vulnerable; Larsen D is also identified as a subsection of the region. Basin geology affects ice-shelf stability. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not book canon.** Research gathered for the project describes Föhn-induced melting over Larsen C and examines how atmospheric-river shape and landfall affect melt patterns. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

During the climax, a major fracture opens in Larsen C, but Larsen C does not fully collapse. The team cannot reverse the climate trend. They stop the drilling that is worsening local instability and transmit the evidence. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Sea-ice loss and krill decline form the ecological cascade. Larsen C fracture is a separate ice-shelf hazard intensified by atmospheric and oceanic warming. The drilling worsens local risk but does not single-handedly cause global collapse. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

### The Himalayas

The Himalayas function as a physical setting and as a source of family, cultural, and scientific history. The material places the 2026 disaster and Roman’s 2042 accident in Nepal. `(rules/decisions.md; input/amelia.md; input/seb.md)`

Sitara’s heritage is Thakali and Nepalese. Her fictional hometown lies in the Kali Gandaki region. The wider Himalayan cultural material includes yak herding, high-pass trade with Tibet, cold-climate agriculture, Mani Rimdu, and foods such as tsampa, thukpa, fermented yak-butter tea, and potatoes. `(rules/decisions.md; input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not book canon.** Research notes describe the EPAS1 gene variant associated with Sherpa high-altitude adaptation and oxygen use. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### Colorado

Colorado is a research, corporate, and conservation setting. The material includes the Rocky Mountains, drying rivers and lakes, declining snowpack, trout habitat, university research, corporate headquarters, research laboratories, drilling sites, and AI-driven recycling infrastructure. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Colorado conservation is connected to the Antarctic waste-to-value work. Antarctic sea-ice and krill data demonstrate the urgency of conservation investment, while the bounded Project 863 process is applied to localized waste streams in Colorado. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Real-world reporting, not book canon.** A USGS study gathered for the project projects approximately **52% mean summer streamflow decline** and physical habitat loss for greenback cutthroat trout under future climate scenarios. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

The book’s environmental baseline is a world of climate-driven instability:

- Antarctic sea ice is declining;
- Larsen C is structurally vulnerable;
- krill habitat and reproduction are threatened by changing sea-ice conditions;
- Himalayan glaciers and ice routes are dangerous and changing;
- Colorado snowpack and streamflow are declining;
- Colorado rivers and lakes are drying out;
- cold-water trout habitat is shrinking;
- food supplies are low and food prices are high;
- sea levels are rising as land-based ice becomes less restrained. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md; input/forge-characters.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The project uses ice, snow, and water as physical systems rather than abstract symbols. A crack, collapse, flood, dried river, disappearing snowpack, or damaged ecosystem has material consequences for travel, work, food, research, and survival. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md)`

## Institutions and Power

### Clear Spring Global

**Clear Spring Global (CSG)** is a corporate organization operating in Colorado and Antarctica. It conducts chemical, climate, drilling, plastics, and Antarctic research and employs scientists and engineers. Its owner is **Benedict Croft**, a wealthy, old-money oil tycoon. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

CSG’s interests include fossil-fuel extraction and control of research with consequences for the energy system. Croft believes controlled suppression is necessary to preserve CSG’s power and the energy system. He tells himself that continued corporate dominance will eventually allow him to fund beneficial work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

CSG maintains the Falcon exploratory drilling and extraction-monitoring operation and the historical Nexus laboratory. It operates Falcon under a legitimate scientific-drilling permit, disguising resource exploration as climate and ice-core work. Subcontractors, falsified manifests, restricted communications, and isolated weather windows help it evade inspection. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### The waste-to-value organization

The waste-to-value organization began as a CSG research division. Nexus was its scientific laboratory, while Falcon was CSG’s covert extraction and monitoring facility. `(rules/decisions.md)`

It is a nonprofit public-benefit corporation funded initially by CSG and governed by scientists, conservation representatives, and affected communities. It has authority over conservation deployments but not private extraction. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Its long-term ownership, financing, and governance remain contested after the ending’s public release and Colorado pilot. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Antarctic research community

Antarctica contains a small community of research bases and homes. People live and work in isolated conditions, with access to laboratories, drilling sites, archives, communications, and field equipment. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The specific governing authority of the community, the national status of every base, and the full enforcement process for treaty violations remain unestablished.

### The Falcon facility

Falcon is a covert exploratory drilling and extraction-monitoring site rather than a conventional mine. It contains directional boreholes, seismic equipment, pumps, heated shelters, fuel stores, and concealed waste-handling systems supporting sub-ice resource surveys. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Its visible entrance is a snow-covered, submarine-like metal hatch set into the Antarctic ground, with a Falcon symbol engraved in the exterior. Inside are maps and charts, wall-mounted computers, rolling chairs, Falcon-branded chairs, lock screens, pens, and records concerning drilling and the operation’s damage. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

TJ can answer the bunker’s machine challenge because its salvaged Falcon authentication module was inherited through Bi11bot’s reconstruction. This access does not grant TJ access to every system inside. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Nexus

Nexus is the decommissioned scientific laboratory of the CSG-linked program. It is built into a coastal Antarctic cave system and contains an office formerly used by Orien Keel, old paper files, Project 863 records, ecological data, and a krill ecosystem tank. `(rules/decisions.md; input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Power is off. Insulated archives and limited emergency systems remain. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The krill tank is dead but preserves sensor data and sediment samples showing that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Law and Governance

The Madrid Protocol is part of the world’s Antarctic legal framework, and mining in Antarctica is prohibited. CSG’s operation is illegal exploratory drilling intended to claim data, access, and future rights before the resource becomes legally exploitable. It is not yet commercially extracting material. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The operation is concealed through a legitimate scientific-drilling permit, subcontractors, falsified manifests, restricted communications, and weather-dependent isolation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Falcon facility documents the operation and its local environmental damage. Nexus preserves the ecological and materials research that explains the consequences. The two sites belong to the same CSG-linked program but serve different functions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

After exposure, independent scientists authenticate the evidence. Publication produces hearings and an inspection request but no immediate arrest. The realistic institutional victory is making suppression impossible and creating a public record. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

## Economics, Labour, and Ownership

The 2046 economy is under pressure from food scarcity, high food prices, declining water access, climate damage, and unequal control of technical resources. `(input/seb.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

CSG employs chemical engineers and operates across research and extraction. Corporate employment can determine access to laboratories, archives, travel assignments, research data, food, and water. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Recycling and waste sorting require significant labour and technical infrastructure. AI-guided optical sorting and air-jet systems can separate metals and other recyclables, but the material does not establish who ultimately owns recovered material or receives financial benefit. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### Real-world reporting

AMP Robotics, based in Louisville, Colorado, is cited as a real-world example of AI-guided sorting systems. The gathered material describes optical sorting and automated air jets separating ferrous metals, non-ferrous metals, and other recyclables, with reported efficiency of up to 90% in a next-generation Commerce City facility. This is research about the real world, not automatically a 2046 story fact. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

## Technology

### Hard-science baseline

The world follows an **80/15/5** structure:

- **80 percent Truth:** familiar present-day systems, institutions, materials, and habits;
- **15 percent Educated Guess:** emerging technologies made common, cheaper, more rugged, or more integrated;
- **5 percent Speculation:** a small number of genuinely new elements. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The future should look retrofitted rather than replaced: current buildings, vehicles, paper records, old bunkers, institutional logos, ordinary clothing, legacy infrastructure, and newer systems coexist. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### TJ

TJ is one robot dog with a Himalayan sheep-dog body, seamless silver paneling, a green-and-black screen, side buttons, electronic dog-like eyes, and red insulated booties. Bi11bot built TJ from parts salvaged from a decommissioned Nexus support unit. TJ therefore carries inherited Nexus systems, including the Falcon authentication module that allows it to answer the bunker’s machine challenge. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ’s established capabilities include:

- scanning surroundings for usable raw materials, including scrap metal, salvaged plastics, and ice-compatible alloys;
- fabricating only small tools and replacement parts from sorted feedstock;
- requiring time and power for fabrication;
- overheating under sustained fabrication use;
- using high-beam lights in its eyes;
- interpreting records and communicating through a screen and speakers;
- communicating with Roman through the limited neural handshake;
- operating under human direction rather than having unlimited autonomy or network access. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**EG.** TJ’s fabrication and AI systems are educated guesses grounded in emerging robotics, material sorting, and field fabrication. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

TJ’s physical actions appear in the art. Its dialogue may report a scan, a possible fabrication, or a communication, but it does not narrate its own physical actions in stage-direction form. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Neural implants and Roman–TJ bond

**EG.** Neural implants exist in the 2042–2046 world. Roman has one. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**S.** The lightning event caused a persistent low-bandwidth neural handshake between Roman’s implant and TJ’s onboard systems. Roman receives status, direction, and urgent sensory signals from TJ. He cannot read all data or remotely control TJ. Range and reliability are limited. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The handshake is not unlimited network access, unrestricted mind-reading, or remote motor control. It remains useful for survival and urgent communication but cannot replace spoken communication, screen display, records, or human judgment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Artificial intelligence and integration

**EG.** AI can be integrated into machines, sorting systems, research workflows, and communications. TJ is a field AI, while waste-to-value systems use AI-guided optical sorting. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

TJ has no unlimited network access. Its ability to interact with Falcon derives from inherited authentication hardware and answers only the bunker’s machine challenge; it does not automatically unlock every internal system. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Project 863: catalytic chemical recycling

**L — sole major license.** Project 863 uses catalytic chemical recycling to depolymerize selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. Its nearest real-world basis is chemical recycling and waste-to-fuel research. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Project 863 has fixed limits:

- feedstock must be sorted and sufficiently clean;
- it cannot process all plastics;
- the process requires heat and energy;
- it produces residual waste and emissions;
- it is useful mainly for localized waste streams;
- it cannot replace fossil fuels;
- it does not end global warming or reverse the ecological cascade;
- its deployment must be evaluated against energy sources, contamination, emissions, and waste disposal. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The process is therefore a bounded waste-to-value response, not a universal climate solution. The waste-to-value organization applies it in a Colorado pilot after the public release. Long-term ownership, financing, and governance remain contested. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Infrastructure and Communication

### Travel and logistics

The Antarctic bases are linked by tracked vehicles and occasional aircraft. The journey from the current CSG site to Nexus takes approximately two hours by a weather-dependent over-ice route. Satellite communications are delayed or intermittent. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The bases require imported or stored food, water processing, heating, power, replacement parts, and communications. Specific supply contracts and emergency procedures are not established.

### Archives and data

CSG and the Antarctic facilities retain both digital and paper records. Some records are redacted or removed from servers, while paper files survive in old offices and archives. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The information infrastructure is mixed: networked servers and wall computers coexist with paper archives, physical coordinates, passwords, logos, and mechanical access systems. `(input/amelia.md; input/seb.md)`

### Secret messages from Antarctica

A Falcon scientist—Orien Keel—secretly transmits automated compressed bursts from Antarctica. The messages contain:

- drilling coordinates;
- ice-movement data;
- warnings that the extraction program is accelerating Larsen-system instability. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The messages are automated warnings prepared by Orien after he discovered Falcon’s extraction program and hid corroborating records before Croft fired him. `(rules/decisions.md)`
