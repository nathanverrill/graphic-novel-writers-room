# Merritt Station — Hidden Knowledge Beneath the Ice

## Overview

**Facility:** Merritt Station  
**Type:** Antarctic research facility inspired by Wilkes Station  
**Location:** Wilkes Land, East Antarctica  
**Original operation:** 1960s–1990s  
**Abandoned for:** Approximately 30+ years  
**Status in 2046:** Partial restoration  
**Access:** Restricted and hazardous  
**Primary purpose in 2046:** Research, remediation, and scientific discovery  

Merritt Station is a long-abandoned Antarctic research facility inspired by Wilkes Station. By 2046, it is being partially restored after decades of exposure to shifting ice, snow accumulation, structural deformation, flooding, and environmental damage.

Although the station is physically degraded, it remains scientifically valuable.

Decades of abandonment have preserved or concealed:

- Lost research
- Scientific records
- Ice cores
- Physical samples
- Biological samples
- Legacy infrastructure
- Access routes to subglacial environments
- Geological records
- Potential extremophile ecosystems

Merritt Station functions as both a hazardous remediation site and a scientific time capsule.

# Real-World Damage from Shifting Ice

Antarctic facilities can experience significant damage when built in environments where ice continues to move.

---

## Structural Deformation

Ice pressure and creep can deform:

- Walls
- Floors
- Ceilings
- Door frames
- Structural supports

Possible results include:

- Jammed doors
- Uneven floors
- Cracked walls
- Distorted rooms

---

## Misaligned Passageways

Ice movement can shift different parts of a facility at different rates.

This creates:

- Offset tunnels
- Structural steps
- Gaps
- Crushed corridors
- Dead-end passages

Original maps may no longer accurately describe the station.

---

## Collapsed Sections

Accumulated snow, ice load, and structural stress can cause:

- Roof collapse
- Wall failure
- Support failure
- Long-term structural degradation

Some station sections may become completely inaccessible.

---

## Ice and Snow Infill

Windblown snow and compacted ice can enter through:

- Damaged doors
- Vents
- Cracks
- Collapsed walls
- Broken ventilation systems

Over decades, internal rooms and corridors can fill with snow and ice.

---

## Flooding and Ice Intrusion

Meltwater can enter fractured station areas.

Possible effects include:

- Internal flooding
- Refreezing
- Ice growth inside corridors
- Damage to equipment
- Blocked routes

---

## Crevasses and Surface Cracks

Surface ice movement can create:

- Crevasses
- Rifts
- Cracks
- Terrain deformation

These features make both surface travel and excavation hazardous.

# Storyworld Significance

Merritt Station is useful narratively because the site itself creates mystery without requiring an artificial secret laboratory.

The station was once a legitimate research facility.

Its mysteries emerge because decades of abandonment created inaccessible layers.

The narrative can therefore uncover:

- Forgotten research
- Unpublished findings
- Old samples
- Scientific disputes
- Incomplete records
- Infrastructure hidden beneath accumulated ice
- Previously unreachable subglacial environments

Every deeper level represents both increased danger and older or more difficult-to-access knowledge.

# Narrative Model

Merritt Station works best when treated as:

**A damaged research station that accidentally became a time capsule.**

The mystery does not need to depend on the station having been built for a secret purpose.

Instead:

1. Legitimate research was conducted there.
2. The station was abandoned.
3. Ice movement gradually buried and damaged the facility.
4. Records and samples remained behind.
5. New technology makes portions of the facility accessible again.
6. Restorers discover information that was previously lost or unreachable.
7. Deeper exploration eventually provides access to subglacial environments.
8. The discoveries create scientific, commercial, environmental, and ethical conflicts.
