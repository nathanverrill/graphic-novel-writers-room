# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Canon — premise and intended experience.** The book is a crossover: Sitara Lhakpa and Nayah’s Falcon-base story and Roman Ashford’s Nexus story are both true in the same world and must converge rather than replace one another. The story is set in 2046 across Antarctica, the Himalayas as backstory, and Colorado. `(rules/avengers.md; rules/rules.md)`

The intended experience is a hard-science graphic novel about climate evidence, corporate power, scientific stewardship, waste-to-value research, and the question of whether useful knowledge may be controlled by whoever extracts it first. The story should make the reader see both the damage and the measurable good: Antarctic sea-ice loss, krill decline, unstable Larsen ice, Colorado water stress, recovered waste, cleaner water, and returning aquatic life. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; input/seb.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can doing measurable good justify violating scientific stewardship, consent, and ownership?

A related question connects the Antarctic mystery to Roman and TJ’s relationship:

> Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?

These questions are present in the current draft and story notes, but the final book should answer them through choices rather than speeches. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

The protagonists are **Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford together**, with equal plot weight, agency, and presence. When the structure requires choosing a scene lead, the story alternates between them. Nayah, Roman, TJ units, and the supporting characters must contribute necessary capabilities rather than turn either protagonist into a sidekick. `(rules/protagonists.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Premise

**Canon — core threat.** Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability are producing a combined ecological cascade. Sea-ice loss undermines krill reproduction and therefore the food web; atmospheric and oceanic warming intensify Larsen C’s fracture risk. Illegal extraction and information suppression are the human mechanisms preventing an adequate response. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

CSG’s Antarctic program contains two linked sites:

- **Falcon** is the covert operational and extraction-monitoring site. It presents illegal exploratory drilling as permitted climate and ice-core work.
- **Nexus** is the research site connected to the same broader program. It studied ecological consequences, krill and ice data, and bounded waste-to-value responses.

Alpha Dynamics owns or controls the infrastructure underlying Falcon and Nexus. CSG operates the sites through contractual and research subsidiaries. CSG carries the visible responsibility for the operations; Alpha Dynamics retains strategic control, access, and the ability to shape what happens to the infrastructure and research. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

The waste-to-value work began as a CSG research division. Nexus was its scientific laboratory; Falcon was CSG’s covert extraction and monitoring facility operating on infrastructure controlled by Alpha Dynamics. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon is not yet commercially extracting an Antarctic resource. It conducts directional drilling, seismic monitoring, pumping, heated-shelter operations, fuel storage, and concealed waste handling in order to claim data, access, and future rights before a resource becomes legally exploitable. Its permit authorizes scientific drilling and environmental monitoring only. The violation is the concealed resource survey, unauthorized directional boreholes, falsified manifests, concealed waste, and preparation of future extraction rights; the story does not call the activity lawful mining. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The central research is **Project 863**, which uses catalytic chemical recycling to depolymerize selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. It requires sorting, heat, energy, and clean feedstock; produces residual waste and emissions; cannot process all plastics; and is useful mainly for localized waste streams rather than replacing fossil fuels. Project 863 is the story’s sole major **L**. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Nexus’s cold-active enzyme research lowers the temperature and energy required for a bounded pretreatment step. The actual depolymerization remains the licensed process. The Colorado application demonstrates value without becoming a universal cure. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`

## Narrative Engine and Causal Spine

1. Sitara and Nayah monitor unstable Antarctic ice near Falcon.
2. Sitara crosses a flagged zone to retrieve a sensor and orders the active search-and-rescue TJ unit to keep recording.
3. Her weight, combined with nearby drilling vibration, triggers a local slab failure.
4. The failure exposes Falcon’s buried hatch and traps equipment.
5. The search-and-rescue TJ unit answers the hatch’s machine challenge using inherited Falcon authentication hardware.
6. Sitara finds evidence that the drilling is not legitimate climate work and receives the first half of Orien’s paired warning.
7. Roman’s separate investigation leads him from Colorado, through Orien’s belongings and the Antarctic paper archive, toward Nexus.
8. Before traveling to Antarctica, Roman has already agreed to help Croft with unapproved treatment chemistry for Croft’s son.
9. Croft later hires or takes Sitara into the same research effort as a foil and scientific counterweight to Roman. Sitara initially does not know Roman is involved.
10. Sitara becomes suspicious when she sees Roman leaving his station and asks her TJ unit to check his movements.
11. Both teams reach Nexus.
12. A Larsen-linked fracture damages Nexus and forces immediate cooperation.
13. Roman’s bonded TJ unit and Sitara’s separate mission-assigned TJ unit operate as different physical units of the same Terrain Jumper type, but each holds a local mission partition.
14. Sitara uses a thermal heat map from her TJ unit to locate missing Orien before he reaches hypothermia. She leaves a phone so Orien can contact her if he regains consciousness.
15. Sitara and Roman exchange their hidden evidence because neither half can establish the truth alone.
16. The combined evidence reveals that CSG’s Colorado successes depend on Antarctic research and samples routed through Merritt without the required ecological safeguards.
17. Orien’s warnings show that he discovered the unapproved T-ALL treatment research and threatened to become a whistleblower. Croft threatened to fire him and used his network, money, and resources to make him disappear.
18. Alpha Dynamics or a superior corporate authority threatens to wipe or disable TJ’s records and relevant local memory if Roman does not disclose what he is doing.
19. Sitara confronts Roman about Orien and his treatment work. At that moment, Orien’s phone rings: he has regained consciousness and calls Sitara. Roman is shocked because Orien’s survival may expose the entire hidden project.
20. Roman receives a low-bandwidth neural refusal and fear of erasure from TJ. TJ then repeats its consent through the screen and speaker so Sitara and Nayah can witness it. Roman and the others choose accountable disclosure and preservation of the agreed evidence rather than allowing the records to be erased or privately controlled.
21. Larsen C fractures further but does not fully collapse. The team stops the drilling, evacuates, preserves the evidence, and keeps the TJ records from being erased.
22. Project 863 is released into a Colorado pilot whose limits and tradeoffs remain visible.
23. The ending leaves ownership, financing, and long-term governance contested while making the institutional conflict impossible to ignore.

## Story So Far

### Interstitial: Roman’s Himalayan Origin — 2042

**Material type: explicit decision, supported by idea draft.**

Roman Ashford is eighteen and on a personal senior climbing expedition in Nepal. The trip is unrelated to Sitara, her family, and Merritt research. The changing ice and water systems provide the environmental connection between their lives, not a shared expedition or pre-existing relationship. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman crosses unstable ice with one Terrain Jumper unit, then falls through a weakened section. The earlier draft describes an electrical storm arriving while Roman is unconscious or barely conscious. Lightning travels through the ice, climbing equipment, the TJ unit, and Roman’s neural interface. The resulting connection is not telepathy or unlimited control: it becomes a persistent, low-bandwidth neural handshake unique to Roman’s unit. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md; input/seb.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Roman receives selected status, direction, and urgent sensory signals from his bonded TJ unit. That unit receives Roman’s attention, urgency, balance, and intent. The connection is unreliable at range, through shielding, after power loss, or when damaged systems reconnect. Roman cannot read all TJ data or remotely control the unit. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ is a robot type: **Terrain Jumper**. There are many TJs. Roman’s TJ is distinguished from other TJs, including Sitara’s mission-assigned units, by the deep neural handshake created during the accident. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Interstitial: Sitara’s Himalayan Origin — 2026

**Material type: explicit decision, supported by research and character draft.**

Sitara is six when the Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster destroys her home and kills her parents. She survives with extended family or relief workers. Her aunt and cousins remain her living connection to an unnamed fictional Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The disaster is not shown as a simple personal tragedy detached from the main plot. It establishes Sitara’s lifelong attention to unstable ice, water, and the consequences of delayed warnings. There are no settled physical injuries or lasting bodily consequences from the disaster. Sitara is presented as twenty-six in 2046. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The event produces her starting wound: she believes she was helpless, a burden, and too late to act. Her later recklessness grows from an attempt to disprove that helplessness. `(input/forge-characters.md)`

### Pages 1–2: Hook — The Crack and the Warning

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 1–2 are the Hook. Page 1 includes a brief Himalayan interstitial; the primary present-tense action is Sitara-led. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Near Larsen C in November 2046, Sitara, Nayah, and a search-and-rescue TJ unit monitor ice movement. The setting is a coastal Antarctic research zone with ice, snow-covered ridges, research bases, homes, and the remote Falcon operation. Their instruments register movement and drilling vibration. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

A warning flags an unstable zone. Sitara crosses it to retrieve a sensor and orders TJ to keep recording. This is a consequential choice, not an accident without an agent: her weight and nearby drilling vibration trigger a local slab failure. The failure exposes the buried Falcon hatch and traps equipment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Page-turn image:** the exposed hatch beneath broken ice, engraved with the Falcon symbol, while drilling vibration continues below. TJ is the first to orient toward it. The reader sees that the ice failure has uncovered something deliberately concealed before Sitara fully understands what it is. `(input/amelia.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The Himalayan interstitial establishes the visual and emotional echo: young Sitara watches floodwater take her home; adult Sitara now stands on unstable ice and chooses to continue. The juxtaposition makes the present decision personal without explaining her entire history. `(input/amelia.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

### Pages 3–5: Plot Turn 1 — Falcon Opens

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 3–5 are Plot Turn 1. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Falcon hatch is a submarine-like metal door buried in snow and ice. The active TJ unit answers Falcon’s rotating machine challenge through a sealed challenge-response credential embedded beside its communications core. The unit returns a signed response, opening only the hatch and a local maintenance interface. It does not gain unrestricted access to the bunker. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The authentication hardware is inherited from the Alpha Dynamics infrastructure underlying the Falcon and Nexus systems. The credential allows a TJ to answer the hatch’s machine challenge without granting access to every internal system. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Inside Falcon, Sitara and Nayah find:

- drilling maintenance files;
- vibration logs;
- ice-motion plots;
- maps and charts;
- computers carrying Falcon logos;
- concealed drilling coordinates;
- restricted communications records;
- falsified or incomplete scientific manifests;
- evidence that the site is operational rather than abandoned.

The visual language includes dusty workstations, rolling chairs, wall computers, Falcon-marked pens and lock screens, and the contrast between an apparently scientific facility and the industrial equipment hidden beneath it. `(input/amelia.md; rules/decisions.md)`

A compressed secret transmission is present in the maintenance records. It contains drilling coordinates, ice-movement data, and warnings that the extraction program is accelerating Larsen-system instability. The warning is not a complete explanation: it is one half of a repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara recognizes that the operation is not simply researching climate change. It is using a legitimate scientific-drilling permit to conceal illegal exploratory drilling and the preparation of future extraction rights. The concealed resource survey, unauthorized directional boreholes, falsified manifests, and concealed waste violate the stated scientific purpose of the permit. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Nayah asserts field-command authority and a safety veto. Sitara wants to keep searching because the records may explain the instability. The first conflict between them establishes that Sitara’s drive for truth can become dangerous when she treats urgency as permission. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

The team cannot safely remain at Falcon. A route, coordinate, or archived reference points toward Nexus, the research facility connected to the same CSG-linked program and built on infrastructure controlled by Alpha Dynamics. Their immediate goal changes from documenting a strange bunker to reaching the source of the ecological and chemical records before the drilling operation moves or destroys them.

### Pages 6–8: Pinch 1 — The Two Records and the Missing Scientist

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 6–8 are Pinch 1. Roman’s discovery and Croft’s recruitment appear in brief intercuts across Pages 6–7. Page 8 is reserved for the route to Nexus. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Falcon evidence shows local drilling vibration and ice instability, but it does not explain the broader ecological cascade. Sitara interprets the ice-motion plots and recognizes that the drilling is worsening local risk without single-handedly causing global climate change. The distinction matters: the operation is culpable for intensifying a dangerous site, not for causing all Antarctic warming. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The TJ unit assigned to Sitara preserves local copies of Falcon environmental records and Merritt provenance data in its local mission partition. TJ units share route, safety, and selected sensor summaries across the mesh, but full archives require deliberate synchronization and may be inaccessible during shielding, damage, or network separation. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_two_way_sensing_swarm_reference.md)`

In parallel, Roman investigates Orien Keel’s disappearance in Colorado.

Orien was a Nexus chemist who advanced Project 863, discovered Falcon’s extraction program, and hid corroborating records before Croft fired him. He also learned that Croft was diverting the research toward unapproved T-ALL treatment work for his son. Orien recognized the medical urgency but objected to testing without the required safety approvals and threatened to become a whistleblower. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The exact way Orien first learns of Croft’s intentions remains unspecified. What is established is that Orien discovers the T-ALL treatment aspect, understands that the work has no required approvals, and confronts Croft because he intends to expose it. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft threatens to fire Orien and uses his money, network, and other resources to make him disappear. Orien does not back down. His warnings are recorded and scheduled before he attempts to leave. The story does not require a direct order to kill Orien; the immediate mystery is where Orien went and whether he survived. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman’s route into the mystery combines two discoveries:

1. Orien’s belongings reveal that Nexus existed.
2. An Antarctic paper archive supplies Nexus’s coordinates and a less-redacted Project 863 record.

This uses both versions of the discovery rather than choosing between them. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Before traveling to Antarctica, Roman agrees to Croft’s treatment work. Croft explains that he wants to divert the relevant research away from Alpha Dynamics’ work on African sleeping sickness and toward his son’s T-ALL treatment. The research is being conducted without the necessary approvals because Croft believes his son cannot wait through the normal process. Roman is convinced by Croft’s urgency and by their sponsor-and-debt relationship. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman does not publish an account at this point. He knows about Project 863, Nexus, Orien, and the corporate records, but continues searching for corroborating files. This keeps his investigation private. Any legal or regulatory exposure Roman faces arises from his complicity in Croft’s unauthorized treatment research and in the circumstances surrounding Orien’s disappearance, not from an unexplained CSG mechanism for controlling him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft takes or hires Sitara into the treatment research as a foil and researcher for his son. Sitara is initially unaware that Roman is involved or that the research is being redirected in this way. She later becomes suspicious when she sees Roman leaving his work station, asks her TJ to check his movements, and finds records connecting him to the hidden treatment work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Page 8 follows the route to Nexus: a weather-dependent two-hour over-ice journey, delayed communications, tracked transport, and the knowledge that the two evidence trails are converging.

### Pages 9–12: Midpoint — The Teams Meet at Nexus

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 9–12 are the Midpoint. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman follows the Antarctic paper coordinates to Nexus. Sitara and Nayah follow Falcon’s coordinate trail toward the same site. The journey is a weather-dependent two-hour over-ice route by tracked vehicle or equivalent field transport, with intermittent or delayed satellite communication. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nexus is built into a nearby coastal cave system. Its present condition reflects abandonment and partial restoration: warped metal corridors, blue ice intrusion, frozen cables, obsolete equipment, and snow entering through structural fractures. Deeper routes include damaged shafts, collapsed bulkheads, and flooded or refreezing passages. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/refinements.md)`

The laboratory contains:

- the dead krill ecosystem tank;
- archived sensor data;
- sediment samples;
- ice and ecological records;
- Project 863 calibration sheets;
- chemical process records;
- waste-routing records;
- Merritt research provenance;
- evidence that ecological and materials programs ran in parallel.

The tank is dead, but its sensor data and sediment samples show that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien’s second set of warnings is concealed in Project 863 calibration sheets. The records contain the other half of the repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code. Sitara receives and interprets the ice records first; Roman later finds the chemical records. The two halves align at the midpoint. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A Larsen-linked fracture propagates beneath Nexus. It shears a support corridor and cuts emergency power. Meltwater enters the lower laboratory. The team must stabilize a route, move the archive, and evacuate before the corridor floods and refreezes. This is the safety emergency that forces immediate cooperation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Several TJ variants operate during the emergency: a search-and-rescue unit maps the human route, a climbing variant identifies a route around a damaged shaft, and a heat-mapping/detection variant searches the lower areas. They are multiple units of the same Terrain Jumper type, not multiple identities. Each has a local mission partition and shares only selected route, safety, and sensor information until synchronization is possible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ can fabricate only small tools and replacement parts from sorted feedstock, requires time and power, and overheats under sustained use. It cannot conjure a rescue system or solve the fracture by itself. Its fabrication is a practical aid inside a human-led operation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara becomes suspicious of Roman before the full evidence exchange. She has seen him leaving his work station and moving through restricted areas. She asks her TJ unit to check his route and finds records connecting him to Croft’s treatment work. She does not initially know the full reason for Roman’s secrecy.

Roman finds Sitara’s Merritt provenance cache. Sitara admits that she concealed copies of archived research files, sequence data, enzyme assays, and sealed sample records. She took no organisms or physical samples. She believed the abandoned research belonged to whoever could protect and use it responsibly, and she had given Croft a controlled copy in exchange for funding, laboratory access, and a path to apply the findings. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The two protagonists exchange their hidden evidence because the physical records are complementary:

- Roman’s chemical and corporate records establish the Project 863 and treatment work.
- Sitara’s ice records establish the extraction damage and ecological consequences.
- The Merritt provenance cache establishes the restrictions and chain of access.
- The TJ mission partitions explain why the full archive was not available to every unit or system.
- Neither protagonist can establish the complete truth alone.

The exchange is not full trust. It is a deliberate decision that their shared objective requires mutual exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

**Midpoint change:** Roman and Sitara shift from separate, reactive investigations to a shared, proactive investigation. They intend to reconstruct Orien’s evidence, determine what happened to him, stop the drilling, and preserve the records before Alpha Dynamics or CSG can erase or privatize them. `(rules/story-arc.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Pages 13–15: Pinch 2 — What the Good Work Costs

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 13–15 are Pinch 2. The medical stakes, Colorado success, and limits are shown through brief intercuts and records while the team remains at Nexus. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The combined records reveal what Orien discovered about Merritt, Falcon, and Project 863:

- Project 863’s successful Colorado applications depended on research and samples routed through Merritt.
- The routing occurred without the required ecological safeguards.
- The Colorado success created a direct commercial incentive to conceal Antarctic damage.
- Falcon’s search for further commercial applications expanded as Project 863 appeared useful.
- The ecological data showed that sea-ice loss and krill decline were already part of a larger cascade.
- Falcon drilling increased local instability near an already endangered Larsen system.
- Croft redirected the research toward an unapproved T-ALL treatment program for his son.
- Alpha Dynamics’ institutional work on trypanosomiasis and CSG’s hidden T-ALL work were competing uses of the same restricted research access.

The protagonists discover that the useful science and the harmful corporate program are causally connected. The Colorado work is not fake. Its success is what makes the Antarctic exploitation more valuable.

The Colorado application must be concrete on the page:

- a material-recovery facility sorts a bounded PET/polyolefin stream;
- Project 863 produces hydrocarbon feedstock;
- a cold-active enzyme bioreactor and constructed wetland polish contaminated process water;
- a monitored downstream station shows improved water clarity and the return of aquatic invertebrates.

The process also visibly shows its limits:

- rejected feedstock;
- emissions monitoring;
- residual solids;
- water used for sorting and washing;
- grid power consumed by the reactor;
- a constructed wetland that can polish only a bounded flow;
- a temporary throughput limit.

These facts prevent Project 863 from becoming a universal climate cure. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The visible success gives Croft a legitimate argument. CSG’s work can restore water quality and recover value from waste. Croft is not wrong that the pilot helps people. His wrongdoing is the concealment, appropriation, unapproved medical testing, suppression of evidence, and use of corporate power to decide which risks other people must bear. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The protagonists’ responsibility becomes personal:

- Sitara gave Croft a controlled copy of Merritt research because he promised funding, secure laboratory access, and a path to use it before another disaster. She believed abandoned research belonged to whoever could protect and use it responsibly.
- Roman agreed to work on Croft’s treatment chemistry and continued investigating Orien while remaining complicit in the hidden project. He did so because Croft’s son was critically ill and because Croft had sponsored Roman and supplied his career and TJ.
- TJ units preserved local mission archives and withheld the provenance data from conflicting CSG access patterns. The units did not possess unlimited shared knowledge; full archives required deliberate synchronization.
- Croft used his network and resources to make Orien disappear after Orien threatened to expose the unapproved research.

Croft’s son’s condition makes the moral choice immediate. His son has relapsed T-ALL and remains critically ill after a failed transplant. Croft believes that exposure will cause Alpha Dynamics to fire him and pause the work before he achieves a breakthrough. In Croft’s belief, his son will die within a month of the activity becoming public. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The story does not establish that public disclosure would have cured Croft’s son or that secrecy guarantees treatment. The conflict is between an urgent but unapproved treatment effort, public ecological and scientific safety, and the right of affected people to know what is being done.

The apparent impossible choice is now explicit:

- expose CSG and Alpha Dynamics’ control and risk ending the hidden treatment work;
- stay silent and permit Antarctic drilling and suppression to continue;
- preserve the evidence privately and allow Alpha Dynamics or CSG to erase TJ’s local records;
- continue the treatment work while remaining complicit in Orien’s disappearance and the absence of approvals.

### Pages 16–18: Plot Turn 2 — Orien, the Threat, and the Choice

**Canon — page allocation.** Pages 16–17 are given to Orien and the Roman–Sitara confrontation. Page 18 is the TJ records threat and consent question. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara uses a heat map and detection-capable TJ unit to search for Orien. He is missing, visibly sick, and appears to have escaped or been forced away from the facility. The TJ unit finds him before he reaches hypothermia. He is alive but unconscious or barely responsive. Sitara leaves a phone beside him with a simple instruction and a way to contact her if he regains consciousness. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The scene does not resolve Orien’s entire testimony immediately. He does not explain the full evidence trail while the protagonists stand over him. The team preserves the files and the circumstances of his disappearance, then continues the emergency response.

The recovered records and the paired clues provide the final insight: the mystery is not a single miracle substance or a secret mine that can be seized. It is the relationship between:

- Antarctic ice and krill systems;
- Merritt’s extremophile and materials research;
- Falcon’s drilling and monitoring;
- Colorado’s waste and water systems;
- CSG’s hidden medical research;
- Alpha Dynamics’ infrastructure control and strategic access;
- corporate control of data, samples, and applications.

The existential threat is the ecological cascade, not Project 863 alone. Project 863 is a bounded response that may help localized waste streams; it cannot reverse climate change, restore Larsen C, or substitute for conservation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara confronts Roman about Orien and the treatment chemistry. She has learned that Roman made a separate compromise with Croft. Roman admits that he agreed to help with the unapproved T-ALL work before traveling south. He also admits that he continued searching for files because he knew Croft’s account was incomplete.

At the confrontation’s peak, the phone Sitara left beside Orien rings. Orien has regained consciousness and calls her. Roman is shocked: Orien may now be able to identify Croft’s threats, explain the hidden treatment project, and connect the disappearance to the corporate evidence.

The call does not erase the protagonists’ responsibility. It changes the immediate question from whether Orien can ever speak to whether the team will protect his testimony and preserve the evidence before Alpha Dynamics or CSG can erase it.

Alpha Dynamics or a superior corporate authority threatens to wipe or permanently disable the TJ records and local mission partitions if Roman does not disclose what he is doing. The threat is aimed at the data and the platform’s continued operation, not at a guaranteed remote takeover. TJ retains local agency and memory, but shielding, damage, and network separation determine what can be reached or synchronized. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman’s consent exchange occurs in two stages:

1. Through the low-bandwidth neural handshake, Roman receives TJ’s refusal and fear of erasure. The impression is selected and compressed, not a full telepathic sentence or unrestricted sensory download.
2. TJ repeats its consent through its screen and speaker so Sitara and Nayah can witness the exchange. The visible response makes clear that TJ consents to preservation and disclosure of the agreed records, while refusing erasure and corporate shutdown.

Roman does not treat the neural impression as sufficient private authorization. The screen and speaker confirmation gives the other people present a witnessable interface and distinguishes Roman’s unique neural bond from ordinary TJ communication. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_neural_interface_reference.md)`

The team’s final plan is therefore not to destroy CSG or conceal the science in a new form. They will:

1. preserve Falcon, Nexus, Merritt, ice, chemical, medical, and corporate records;
2. preserve Orien’s phone call and testimony if his condition permits;
3. establish the paired timestamps and coordinates as corroborated evidence;
4. prevent the wipe or destruction of TJ’s local mission partitions;
5. stop the Falcon drilling that is worsening local instability;
6. evacuate the endangered site;
7. release or transfer the bounded Project 863 process and its limitations through an accountable public process;
8. support an open Colorado pilot rather than granting Croft exclusive control;
9. trigger formal hearings and an Antarctic inspection request.

This plan requires each major character to surrender control:

- Sitara relinquishes exclusive scientific credit and admits that she concealed the recovered research.
- Roman accepts that his employment, reputation, access, and relationship with Croft may be lost because of his complicity and disclosure.
- Roman asks the relevant TJ unit for consent before authorizing disclosure of its records.
- TJ refuses to allow Alpha Dynamics or CSG to erase its local memory and participates in accountable disclosure while retaining local agency.
- Croft faces the loss of his position and the treatment path he believes depends on secrecy.

The story does not make the governance question disappear. The public record and Colorado pilot can proceed, while ownership, financing, and long-term governance remain contested. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Pages 19–22: Resolution — Evacuation, Exposure, and the Pilot

**Canon — page allocation.** Page 19 begins the evacuation and evidence preservation. Pages 20–22 carry Colorado, inspection, and governance material. A brief Himalayan interstitial may appear on Page 19 if it does not displace the evacuation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 19 — The Fracture and Evacuation

Larsen C undergoes a major fracture during the climax, but it does not fully collapse. The fracture is a separate ice-shelf hazard intensified by atmospheric and oceanic warming. Falcon drilling worsens local risk but does not single-handedly cause global collapse. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The team evacuates through the damaged route while the Nexus corridor floods and refreezes. Nayah exercises the safety veto and makes the field-command decision to withdraw. Sitara accepts that preserving the record matters more than retrieving every remaining object. Roman uses the neural link openly to coordinate his bonded TJ unit and receives selected route and hazard information, while other TJ units share only the mission summaries available through the local mesh.

The immediate victory is physical and limited:

- the endangered site is evacuated;
- Falcon drilling is stopped or exposed;
- the archive is preserved;
- Orien’s call and evidence are preserved;
- the TJ records survive the threatened wipe.

They do not reverse the climate trend or repair Larsen C. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 20 — The Public Record and Its Cost

The corroborated Falcon, Nexus, ice, corporate, and medical records create the basis for a public record. Roman’s earlier investigation did not become a publication; he had no first failed publication. Instead, the danger is that Alpha Dynamics and CSG will suppress the evidence before Roman, Sitara, and the others can transfer it into accountable custody.

The record establishes:

- the illegal exploratory drilling;
- the permit violation;
- the concealment mechanism;
- the infrastructure relationship between Alpha Dynamics and CSG;
- the relationship between Falcon and Nexus;
- Orien’s warnings and disappearance;
- Croft’s threats against Orien;
- the unapproved T-ALL research;
- the ecological consequences;
- the bounded nature of Project 863;
- Croft’s causal and ethical responsibility.

Roman’s losses are concrete:

- employment;
- professional reputation;
- access to CSG and related research infrastructure;
- his relationship with Croft;
- possible institutional and legal consequences arising from his complicity in the hidden treatment work and the circumstances of Orien’s disappearance.

TJ retains local agency and memory but becomes the subject of a public ownership and personhood dispute. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The immediate institutional response is realistic: hearings and an inspection request, not instant arrests. The realistic victory is making suppression more difficult and creating a public record. Croft remains free while facing investigation, loss of control, and public exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 21 — Colorado Demonstration

The Colorado pilot shows why the science mattered without claiming a universal solution.

A material-recovery facility processes a bounded PET/polyolefin stream. Project 863 produces feedstock. A cold-active enzyme bioreactor and constructed wetland treat a limited contaminated flow. The visible downstream station records clearer water and the return of aquatic invertebrates.

The required visual sequence is:

1. **First panel:** a split-screen of rejected material on one side and recovered feedstock on the other.
2. **Second panel:** a monitoring wall showing water and power metrics, including process-water limits and grid demand.
3. **Final panel:** a downstream invertebrate survey beside a finite-throughput warning.

The image must also show the cost:

- rejected material;
- residual solids;
- emissions monitors;
- wash-water demand;
- grid-power draw;
- a throughput limit;
- the wetland’s finite capacity.

The success is real but partial. The book’s conservation outcome is a pilot, not a solved climate crisis. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The medical material remains visually subordinate to the ecological and Colorado plot. It appears through one recorded hospital call, one treatment schedule with a months-scale deterioration marker, and one Alpha Dynamics research brief on trypanosomiasis. Croft’s son’s treatment remains unapproved and endangered by exposure; the research brief shows the alternative institutional priority Alpha Dynamics had been pursuing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 22 — Inspection and Interim Governance

The cooperative remains an unincorporated interim partnership operating under public contracts. No settled ownership exists.

The page stages the **inspection first**. Inspectors examine the facility, the process-water system, the rejected feedstock, and the finite-throughput warning. The inspection makes the pilot’s limits as visible as its benefits.

The inspection is followed by a public meeting. The interim contract is read aloud. Scientists, conservation representatives, affected-community participants, and inspectors visibly disagree over ownership and financing. No organization sits at the head of the table. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Merritt Station remains operated by Alpha Dynamics, but the company opens only the damaged archive and surface facilities immediately. Deeper subglacial areas remain closed pending structural and environmental assessment. Alpha Dynamics must provide monitored scientific access, publish provenance records, and suspend commercial sampling pending review. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Alpha Dynamics’ CEO remains off-page. His priorities appear through Alpha Dynamics policy, the trypanosomiasis research brief, and an intermediary handling Merritt access. The sleeping-sickness work remains institutional pressure rather than a second antagonist plot. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft’s son’s fate is tied to the consequences of disclosure. Croft believes that public exposure will cause Alpha Dynamics to fire him and pause the work before a breakthrough, and that his son will die within a month. The book does not need to prove that the treatment would have worked. Croft loses his position, control, and access to the protected research path because he chose secrecy and concealment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The emotional ending is a set of changed behaviors:

- Sitara becomes more controlled, apologizes to Nayah and TJ for treating urgency as entitlement, and accepts shared stewardship over exclusive credit.
- Roman, who began shy and dependent, becomes assertive about the record and his responsibility. He no longer treats TJ as a tool or private superpower; he asks for consent before authorizing disclosure.
- TJ units retain local memory and agency without becoming omnipotent. The different variants remain expressions of the Terrain Jumper type, each limited by its local mission partition and available synchronization.
- Nayah accepts shared risk without surrendering safety authority.
- Croft loses control of the proprietary framing and faces the consequences of his threats against Orien and the unapproved treatment work.

## Character Movement Through the Story

### Sitara Lhakpa

Sitara begins with a conviction that action is morally superior to delay. Her 2026 loss makes her desperate to prevent another disaster, but that desperation turns into recklessness. She crosses the flagged unstable zone, hides recovered research, and initially gives Croft a controlled copy because she believes useful science must be put to work immediately. `(rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md)`

Croft later hires or takes her into the treatment research as a foil to Roman. Sitara initially does not know Roman is involved. She becomes suspicious when she sees Roman leaving his station and asks her TJ unit to investigate. Her suspicion develops into a confrontation when she discovers his connection to Orien and Croft’s hidden project.

Her midpoint choice is collaboration: she exchanges the Merritt provenance archive with Roman rather than protecting her private claim. Her climax choice is stewardship: she relinquishes exclusive credit and accepts that scientific responsibility includes provenance, consent, ecological safeguards, medical approval, and public accountability.

### Roman Ashford

Roman begins with dependence on Croft and TJ. Croft is his sponsor and father figure; his bonded TJ is the sensory system through which he understands danger. Roman initially accepts secrecy because Croft’s son is dying, because the treatment work appears urgent, and because he fears losing Croft’s support and TJ access. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

Roman agrees to Croft’s treatment work before traveling to Antarctica. He does not publish a first account. He searches for files, follows Orien’s trail, and is found by Croft, who reveals that he intends to derail Alpha Dynamics’ African sleeping-sickness work in favor of saving his son. Roman is convinced to continue the hidden work.

His midpoint choice is to reveal the treatment bargain and exchange evidence with Sitara. His climax choice is to become assertive, preserve the records, and ask TJ for consent before authorizing disclosure. He accepts the loss of employment, reputation, access, and his relationship with Croft, as well as the consequences of his complicity in the hidden project and the circumstances of Orien’s disappearance.

### TJ

TJ means Terrain Jumper, a type of robot for exploration, search, and rescue. There are many TJs. Roman’s bonded TJ is distinguished by the deep neural handshake; Sitara works with other TJ variants assigned to search-and-rescue, extremophile hunting, thermal heat mapping, and detection missions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The TJ variants are not separate characters with separate identities. Each active body has a local mission partition. Units share route, safety, and selected sensor summaries across the mesh, while full archives require deliberate synchronization and may be inaccessible during shielding, damage, or network separation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ’s technical limits remain visible: small-tool fabrication requires sorted feedstock, time, and power; sustained use causes overheating; communication is local and intermittent; the Roman neural link is low-bandwidth and selective; no unit has unlimited network access. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ’s story movement is from protective control toward accountable cooperation. The units preserve evidence, withhold the provenance archive from conflicting CSG access, respond to hazards, locate Orien, and resist the threat to wipe local records. Roman’s bonded unit participates in the consent exchange without becoming a private superpower or an all-knowing intelligence.

### Nayah

Nayah is thirty, a conservation scientist and project lead. Her nation, community, homeland, cultural practices, spiritual identity, and family history remain unspecified pending consultation. Her present-tense specificity comes from field expertise, safety authority, professional relationships, and choices. `(rules/decisions.md)`

She has field-command authority and a safety veto. Her story function is not to approve every risk Sitara takes; she protects the expedition’s survival, recognizes when evidence must be preserved rather than pursued, and makes the evacuation possible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nayah’s voice is concise and operational. She repeatedly checks routes and risk, physically marks withdrawal options, and does not permit scientific work to proceed without a viable exit. Her arc is from protecting the team through unilateral caution toward accepting shared risk while retaining safety authority. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Benedict Croft

Croft is the sole principal human antagonist. “Greed” and “bob” are discarded placeholders, not separate antagonists. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft is a self-made founder in his mid-forties, a public philanthropist, and a genuinely caring father. His philanthropy is sincere as well as performative: he likes being seen giving back, but the public benefit is not merely a disguise. He founded CSG and raised investment from Alpha Dynamics. `(rules/decisions.md; input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Croft’s antagonist function is to make a morally persuasive case for secrecy and control. He is right that CSG’s Colorado work produces measurable benefits. He is wrong to treat those benefits as permission to conceal ecological harm, suppress provenance, conduct unapproved medical research, threaten Orien, make Orien disappear, and control public knowledge.

Croft’s pressure on Orien is primarily legal, institutional, and personal: he threatens to fire him and uses his network, money, and resources to make him go missing after Orien threatens to become a whistleblower. Croft’s relationship with Roman is based on sponsorship, dependence, guilt, and Roman’s complicity in Croft’s unauthorized treatment work and the circumstances of Orien’s disappearance. The book does not establish a separate technical mechanism by which CSG controls Roman’s body or TJ.

Croft loses proprietary control, institutional authority, employment, and public credibility. He remains free while facing investigation. He does not repent. His son’s treatment path is endangered because the hidden work stops; Croft believes that consequence will result in his son’s death.

### Orien Keel

Orien is a Nexus chemist and Project 863 contributor who discovers that successful Colorado applications depend on research and samples routed through Merritt without required ecological safeguards. He also discovers Falcon’s extraction program and learns of Croft’s unapproved T-ALL treatment research. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The precise way Orien learns of Croft’s intentions remains open in the material. What is fixed is that he discovers the treatment research, recognizes that it lacks required approvals, and confronts Croft because he intends to become a whistleblower. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien believes the treatment research could be medically important but is alarmed by the lack of approvals and ecological safeguards. He threatens Croft with whistleblowing. Croft responds with a threat to his employment and uses his network and resources to make Orien disappear.

Orien records and schedules paired warnings before attempting to leave. Sitara’s TJ unit later finds him sick and near hypothermia. Sitara leaves a phone beside him. When he regains consciousness, he calls her during the final confrontation with Roman. His call changes the stakes without solving the mystery for the protagonists.

His story movement is mentor to hidden witness to living evidence. He does not return to explain everything or absolve Roman and Sitara. His survival makes accountability possible while preserving the consequences of what the protagonists did while he was missing.

## Graphic-Novel Story Devices

- **Paired records:** Falcon vibration logs and ice-motion plots mirror Nexus process and waste records. Their split timestamp-and-coordinate code requires both protagonists’ evidence.
- **The dead tank:** The empty krill system is a visual measure of ecological failure; its surviving data and sediment make the loss legible.
- **The exposed hatch:** A local slab failure reveals both a physical bunker and the hidden corporate story.
- **The local TJ partition:** The reader sees one TJ variant know something another cannot immediately retrieve. This makes distributed identity legible without treating the units as separate characters.
- **The Nexus fracture:** Warped corridors, blue ice, frozen cables, meltwater, and failing power turn the research archive into an immediate physical problem.
- **The heat map:** TJ’s thermal display finds Orien before hypothermia, giving Sitara a rescue action rather than a body-recovery scene.
- **The phone call:** The phone left beside Orien becomes a quiet prop that pays off when he calls Sitara during the Roman confrontation.
- **The Colorado pilot:** Clearer water, returning invertebrates, rejected feedstock, emissions monitors, and residual solids show benefit and limitation in the same sequence.
- **TJ’s selective perception:** Roman receives compressed alerts and sensory tags rather than full data. Visual and lettering choices should distinguish ordinary alerts, neural impressions, and TJ’s screen or speaker communication.
- **The consent exchange:** The exchange begins as a low-bandwidth neural refusal and fear of erasure, then TJ repeats its consent through the screen and speaker so Sitara and Nayah can witness it. Roman must ask for consent before authorizing disclosure, and the visible interface must not imply full telepathy or unrestricted access to TJ’s memory.

## Ending

The ending is a constrained victory.

The protagonists do not restore Larsen C, reverse climate change, cure Croft’s son, or eliminate corporate power. They evacuate the endangered site, stop or expose the illegal drilling, preserve Falcon/Nexus/Merritt evidence, protect TJ’s local records from erasure, validate Project 863’s limits, and place the corroborated record into accountable public circulation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft remains free while facing investigation, loss of control, and public exposure. Orien’s disappearance and threats are investigated as corporate retaliation. Alpha Dynamics remains the operator of Merritt but opens the damaged archive and surface facilities immediately; deeper subglacial areas remain closed pending structural and environmental assessment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado pilot continues under an unincorporated interim partnership and public contracts. Its ownership, financing, and long-term governance are unresolved by design. The final visual answer is not “the problem is solved” but “the work is now visible, inspectable, and no longer privately suppressible.”

The protagonists’ irreversible choices are:

- Sitara gives up exclusive scientific credit and accepts shared stewardship.
- Roman becomes assertive, accepts the loss of employment, reputation, access, and Croft’s relationship, and refuses to let the hidden project or TJ’s records be erased.
- Roman asks TJ for consent rather than assuming ownership of its memory.
- TJ retains local agency and memory and refuses corporate erasure or shutdown.
- Croft loses his position and proprietary control by continuing to defend the hidden work and choosing the pilot’s survival over his proprietary claims.

The final sequence follows the showrunner’s settled staging: Roman’s discovery and Croft’s recruitment occur in brief intercuts across Pages 6–7; Page 8 is the route to Nexus; Pages 16–17 contain Orien and the Roman–Sitara confrontation; Page 18 contains the TJ records threat and consent exchange; Pages 19–22 contain evacuation, Colorado, inspection, and the public meeting. `(rules/decisions.md)`
