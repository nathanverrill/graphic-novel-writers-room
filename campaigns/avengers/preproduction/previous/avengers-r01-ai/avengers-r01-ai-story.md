# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

### Intended premise and experience

The book is a crossover: the Sitara/Nayah/Falcon-bunker story and the Roman/TJ/Clear Spring Global/Nexus story both happened in the same world and must converge rather than replace one another. The settings are Antarctica, Colorado, and the Himalayas, with the Himalayas functioning primarily as backstory and motive. `(rules/avengers.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The story begins in Antarctica and uses a mystery structure: an active drilling operation, an abandoned or hidden research facility, secret messages from Antarctica, evidence of illegal American mining, and two teams who gradually discover that their apparently separate investigations concern the same existential environmental threat. `(rules/rules.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The intended experience includes:

- “Hard sci-fi” grounded in climate science, cryosphere research, conservation, recycling, and institutional behavior. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`
- A visually striking Antarctica: ice shelves, glaciers, coastal mountains, drilling sites, research bases, caves, abandoned facilities, shifting ice, and a landscape that carries evidence of hidden activity. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
- An environmental loop in which an organization attempts to conserve one damaged ecosystem while its activities or sponsors damage another. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
- A mystery whose evidence moves between the landscape, hidden facilities, paper records, computer systems, scientific data, and secret messages. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
- A story in which no single character or team can solve the central threat alone. Sitara, Nayah, Roman, TJ, and the other scientists each bring something the others lack. `(rules/avengers.md)`
- Graphic-novel images that carry story information: a glacier collapse, a memory of a Himalayan disaster, a concealed hatch, the Falcon symbol, drill machinery, a krill ecosystem tank, secret coordinates, and the contrast between damaged Antarctic and Colorado ecosystems. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Central dramatic question

Can the characters expose and counter a corporate and industrial system that is exploiting Antarctica while developing a credible waste-to-value response to climate damage, before ice-shelf failure and cascading ecosystem loss become irreversible?

The material does not yet establish the final form of the existential threat, the exact relationship between the illegal mining, the Nexus research, the secret messages, and the ice-shelf crisis, or the final solution. Those questions remain open. `## Open`

### Theme and dramatic engine

The story repeatedly connects environmental systems that appear distant from one another:

- Antarctic sea-ice loss threatens krill, a foundation of the food web.
- Reduced Colorado snowpack and streamflow threatens cold-water trout and other ecosystems.
- Himalayan disasters destroy homes, infrastructure, and family histories.
- Fossil-fuel extraction and illegal Antarctic mining create the conditions that the characters are trying to repair.
- Waste-to-value research may offer a way to reduce the need for new extraction, but it may also be controlled by the same interests that caused the damage. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The major conflict is therefore not only “humans versus nature.” It is also:

- evidence versus suppression;
- conservation versus extraction;
- public science versus corporate control;
- individual grief and ambition versus collective responsibility;
- a technology intended to help people versus the institutions that decide who controls it. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

## Premise

In 2046, Sitara Lhakpa and Nayah are working in the Larsen region of Antarctica when they encounter evidence of an illegal American mining operation and discover a concealed Falcon facility beneath the ice. At the same time, Roman Ashford, a young chemical engineer connected to the robot dog TJ, follows the suppressed research of his former mentor Orien Keel to an abandoned CSG laboratory called Nexus.

The Falcon bunker and Nexus are not separate mysteries. Both are connected to the environmental crisis affecting Antarctica, Colorado, and the Himalayas, and both contain evidence that powerful people have attempted to conceal. The two teams must combine cryosphere knowledge, conservation experience, chemical engineering, artificial intelligence, recovered data, and personal knowledge of environmental disaster to understand the threat and act before the Antarctic system reaches a point of catastrophic change. `(rules/avengers.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

## Story So Far

The material contains two detailed story tracks and several shorter structural proposals. They are preserved below as evidence of the current project state rather than treated as a settled final sequence.

### Sequence 1 — The Himalayan backstories

#### Sitara's Himalayan history

Sitara Lhakpa is from Nepal and is identified in the material as Thakali. Her home and family ties in the Himalayas are part of her motive. The material associates her with Buddhist belief, Himalayan life, and the cultural and environmental knowledge of the region. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

A research reference describes a 2026 rock-ice avalanche from the Lirung massif. The avalanche became a debris flow through the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system and struck the Nepal–Tibet border at Gyirong Port. The reported consequences included destruction across a 72 km stretch of river in Nepal, more than 1,451 deaths in Nepal, more than 43 in Tibet, more than 6,264 people missing across Nepal and Tibet, destruction of roads, bridges, houses, power projects, and border facilities, and bodies carried as far as 240 km downstream into India. This material is real-world research, not yet a settled fictional event, but the brainstorm uses a Himalayan disaster in Sitara's past in which a flood takes her parents and home. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The proposed emotional consequence is that Sitara becomes passionate, reckless, and desperate to uncover the truth about environmental damage. She is drawn toward scientific investigation and eventually to Colorado, possibly through study at CU Boulder, though the institution and exact chronology are not settled. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

#### Roman and TJ's Himalayan incident

One detailed story draft places Roman Ashford in Nepal in 2042, when he is 18 and has just graduated high school. He travels alone on a senior trip and brings TJ, an AI-equipped robot dog with tools and emergency functions. Roman has a neural implant, described in the draft as common by 2042. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman and TJ cross an iceberg or glacier. The ice splits beneath Roman because melting has weakened its tensile strength, leaving him trapped and unconscious in the opening. An electrical storm arrives. Lightning strikes nearby and causes Roman's neural implant to link with TJ's artificial intelligence. The connection is initially confusing, but TJ's emergency capabilities and the two companions' cooperation allow Roman to escape. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

A separate TJ prompt describes the incident as Roman falling through a collapsing iceberg while TJ tries to save him, with lightning unexpectedly linking Roman's neural implant to TJ's systems. This is the origin of their unusual bond. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The binding crossover rule requires the two TJ histories to be one history. The Himalayan-sheep-dog educator with the green-and-black screen and the scavenger-built fabricator bonded to Roman by lightning are the same robot dog. The material does not yet establish how TJ passed from Bi11bot's construction and release into the later Falcon or Sitara/Nayah story, or how TJ's relationship with Roman intersects with the other team's history. `(rules/avengers.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Sequence 2 — Colorado, four years later

By 2046, Roman is 22, has a B.S. in chemical engineering, and lives in Colorado. He works for Clear Spring Global, or CSG, an organization involved in waste-to-value research and Antarctic activity. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Colorado is experiencing severe environmental stress. The material describes drying rivers and lakes, reduced snowpack and streamflow, food scarcity, rising food prices, and a decline in natural water sources. Research supplied to the project connects reduced snowpack and streamflow to loss of habitat for native cutthroat trout; one cited projection estimates mean summer streamflow declines of approximately 52% under future climate scenarios. This research grounds the Colorado stakes but does not by itself establish the fictional plot events. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

Roman works under Benedict Croft, described in the draft as the wealthy, old-money owner of CSG and an oil tycoon. Orien Keel, a senior CSG chemical engineer, mentors Roman and investigates the relationship between dried Colorado water sources, severe global warming, and oil drilling and use. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Croft discovers Orien's research. Because Orien is connecting the environmental crisis to fossil-fuel extraction, Croft has him fired. The detailed draft states that Orien is unable to afford food and water after losing his job and eventually starves to death. Orien is solitary, and Roman is his only next of kin. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Orien's death gives Roman both a personal motive and physical access to evidence. While going through Orien's belongings, Roman finds a cabinet of files from Orien's time with CSG in Antarctica. One file identifies a CSG laboratory called Nexus and a project called “863,” described as research into how to fix global warming. Roman searches CSG's servers but finds no evidence of the laboratory or project. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

A different version of this Colorado sequence has Roman discover a suspicious, heavily redacted file while working for Croft, nearly get caught investigating it, steal it, and later find a less-redacted copy when reassigned to Antarctica. Both versions establish the same narrative function: Roman finds evidence that CSG has concealed an Antarctic facility and a research program. The exact discovery route remains unresolved. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman requests a transfer to CSG's current Antarctic drilling site. The stated reason is to investigate Nexus and continue Orien's work without attracting attention. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Sequence 3 — The Antarctic present

The book is intended to start in Antarctica, with immediate tension and an explanation of why the reader should care. A proposed opening image shows ice-melt research and a glacier collapse. Another proposed opening uses a glacier falling, flooding, or cracking before moving into the research setting. These are opening proposals, not settled pages. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The Antarctic setting includes the Larsen region, especially Larsen C: a coastal mountain-and-ice landscape, sea ice, glaciers, a small community of research bases and homes, an active living base, drilling sites, and an abandoned research facility or cave laboratory far from the main community. The material describes the area as having little wildlife left. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The project research establishes the relevant danger as climate-driven ice-shelf instability. Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B in 2002; Larsen C is described as thinning and vulnerable. A major structural failure would reduce the buttressing effect of the floating shelf and allow land-based glaciers behind it to accelerate. The project calls this a “cork out of the bottle” effect. These are research-grounded stakes, while the timing and exact fictional consequences in 2046 remain to be determined. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The Antarctic team includes Sitara and Nayah. They are on an expedition or working at the Antarctic research community when they encounter a concealed bunker and evidence of drilling or mining. Nayah is connected to the Rocky Mountain plains and wants to protect her Native homeland; her Colorado conservation motive links the Antarctic investigation to the Colorado storyline. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

America is conducting illegal mining in Antarctica despite the Madrid Protocol's ban on Antarctic mining. The material does not yet establish exactly what is being mined, how the operation is disguised, who commands it, or whether CSG, Croft, the Falcon organization, or another institution is responsible. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### Sequence 4 — Finding the Falcon bunker

Sitara and Nayah investigate an underground facility in or near the Larsen region. One version calls it an old research facility or bunker whose country failed to remove it; another identifies it as an abandoned Falcon base. The exterior is a snow-covered “submarine” hatch set into the ground, with a Falcon symbol engraved in the metal door. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The bunker contains:

- maps and charts on tables;
- computers mounted on the walls;
- rolling chairs;
- Falcon logos on chairs, computer lock screens, and pens;
- information about who is doing the drilling;
- technology and records;
- dust suggesting that the facility has not been used recently. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The bunker lid or door has codes. TJ discovers the facility and is the only one able to open the door in one proposed sequence. This creates a direct connection between TJ and the Falcon facility, but the reason for TJ's access is not established. It may involve his construction, a prior mission, stored credentials, his AI integration capability, or a connection between Falcon and Nexus; all remain open. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

A proposed sequence is:

1. Sitara and Nayah are working amid drilling sounds and shifting ice.
2. TJ detects or finds the concealed bunker.
3. The team discovers the hatch and its codes.
4. TJ opens the door when the others cannot.
5. The group enters and finds evidence of the drilling operation.
6. The bunker records point toward a larger hidden program and secret messages from Antarctica. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The exact sequence, and whether Roman and TJ are present when the bunker is opened, are not settled.

### Sequence 5 — Roman and TJ reach Nexus

After arriving at the current Antarctic CSG site, Roman spends several weeks working without attracting attention. He listens for anyone mentioning Nexus but finds that no current employee is old enough to have worked there. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman eventually takes TJ to the drill site and sneaks into an old paper-file room. TJ scans the records and finds a surviving Nexus file buried behind a filing cabinet. The file supplies coordinates for the old facility. A draft includes a possible exchange in which Roman asks TJ to lock the key again and TJ says that his credits have expired; this line is not developed enough to establish its meaning. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman and TJ leave at midnight to search for Nexus. TJ's eyes function as high-beam lights, allowing them to travel through the darkness. The trip from the current CSG site to the old laboratory takes approximately two hours in the draft. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Nexus is built into a cave and boarded up. From the outside it appears to be a massive abandoned laboratory. Roman and TJ break in and search for research, files, and experiments that might help address global warming. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The Nexus programs include:

- krill-species research;
- a contained krill ecosystem tank;
- plastics research;
- the early research associated with Project 863. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman finds Orien's former office. The office contains undeveloped research into breaking down used plastics into gasoline. Roman believes the concept could reduce the need for fossil-fuel extraction and help address global warming. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The material describes this plastics-to-fuel process as the story's speculative heart and its 5% change. Under the hard-sf rules, it must have fixed limitations and consequences; the actual chemistry, energy costs, emissions profile, scale, and relationship to existing recycling must be decided before it can function as the solution. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Sequence 6 — Roman confronts Croft

Roman returns to Colorado and meets Benedict Croft at CSG headquarters. Roman tells Croft that the Nexus research could change or save the world. Croft agrees that the information is important, asks Roman to hand over everything, and tells him to stop pursuing it because Croft will handle it. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

After several weeks, Roman sees no evidence that Croft has created a research team, advanced Project 863, released the findings, or otherwise acted on the work. Roman investigates and finds that nothing is happening. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman begins trying to complete the research himself. Croft discovers this and fires him. Roman interprets this as evidence that Croft treated Orien the same way, and concludes that Croft's actions contributed to Orien's death. His motive shifts from scientific investigation and climate action toward anger and personal retaliation. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman publishes the research that has already been completed. He does so both to make the work public and because he wants to harm Croft. Croft uses his money and influence to discredit Roman and convince people that the research is fake news. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Roman decides that publishing is not enough. He continues trying to finish the research himself, with TJ's assistance. The draft says this takes several months. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Sequence 7 — The two investigations converge

The crossover rule requires the Falcon-bunker investigation and the Nexus investigation to converge on one existential threat. The current material establishes the following shared elements:

- illegal American mining in Antarctica;
- CSG and Benedict Croft's corporate power;
- secret messages sent from Antarctica;
- an active drilling operation;
- concealed or redacted records;
- a hidden or abandoned research facility;
- climate damage affecting Antarctic ice, krill, Colorado waterways, and Himalayan communities;
- research into plastics, waste-to-value systems, and conservation;
- a robot dog capable of integrating with systems and fabricating or recovering tools;
- the need for both teams to work together. `(rules/avengers.md; rules/rules.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The precise convergence has not been written. The material does not yet state:

- whether Falcon and Nexus were allied, competing, or sequential programs;
- whether Falcon's drilling records identify Project 863;
- whether Project 863 caused, predicted, or could mitigate the ice-shelf threat;
- what the secret messages say;
- who is sending them and to whom;
- whether Croft knows the full threat;
- whether the illegal mine is extracting a resource, disposing of waste, or accessing a scientific site;
- how Sitara, Nayah, Roman, and TJ first meet;
- what unique contribution each character makes to the final response. `## Open`

### Sequence 8 — Possible research and public-response ending

The detailed Roman draft ends with a possible scientific-public strategy rather than a completed action sequence. Roman sends his findings for peer review, then contacts news organizations and the government. The findings are described as definitive: the process of turning recycled materials into gasoline works, significantly reduces pollutants, slows global warming, and may eventually stop it. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

This claim is not yet compatible with the hard-sf rules as a settled story fact. The process, its limits, and its actual climate effect need to be defined. The material also does not establish whether public release succeeds, whether Croft is exposed, whether the illegal mining operation stops, or whether the Antarctic threat is physically averted. `## Open`

The crossover premise requires the ending to depend on collective action rather than Roman's research alone. The final response must therefore include the knowledge and capabilities of Sitara, Nayah, Roman, TJ, and possibly the people or institutions connected to Falcon, CSG, conservation, and Antarctic research. `(rules/avengers.md)`

## Causality and Escalation

The currently established causal chain is:

1. Climate change damages Himalayan ice, Antarctic ice, and Colorado water systems. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`
2. Sitara's Himalayan loss drives her toward scientific investigation and a need to uncover the truth. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`
3. Roman's Himalayan accident links him to TJ and establishes their unusual human-AI partnership. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
4. Orien investigates the connection between Colorado's environmental decline and fossil-fuel extraction. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
5. Croft removes Orien from CSG, and Orien dies after losing access to food and water. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
6. Roman inherits Orien's files and discovers evidence of Nexus and Project 863. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
7. Roman transfers to Antarctica because the digital record has been erased or concealed and the surviving evidence points there. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
8. Roman and TJ recover Nexus coordinates from a buried paper file. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
9. They enter Nexus and recover early plastics-to-fuel research, including Orien's work. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
10. Roman brings the research to Croft, who claims he will handle it but suppresses it instead. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
11. Roman's attempt to complete and publish the research provokes Croft's retaliation and turns Roman from employee and investigator into an adversary. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`
12. Sitara and Nayah independently discover the Falcon facility, drilling evidence, and secret Antarctic information. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`
13. The Falcon and Nexus discoveries reveal a common threat that cannot be addressed by one team alone. `(rules/avengers.md)`
14. The teams combine their evidence and expertise. The exact action and outcome are open. `## Open`

## Character Movement Through the Story

### Sitara Lhakpa

Sitara begins as a scientist or expedition member whose Himalayan history has made environmental loss personal. Her passion is also a liability: the material describes her as reckless and desperate to uncover the truth. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

Her investigation of the Antarctic bunker forces her to move from private grief and individual truth-seeking toward cooperation with people whose methods and histories differ from hers. The precise change is not yet written, but the binding crossover requires her knowledge to be necessary to the shared solution. `(rules/avengers.md)`

### Nayah

Nayah enters the story with a motive tied to protecting her Native homeland and the Rocky Mountain plains. Her work in Antarctica connects local conservation to a planetary system. She is described as protective and not especially trusting. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The Antarctic evidence tests whether her protectiveness becomes isolation or develops into coalition-building. Her exact scientific, cultural, or practical contribution to the convergence is open. `## Open`

### Roman Ashford

Roman begins as a young chemical engineer shaped by his connection to TJ and by his respect for Orien. He investigates environmental decline first as a scientist and employee, then as Orien's surviving relative, then as a whistleblower, and finally as someone motivated partly by anger at Croft. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

His path currently moves from secrecy to investigation, from investigation to public exposure, and from public exposure to collaboration. The final material must determine whether Roman can move beyond revenge and accept that the research cannot be controlled or completed by him alone. `## Open`

### TJ

TJ begins as Roman's companion and technical partner. One source describes him as loyal, protective, practical, resourceful, and dryly humorous; another gives him an educational, Baymax-like function for Sitara and her friends. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

His established story capabilities include scanning surroundings for usable materials, recognizing scrap metal, salvaged plastics, and ice-compatible alloys, using an internal 3D-printing fabricator, integrating into systems, communicating with Roman through the neural link or a communications channel, and serving as a source of scientific and practical information. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The crossover requires TJ to be a single character whose history includes:

- construction from spare parts by Bi11bot;
- a Himalayan sheep-dog form;
- green-and-black screen and controls;
- a lightning-induced bond with Roman;
- a connection to the Falcon bunker and/or Sitara and Nayah;
- a role in the final collective response.

The order and causality of these facts remain open. `## Open`

### Orien Keel

Orien is Roman's senior colleague and mentor at CSG. He investigates the environmental causes of Colorado's water decline and connects them to oil extraction. Croft fires him, after which he dies from deprivation. His files and research initiate Roman's investigation. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

Orien's precise connection to Nexus, Project 863, Falcon, the illegal mine, and the secret messages is unresolved. `## Open`

### Benedict Croft

Croft is the primary corporate antagonist in the detailed Roman draft: a wealthy, old-money oil tycoon who owns CSG. He suppresses Orien's research, takes control of Roman's recovered Nexus material, does not advance it publicly, fires Roman, discredits Roman's publication, and uses money and influence to control the narrative. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The shorter material calls the antagonist “Greed,” with power as the motive. Whether Greed is a thematic name, Croft's characterization, a larger organization, or a separate antagonist is not settled. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

## Stakes

### Personal

- Sitara's grief over her Himalayan home and family.
- Nayah's responsibility to protect her Native homeland and Rocky Mountain ecosystems.
- Roman's grief over Orien, anger at Croft, and fear that the research will be buried.
- Roman and TJ's survival in Antarctic ice and their unusual neural-AI bond.
- TJ's autonomy, memory, and possible exploitation by the institutions that built or use him. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Relational

- Sitara's ambition and recklessness may endanger Nayah or the expedition.
- Nayah's distrust may obstruct cooperation.
- Roman's anger may make him pursue exposure or revenge at the expense of safety and collaboration.
- The two teams must decide whether to trust one another with evidence and technology.
- TJ's loyalty to Roman must expand into responsibility to the larger group without erasing his bond with Roman. `## Open`

### Institutional

- CSG and Croft can suppress scientific evidence, manipulate public credibility, and control resources.
- Illegal Antarctic mining violates the Madrid Protocol and threatens the legitimacy of Antarctic research.
- The Falcon and Nexus records may expose institutional complicity or a hidden conflict between conservation and extraction.
- Public science, peer review, journalism, and government action may be too slow or compromised to respond without direct evidence. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Wider

- Larsen C ice-shelf instability and the possible acceleration of land-based glaciers.
- Antarctic krill and the food web dependent on them.
- Colorado rivers, snowpack, and cold-water trout habitat.
- Himalayan communities already exposed to flood, avalanche, and water loss.
- Global sea-level rise, food scarcity, water scarcity, and continued fossil-fuel extraction. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

## Key scenes

### Antarctic ice failure

- **Where:** Opening sequence; exact chapter and page placement undecided.
- **Where it happens:** Larsen region C, near an Antarctic research or drilling site.
- **Who is in it:** Sitara, Nayah, and possibly TJ; Roman's presence is unresolved.
- **What happens:** Ice shifts, cracks, or collapses while research or drilling is underway. The event establishes immediate physical danger and the larger climate stakes.
- **The one image the reader must see:** A massive Antarctic ice face breaking against a landscape of research infrastructure, with the characters dwarfed by the failure.  
- **Status:** Opening proposal, not settled. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Sitara's Himalayan disaster

- **Where:** Sitara's backstory or an early memory.
- **Where it happens:** A Himalayan settlement and river system; the exact fictional location is undecided.
- **Who is in it:** Sitara, her parents, and the people affected by the disaster.

## Ending

The material does not establish a final ending.

The strongest existing trajectory is:

1. Roman recovers and completes the suppressed research.
2. He seeks peer review and contacts news organizations and government.
3. The research becomes public.
4. The teams expose the relationship between environmental damage, illegal Antarctic mining, and corporate suppression.
5. The characters combine their knowledge and resources to respond to the shared existential threat.
6. The result changes the characters' relationship to one another and changes the public or institutional response to the crisis.

However, none of the following is settled:

- whether the plastics-to-fuel process works at useful scale;
- whether it reduces emissions after accounting for energy costs;
- whether it replaces new fossil-fuel extraction or merely creates another fuel source;
- whether Larsen C fails, is stabilized, or remains in danger;
- whether the illegal mining operation is stopped;
- whether Croft is exposed, arrested, defeated economically, or remains powerful;
- whether Orien's death receives public recognition or legal consequence;
- whether the secret messages are warnings, requests for help, automated transmissions, or evidence of another surviving group;
- whether the ending is hopeful, partial, tragic, or open-ended.

The ending must honor the binding requirement that no individual character or team can defeat the shared threat alone. `(rules/avengers.md)`
