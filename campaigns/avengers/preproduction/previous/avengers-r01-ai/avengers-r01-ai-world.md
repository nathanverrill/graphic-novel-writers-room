## Overview

This is a near-future world in which climate pressure has become an ordinary organizing fact of life. Antarctica is warming and unstable; Colorado is losing snowpack, river flow, and cold-water habitat; and the Himalayas have already been marked by catastrophic ice, rock, and flood events. The future is not a replacement for the present: research stations, paper files, old bunkers, ordinary corporate offices, drilling equipment, neural implants, and improvised robotics coexist. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The main environmental parallel is the loss of a keystone physical resource:

- In Antarctica, declining sea ice threatens krill, the base of the food web.
- In Colorado, reduced snowpack and streamflow stress trout and other cold-water species.
- In both places, the loss of ice or snow propagates through an ecosystem rather than remaining a local visual symptom. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The visible public world contains research communities, conservation programs, universities, industrial sites, and corporate climate work. Beneath it are abandoned or concealed laboratories, redacted records, illegal extraction, and private systems that can operate outside public accountability. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Earlier Antarctic change

Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B collapsed in 2002. Larsen C is fragile, thinning, and vulnerable. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The material describes a possible major structural failure or total collapse of Larsen C around 2046. Floating ice shelves act as buttresses; if Larsen C is removed, land-based glaciers behind it could accelerate by up to five times, contributing several millimeters to global sea-level rise. This consequence is drawn from the supplied research and is not yet specified as a settled in-book event. **Research-informed proposal.** `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The geology of the basin affects ice-shelf stability. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

### The 2026 Himalayan disaster

The supplied research describes a massive rock–ice avalanche from the Lirung massif. It became a debris flow through the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system and struck Gyirong Port on the Nepal–Tibet border.

The reported consequences were:

- a wall of water, mud, and rock;
- flash floods through towns and border facilities;
- destruction along a 72 km stretch of river in Nepal;
- more than 1,451 deaths in Nepal and more than 43 in Tibet;
- more than 6,264 people missing across Nepal and Tibet;
- destruction of homes, roads, bridges, and power projects;
- bodies carried up to 240 km downstream into India;
- foreign nationals among the missing, including Americans, Indians, British, Australians, and Ukrainians. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The material also describes this disaster as affecting Sitara's home and family, but the event's exact relationship to Roman's separate Himalayan incident is unresolved. `(input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; Open)`

The Larsen region is vulnerable to warming, föhn-induced melting, atmospheric-river patterns, basin geology, and unstable ice. - unstable shelves;

Sea-ice decline threatens krill, which form a base of the Antarctic food web. The book's Colorado ecosystem is shaped by earlier runoff, declining snowpack, shrinking streams, and reduced habitat for cold-water fish. The exact watershed and conservation program are not specified. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md; Open)`

## Technology

### Hard-science technology baseline

The project uses a near-future technology layer rather than a wholly transformed future. Current research practices, paper records, old buildings, manual tools, and corporate bureaucracy remain present alongside more advanced systems. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Every new technology must be labelled:

- **T, Truth:** documented and already real;
- **EG, Educated guess:** a likely extension of current technology;
- **S, Speculation:** a development requiring particular conditions;
- **L, License:** a declared departure from known science;
- **Cut:** not permitted unless licensed.

`(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

- Lhakpa — Wednesday;
