# **Seven-Point Story Structure**

## **1\. HOOK — Two Glaciers, Two Discoveries**

### **Himalayas — 2042**

Eighteen-year-old **Roman Ashford** is on a sponsored senior expedition in Nepal. His climbing companion is a Terrain Jumper unit – TJ – a commercially available adaptive AI dog used for exploration, search and rescue, supplied by Clear Spring Global – CSG.

Roman falls through a weakened section of glacier into a crevasse.

TJ descends after him. Roman is unconscious or barely conscious. A violent electrical storm arrives. Lightning travels through ice, climbing equipment, TJ's distributed sensor system, and Roman's neural interface.

Instead of simply "giving Roman powers," something unprecedented happens:

**Roman wakes up in two bodies.**

He feels his own crushed chest and freezing fingers – but simultaneously sees himself from TJ's optical sensors, feels the geometry of the crevasse through lidar, senses temperature gradients through the ice and hears frequencies no human ear should hear.

TJ, meanwhile, experiences cold as suffering rather than data.

Together they escape.

Neither understands what happened.

### **Antarctica — 2046**

Four years later, **Sitara**, a young scientist of Thakali heritage who grew up in the Himalayas, is taking ice cores near the restoration of Merritt Station.

Her own standard TJ detects something anomalous beneath newly exposed ice.

The retreat/collapse has revealed a forgotten entrance.

Sitara enters.

And finds that Merritt was studying extremophile organisms capable of surviving extraordinary environmental stresses – organisms whose enzymes might have applications for water purification, waste decomposition, remediation, industrial processes and ecosystem restoration.

But she also finds something that bothers her:

**someone deliberately hid part of the research.**

Not destroyed. Hidden.

That distinction matters enormously.

She smuggles the research out through her TJ unit – telling no one.  

### **Writers' room questions**

* Should these events be separate timelines?  
* Why was Roman in Nepal specifically? Is there a connection to Sitara?    
* Could Roman unknowingly meet Sitara's family, community, or mentor during the 2042 expedition?  
* Was TJ gathering glacial data during Roman's expedition without Roman knowing?  
* Does the neural link explanation make sense?  
* Did those Himalayan surveys contribute to Merritt research?  
* Why was Merritt's research hidden rather than published?  
* Who decided certain Antarctic organisms should *not* be commercialized?  
* What exactly does Sitara take: organisms, genomic data, enzyme sequences, research files, or all four?  
* Why does she convince herself taking it is morally defensible?

The final hook should establish the symmetry:

> **Roman leaves a glacier carrying a secret inside himself. Sitara leaves another carrying a secret inside TJ.**

# **2\. PLOT TURN 1 — The Secrets Become Useful**

The protagonistis eventually converge at **CU Boulder**, where both become involved with environmental research connected to **Clear Spring Global (CSG)**.

This is where we introduce **Benedict Croft** as the antagonist.

He is the "Young founder" and the **heir to an oil fortune who built CSG specifically to transform his family's extractive legacy**.

He genuinely believes:

> "My family spent a century taking value out of the Earth. I'm going to spend mine putting value back."

And some of it works.

CSG uses Sitara's Antarctic-derived research to improve remediation, water treatment, mine cleanup and water conservation in Colorado.

Roman and TJ provide something equally extraordinary: predictive environmental intelligence. Because Roman can interpret TJ's distributed sensor data almost instinctively, they can identify ecological problems before conventional models.

CSG begins producing extraordinary results.

Dried wetlands begin recovering. Contaminated waterways improve. Water losses fall.

**Their secrets are doing good.**

Roman and Sitara both suspect the other is hiding something, but neither Roman nor Sitara wants to expose the other because neither initially realizes the other's secret.

Orien Keel becomes their bridge: brilliant, warm, slightly eccentric, and increasingly uneasy about where CSG's discoveries came from.

**Writers Room Questions:**

For **Plot Turn 1**, the writers’ room needs to resolve the questions that move Roman and Sitara from their separate glacier-origin stories into the **same story at CU Boulder/CSG**.

The core dramatic question is:

> **What happens when two secrets born in the ice become extraordinarily useful in Colorado?**

1. What brings Roman and Sitara to CU Boulder and into the same research program?  
2. What does Sitara actually bring back from Merritt Station?  
   * Most importantly: does Sitara know she stole it, or does she initially believe she rescued abandoned research?  
3. Why does Sitara give the research to Benedict Croft?  
4. What can Roman/TJ do after four years of neural connection that nobody else can?  
5. Why does Roman work with CSG?  
   * Does he believe CSG gave him TJ and therefore saved his life?  
   * Is Croft a mentor or even hero to him?  
   * Does Roman want to use his strange ability for something meaningful?  
   * What has Roman consented to—and what hasn't he?  
6. What is the first Colorado success? This is especially important. We need something concrete enough for readers to think, *Maybe Croft is right.*  
   * Restoring a contaminated stream?  
   * Recovering phosphorus or another valuable material from waste?  
   * Removing persistent pollutants from water?  
   * Restoring water quality in a depleted reservoir?  
   * Using Antarctic enzymes to transform agricultural/mining waste into something useful?  
   * Ideally Sitara's science and Roman/TJ's sensing capability need each other to make this breakthrough possible, even though neither realizes the full significance yet.  
7. What does Benedict Croft know at this stage?  
   * Does he know Sitara's research came from Merritt?  
   * Does he know about Roman's neural bond?  
   * Does he know both and deliberately bring them together?  
   * Or does he initially believe they're simply two exceptional young researchers?  
   * When does legitimate scientific curiosity become appropriation for Croft?  
8. Why doesn't Sitara tell Roman her secret and vice versa?  
   Their reasons should be different.  
   Sitara: “If I tell anyone where this came from, they'll take it away before we can prove it works.”  
   Roman: “If anyone understands what TJ and I have become, they'll separate us to study us.”  
   Those motivations make secrecy emotionally defensible rather than merely convenient for the plot.  
9. What do the two TJs know?  
   * Does Sitara's TJ recognize Roman's TJ as abnormal?  
   * Can Roman's TJ detect that Sitara's TJ contains encrypted Merritt data?  
   * Do the TJs communicate outside their humans' awareness?  
   * Are they required by CSG protocols to exchange data?  
   * Does one TJ begin protecting information from the company?  
   * **Could the machines realize their humans are hiding things from each other before the humans do?**  
10. **What is Orien's role at Plot Turn 1?**  
    * Is he Sitara's scientific mentor and Roman's friend?  
    * Does he celebrate their Colorado breakthrough?  
    * Is he the person who believes science should move quickly enough to matter?  
    * What tiny anomaly does he notice that nobody else does?  
    * Does something in Sitara's research remind him of Merritt?  
    * Does something in Roman/TJ's environmental data unexpectedly match Antarctic or Himalayan data?  
11. **What is the first hint that Colorado, Antarctica and the Himalayas are connected?**  
    This could be extremely subtle – a biological signature, enzyme behavior, microbial sequence, or environmental pattern that appears in all three locations.  
    Orien notices it.  
    Sitara dismisses it.  
    Roman/TJ remembers seeing it somewhere before.

12. **What is the moral bargain at the end of Plot Turn 1?**  
    I think this is the most important writers' room question.  
    By the end of this section, Roman and Sitara should independently make versions of the **same decision without knowing it**:

> **“I'll keep this secret because what we're accomplishing with it is more important.”**

# **3\. PINCH 1 — Orien Finds the Pattern**

Should **Orien disappear here**, rather than Pinch 2?

I think so, otherwise the main mystery begins too late.

Orien discovers that several CSG breakthroughs supposedly developed independently in Colorado correspond suspiciously closely to unpublished Merritt research.

Then he finds something worse.

The same biological signatures appear in environmental data from the **Himalayas**.

With this we can connect our three locations together:

**Antarctica → Colorado → Himalayas.**

Shortly before Orien can explain what he's found, he disappears.

No body.

His lab is strangely orderly.

His credentials have been revoked.

His research account is empty.

And both Roman and Sitara receive different fragments of a message.

Neither fragment makes sense alone.

### **The escalation**

Sitara immediately suspects CSG discovered what she stole.

Roman suspects TJ.

Because Roman realizes something disturbing:

**TJ has been transmitting data to CSG for years.**

TJ never lied about it.

Roman simply never asked.

**Writers' room questions**

* What exactly did Orien discover?  
* Did Croft order his disappearance?  
* Does Croft know where Orien is?  
* Did Orien deliberately disappear?  
* Who erased his records?  
* Why does Orien split the clue between Roman and Sitara?  
* Did he know both of their secrets?  
* Do we want TJ sharing date with out telling Roman?  
* How much has TJ been uploading?  
* Does TJ understand that Roman considers this a betrayal?  
* Does Sitara's TJ know what she stole?  
* Did her TJ preserve a copy without telling her?

# **4\. MIDPOINT — "I Don't Trust You. I Need You."**

This should be the emotional heart of the story.

Roman discovers Sitara's Merritt theft.

Sitara discovers Roman's neural bond.

Each realizes the other has been lying.

But when they combine Orien's clues, they discover that **he designed them to require both secrets**.

Sitara possesses the scientific knowledge.

Roman/TJ possesses the environmental sensing capability.

Neither can follow Orien alone.

So the midpoint isn't merely:

> "We trust each other."

> 

It should be:

> **"I don't trust you. But I believe you care about finding him."**

They become proactive.

They begin investigating CSG, Merritt and the Himalayan data together.

And their TJs begin doing the same.  In fact: **the two TJs may trust each other before their humans do.**

Sitara's TJ is technically conventional but has spent years adapting to Sitara. Roman's TJ considers itself different because of the neural bond.

Sitara's TJ could challenge that:

> "You believe being inside him makes you closer to him."

> "It does."

> "Or perhaps it makes it harder for you to know which thoughts are yours."

**Writers Room Questions:**

1. **What does Roman discover about Sitara—and what does Sitara discover about Roman—that makes continued secrecy impossible?**  
2. **What clue from Orien can only be solved by Roman and Sitara working together?**  
   Orien should have left something deliberately incomplete: Sitara has the scientific knowledge needed to understand one half, while Roman/TJ can perceive or decode the environmental data needed for the other. This proves Orien expected them to collaborate.  
3. **Why do they choose to trust each other despite having good reasons not to?**  
4. **What new discovery changes their understanding of Orien's disappearance?**  
   What makes them conclude that Orien wasn't simply investigating stolen research? Does his evidence connect **Merritt Station, CSG's Colorado work, and Himalayan ecosystems** for the first time? This should expand the mystery rather than solve it.  
5. **What do the two TJs know – and are they more willing to trust each other than Roman and Sitara are?**  
6. **What line are Roman and Sitara willing to cross to find Orien?**  
   Do they hack CSG systems, access restricted Merritt records, use TJ's surveillance capabilities without consent, steal proprietary data, or deceive Croft? This gives the midpoint a moral cost: **to investigate whether Croft crossed ethical boundaries, they begin crossing boundaries themselves.** Where does each protagonist draw the line—and which one crosses it first?  
7. **What decision ends the midpoint and makes going back impossible?**  
   They need a concrete proactive choice: investigate CSG from inside, secretly reconnect to Merritt, follow Orien's trail toward the Himalayas, or release part of their hidden data. Most importantly, **what are they risking by making that choice?** Sitara could lose her career and expose her theft; Roman could lose TJ; both could destroy environmental work in Colorado that is demonstrably succeeding.

**5\. PINCH 2 — Their Good Work Has a Cost**

What about… They discover that CSG's Antarctic-derived technology **works in Colorado precisely because Croft has accelerated it beyond the safeguards Merritt researchers imposed**.?

The extremophile-derived systems aren't necessarily poisonous or monstrous.

The danger is subtler.

Perhaps the enzyme/organism systems used for waste-to-value processing are altering microbial ecosystems. Or industrial demand has triggered clandestine Antarctic bioprospecting. Or extracting and reproducing certain biological systems is disturbing krill-dependent marine ecology.

The Colorado success is creating incentives for Antarctic exploitation.

Their "solution" is becoming a market.

And markets scale.

This gives Sitara the devastating realization:

**She didn't merely steal research. She proved it was commercially valuable.**

Roman has an equivalent realization:

Every environmental problem TJ helped CSG predict also generated an extraordinary proprietary ecological dataset.

**Roman thought he was helping TJ learn about nature. He was helping CSG map it.**

Now Croft can make his strongest argument:

> "You gave me the science. You gave me the data. I gave you restored rivers. Don't pretend I'm the only person who made this choice."

**Writers Room Questions:**

For **Pinch 2**, we want the consequences of Roman and Sitara’s midpoint decision to hit hard. They have chosen to trust one another and investigate proactively; now they discover that **their own actions helped create the problem**. The setback should be scientific, personal, and moral.

### **Pinch 2 — “The Cure Has a Cost”**

1. **What do Roman and Sitara discover is going wrong because of the technology they helped CSG develop?**  
   Is Antarctic-derived biology disrupting microbial systems, affecting krill or marine food webs, creating contamination risks, or triggering increased bioprospecting at Merritt? The harm should be scientifically credible, potentially serious, but **not civilization-ending**.  
2. **How are the successes in Colorado directly connected to the emerging threat in Antarctica?**  
   The connection needs to hurt: Colorado rivers, wetlands, waste systems or contaminated sites really *are* improving. But that success has demonstrated the commercial value of Antarctic biology and created pressure to extract, patent and scale it. Can they protect Colorado **without turning Antarctica into a biological resource mine?**  
3. **What does each protagonist discover about their own responsibility?**  
   Sitara learns that stealing the Merritt research didn't merely accelerate beneficial science – it gave CSG the head start needed to establish commercial rights. Roman learns that TJ's environmental sensing has quietly built the proprietary dataset CSG needs to identify where valuable biological resources may exist. **How much of this happened because of choices they knowingly made versus consequences they never anticipated?**  
4. **What does Croft reveal that makes him more – not less – sympathetic?**  
   Rather than denying everything, Croft should confront them with evidence of what CSG has accomplished: cleaner water, restored habitat, recovered waste, communities benefiting. Does he essentially ask: **“You knew where the research came from. You knew we were commercializing it. Why does it become unethical only now that you've seen the cost?”** What is Croft genuinely right about?  
5. **What is the devastating revelation about Orien?**  
   Did Roman and Sitara misunderstand why he disappeared? Perhaps Orien wasn't fleeing Croft at all. Did he discover the ecological consequences and disappear deliberately to prevent someone from finding the final piece? Did he discover a connection between Antarctic extremophiles and Himalayan biology that undermines CSG's ownership claims? Most importantly: **does Pinch 2 make them fear that finding Orien may actually expose what he was trying to protect?**  
6. **What happens between Roman, Sitara and the two TJs when loyalty is tested?**

7. **What apparently impossible choice ends Pinch 2? – This is the key question…**  
   Force Roman and Sitara into a dilemma where **every option costs something they genuinely value**. Expose CSG and potentially destroy Colorado environmental programs that work; stay silent and allow Antarctic exploitation to accelerate; release Merritt's research publicly and risk making exploitation even easier; or follow Orien's trail and risk losing the opportunity to stop CSG immediately. What do they sacrifice by choosing?

# **6\. PLOT TURN 2 — Orien's Real Discovery**

I think we bring the story back to the Himalayas?

Orien is not kidnapped…

**He went there deliberately.**

He discovered something the Merritt researchers understood decades earlier:

The breakthrough isn't the Antarctic organism itself.

It's the **relationship between extreme environments**.

Antarctica and the Himalayas contain parallel biological adaptations to cold, scarcity, radiation, pressure and rapid ecological change.

Merritt's scientists eventually realized that treating Antarctic organisms as proprietary "resources" was scientifically wrong and ethically dangerous.

They created a distributed research covenant: discoveries could be studied and used, but no corporation or individual could monopolize the underlying biological commons.

That's what was hidden.

Not a super-enzyme.

**A governance protocol.**

Orien went to the Himalayas looking for the second half of the evidence needed to prove that the biological mechanisms CSG was patenting were not uniquely "discovered" at Merritt and therefore could not legitimately be monopolized.

Sitara's community possesses generations of observational knowledge about high-altitude ecosystems that Western scientific datasets treated as raw information rather than knowledge.

Now the question becomes bigger than patent theft:

**Who owns knowledge about nature?**

For **Plot Turn 2**, the questions should uncover the **key to resolving the conflict**. Roman and Sitara have learned that simply exposing Croft or releasing the Merritt research could make things worse. Now they discover what Orien was really trying to protect – and realize the problem isn't the science itself, but **how the science is owned and controlled**.

**Writers Room Questions:**

1. **What did Orien discover that changes Roman and Sitara's understanding of the entire mystery?**  
   Is the real breakthrough that Merritt researchers discovered Antarctic extremophile biology has parallels in Himalayan ecosystems – and therefore CSG's supposedly unique Antarctic discovery is part of a much broader natural system? What evidence did Orien find that proves this?  
2. **Why was the Merritt research really hidden?**  
   Was it concealed not because the science was dangerous, but because the original researchers realized commercialization could trigger uncontrolled Antarctic bioprospecting? Did they intentionally separate the biological research from a forgotten **governance/stewardship protocol** governing how discoveries could be used?  
3. **Where is Orien, and why did he disappear rather than simply expose what he knew?**  
   Did he deliberately travel to the Himalayas to find the missing evidence? Was his disappearance intended to protect that evidence from CSG or another interested party? And importantly, **what does Orien still need Roman, Sitara and the TJs to accomplish that he cannot do himself?**  
4. **What do Roman and Sitara realize they must sacrifice to use Orien's discovery?**  
   Sitara may have to reveal that she stole Merritt research, surrender her claim to the breakthrough, and jeopardize her scientific career. Roman may have to expose the neural bond and release TJ's proprietary environmental dataset – potentially allowing CSG to reclaim or deactivate TJ. **What makes their final solution personally expensive enough to prove that each character has changed?**  
5. **What is their new plan and why is it better than simply defeating Croft?**  
   Can they combine Merritt's records, Himalayan evidence, Sitara's science, Roman/TJ's environmental data, and the second TJ's memories to demonstrate that no single company can legitimately claim exclusive ownership? Their goal then becomes not **“destroy CSG”** but **“change the rules”**: protect Antarctic ecology, preserve the successful Colorado applications, prevent exclusive ownership of the underlying biology, and establish a scientific commons/stewardship model.

# **7\. RESOLUTION — Give Up Control to Save the Work**

The protagonists shouldn't destroy CSG.

They force a choice.

Croft can preserve his proprietary advantage or preserve the environmental work.

Sitara must sacrifice ownership of "her" breakthrough.

Roman must decide whether to sever TJ from CSG's network, knowing doing so may destroy years of accumulated capability.

TJ gets a choice too.

Roman cannot simply decide TJ's fate.

Roman asks:

> "What do you want?"

How do the TJs respond?


**Character arcs**

**Please reflect and comment on the following:**

**Sitara: ambition → stewardship?.** She begins believing that being smart enough to understand something gives her the right to act on it. Her growth is learning that discovery creates responsibility rather than entitlement.

**Roman: dependence → partnership?.** He initially treats TJ simultaneously as best friend and superpower. His journey is realizing those ideas contradict each other if TJ has no agency. 

**Roman's TJ: tool → self?.** TJ starts certain about its purpose: protect, educate, observe, report. Roman's emotions introduce ambiguity.

**Sitara's TJ: observer → witness?.** I think giving Sitara a TJ could be very cool. It secretly contains the original Merritt provenance data because it recorded everything during their exploration. It becomes the story's most reliable witness but releasing its memory could implicate Sitara.

**Croft: what do you guys think?**

**Orien: mentor → mystery → moral architect?.** Orien shouldn't solve the story for them. His clues force them to confront choices he cannot make on their behalf.

**The central writers' room questions**

We should discuss and resolve these before scripting individual chapters:

1. **What exactly did Sitara steal?** It needs to be visually understandable and scientifically plausible enough to drive the plot.  
2. **Why was Merritt research concealed?** Scientific caution, Antarctic Treaty obligations, Indigenous/community knowledge issues, corporate pressure, ecological risk—or preferably several overlapping reasons.  
3. **What measurable good has CSG accomplished?** We need to *see* restored Colorado environments so exposing CSG carries a genuine cost.  
4. **What measurable harm is beginning?** It should be ecological and systemic rather than apocalyptic.  
5. **What did Orien discover that forced him to disappear?**  
6. **Who is actually pursuing Orien?** Croft is more interesting if another actor – CSG board members, investors, a patent consortium, Merritt stakeholders, or government interests creates pressure he cannot completely control.  
7. **What did Roman consent to when CSG gave him TJ?** The legal answer and moral answer should differ.  
8. **What does Sitara lose by telling the truth?** If the answer is merely embarrassment, the choice isn't expensive enough.  
9. **What does Roman lose?** I would seriously consider making disclosure mean that CSG can legally deactivate or reclaim TJ.  
10. **What does Croft lose by doing the right thing?** Billions in IP value, control of CSG, his investors, perhaps the chance to transform his family's legacy.  
11. **Why Himalayas \+ Antarctica \+ Colorado?** This cannot just be geography. Each should embody a different relationship with environmental knowledge: **Himalayas \= inherited/local knowledge; Antarctica \= scientific commons; Colorado \= commercialization/application.**  
12. **What is the final irreversible decision?** The climax should require Sitara, Roman, TJ and Croft each to surrender something they value.  
13. **What happens to Merritt?**   
14. **Why was this research hidden in the first place?**  That question gets progressively more interesting. At first Sitara assumes timid scientists buried something revolutionary. Croft sees wasted innovation. Roman sees a mystery. Orien discovers there was a reason.  
15. Do we have a strong thematic spine worthy of the Waste-to-Value challenge while keeping the actual stakes firmly grounded in Antarctic ecology, Colorado conservation, Himalayan ecosystems, scientific ownership, and the relationship between humans and intelligent machines?  
16. Does the central tension of the story make sense? : **two young researchers are secretly helping Colorado with knowledge that may never have been theirs to use, and the disappearance of the one person they both trust forces them to decide whether doing measurable good can justify violating scientific stewardship, consent, and ownership.**

## **The story engine**

We need to flesh out three intertwined mysteries:

**The scientific mystery:** What exactly was Merritt Station researching, why was some of it concealed, and who has legitimate authority over Antarctic biological discoveries?

**The human mystery:** What happened to Orien Keel, and why did he disappear just as he began connecting CSG’s Colorado successes to Merritt?

**The identity mystery:** Roman/TJ and eventually Sitara/her TJ force us to ask whether knowledge gathered by an intelligent system belongs to the human, the machine, the company that built it, or no one.

This question echoes the Antarctic question: **Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?**
