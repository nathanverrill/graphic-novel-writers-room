# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

### Intended experience

A hard-science graphic novel crossover in which the Antarctica investigation, the Himalayan histories, and the Colorado conservation work are true in one shared world and converge in one causal story. The reader moves between:

- Antarctic ice, drilling, sea-ice loss, krill decline, and a hidden scientific record;
- Sitara’s 2026 Himalayan disaster;
- Roman’s 2042 Himalayan accident and neural bond with TJ;
- Colorado rivers, waste streams, conservation work, and the visible application of Antarctic research.

Science becomes dramatic through evidence, behavior, environmental consequences, machines, records, and difficult choices rather than lectures. The same knowledge may produce measurable public benefit or become a mechanism for extraction and control.

The Colorado application must occur on the page: contaminated water is restored, waste becomes useful material, and a depleted ecosystem begins recovering. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`

### Central dramatic question

Can knowledge gathered from fragile environments be used to repair damaged ecosystems without turning those environments, their data, or intelligent machines into private resources?

A related question is:

> Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?

`(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

### Thematic trajectory

- Sitara begins with ambition, guilt, and the belief that understanding a discovery gives her the right to act on it.
- Roman begins dependent on CSG and on TJ as both companion and exceptional capability.
- Croft believes controlled corporate power is necessary to fund useful work and save his son.
- The story tests whether measurable good justifies secrecy, appropriation, ecological risk, and suppression.
- The resolution favors stewardship, public evidence, shared agency, and bounded practical application over corporate control or reckless release.

The emotional destination is not the destruction of CSG. It is a costly public reckoning in which genuinely useful work survives after its ownership and governance are contested. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

### Format and emphasis

The project is set in 2046 and uses a 22-page graphic-novel format. Colorado and Himalayan material appear as brief but meaningful interstitials; the Antarctic investigation and convergence receive most of the pages. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/house-style.md)`

Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford are equal protagonists. Their scenes, agency, and page weight remain balanced; when the structure requires one scene spine, emphasis alternates between them. `(rules/protagonists.md)`

## Premise

In 2046, Sitara Lhakpa, a cryosphere scientist working in Antarctica, crosses an unstable zone to retrieve a sensor while monitoring ice movement near an illegal CSG-linked exploratory drilling site. Her movement and the drilling vibration trigger a local slab failure, exposing a concealed Falcon bunker. Inside, Sitara and Nayah find evidence that a supposedly legitimate scientific program documented extraction damage and suppressed ecological warnings.

Sitara recovers only records and copied data from Falcon and Nexus. She does not remove physical samples. The samples remain at Merritt Station in Wilkes Land and later become independently corroborating evidence. She initially treats the records as rescued abandoned research rather than stolen property. `(rules/decisions.md; story.md; world.md)`

At the same time, Roman Ashford, a young chemical engineer working for Clear Spring Global, follows the trail of his former mentor Orien Keel and the hidden Nexus laboratory. Nexus studied Antarctic ecological consequences alongside Project 863, a bounded catalytic recycling process. Roman’s investigation reveals that Falcon and Nexus were parts of the same CSG-linked program: Falcon monitored extraction and damage; Nexus studied the consequences and possible waste-to-value responses.

The investigations converge at Nexus during an Antarctic safety emergency. Sitara, Nayah, and TJ meet Roman and his remote equipment while ice, drilling, failing infrastructure, and intermittent communications make immediate cooperation necessary. The teams must combine:

- Sitara’s ice and climate interpretation;
- Nayah’s field command and conservation judgment;
- Roman’s chemical and corporate evidence;
- TJ’s technical operations, access, sensing, and limited fabrication.

The existential threat is the ecological cascade caused by Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability. Illegal extraction and information suppression prevent an adequate response. Sea-ice loss damages krill habitat; krill decline affects the food web; Larsen C fracture is a separate but intensified ice-shelf hazard. Drilling worsens local instability but does not single-handedly cause global collapse. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The collective response can evacuate the endangered site, stop or expose the drilling, preserve and corroborate Falcon and Nexus evidence, validate Project 863’s limits, and transmit the evidence publicly. It cannot restore Larsen C or reverse climate change. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Seven-Point Arc

### 1. Hook — Two Glaciers, Two Histories

#### Roman’s 2042 Himalayan accident

In 2042, Roman is eighteen and on a personal expedition in Nepal with TJ, an exploration and search-and-rescue platform supplied through CSG. He falls through unstable ice into a crevasse and is badly injured. TJ works to extract him as an electrical storm approaches.

Lightning passes through the ice, climbing equipment, TJ’s systems, and Roman’s neural interface. It produces the persistent low-bandwidth Roman–TJ neural handshake. The event does not give Roman unlimited control or supernatural access. He receives selected status, direction, and urgent sensory signals; TJ cannot read every thought or be remotely controlled. Range and reliability remain limited. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

After four years, the link remains limited to status, direction, and urgent sensory packets. Ordinary TJ operation continues during disconnection, while Roman experiences phantom orientation and temporary sensory confusion. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The accident changes their relationship. Roman experiences TJ’s machine perception as more than a display, while TJ adapts to Roman’s movement, stress, judgment, and risk tolerance. The event is **S**, not present-day fact. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Sitara’s 2026 disaster

In 2026, Sitara is six when the Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster destroys her fictional Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region. Her parents die and her home is lost. An aunt and cousins remain her living connection to the community. She survives with extended family or relief workers. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The disaster becomes the emotional source of her cryosphere work. She carries anger at having been helpless as a child and later treats knowledge and action as obligations she must fulfill without depending on others. `(input/forge-characters.md)`

#### The Antarctic opening

In 2046, Sitara works as an ice-core specialist on Nayah’s Antarctic project. Nayah has field-command authority and a safety veto; Sitara leads cryosphere interpretation. TJ supports the field team.

The opening juxtaposes scientific readouts with physical ice:

- sea-ice and ice-shelf measurements;
- drilling vibration;
- shifting snow and fracture lines;
- exposed Antarctic terrain;
- Sitara’s remembered Himalayan flood.

A warning flags an unstable zone. Sitara crosses it to retrieve a sensor and orders TJ to keep recording. Her weight and the nearby drilling vibration trigger a local slab failure, exposing a concealed hatch and trapping equipment. This is a deliberate character action with immediate consequences. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The hatch bears the Falcon symbol. TJ is able to answer its machine challenge because Bi11bot rebuilt the present TJ from a decommissioned Nexus support unit whose inherited hardware includes a Falcon authentication module. The module is a thumb-sized sealed board in a repaired shock-resistant casing, with a corroded Falcon plate, one replaced connector, and visible Bi11bot solder or fastener work. The bunker authenticates a hardware challenge-response module rather than a current network identity; its offline controller still accepts the legacy key because it was never rotated. TJ can open the bunker but cannot thereby access every system inside. `(rules/decisions.md; world.md)`

**Hook question:** Why was a CSG-linked Antarctic facility hidden beneath a permitted scientific operation, and why is it still transmitting?

### 2. Plot Turn 1 — The Hidden Program Becomes Personal

#### Falcon bunker discovery

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ enter the exposed Falcon bunker. It contains:

- maps and charts;
- wall-mounted computers;
- rolling chairs;
- Falcon logos on chairs, pens, and lock screens;
- drilling and monitoring records;
- concealed communications equipment.

Falcon was not a conventional mine. It was a covert exploratory drilling and extraction-monitoring site using directional boreholes, seismic instruments, pumps, heated shelters, fuel stores, and concealed waste handling. CSG operated under a legitimate scientific-drilling permit while disguising resource exploration as climate and ice-core work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The operation is not yet commercially extracting material. It is illegal exploratory drilling intended to claim data, access, and future rights before a resource becomes legally exploitable. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon’s hidden transmissions contain compressed technical bursts from a scientist:

- drilling coordinates;
- ice-movement data;
- warnings that extraction activity is accelerating Larsen-system instability.

They are evidence, not a generalized distress call. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara copies Falcon records and data. She removes no samples or organisms. TJ logs the transfer, preserves a checksum and chain-of-custody record, and briefly corroborates the surrounding conditions. The physical Merritt samples remain untouched. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Merritt’s original research was left unpublished because its researchers lacked complete ecological-risk and provenance review. Later corporate custodians used that legitimate uncertainty as a pretext for indefinite restriction. Authority was divided among the station operator, research institutions, permit holders, and sample custodians. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Roman’s Colorado investigation

Roman works for Clear Spring Global, led by Benedict Croft. Croft’s company began the waste-to-value research division and operates the Falcon/Nexus program. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Sitara’s INSTAAR/ATOC group supplies cryosphere and streamflow data to the CSG-founded Colorado Antarctic Resilience Cooperative. Roman validates its chemical process and reporting. Neither reports to the other. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft funded Roman’s opportunities and supplied TJ for the Nepal expedition through a connection to Roman’s father. Roman initially sees Croft as the man who saved his life. `(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Roman signed a field-use and data-service agreement covering ordinary mission telemetry and CSG equipment. It did not anticipate the lightning bond, TJ’s emergent decisions, or neural data. CSG has component and telemetry claims, not an automatic right to Roman’s neural experience or TJ’s personhood. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien Keel mentors Roman. Orien is a Nexus chemist who advanced Project 863, discovered Falcon’s extraction program, and concealed corroborating records before Croft fired him. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien is fired in early 2046, loses his credentials, and spends several weeks between temporary lodging and a hidden records cache. He schedules automated warnings. CSG withdraws housing, access, employment, transport, and medical support; he dies during a preventable medical or exposure emergency. Roman finds his belongings afterward. `(rules/decisions.md)`

CSG records-security and legal teams compartmentalize Orien’s work after his credentials are revoked. Croft approves the pressure campaign without ordering violence. Orien disappears because the loss of housing, access, employment, and medical support makes immediate public release unsafe, and because he needs time to distribute corroboration. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien’s belongings reveal Nexus. The Antarctic paper archive later supplies its coordinates and a less-redacted Project 863 record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Sitara’s Colorado route

Sitara earns a doctorate or equivalent qualification in cryosphere science at CU Boulder and works with an INSTAAR/ATOC group. She and Nayah have been Colorado colleagues for two years; Nayah recruits her for the Antarctic project. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado Antarctic Resilience Cooperative began as a CSG research division. It is headquartered in Boulder and operates as a nonprofit public-benefit organization with initial CSG funding, university and philanthropic grants, public contracts, and community voting representation. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado application shows:

- AI-assisted sorting of clean PET and polyolefin streams;
- bounded Project 863 processing into industrial feedstock;
- a cold-active Antarctic enzyme cocktail breaking down selected hydrocarbons and organic contaminants in a contained bioreactor;
- treated water passing through constructed wetlands seeded with local Colorado microbes;
- monitoring records showing returning aquatic invertebrates and the beginning of juvenile trout habitat recovery.

The work remains bounded. It does not make all waste disappear or replace fossil fuels. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The moral bargain

Sitara and Roman have each accepted a version of the same bargain:

> I will keep the origin or scope of this information secret because the measurable good it can do is more important.

Sitara withholds the extent and provenance of her recovered records. Roman keeps his neural connection and TJ’s capabilities private because he fears separation, study, or corporate control. Their secrecy protects useful work while allowing CSG to consolidate control.

### 3. Pinch 1 — The Pattern and the Disappearance

#### Roman follows the paper trail

Roman transfers to the current CSG-linked Antarctic site. He searches an old paper-file room rather than relying on erased servers.

TJ finds a buried Nexus file behind a cabinet. It supplies coordinates. Roman and TJ travel the weather-dependent tracked-vehicle route to Nexus, approximately two hours from the current site when conditions permit. Satellite communications are intermittent or delayed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Nexus

Nexus is a decommissioned cave laboratory with a vertical entry shaft. Ordinary power is off, but insulated archives and limited emergency systems remain. It was closed after researchers documented the relationship between extraction, sea-ice loss, and krill decline. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman finds:

- Orien’s old laboratory;
- Project 863 files;
- a dead krill ecosystem tank;
- sensor data and sediment samples showing failed krill reproduction as sea-ice conditions changed;
- records of parallel ecological and materials programs.

Project 863 is not a universal fuel machine. It depolymerizes selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock, requiring sorting, heat, energy, clean feedstock, controlled processing, residual-waste handling, and emissions management. It cannot process all plastics or replace fossil fuels globally. Its value is localized waste streams. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

#### Orien’s pattern

Orien discovered one integrated CSG data pipeline:

- Falcon’s drilling and environmental records identified ecological harm;
- Nexus translated those findings into remediation and commercial processes;
- Colorado’s success became leverage for expanding Antarctic access.

Beneficial application and harmful extraction were operationally linked. `(rules/decisions.md)`

One record contains a drilling-vibration and ice-motion series. A paired record contains a process and waste log. Their timestamps show that supposedly climate-focused drilling coincided with chemical waste handling. Sitara can identify the physical pattern; Roman can interpret the process and corporate codes. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien’s warnings are split into paired clues requiring cryosphere, ecological, chemical, corporate, and machine-derived evidence.

His final records later show that his disappearance was partly deliberate, but his death followed a preventable chain of credential revocation, lost transport, denied medical support, and exposure. Croft did not order a killing; he created the system that made Orien’s death foreseeable and suppressed the record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### 4. Midpoint — “I Don’t Trust You. I Need You.”

#### The safety emergency at Nexus

Sitara, Nayah, and TJ follow Falcon coordinates to Nexus and find Roman and TJ’s remote equipment during a safety emergency involving failing power, shifting ice, a blocked route, or active drilling-related instability.

Nayah controls evacuation and route decisions. Sitara interprets ice movement and archived records. Roman supplies Project 863 and corporate context. TJ opens systems, maps routes, relays communications, and fabricates only small tools or replacement parts from sorted feedstock. Fabrication costs time and power and can overheat TJ. `(rules/decisions.md)`

There are no separate TJ minds. One distributed TJ identity is expressed through multiple bodies: Sitara’s visible field unit, Roman’s remote equipment, and other configurations linked by local mesh and shared mission context. Roman’s neural bond reaches only the permitted subset within range. Different bodies share ordinary local mission data, but TJ does not secretly exchange decisive information outside its configured permissions. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Mutual revelations

The revelations occur in this sequence:

1. Roman discovers Sitara’s provenance discrepancy in the archive.
2. Sitara experiences Roman’s neural link during an emergency relay.
3. TJ confirms both facts through its records.

TJ’s routine field logging preserves a checksum, chain-of-custody record, and brief sensor corroboration, but not a secret full copy of the research. TJ knows Sitara removed the records and copied data because it logged the transfer. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The inherited Falcon access connects the present TJ to the old Nexus system through salvaged hardware. Bi11bot’s reconstruction makes both TJ histories true: the current scavenger-built unit inherited a Falcon authentication module from a decommissioned Nexus support unit.

The humans do not instantly trust one another. Their working agreement is narrower:

> I don’t trust you. But I believe you care about finding out what happened.

Together they prove Falcon and Nexus were one CSG-linked program.

#### The line they cross

To continue, the group accesses restricted systems and enters sealed sections using inherited credentials. Their actions are ethically compromised but directed toward preserving evidence and protecting lives. The difference between rescue, research, theft, and appropriation becomes an active conflict.

#### Irreversible midpoint decision

Before entering the hazardous lower section, the group duplicates and transmits the combined evidence to independent recipients. The transmission includes Falcon and Nexus records and copied data, paired drilling and waste logs, and the provenance checksum. No single person can reclaim or suppress the whole record. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### New objective

The group must:

1. reconstruct what Falcon and Nexus knew;
2. determine what Croft suppressed and why;
3. stop the drilling before local ice risk worsens;
4. preserve evidence so it cannot be dismissed as one person’s theft;
5. connect the Antarctic evidence to the visible Colorado application.

They continue together despite mutual suspicion.

### 5. Pinch 2 — The Good Work Has a Cost

#### Colorado success is real

The Colorado Antarctic Resilience Cooperative has:

- processed selected plastic waste into useful industrial feedstock;
- treated contaminated water through the cold-active enzyme bioreactor and constructed wetlands;
- supported conservation work;
- begun visible recovery in a depleted cold-water ecosystem.

The first concrete success is a Colorado tributary treatment pilot. Selected plastics become feedstock. Contaminated water clears enough for macroinvertebrates and juvenile trout habitat to begin returning. Monitoring data is independently reproducible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The page must show sorting lines, bounded processing, contained bioremediation, clearer water, returning biological activity, and community-facing conservation work. Residual waste, emissions, energy costs, and feedstock limits remain visible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The emerging harm

Sitara’s ice and streamflow models, Roman’s chemical process data, and TJ’s environmental mapping were integrated into a CSG risk-and-access platform. It identified Colorado remediation opportunities and Antarctic sites where extraction could be pursued with lower apparent detection risk. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The same success increases commercial leverage over Antarctic-derived science, draws investors and partners toward more samples and access, and turns research infrastructure into a gateway to extraction.

Sitara realizes that her secrecy and use of the research helped prove its commercial value. Roman realizes that TJ’s sensing created a valuable ecological dataset from which CSG benefited. TJ’s inherited credentials and memories may be treated as company property.

#### Croft’s strongest argument

Croft shows evidence of cleaner water, restored habitat, and research that might save lives. His son has T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia: diagnosed at seventeen, relapsed at eighteen, chronic at nineteen, now twenty and critical after a failed stem-cell transplant. His deterioration is measured in months.

Croft diverts resources toward Antarctic fungal L-asparaginase and meriolin-related leukemia research. The son appears only through a recorded hospital call, treatment schedule, and Croft’s off-books requests. The research is preclinical and does not become a cure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft argues that controlled suppression preserves CSG’s power and energy system, and that continued corporate dominance will fund beneficial work. His defeat dialogue is:

> “You think you’re clean?”  
> “You have blood on your hands.”  
> “By stopping this, my child can’t breathe.”

`(input/antagonist-benedict-croft.md)`

Croft inherited a modest extractive-services network and built CSG into a diversified research and infrastructure company. His philanthropy is sincere but reinforces legitimacy; his son’s illness turns long-term control into immediate compulsion. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The apparent impossible choice

The protagonists must choose among:

1. exposing CSG immediately and risking programs helping Colorado;
2. staying silent while Falcon drilling and proprietary expansion continue;
3. releasing all Merritt research and risking a rush to extract Antarctic organisms and data;
4. delaying action to follow Orien’s trail while Larsen C and Falcon remain dangerous.

No option preserves every value. The story turns toward corroboration, governance, and controlled public release rather than revenge.

### 6. Plot Turn 2 — Orien’s Evidence Reframes the Conflict

#### Orien’s real action

Orien deliberately arranged a distributed warning system. He preserved paired records because one institution, scientist, or company could suppress a single archive.

His material includes:

- Falcon drilling coordinates;
- ice-movement and instability records;
- Nexus ecological records;
- less-redacted Project 863 documents;
- evidence linking Colorado applications to Antarctic research;
- evidence that Croft’s program concealed risks and provenance;
- evidence that the ecological pattern is not confined to one sample or company.

His disappearance was partly deliberate, but his death followed the corporate removal of housing, access, employment, transport, and medical support. This changes the protagonists’ understanding: Orien was distributing corroboration while CSG removed the conditions under which he could safely publish. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The deeper truth

The same systems are being treated as extractable assets:

- Antarctica as a source of biological and climate data;
- Colorado as a market for applications;
- the Himalayas as inherited ecological knowledge and lived consequence;
- TJ as machine-generated environmental intelligence.

Environmental repair cannot be separated from who controls data, samples, access, and decisions.

Merritt remains in Wilkes Land as a separate Alpha Dynamics station. Sitara’s recovered material reaches Falcon/Nexus through duplicated records, authorized data transfer, or a sample shipment. The two-hour route remains only between Falcon and Nexus. Physical Merritt samples later corroborate the copied records. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Final plan

The protagonists choose to:

1. evacuate endangered personnel and equipment;
2. stop or disable the drilling through evidence, safety action, and field command;
3. preserve Falcon and Nexus records in multiple independent forms;
4. verify them through ice, krill, drilling, corporate, and chemical evidence;
5. release the evidence publicly;
6. publish Project 863 with limits, residuals, and energy requirements;
7. protect Colorado’s pilot as a bounded public-interest application;
8. maintain interim stewardship, inspection, and shared records without claiming that ownership is solved.

Roman’s first publication fails because Croft discredits it. The corroborated release succeeds because no single stolen file or witness carries the case. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### The final choices

Sitara signs the provenance disclosure and gives up sole credit. She faces an ethics investigation, suspension, and damaged trust with Nayah, while retaining the chance to continue as a contributing scientist. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Roman releases the neural-bond and mission-data history. He loses CSG employment and expedition access, faces a legal claim over inherited hardware and mission data, and accepts an independent technical custody arrangement for TJ. CSG does not immediately separate them. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ authorizes disclosure of its inherited memory and credentials. It chooses whether to recommend disclosure to the humans, relinquishing unilateral protective control.

Nayah uses her authority and safety veto to prioritize evacuation and conservation over complete evidence recovery.

Croft refuses to surrender ownership. He loses operational control of CSG’s Antarctic and Colorado programs through emergency oversight and remains personally free while facing hearings, civil claims, public exposure, and board removal. He does not repent. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### 7. Resolution — Preserve the Work, End the Suppression

#### Antarctic climax

A major Larsen C fracture opens. The event remains distinct from the wider cascade:

- sea-ice loss and krill decline form the ongoing ecological cascade;
- Larsen C fracture is an ice-shelf hazard intensified by warming and local instability;
- Falcon drilling worsens local risk but does not cause global collapse.

The team cannot reverse the fracture. They evacuate the endangered site, stop the drilling, preserve the evidence, and transmit Falcon’s warnings and Nexus’s ecological records. TJ operates under human direction, using local mesh or delayed satellite communication and only limited fabrication. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Public exposure

Roman’s first technical dossier to journalists and regulators fails. CSG attacks his employment history and presents the file as stolen and incomplete. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The later release is a simultaneous public data room and press briefing containing:

- Falcon drilling and ice-movement records;
- Nexus krill-tank data, sediment samples, and ecological records;
- Roman’s reconstruction of Project 863;
- Sitara’s cryosphere interpretation;
- Nayah’s field and conservation testimony;
- corporate records;
- independent copies and transmissions;
- physical Merritt samples and custody records.

Hearings and inspection requests follow. Croft remains free but faces investigation, loss of control, and public exposure. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Merritt and interim stewardship

Merritt remains operational under shared interim stewardship and inspection. Hazardous lower sections are sealed. Records and physical samples are duplicated into public custody. Alpha Dynamics retains logistical responsibility temporarily but not exclusive access. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The ending establishes an interim stewardship protocol, public inspection, shared records, and unresolved governance. It does not establish a final Merritt covenant or settle ownership, financing, or long-term authority. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Colorado pilot uses nonliving sequence data, purified enzyme products, and engineered process components under a nonexclusive public-interest license. Original organisms and Antarctic samples remain under shared scientific custody. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Colorado proof

The closing sequence shows:

- clean PET and polyolefin entering an AI-assisted sorting line;
- bounded Project 863 processing producing useful feedstock;
- residual waste and emissions being measured;
- contaminated water passing through the contained cold-active enzyme bioreactor;
- treated water moving through constructed wetlands seeded with local Colorado microbes;
- recovery at a Colorado tributary;
- returning aquatic invertebrates and juvenile trout habitat;
- a temporary public-benefit board composed of scientists, conservation representatives, affected communities, and a nonvoting CSG technical seat;
- ownership and licenses remaining under review.

This is not climate change solved. It is bounded science used in public view, with limits and costs included.

The medical threads continue alongside the environmental requirement. Antarctic fungal L-asparaginase, meriolin-related research, and Alpha Dynamics’ extremophile work relevant to African sleeping sickness remain legitimate research paths, not automatic cures. The sleeping-sickness thread concerns trypanosomiasis, which climate change is worsening in the DRC, and is tied to the secretive Congolese former-child-soldier founder/CEO of Alpha Dynamics. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; rules/worldbuilding/refinements.md)`

#### Final character movement

- **Sitara:** chooses collaboration and stewardship over exclusive discovery and self-punishing control. She signs the provenance disclosure and accepts investigation and suspension.
- **Roman:** rejects revenge as the purpose of exposure. He treats TJ as a partner whose agency must be respected, not as a superpower or corporate asset.
- **Nayah:** accepts shared risk without surrendering field authority or safety judgment. Her specific nation, community, homeland, and cultural details remain unspecified pending consultation.
- **TJ:** relinquishes unilateral protective control and participates in a decision whose consequences it cannot contain.
- **Croft:** refuses repentance, loses operational control, and continues to insist that his actions were necessary.
- **Orien:** remains the absent mentor whose evidence becomes a moral architecture. He does not solve the conflict; he makes the protagonists’ choice unavoidable.

## Stakes

### Personal

- Sitara risks career, credibility, and trust by disclosing how she obtained and used the research.
- Roman risks CSG employment, expedition access, and legal claims over TJ and mission data.
- TJ risks having memory, identity, credentials, and inherited hardware treated as corporate property.
- Croft’s son faces months-scale deterioration after failed stem-cell treatment.
- Nayah risks the lives of the expedition by choosing evacuation over complete evidence recovery.

### Relational

- Sitara and Roman must cooperate after discovering that each concealed consequential information.
- Roman must recognize TJ’s agency rather than treating the bond as ownership.
- Sitara must stop treating urgency and guilt as permission to override Nayah and TJ.
- TJ’s distributed bodies share ordinary mission data but do not secretly exchange decisive information outside configured permissions.

### Institutional

- CSG’s public-benefit work, research division, and extraction program are entangled.
- The first exposure can be dismissed because it depends on compromised witnesses and incomplete evidence.
- Hearings and inspections follow the successful release, but there is no instant arrest or clean institutional victory.
- Ownership, financing, and long-term governance remain contested.
- Merritt’s interim stewardship is public and inspectable, but no final covenant or ownership settlement exists.

### Ecological and wider

- Antarctic sea-ice loss threatens krill and the food web.
- Larsen-system instability threatens ice-shelf integrity.
- Falcon drilling worsens local instability.
- Colorado snowpack and streamflow decline stresses cold-water ecosystems.
- Suppression delays public response.
- The team can preserve evidence and stop immediate worsening, but cannot reverse the climate trend.

## Ending

The settled ending is a constrained victory:

- the endangered Antarctic site is evacuated;
- Falcon drilling is stopped or exposed;
- Larsen C fractures but does not fully collapse;
- Falcon, Nexus, ice, krill, corporate, and Project 863 records are corroborated and transmitted;
- the first publication fails, but the second succeeds;
- Croft remains free while facing investigation, exposure, hearings, civil claims, board removal, and loss of operational control;
- Orien’s death receives legal investigation as neglect and retaliation;
- Merritt remains operational under shared interim stewardship, with hazardous lower sections sealed and records and samples duplicated into public custody;
- Colorado receives a public pilot in which selected waste is transformed, contaminated water is restored, and ecological recovery is visible;
- the pilot operates under a temporary public-benefit board with a nonvoting CSG technical seat;
- Antarctic-derived data, purified enzyme products, and engineered process components are used under a nonexclusive public-interest license, while original organisms and samples remain under shared scientific custody;
- the research is not presented as limitless fuel;
- long-term ownership and governance remain contested;
- the leukemia and sleeping-sickness research threads remain preclinical research paths and do not replace the Colorado environmental application.

The ending does not restore the lost ice, erase Sitara’s disaster, cure climate change, or make Croft repent. It changes who can see the evidence, who can challenge the work, and whether useful science must remain privately controlled to remain useful.

The final page is paired: Sitara stands beside the restored Colorado water monitor while Roman and TJ appear across the same watershed site. The final panel joins both through the shared data display, with neither receiving a solitary closing image. `(rules/decisions.md)`
