## Overview

This is a near-future world in which climate damage is no longer an abstract forecast. The physical systems that support communities are visibly failing:

- Antarctic sea ice is declining.
- Krill habitat is being lost.
- Larsen C is thinning and structurally vulnerable.
- Colorado snowpack and streamflow are falling.
- Cold-water fish habitat is shrinking.
- Water sources are drying.
- Food is more expensive because supply is increasingly unreliable.
- Remote infrastructure is becoming more dangerous to operate and more valuable to control. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The world still contains familiar universities, corporations, laboratories, research bases, supply chains, legal frameworks, paper records, offices, field clothing, tracked vehicles, aircraft, satellites, and ordinary human compromises. The future arrives as a layer over those systems rather than replacing them. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The central material contrast is between three relationships to environmental knowledge:

- **The Himalayas:** inherited and local knowledge of land, water, ice, and survival.
- **Antarctica:** scientific archives, ecological commons, remote infrastructure, and fragile environments.
- **Colorado:** commercialization, public application, conservation deployment, and the question of who benefits. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

## Time and Geography

### 2046

The story's present is 2046. Roman's Himalayan accident occurs in 2042, when he is eighteen. Sitara's Himalayan disaster occurs in 2026, when she is six. These are separate events. `(rules/rules.md; rules/decisions.md; input/forge-characters.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Antarctica

The Antarctic settings are concentrated in an accessible coastal research zone near the Antarctic Peninsula and the Larsen system.

The region includes:

- Coastal research bases and homes.
- A small research community.
- Active scientific and logistical facilities.
- Falcon, a covert drilling and extraction-monitoring site.
- Nexus, a decommissioned cave laboratory.
- The Larsen ice-shelf system.
- Sea ice, crevasses, moving ice, exposed rock, and sheltered cave systems.
- Wildlife only where habitat and season support it. `(rules/decisions.md; input/amelia.md)`

Falcon and Nexus are not unrelated facilities. They belong to the same CSG-linked program:

- **Falcon** was the operational and extraction-monitoring site.
- **Nexus** was the research site.
- Falcon documented drilling activity and environmental damage.
- Nexus studied the ecological consequences and investigated waste-to-value responses.
- The waste-to-value organization began as a CSG research division.
- Nexus was its scientific laboratory.
- Falcon was CSG's covert extraction and monitoring facility. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The two sites are connected by a weather-dependent over-ice route. The journey from Falcon to Nexus takes approximately two hours under usable conditions. Tracked vehicles and occasional aircraft connect Antarctic sites, while satellite communications may be delayed or intermittent. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Larsen system

Larsen A collapsed in 1995 and Larsen B collapsed in 2002. Larsen C remains fragile, thinning, and vulnerable. Föhn winds and atmospheric-river patterns affect melt distribution, while basin geology affects ice-shelf stability. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; input/amelia.md)`

The book must distinguish among three related but different conditions:

1. **Sea-ice loss**, which reduces seasonal habitat and affects the food web.
2. **Krill decline**, which follows changes in sea-ice conditions and threatens the base of the Antarctic marine ecosystem.
3. **Larsen C fracture**, an ice-shelf hazard intensified by atmospheric and oceanic warming. Drilling can worsen local instability, but it does not single-handedly cause global collapse. `(rules/decisions.md)`

During the climax, a major fracture opens in Larsen C, but Larsen C does not fully collapse. The team cannot reverse the climate trend. They can stop the drilling that is worsening local instability and transmit the evidence. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Himalayas

The Himalayan setting includes high-altitude glaciers, ice, rock, avalanche terrain, mountain communities, trails, and water systems affected by changing ice conditions.

Sitara's fictional Thakali settlement is in the Kali Gandaki region. Her surviving aunt and cousins remain connected to that community. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The 2026 Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster is a settled historical event in the project:

- A rock–ice avalanche collapsed from the Lirung massif.
- The collapse became a debris flow and entered the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system.
- Floodwater, mud, and rock struck the Nepal–Tibet border at Gyirong Port.
- A 72-kilometre stretch of river was damaged.
- Homes, roads, bridges, power projects, and border facilities were destroyed.
- Sitara's parents died and her home was lost.
- Sitara survived with extended family or relief workers. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Roman's 2042 Himalayan accident is separate. It occurs during a personal trip in Nepal, where changing ice conditions provide a connection to the wider climate story but not to Sitara's 2026 disaster. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The setting includes:

The Colorado outcome must be visible and concrete:

## History and Ecological Pressure

### The climate cascade

The central existential threat is not a single weapon, organism, or villainous machine. It is an ecological cascade:

1. Atmospheric and oceanic warming alter Antarctic sea ice.
2. Reduced sea ice damages krill recruitment and habitat.
3. Krill decline threatens the food web.
4. Ice-shelf instability increases hazards and can accelerate land-ice loss.
5. Reduced Himalayan ice and snow alter downstream water systems.
6. Colorado snowpack and streamflow decline.
7. Water, food, habitat, and infrastructure become more expensive to maintain.
8. Corporate and state actors treat scarce environmental data and adaptation technologies as strategic assets. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The characters can mitigate local damage and prevent some acceleration. They cannot restore Larsen C, reverse climate change, or make the wider cascade disappear. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The Madrid Protocol and Antarctic extraction

The Madrid Protocol prohibits mineral-resource activities in Antarctica. In the story, the operation at Falcon is not a conventional commercial mine and is not legally authorized extraction. It is covert exploratory drilling intended to claim data, access, and future rights before a resource becomes legally exploitable. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; rules/decisions.md)`

CSG disguises the operation as legitimate scientific drilling:

- It operates under a scientific-drilling permit.
- Resource exploration is presented as climate or ice-core work.
- Subcontractors obscure responsibility.
- Manifests are falsified.
- Communications are restricted.
- Weather windows and isolation reduce the chance of inspection.
- Seismic equipment, directional boreholes, pumps, heated shelters, fuel stores, and concealed waste handling support the work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The company is therefore exploiting legal and logistical gray areas rather than behaving like an openly lawless cartoon organization.

### Merritt Station

Merritt Station is an older Antarctic facility inspired by Wilkes Station, in the Wilkes Land region of East Antarctica. It operated approximately from the 1960s through the 1990s and was abandoned for roughly thirty years before partial restoration between 2042 and 2046. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_reference.md)`

Merritt is both a remediation site and a scientific time capsule. Abandonment preserved or concealed:

- Lost research.
- Unpublished studies.
- Physical archives.
- Ice cores.
- Biological samples.
- Legacy instruments.
- Buried passageways.
- Access routes toward subglacial environments.
- Geological and climate records. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md)`

The restored facility provides:

- Laboratories.
- Cold storage.
- Data facilities.
- Research logistics.
- Robotics testing.
- Access to ice cores and subglacial environments.
- A staging point for field work.
- A long-term strategic presence. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_reference.md)`

The current surface may be 3–20 metres above the original station surface because of snow accumulation, drifting, and ice movement. Original corridors may now be offset, crushed, or blocked. Surface scenes may show warped metal corridors, blue-ice intrusion, frozen cables, obsolete equipment, and snow entering through structural fractures. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/refinements.md)`

Exploration proceeds in phases:

1. Surface entry and stabilization.
2. Structural mapping with radar, robots, sensors, and remote imaging.
3. Subsurface exploration through fractured, flooded, or collapsed sections.
4. Carefully controlled subglacial access. `(rules/worldbuilding/merritt_station_hidden_knowledge_reference.md)`

Teams may rappel through damaged shafts toward sealed lower sections. TJ units identify narrow routes around collapsed bulkheads and ice-blocked access points. `(rules/worldbuilding/refinements.md)`

Antarctic operations are formally constrained by the Antarctic Treaty framework and Madrid Protocol. ## Technology

### Hard-science limits

The world uses existing or near-term technology wherever possible. A device does not remove the need for energy, maintenance, skilled operators, permissions, transport, or physical access. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Project 863

**FIXED.** Project 863 uses catalytic chemical recycling to depolymerize selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. It requires:

- Sorting.
- Heat.
- Energy.
- Clean feedstock.
- Controlled processing.
- Handling of residual waste.
- Emissions management. `(rules/decisions.md)`

It cannot process every plastic and cannot replace fossil fuels at global scale. Its practical value is localized waste streams, especially where collection and sorting are already available. It may produce useful feedstock for later manufacturing or fuel synthesis, but it is not a limitless climate solution. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Project 863 is the sole major **L** in the story. Its nearest honest version is catalytic chemical recycling of selected plastics. The licensed step is the story's bounded near-future breakthrough, not permission for other systems to ignore hard-science constraints. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Neural interface

**S.** A lightning strike causes a persistent low-bandwidth neural handshake between Roman's neural implant and TJ. The interface provides status, direction, and urgent sensory signals. It does not allow Roman to read all TJ data, remotely control TJ, or maintain unlimited range. Reliability and bandwidth are limited. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The interface is not telepathy. It is a speculative biological-machine communication channel with constrained information flow. Roman can receive selected machine perception, while TJ receives selected human context, balance, proprioception, intent, and urgency. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_neural_interface_reference.md)`

## Ecological Science and Conservation

### Krill

Nexus's ecological work includes krill research. The surviving krill tank is dead but retains sensor data and sediment samples showing that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The tank is therefore not a living miracle or a replacement ecosystem. It is a damaged archive of a biological failure.

### Extremophile research

Antarctic extremophiles can reveal:

- Cold adaptation.
- Low-energy metabolism.
- Pressure tolerance.
- Chemical stress resistance.
- Waste transformation pathways.
- Protective molecules.
- Potential remediation mechanisms. `(rules/worldbuilding/antarctic_extremophiles_reference.md)`

The same discoveries can support environmental restoration or commercial extraction. The world does not treat “useful” and “ethical” as synonyms.

### Colorado conservation

The Colorado pilot applies bounded processes to localized problems:

- Selected waste plastics are sorted and depolymerized.
- Contaminated water is treated.
- Valuable feedstock or materials are recovered.
- Habitat is monitored.
- Cold-water ecosystems are given improved conditions.
- Results are measured rather than announced as a global cure. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`
