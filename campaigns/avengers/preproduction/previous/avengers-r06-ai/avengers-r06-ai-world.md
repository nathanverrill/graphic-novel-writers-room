## Overview

This is a near-future world in which most life remains recognizable as the present layered forward rather than replaced by futuristic systems. Buildings, laws, research institutions, corporate hierarchies, field equipment, paper archives, satellite links, tracked vehicles, and improvised repairs coexist with emerging robotics, neural interfaces, autonomous sensor systems, and advanced chemical recycling. The hard-science balance is approximately **80% established reality, 15% emerging technology made ordinary, and 5% genuinely speculative material**. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; input/seb.md)`

The world’s central material pressure is the loss or destabilization of frozen water. In Antarctica, declining sea ice threatens krill, while warming and atmospheric processes stress ice shelves. In Colorado, declining snowpack and streamflow threaten cold-water species and conservation systems. These are not separate climate stories: the same loss of ice and snow propagates through food webs, water supplies, logistics, and political decisions. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The most powerful actors are not necessarily governments. Private corporations control capital, logistics, research infrastructure, data access, and employment. A company can provide real environmental benefits while using that legitimate work to gain information, influence, and commercial leverage. Legal compliance therefore does not guarantee public accountability. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_merritt_station_reference.md)`

## Time and Geography

### 2046

The story’s present is 2046. `(rules/rules.md)`

By this date, the world material describes:

- climate-driven pressure on food and water;
- drying rivers and lakes in Colorado;
- declining snowpack and altered runoff;
- severe Antarctic sea-ice loss;
- pressure on krill and other cold-water ecosystems;
- rising ocean levels and glacier instability;
- increasing dependence on remote sensing, autonomous systems, and climate infrastructure. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; input/seb.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The material also describes low food supply and high food prices in Roman’s Colorado setting. This appears in the draft as lived-world background rather than a quantified global condition. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

### Antarctica

The Antarctic setting is the **Larsen region**, especially the area around Larsen C. It includes:

- a snow-covered coastal mountain range;
- floating sea ice and open water;
- glaciers and unstable ice;
- a small community of research bases and homes;
- an active CSG-linked operational base;
- the Falcon drilling and monitoring site;
- the older Nexus laboratory in a nearby coastal cave system;
- tracked-vehicle routes and occasional aircraft links;
- weather-dependent over-ice travel;
- intermittent or delayed satellite communication. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon and Nexus are close enough to be connected by a roughly **two-hour over-ice journey under best-case conditions**, but the route is intermittently relocated, surveyed, or abandoned as crevasses and ice movement change. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The route is not dependable infrastructure. During the emergency, ice movement and weather close it, making return by the usual route impossible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Human habitation is concentrated in accessible coastal research zones. Falcon is inland and operationally isolated. Nexus occupies a nearby coastal cave system. Wildlife appears only where habitat and season permit it; the material emphasizes that little wildlife remains visible around the human community. `(rules/decisions.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The production anchors for the central Antarctic community are:

- the Falcon hatch, control room, and drill zone;
- the Nexus entry shaft, archive, and ecological tank;
- a separate heated community shelter used for evacuation.

Exact dimensions and floor plans remain open. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Larsen system

The Larsen system is divided in the material into:

- **Larsen A**, which collapsed in 1995;
- **Larsen B**, which collapsed in 2002;
- **Larsen C**, fragile, thinning, and vulnerable;
- **Larsen D**. `(input/amelia.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Sea-ice decline, krill decline, and Larsen C failure are related but not identical:

- sea-ice loss changes the ecological conditions supporting krill;
- krill decline affects the Antarctic food web;
- Larsen C is an ice-shelf hazard intensified by atmospheric and oceanic warming;
- local drilling can worsen local instability but does not single-handedly cause global ice-shelf collapse. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The setting uses real operational responses to unstable ice: camps may be relocated, airborne surveys may replace some ground surveys, crews avoid crevasse zones, and ice movement is monitored in real time. Routes are surveyed and may be shifted or abandoned as conditions change. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Himalayas

The Himalayas are both a backstory setting and a continuing emotional and scientific reference point. The project material places Sitara’s family history in a fictional Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region and uses Nepal as the location of Roman’s 2042 accident. `(rules/decisions.md; input/amelia.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

The 2026 Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster is Sitara’s formative event. The research reference describes a rock–ice avalanche becoming a debris flow, entering the Lhende Khola and Trishuli River system, and striking the Nepal–Tibet border at Gyirong Port. It destroyed homes, roads, bridges, power projects, and border facilities across a reported 72-kilometre stretch. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The material reports more than 1,451 deaths in Nepal, more than 43 in Tibet, and more than 6,264 missing across both sides, though these figures remain research-derived background rather than a settled on-page count. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

Roman’s 2042 Himalayan accident is separate from Sitara’s 2026 disaster. It occurs during Roman’s personal trip to Nepal, where changing ice conditions provide a shared regional connection between their histories. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## History

### Antarctic scientific and corporate history

The Antarctic sites were originally framed as legitimate climate, ice-core, ecological, and materials research facilities. CSG later operated two connected functions:

1. **Falcon:** an operational and extraction-monitoring site disguised as scientific drilling.
2. **Nexus:** a research site studying ecological consequences, krill decline, ice data, and waste-to-value chemistry. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nexus was eventually decommissioned after its researchers documented connections among extraction, sea-ice loss, and krill decline. Its power is off, but insulated archives and limited emergency systems remain. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Inside Nexus, the surviving systems are deliberately partial. Air is breathable near open access points. Heat exists only in one archive chamber. Water comes from melted ice and must be treated. Data survives on insulated offline media rather than a functioning network. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Nexus’s ecological program included a krill ecosystem tank. The tank is dead in 2046, but its sensor data and sediment samples preserve evidence that krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The station’s older paper records matter because portions of the research were never fully digitized or were later redacted. Paper archives survive in filing rooms, offices, and insulated storage. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; input/seb.md)`

### CSG’s waste-to-value division

Project 863 began inside a CSG research division. Its purpose was not an unlimited climate cure. It explored catalytic chemical recycling of selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The research developed alongside ecological work rather than replacing it:

- krill and ice records demonstrated the urgency of conservation;
- materials research explored how selected plastic waste might become feedstock;
- Colorado became the intended site for bounded application;
- Antarctic facilities supplied difficult field conditions, waste streams, data, and research access. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

### Illegal exploratory extraction

The Antarctic operation is not yet commercially extracting a named resource. It is conducting illegal exploratory drilling to claim data, access, and future rights before a resource becomes legally exploitable. The resource remains deliberately agnostic in the book. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon is therefore a covert exploration and monitoring installation rather than a conventional mine. Its systems include:

- directional boreholes;
- seismic equipment;
- pumps;
- heated shelters;
- fuel stores;
- concealed waste-handling systems;
- communications and monitoring equipment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

CSG operates under a legitimate scientific-drilling permit and disguises resource exploration as climate and ice-core work. Its concealment methods include subcontractors, falsified manifests, restricted communications, and use of isolated weather windows to evade inspection. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Suppression and secret messages

The initial messages have an unidentified sender. They are recovered from a damaged local storage unit rather than received as a fully reliable live transmission. The messages contain compressed bursts with drilling coordinates, ice-movement data, and warnings that extraction is accelerating Larsen-system instability. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The visual presentation is deliberately austere and partially unreadable. Damaged local storage yields fragmented coordinate packets and timing marks. The warning schedule becomes clear only when compared against Orien’s notes. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Orien Keel’s authorship is established only when his notes reproduce the warning schedule. The warnings are automated and triggered by new drilling or ice-movement data. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Institutions and Corporate Power

### Clear Spring Global

**Clear Spring Global, or CSG,** is the principal corporate institution in the established story world. It links Falcon, Nexus, Project 863, Antarctic logistics, Colorado research, and Benedict Croft’s authority. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

CSG’s functions include:

- corporate energy and extraction interests;
- Antarctic scientific drilling;
- ecological monitoring;
- chemical research;
- waste-to-value development;
- research logistics;
- control of files, facilities, staff, and communications. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

CSG’s incentives are mixed:

- it can fund useful research;
- it can support conservation infrastructure;
- it can develop practical recycling chemistry;
- it can conceal damaging evidence;
- it can treat scientific access as commercial leverage;
- it can prioritize institutional survival and energy-system control over public disclosure. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

The company is not required to behave like a cartoonishly evil organization. Its power comes from ordinary corporate dependencies intensified by remote environments, proprietary data, capital, logistics, and legal gray areas. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

### Benedict Croft’s corporate model

Benedict Croft is the sole principal human antagonist. “Greed” and “bob” are discarded placeholder names for the antagonist function, not separate characters or organizations. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft is a man in his early sixties with white hair. He wears a weathered but expensive field coat over formal clothing. His calm institutional voice repeatedly uses “responsibility,” “stability,” and “timing.” `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft believes controlled suppression is necessary to preserve CSG’s power and the existing energy system. He tells himself that continued corporate dominance will allow him to fund future beneficial work. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft’s power derives from:

- ownership or executive control of CSG;
- money and legal resources;
- relationships and institutional dependencies;
- influence over research employment;
- control of publication channels and corporate records;
- the ability to discredit or isolate researchers. `(input/nemesis-threats.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The corporate system can punish researchers through firing, isolation, loss of access, and reputational attack. Orien’s death is investigated as neglect and corporate retaliation. Evidence establishes Croft’s causal and ethical responsibility, but not that he directly ordered a killing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Croft’s first public response to Roman’s publication concedes that Project 863 exists while claiming that Roman conflated an experimental recycling process with unrelated drilling data and violated confidentiality. This makes the release appear both ethically and technically compromised. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### The nonprofit public-benefit organization

The waste-to-value organization began as a CSG research division and later exists as a nonprofit public-benefit corporation. It is initially funded by CSG but governed by:

Its authority extends to conservation deployments, but not private extraction. It may operate between Antarctic evidence and Colorado pilot programs, but its ownership, financing, and long-term governance remain contested at the end. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The organization’s legal name, exact headquarters, and exact operational scale remain open until the Director approves the institution. Development materials use a temporary descriptive label. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The ending’s governance conflict is specifically among CSG licensing interests, university open-publication interests, conservation representatives seeking watershed reinvestment, and affected Colorado communities seeking voting authority over deployment. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Alpha Dynamics and Merritt Station reference

The Alpha Dynamics documents describe a separate privately held global conglomerate headquartered in Luxembourg and controlled by a secretive founder/CEO. They describe a restored Antarctic facility called Merritt Station in Wilkes Land, East Antarctica, operating as a research, logistics, remediation, robotics, and strategic-access hub. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_merritt_station_reference.md)`

These documents are worldbuilding reference material rather than a binding replacement for CSG, Falcon, or Nexus. They provide a model for how a sophisticated conglomerate can gain influence through:

- restoration;
- environmental remediation;
- logistics;
- early access to scientific data and samples;
- robotics and AI testing;
- off-continent commercialization;
- legal and regulatory compliance combined with private advantage. `(rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_merritt_station_reference.md)`

Merritt Station is separate from the Larsen action. It does not appear physically in this book and has no established ownership or operational connection to CSG, Falcon, or Nexus. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Governance and Law

### Antarctic governance

The Antarctic Treaty framework and Madrid Protocol constrain activity in Antarctica. The Madrid Protocol prohibits mining. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

CSG’s illegal operation depends on disguising exploration as permitted scientific drilling rather than openly rejecting Antarctic law. Its operating pattern is:

1. obtain or use a legitimate scientific-drilling permit;
2. describe operations as climate or ice-core research;
3. route work through subcontractors and restricted communications;
4. falsify manifests;
5. operate during isolated weather windows;
6. keep the extraction purpose inside a compartmentalized corporate program. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Formal inspection and enforcement are difficult because of remoteness, weather, communications delays, and the company’s ability to present compliant paperwork. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The public release triggers:

- a U.S. congressional or executive hearing;
- a national environmental and permitting investigation;
- a formal Antarctic Treaty inspection request.

The book names no single global police authority and does not settle penalties. The ending shows investigation and inspection rather than immediate arrest. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Authority at the field sites

Field authority is divided:

- Nayah has field-command authority and a safety veto.
- Sitara leads cryosphere interpretation.
- Roman leads chemical and corporate evidence.
- TJ handles technical operations under human direction. `(rules/decisions.md)`

During the Larsen fracture, Nayah orders an evacuation and vetoes further human entry. She authorizes Sitara to interpret the final ice data, Roman to transmit the evidence, and TJ to preserve the records remotely. This is the concrete expression of her acceptance that shared expertise, rather than personal supervision, is the only safe command decision. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Access and credentials

Falcon uses machine-authentication systems. TJ can answer the bunker’s machine challenge because it contains a salvaged Falcon authentication module inherited through Bi11bot’s reconstruction. The module is mostly concealed behind TJ’s rib panel; when TJ opens the Falcon hatch, only a worn institutional plate and connector bundle are revealed. The module’s internal construction is reserved for later production design. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Original Nexus parts retain faded institutional markings, while Bi11bot’s repairs are visibly mismatched and hand-fabricated. TJ’s inherited credential does not grant access to every system inside. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The bunker’s physical access system includes:

- a snow-covered submarine-like hatch;
- a Falcon symbol engraved in the metal;
- machine-readable authentication;
- interior computer locks and corporate identifiers. `(input/amelia.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### Information power

CSG’s most important controls are informational:

- redacted files;
- missing database records;
- restricted servers;
- paper archives;
- secret messages;
- compartmentalized sites;
- delayed communications;
- deliberate suppression. `(input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md; rules/decisions.md)`

Exposure becomes effective only when Falcon’s operational records, Nexus research, ice evidence, ecological data, and corporate records corroborate one another. A single scientist’s account can be discredited; a distributed evidence chain is harder to suppress. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Technology

### Hard-science technology policy

Every invented technology carries a label:

- **T — Truth:** documented or already existing;
- **EG — Educated guess:** a plausible straight-line development;
- **S — Speculation:** requires a particular break in real-world conditions;
- **L — License:** a declared departure from known science;
- **Cut:** not admitted unless separately licensed. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The world should preserve ordinary infrastructure around speculative elements. Retrofit, limited range, repair, heat, power, materials, and human procedure remain important. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Neural interface

The Roman–TJ neural connection is **S**. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Its fixed operating rules are:

- the lightning event caused a persistent, low-bandwidth neural handshake;
- Roman receives status, direction, and urgent sensory signals;
- Roman cannot read all of TJ’s data;
- Roman cannot remotely control TJ;
- range is limited;
- reliability is limited;
- the link does not function as unrestricted telepathy or cloud access. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The interface is therefore useful for urgent coordination and shared awareness, but it cannot eliminate communication problems or make either partner omniscient. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### TJ’s unified architecture

TJ is one familiar canine robot dog in this book. Alternate forms and swarm architecture exist in TJ’s wider history but remain off-page reference material. “TJ units” in reference material refers to remote sensors or equipment, not additional embodied TJs in this book. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Bi11bot built TJ from parts salvaged from a decommissioned Nexus support unit. Bi11bot is living but off-page: a scavenger-engineer who rebuilt TJ for autonomous exploration and released TJ into the world. Roman later encountered TJ during his expeditions. Bi11bot does not appear directly. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ’s inherited hardware includes the Falcon authentication module. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The familiar field body retains:

- Himalayan sheep-dog proportions;
- seamless silver paneling;
- a green-and-black screen;
- buttons on the right side or ribs;
- expressive electronic eyes;
- red insulated booties made from jacket material. `(input/amelia.md; input/team1-scott-base-background.md)`

The 249-gram figure belongs to a minimal swarm unit, not the fully equipped canine field body. The familiar canine body is heavier and carries the battery, protection, screen, tools, and inherited authentication hardware required in this book. `(rules/worldbuilding/tj_terrain_jumper_reference.md; rules/worldbuilding/tj_bio_inspired_form_factors_reference.md; rules/decisions.md)`

### TJ’s fabrication and sorting

TJ’s fabrication and AI sorting are **EG**, not unlimited capabilities. `(rules/decisions.md)`

In this book, TJ has hours of ordinary operation. Fabrication consumes a visible portion of its reserve. It can make only hand-sized parts from clean, sorted feedstock and must cool before another sustained fabrication cycle. `(rules/decisions.md)`

TJ can:

- scan available feedstock;
- sort usable materials;
- fabricate small tools and replacement parts;
- support field repair;
- use salvaged metals, plastics, or ice-compatible alloys where suitable. `(input/seb.md; input/team2-mcmurdo-background.md)`

TJ cannot:

- fabricate large machines;
- create arbitrary matter;
- operate without feedstock;
- replace a full workshop;
- work indefinitely without power;
- avoid material contamination and sorting problems. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Its failure modes include:

- overheating;
- contaminated feedstock;
- damaged locomotion;
- signal loss;
- depleted power. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Fixed limitations include:

- small tools and replacement parts only;
- sorted feedstock required;
- time required for fabrication;
- power required;
- cooling required after sustained fabrication;
- no unlimited network access;
- communication through screen, speakers, or Roman’s limited neural channel. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Project 863

Project 863 is **L**. Its rules are fixed:

- it uses catalytic chemical recycling;
- it depolymerizes selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock;
- feedstock must be sorted and clean;
- the process requires heat;
- the process requires energy;
- it cannot process every plastic;
- it produces residual waste;
- it produces emissions;
- it is useful mainly for localized waste streams;
- it cannot replace fossil fuels at global scale. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The process shown in the Colorado pilot is a small batch reactor processing clean PET. A visibly separate contaminated mixed-plastic sample is rejected. Recovered heat preheats the next batch. Emissions monitoring and residual disposal remain visible. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The nearest honest real-world basis includes catalytic or enzymatic plastic recycling, PET depolymerization, AI sorting, industrial heat, and waste-to-value processing. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/antarctic_extremophiles_reference.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The story’s license is not “plastic becomes clean gasoline and climate change ends.” The licensed step is the specific catalytic process that makes selected waste plastics into useful hydrocarbon feedstock at a narratively important but bounded scale. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### AI sorting

AI-driven optical sorting and robotic separation are grounded in existing recycling systems. Research reference describes systems using computer vision and air jets to separate metals and other recyclables, with reported efficiency of up to 90% in a Colorado example. This is real-world reporting, not automatically a 2046 story specification. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

In the book, AI sorting can support:

- separation of ferrous metals from non-ferrous metals;
- removal of contamination;
- preparation of selected plastic feedstock;
- automated recycling facilities;
- localized conservation and waste-to-value pilots. `(references/team1-scott-base-research.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The Antarctic bases are connected by:

- tracked vehicles;
- occasional aircraft;
- weather-dependent over-ice routes;
- intermittent or delayed satellite communications. `(rules/decisions.md)`

A journey that takes approximately two hours under acceptable conditions is a best-case route. Crevasses and ice movement can force relocation, surveying, abandonment, or closure of the route. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The environment requires:

- powered communications;
- fuel stores;
- route monitoring;

## Environment and Ecological Pressure

### Frozen-water cascade

The world’s ecological crisis is driven by linked physical changes:

1. warming alters snow, ice, and sea-ice conditions;
2. altered ice affects habitat and water timing;
3. species dependent on cold-water or sea-ice systems decline;
4. food webs and conservation systems become unstable;
5. human institutions respond unevenly because evidence, capital, and authority are fragmented. `(input/seb.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

### Krill

Krill are treated as a keystone species at the base of the Antarctic food web. Declining sea ice threatens the conditions supporting krill and therefore affects the larger ecosystem. `(input/seb.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

The dead Nexus tank preserves a local record of failed krill reproduction through sensor data and sediment samples. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Colorado cold-water ecosystems

Reduced snowpack and streamflow stress native cold-water fish, including trout. Research reference reports projected mean summer streamflow declines of approximately 52% and loss of physical habitat for Greenback Cutthroat Trout under future scenarios. This is real-world reporting used to ground the setting, not automatically a precise fictional forecast. `(references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

### Larsen C instability

Larsen C’s fracture is a major hazard, but the story must not represent local drilling as the sole cause of global ice-shelf failure. Drilling worsens local instability and helps suppress the evidence needed for response. Atmospheric and oceanic warming remain the larger physical drivers. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Antarctic extremophiles

Antarctic environments may contain:

- psychrophiles;
- halophiles;
- piezophiles;
- acidophiles and alkaliphiles;
- radiation-resistant organisms;
- endoliths;
- algae, fungi, protists, and other microbial eukaryotes. `(rules/worldbuilding/antarctic_extremophiles_reference.md)`

Potentially useful biological properties include:

- cold-active enzymes;
- freeze-protection molecules;
- pressure-stable proteins;
- unusual metabolic pathways;
- chemical transformation;
- low-temperature bioremediation. `(rules/worldbuilding/antarctic_extremophiles_reference.md)`

These are research possibilities, not a second major license. Any use in the story must remain bounded by sampling, analysis, contamination control, energy, time, and scale. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
