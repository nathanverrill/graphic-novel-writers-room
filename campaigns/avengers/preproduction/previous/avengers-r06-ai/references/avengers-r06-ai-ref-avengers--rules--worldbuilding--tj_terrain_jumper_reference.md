# TJ — Terrain Jumper Reference

## Overview

**Name:** TJ  
**Expanded name:** Terrain Jumper  
**Primary role:** Exploration, search and rescue, reconnaissance  
**Core design idea:** Small, fast, modular, bio-inspired autonomous platform  
**Tagline:** Small. Fast. Many Perspectives. Go Farther Together.

TJ is a compact autonomous exploration and rescue platform designed for difficult terrain and extreme environments.

The system is intentionally small and lightweight rather than built as a large single-purpose robot.

TJ combines:

- Ground mobility
- Climbing
- Aerial movement
- Aquatic movement
- Distributed sensing
- Modular tools
- Swarm behavior
- Autonomous coordination

Its design draws inspiration from animals, insects, and distributed biological systems rather than conventional industrial quadruped robots.

---

# Core Functions

TJ is intended for:

- Exploration
- Search and rescue
- Reconnaissance
- Hazard assessment
- Environmental sensing
- Structural inspection
- Scientific sampling
- Extreme-environment access
- Human support in dangerous terrain

TJ is designed to go into places humans cannot safely or easily reach.

---

# Base Form: Scout

## General Form

The primary TJ configuration is a small ground scout.

Approximate characteristics:

- Small-dog scale
- Compact body
- Lightweight construction
- Multi-legged mobility
- Bio-inspired flexible limbs
- Modular sensor head
- Articulated insect-like joints

The scout form is designed to move quickly across broken, uneven, or steep terrain.

---

## Approximate Size

TJ's base scout form is approximately:

- **Shoulder height:** 30–40 cm
- **Body length:** 40–55 cm

This makes TJ significantly smaller than a human and roughly comparable to a small dog.

The compact scale allows TJ to:

- Enter narrow spaces
- Move through damaged structures
- Traverse tunnels
- Access crevasses
- Operate beneath equipment
- Move through ice passages
- Reduce energy requirements
- Reduce mass carried during expeditions

# Swarm Mode

TJ's defining capability is the ability to disassemble into many specialized units when needed.

Instead of remaining a single robot, TJ can become a distributed system.

The swarm can contain multiple specialized forms operating simultaneously.

---

# Why Swarm Mode Exists

A single body has limitations.

A swarm allows TJ to:

- See from multiple locations
- Search multiple routes
- Explore dangerous areas simultaneously
- Maintain communications across complex terrain
- Sacrifice individual units without losing the entire system
- Divide specialized tasks among different units
- Reassemble after exploration

The swarm concept turns TJ from one robot into a coordinated network of machines.

---

# Distributed Perspective

In swarm mode, TJ can gather information from many viewpoints at once.

This creates:

- Distributed perception
- Redundant sensing
- Parallel exploration
- Larger search coverage
- Faster mapping
- Improved hazard detection

The system behaves more like a colony or organism than a single vehicle.

# Design Philosophy

TJ should not simply look like a conventional robot dog.

The design should communicate that it evolved from a different set of engineering assumptions.

Those assumptions include:

- Small units are easier to deploy.
- Multiple perspectives are more valuable than one large sensor platform.
- Individual robotic bodies can be expendable.
- Form should adapt to mission.
- Mobility should borrow from biology.
- A robot can be emotionally legible without being humanoid.
- Distributed systems can outperform one large machine.
- Exploration robots should be capable of reaching places humans cannot.
