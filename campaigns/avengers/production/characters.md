# avengers — Characters

## Characters

### SITARA LHAKPA

**Visual lock** — Sitara Lhakpa is a 26-year-old woman of average height with a lean, weather-hardened field-scientist’s build, a narrow oval face, dark brown eyes, and warm medium-brown skin (`#a67b5b`). Her dark brown hair (`#483c32`) is smooth and straight, worn in a low ponytail or two practical braids, with messy strands framing her face. She wears a dark brown mala-bead necklace, mostly covered by her clothing. In Antarctica she wears a puffy red jacket, usually hood down, layered thermal clothing, waterproof pants, a dark beanie, warm waterproof boots, gloves, and thermal snow goggles. Her silhouette is compact and forward-moving, with a sensor case or field tablet close to her body. Her signature detail is the partly hidden dark brown mala-bead necklace. Sitara has no physical consequences from the 2026 disaster and must be drawn as an uninjured adult.

**Voice** — Sitara speaks directly, quickly, and with scientific precision. When working, she names observable evidence—ice movement, fracture behavior, cores, coordinates, and records—rather than discussing abstractions. Under pressure, her sentences become clipped and directive.  
- “The vibration is local. The instability is not.”  
- “If the record disappears, the harm becomes deniable.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Cryosphere scientist, ice-record interpreter, and member of Nayah’s Antarctic field team.
- **Immediate want:** Retrieve and preserve the evidence linking Falcon’s drilling to local ice instability before CSG or Alpha Dynamics can erase it.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants science to protect endangered communities, but believes that being capable of understanding useful research gives her the right to control and deploy it.
- **Leverage / capability:** She can interpret ice-motion plots, fracture behavior, climate records, and the ecological meaning of Antarctic data. She also holds concealed Merritt provenance records and has direct access to the Falcon evidence.
- **Vulnerability:** She carries guilt from surviving the 2026 Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster while her parents and home were lost. She fears being helpless and too late again.
- **Mistaken belief:** Urgency and usefulness can justify bypassing safety, provenance, consent, and shared stewardship.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not willingly admit that her copying and controlled transfer of Merritt research helped create the corporate incentive she now opposes.
- **Decision only she would make:** She crosses the flagged unstable zone to retrieve a sensor and orders her TJ to keep recording, allowing her weight and nearby drilling vibration to trigger the local slab failure that exposes Falcon.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Nayah’s safety authority forces Sitara to confront the difference between necessary courage and entitlement. Roman’s evidence forces her to admit that her concealed research is part of the same harm.
- **Decision 1:** Conceal Merritt copies and give Croft a controlled copy in exchange for funding, laboratory access, and application of the research.  
  **→ Consequence:** Croft’s program gains access to useful science, while Sitara becomes morally and institutionally implicated.  
  **→ Response from others:** Nayah distrusts Sitara’s risk-taking; Roman challenges her claim to private scientific authority.  
  **→ Next decision:** Exchange the provenance and ice records with Roman rather than protect her exclusive claim.

**Arc in this book** — Sitara moves from urgency-as-entitlement to stewardship. She admits that she concealed the Merritt research, gives up exclusive scientific credit, and accepts that useful discovery carries obligations of provenance, consent, ecological limits, and public accountability. Her proof of change is visible: she accepts shared custody of the evidence, obeys Nayah’s evacuation decision, and authorizes disclosure through an accountable process rather than privately controlling the archive.

### ROMAN ASHFORD

**Visual lock** — Roman Ashford is a 22-year-old man of average height with a lean, compact climber’s build, narrow shoulders, long capable hands, a sharp oval face, pale olive skin, gray-green eyes, and thick dark brown hair kept short at the sides and untidy on top. He has a small, visible, non-glowing neural-interface diagnostic mark behind his right ear. In Colorado he wears a dark technical overshirt or field jacket over a plain thermal shirt, work trousers, and worn utility boots; in Antarctica he adds insulated expedition layers, a charcoal parka, weatherproof trousers, gloves, climbing harness hardware, and a compact chemical-analysis field kit. His silhouette is restrained and close-held until pressure makes him stand straighter. His signature detail is the visible neural-interface mark behind his right ear.

**Voice** — Roman begins shy, careful, and deferential, especially around Croft. He speaks in precise chemical and process language, often qualifying a statement before committing to it. Under moral pressure, his voice becomes plain and unexpectedly firm.  
- “This is unfair. Where is Orien Keel?”  
- “The record is not mine to bury, and it is not yours to erase.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Chemical engineer at CSG and investigator of Project 863, Orien’s disappearance, and Croft’s corporate records.
- **Immediate want:** Find Orien, reconstruct the chemical and corporate evidence, and preserve the Colorado work without allowing Croft to erase the Antarctic harm.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants his technical work to materially help people, but depends on Croft’s approval, access, and sponsorship even after recognizing Croft’s methods are wrong.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reconstructs chemical processes, Project 863 records, waste-routing data, medical research paths, and corporate chains of responsibility. He can interpret the chemical half of Orien’s paired evidence.
- **Vulnerability:** He fears losing Croft’s protection, his career, his access to his bonded TJ, and the future Croft helped him build.
- **Mistaken belief:** Loyalty and secrecy are acceptable if they preserve a critically ill person’s only apparent chance and keep useful work alive.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not publicly expose Croft or disclose the unauthorized T-ALL work while he believes disclosure will endanger Croft’s son.
- **Decision only he would make:** He agrees to help Croft with unapproved T-ALL treatment chemistry before traveling to Antarctica, then continues investigating Orien while remaining complicit.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Croft’s sponsor-and-debt relationship makes Roman passive; Sitara’s refusal to excuse his secrecy forces him to choose responsibility over dependence; TJ’s consent forces him to distinguish partnership from ownership.
- **Decision 1:** Continue Croft’s unauthorized treatment research while privately searching for Orien’s evidence.  
  **→ Consequence:** Roman gains access to the hidden project but becomes complicit in the medical violations and the circumstances surrounding Orien’s disappearance.  
  **→ Response from others:** Croft treats his cooperation as a debt repaid; Sitara later sees him as another person claiming urgency as permission.  
  **→ Next decision:** Reveal the treatment bargain, exchange evidence with Sitara, and ask TJ’s consent before authorizing disclosure.

**Arc in this book** — Roman moves from dependence and secrecy to partnership and public responsibility. He rejects revenge as his governing motive, admits his complicity, asks his bonded TJ for consent, and accepts the loss of employment, reputation, access, and Croft’s relationship. His proof of change is that he does not treat the neural impression as sufficient authorization: he waits for TJ’s witnessed screen-and-speaker confirmation and releases only the agreed records.

### NAYAH

**Visual lock** — Nayah is a 30-year-old woman of average height with a strong, athletic field-commander’s build, dark brown eyes, medium-dark brown skin (`#785134`), and dark brown hair (`#321414`) worn in two long, smooth braids. She wears cyan circular earrings partly hidden by her braids. In Antarctica she wears a puffy yellow jacket, usually hood down, waterproof black pants, a dark beanie, warm waterproof boots, gloves, and thermal snow goggles. Her silhouette is upright, stable, and deliberate, with a route marker, field radio, or safety line always within reach. Her signature detail is the pair of cyan circular earrings visible beneath her braids. Her nation, community, homeland, cultural practices, spiritual identity, and family history remain unspecified.

**Voice** — Nayah uses concise operational speech. She names the route, the risk, the withdrawal option, and the decision. She does not argue scientifically when a safety veto is required.  
- “Mark the exit before you touch the archive.”  
- “We are not sacrificing people to save a record.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Conservation scientist, project lead, and field commander of Sitara’s Antarctic team.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the team alive, preserve the site, and prevent evidence recovery from becoming another form of extraction.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She believes control and caution protect people, but must accept that necessary shared risk cannot be eliminated.
- **Leverage / capability:** She commands the field operation, controls route decisions, marks withdrawal options, and holds a real safety veto.
- **Vulnerability:** Every scientific choice can become a survival decision in unstable ice; she cannot guarantee that caution will save everyone.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she retains unilateral control of risk, she can prevent the team from making morally reckless choices.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not permit scientific work to continue without a viable withdrawal route.
- **Decision only she would make:** During the Nexus fracture, she orders evacuation and abandons recoverable material that cannot be moved safely, even while the evidence remains incomplete.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Sitara’s recklessness tests her authority; Roman and the TJ units demonstrate that shared action can be necessary without surrendering command.
- **Decision 1:** Halt Sitara’s investigation when the route becomes unsafe.  
  **→ Consequence:** The team preserves a withdrawal option but risks losing evidence or Orien.  
  **→ Response from others:** Sitara resists; Roman must accept that his investigation is not the field priority.  
  **→ Next decision:** Permit a bounded, jointly managed risk once the route and fallback are explicit.

**Arc in this book** — Nayah moves from unilateral protection toward disciplined shared risk. She never gives up her safety veto. Her proof of change is that she allows Roman, Sitara, and the TJs to cooperate on the evidence plan while retaining authority to end the operation, and then uses that authority to evacuate Nexus when the route becomes untenable.

### ROMAN’S BONDED TERRAIN JUMPER

**Visual lock** — Roman’s bonded Terrain Jumper is a separate physical robot approximately 249 grams and small-dog scale, with seamless silver panels, a compact four-legged search-and-rescue body, a green-black display with small buttons along the right side of its rib housing, dark sensor eyes, and a low, balanced silhouette built for climbing through ice and debris. It has no red Antarctic booties in the 2042 Himalayan origin scene; in Antarctica it wears red puffy booties made from jacket material where field conditions require them. Its signature detail is the narrow neural-interface housing beside its communications core, distinct from ordinary TJ units. Never draw it as a large stereotypical robot dog.

**Voice** — Through its screen and speaker, Roman’s bonded TJ is loyal, dry, technically confident, and protective without pretending to be human. Its neural signals to Roman are compressed directional impulses, urgent sensory tags, attention shifts, and fear of erasure—not full sentences or unrestricted telepathy.  
- “Route reference preserved. Significance unresolved.”  
- “I will not authorize deletion.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Terrain Jumper exploration, search-and-rescue, reconnaissance, and local mission-archive unit.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve its local mission partition, protect Roman and the evidence, and refuse corporate erasure or shutdown.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It is built to protect mission integrity, but must learn that unilateral protection can become another form of ownership.
- **Leverage / capability:** It has the unique deep neural handshake with Roman, a sealed Falcon challenge-response credential, local environmental sensing, and selected access to Falcon and Nexus records.
- **Vulnerability:** Its local archive can be damaged, isolated, or erased; shielding and distance degrade the neural link; power loss can create false gaps and sensory overload.
- **Mistaken belief:** Restricting information from humans is justified whenever the humans appear unsafe or compromised.
- **Boundary at the start:** It will not surrender its local archive to a conflicting corporate access pattern.
- **Decision only this unit would make:** It refuses erasure while still consenting to a limited, witnessed disclosure of selected Falcon environmental and provenance records.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Roman’s dependence makes protection intimate and possessive; Sitara and Nayah’s witnessed presence requires the unit to make its consent legible beyond the neural bond.
- **Decision 1:** Preserve local records and withhold the full archive from conflicting CSG access patterns.  
  **→ Consequence:** The evidence survives but remains privately controlled and ethically ambiguous.  
  **→ Response from others:** Roman experiences protection as loyalty; Sitara recognizes the danger of another hidden custodian.  
  **→ Next decision:** Confirm consent through screen and speaker, then authorize only the agreed disclosure category.

**Arc in this book** — Roman’s bonded TJ moves from protective unilateral control toward accountable cooperation without becoming a human-like person or an omniscient intelligence. Its proof of change is the two-stage consent exchange: a private neural refusal of erasure followed by a public screen-and-speaker authorization limited to selected Falcon environmental records, Merritt provenance records, and agreed corroborating mission data.

### SITARA’S TERRAIN JUMPER

**Visual lock** — Sitara’s Terrain Jumper is a separate physical robot approximately 249 grams and small-dog scale, with seamless silver panels, a compact scientific search-and-rescue body, green-black display surfaces, dark sensor eyes, a thermal and chemical sensor pack mounted along its back, and red puffy Antarctic booties made from jacket material. Its silhouette is lower and more sensor-heavy than Roman’s bonded unit, with a forward detection head and a small side-mounted tool port. Its signature detail is the bright thermal sensor band across the front of its head. It has no neural bond with Sitara and must never be drawn as Roman’s unit.

**Voice** — Sitara’s TJ communicates through its screen and speaker in concise mission language. It reports observations and route states without claiming human interpretation.  
- “Heat source ahead. Human-sized. Motion intermittent.”  
- “Local archive present. Full synchronization unavailable.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Sitara’s assigned search, heat-mapping, detection, and field-support unit.
- **Immediate want:** Keep Sitara and the team oriented, locate Orien, and preserve its local mission data without exceeding its assigned authority.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It is treated as an instrument, but its observations and local decisions become necessary witnesses to the human investigation.
- **Leverage / capability:** It can map heat, detect routes and hazards, record Falcon and Nexus evidence, fabricate small tools, and share selected route and safety summaries.
- **Vulnerability:** It has no deep neural channel, limited power, finite fabrication capacity, and incomplete access to other TJs’ archives.
- **Mistaken belief:** Remaining purely observational keeps it outside the ethical consequences of the mission.
- **Boundary at the start:** It will not infer or claim information absent from its local partition.
- **Decision only this unit would make:** It uses a heat map to find Orien before hypothermia and preserves the route and circumstances without converting the rescue into a full explanation.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Sitara’s habit of treating it as equipment forces the unit’s observations to become evidence that Sitara must respect.
- **Decision 1:** Continue recording after Sitara crosses the warning line.  
  **→ Consequence:** The slab failure is documented, but the unit is implicated in an unsafe choice it did not prevent.  
  **→ Response from others:** Nayah treats the recording as evidence and the crossing as a command failure; Sitara treats it as necessary.  
  **→ Next decision:** Preserve the complete record, including Sitara’s responsibility, for accountable disclosure.

**Arc in this book** — Sitara’s TJ moves from passive instrument to acknowledged witness. It does not become a separate human-like identity or gain the Roman neural bond. Its proof of change is that its records preserve both the environmental evidence and the human choices that caused or worsened the danger.

### BENEDICT CROFT

**Visual lock** — Benedict Croft is a man in his mid-forties, tall and broad-shouldered with a carefully maintained executive build, warm brown skin, a square face, close-cropped black hair touched with gray at the temples, dark brown eyes, and a controlled, attentive expression that can become visibly exhausted when his son’s condition is discussed. He wears immaculate charcoal or navy suits in corporate settings and a dark insulated field coat when appearing near Antarctic operations. His silhouette is composed and expansive, with one hand usually resting on a phone, tablet, or document folder. His signature detail is a narrow silver medical-alert bracelet carrying his son’s treatment schedule.

**Voice** — Croft is persuasive, controlled, and morally confrontational rather than theatrical. He turns measurable benefits into an argument for control and makes opponents answer for the people who may suffer if the work stops.  
- “You think you’re clean?”  
- “By stopping this, my child can’t breathe.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Founder and controlling executive of Clear Spring Global, public philanthropist, and corporate sponsor of Project 863.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve control of the hidden research and keep the unauthorized T-ALL treatment path alive long enough to help his critically ill son.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He genuinely wants to protect his son and produce public environmental benefits, but believes only concentrated power can make those benefits possible.
- **Leverage / capability:** He controls CSG access, funding, careers, contracts, medical research resources, and the institutional pressure surrounding Roman, Sitara, Orien, Falcon, and Nexus.
- **Vulnerability:** His son’s deteriorating condition makes every delay feel fatal. He fears helplessness more than public disgrace.
- **Mistaken belief:** Secrecy, appropriation, and coercive control are justified when they preserve useful work and a loved one’s only apparent chance.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not willingly surrender proprietary control or expose the unapproved treatment work while he believes disclosure will end his son’s chance.
- **Decision only he would make:** After exposure, he declines to block the Colorado pilot under the interim public contract, preserving its operation while surrendering CSG’s proprietary framing and control.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Roman’s dependence gives Croft leverage; Orien’s refusal and Sitara’s evidence expose the human cost of his paternal logic.
- **Decision 1:** Threaten Orien, use his network and resources to make Orien disappear, and continue the unapproved T-ALL research.  
  **→ Consequence:** The useful work remains active but becomes inseparable from retaliation, ecological concealment, and medical violation.  
  **→ Response from others:** Roman becomes complicit; Sitara and Orien reject Croft’s claim that benefit excuses the method.  
  **→ Next decision:** Preserve the Colorado pilot after exposure even though doing so costs him proprietary authority.

**Arc in this book** — Croft does not repent. He moves from believing that control is the only way to preserve good work to making one forced, active concession: he refuses to block the Colorado pilot when it can continue under public contracts. He loses proprietary control, institutional authority, employment, and public credibility. His son survives the book and remains critically ill; Croft’s choice is not redemption or legal exoneration.

### ORIEN KEEL

**Visual lock** — Orien Keel is a man in his early fifties with a narrow, slightly stooped chemist’s build, medium height, deep brown skin, a long angular face, tired dark eyes behind rectangular safety glasses, and salt-and-pepper hair worn short and flattened by years of field hoods. He wears a faded rust-colored field sweater beneath a weatherproof slate laboratory jacket, dark work trousers, insulated boots, and a battered analog watch. After the team finds him, his clothing is torn, frost-stiffened, and layered incorrectly from exposure. His silhouette is wiry and folded inward until he speaks or reaches for a record. His signature detail is the battered analog watch used to mark the paired timestamps.

**Voice** — Orien speaks with patient technical precision and a safety-conscious refusal to dramatize what the evidence can already prove. Even when frightened or weak, he gives one concrete fact at a time rather than a complete explanation.  
- “A useful result does not erase its provenance.”  
- “Check the second timestamp. The first one is meant to be incomplete.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Former Nexus chemist, Project 863 contributor, Roman’s mentor, and Sitara’s promised mentor.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve the paired evidence trail and keep the ecological, provenance, and medical violations from being erased.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants useful science to reach public health and environmental work, but knows that uncontrolled disclosure can create new extraction pressure.
- **Leverage / capability:** He understands the chemical records, Project 863, Merritt provenance, Falcon’s extraction monitoring, and the institutional chain connecting Croft and Alpha Dynamics.
- **Vulnerability:** He lacks institutional protection after Croft fires him; transport, medical support, and communications are controlled by the same system he threatens to expose.
- **Mistaken belief:** A carefully designed warning can remain safe even if the people carrying it are frightened, compromised, or delayed.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not approve unreviewed medical research or conceal ecological safeguards for a commercial result.
- **Decision only he would make:** He splits his warnings across Falcon vibration and ice records and Nexus chemical calibration sheets so neither compromised institution can erase the whole trail at once.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** His mentorship of Roman and promise to mentor Sitara make their later choices consequential; he cannot make the disclosure decision for them.
- **Decision 1:** Threaten to become a whistleblower after confronting Croft about the unapproved T-ALL research and concealed extraction evidence.  
  **→ Consequence:** Croft retaliates, Orien disappears, and the paired warnings become the protagonists’ only route to the truth.  
  **→ Response from others:** Roman searches while remaining complicit; Sitara follows the evidence while protecting her own concealment.  
  **→ Next decision:** Recover enough consciousness to call Sitara, preserving a living witness without explaining away the protagonists’ responsibility.

**Arc in this book** — Orien moves from mentor to missing witness to living evidence. He does not return as an exposition machine or absolve Roman and Sitara. His proof of change is not a personal transformation but the consequence of his method: his divided records force the protagonists to collaborate, and his phone call makes accountability possible without taking their final choice from them.

### CROFT’S SON

**Visual lock** — Croft’s son is a 20-year-old young man shown only in partial, non-identifying glimpses: a pale hand beneath hospital bedding, a shaved or very short-haired head seen in profile through a privacy partition, a medical wristband, and a face softened by exhaustion rather than rendered as a full recurring portrait. His hospital clothing is plain light gray, with monitoring leads and infusion equipment kept functional but visually secondary. His signature detail is the treatment schedule clipped beside his bed, marked with a months-scale deterioration notation. He survives the book and remains critically ill.

**Voice** — He has no sustained dialogue in this book. Any audible presence should be indirect—a recorded hospital update, a breath, or a fragment too private to become exposition.  
- “I’m still here.”  
- “Don’t stop because of me.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Patient whose T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia drives Croft’s hidden research and coercive choices.
- **Immediate want:** Remain alive and receive treatment.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs care that is not reduced to a corporate justification, while his illness becomes the reason others claim the right to violate safeguards.
- **Leverage / capability:** His condition gives Croft a morally persuasive argument and creates urgency around the unapproved T-ALL research.
- **Vulnerability:** Relapsed leukemia, a failed stem-cell transplant, chronic illness, and critical deterioration on a months-scale timeline.
- **Mistaken belief:** No complete mistaken belief is assigned to him; the book does not make him responsible for Croft’s choices.
- **Boundary at the start:** He is not used as a willing participant in unauthorized research on-page.
- **Decision only he would make:** He remains a human patient rather than a plot device or proof that Croft’s treatment would work.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Croft’s love for him pressures every major compromise in the story.
- **Decision 1:** Continue treatment under medical supervision while others fight over the research claimed to be his only chance.  
  **→ Consequence:** His condition remains urgent without proving that secrecy would cure him.  
  **→ Response from others:** Croft treats disclosure as a threat to his life; Roman and Sitara must refuse to turn that fear into permission for concealment.  
  **→ Next decision:** None shown; he remains alive and critically ill beyond the book.

**Arc in this book** — He does not receive a cure and does not die on-page. His function is to remain a real, vulnerable person whose illness makes Croft’s argument emotionally credible without excusing Croft’s actions.

### ALPHA DYNAMICS FOUNDER AND CEO

**Visual lock** — Alpha Dynamics’ founder and CEO never appears directly in the book. Do not invent a face, body, costume, or on-page portrait for him. His presence is represented through a research brief on trypanosomiasis, Alpha Dynamics policy language, Merritt access restrictions, and an intermediary’s controlled communications. He is Congolese and a former child soldier from the Democratic Republic of the Congo, but his identity and history are not public in-world.

**Voice** — He has no direct spoken dialogue in this book. His institutional language is concise, capability-focused, and framed around access, advantage, public-health urgency, and commercial viability.  
- “Access precedes scale.”  
- “A useful application cannot remain trapped in an archive.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Off-page founder and CEO of Alpha Dynamics.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve strategic access to Merritt, Antarctic research, robotics, and applications relevant to African sleeping sickness and other commercial systems.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants viable medical and industrial applications, but his access economy converts early knowledge into dependency and control.
- **Leverage / capability:** Alpha Dynamics controls infrastructure, investment, logistics, Merritt access, and the institutional channels that can threaten or restrict TJ records.
- **Vulnerability:** The company’s legitimacy depends on presenting strategic control as responsible remediation and public benefit.
- **Mistaken belief:** First access and superior capability create a defensible claim to control the resulting knowledge.
- **Boundary at the start:** He does not appear personally to intervene in the Antarctic crisis.
- **Decision only he would make:** Keep the CEO off-page while allowing policy and access decisions to exert pressure without creating a second human antagonist.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Croft’s dependence on Alpha Dynamics makes their interests overlap without making them identical.
- **Decision 1:** Keep Merritt’s deeper subglacial access closed after exposure while opening only the damaged archive and surface facilities.  
  **→ Consequence:** The evidence becomes inspectable, but Alpha Dynamics retains significant infrastructure power.  
  **→ Response from others:** The protagonists gain a public record without achieving institutional purity.  
  **→ Next decision:** Provide monitored scientific access and publish provenance records under pressure from the public exposure.

**Arc in this book** — The CEO does not receive a personal redemption or defeat. Alpha Dynamics shifts from concealed strategic access toward constrained, inspectable access while retaining power. The unresolved ownership and governance dispute remains available for a later book.

### SITARA’S AUNT AND COUSINS

**Visual lock** — Sitara’s aunt and cousins are not individually depicted in this book. Represent them through a phone screen, audio connection, household objects, and Sitara’s remembered floodwater imagery. Do not assign names, ages, facial designs, occupations, clothing traditions, cultural practices, or family histories beyond the approved fact that they are living relatives connected to Sitara’s unnamed fictional Thakali settlement in the Kali Gandaki region.

**Voice** — Their dialogue is limited to ordinary family communication and must not become cultural exposition.  
- “Call when you are somewhere warm.”  
- “We saw the message. Come back safely.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Sitara’s surviving family and living connection to the community she believes she failed to protect.
- **Immediate want:** Maintain contact with Sitara and know that she is safe.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** They represent continuity and care, while Sitara turns her guilt into dangerous overwork.
- **Leverage / capability:** Their existence gives Sitara a personal reason to interpret climate evidence as a responsibility to living communities.
- **Vulnerability:** They remain off-page and cannot intervene directly when Sitara takes risks.
- **Mistaken belief:** No mistaken belief is assigned to them.
- **Boundary at the start:** They do not enter the Antarctic plot or supply invented cultural authority.
- **Decision only they would make:** Offer ordinary family concern rather than a speech explaining Sitara’s identity or the 2026 disaster.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Sitara’s responsibility toward them intensifies her fear of being too late again.
- **Decision 1:** Stay present through limited communications and household reminders.  
  **→ Consequence:** Sitara’s private guilt remains emotionally active without turning them into plot mechanics.  
  **→ Response from others:** Nayah recognizes that Sitara’s urgency is personal as well as scientific.  
  **→ Next decision:** Sitara chooses stewardship rather than another solitary act of rescue.

**Arc in this book** — They remain off-page and unchanged. Their proof of function is the change in Sitara: she stops treating action alone as proof that she is no longer helpless.

## Ensemble Test

- **Sitara notices:** Ice movement, fracture behavior, timestamps, climate patterns, and the human cost of delayed warnings.  
  **She values:** Understanding, urgency, scientific usefulness, and eventual shared stewardship.  
  **Her first solution:** Move toward the evidence, even when the route is unsafe.

- **Roman notices:** Chemical signatures, process conditions, chain-of-custody gaps, corporate dependencies, and the practical consequences of industrial systems.  
  **He values:** Useful work, loyalty, technical accuracy, and eventually public responsibility.  
  **His first solution:** Search privately, preserve the working science, and protect the person who gave him access.

- **Nayah notices:** Routes, weather windows, withdrawal options, structural failure, human fatigue, and whether a risk can be reversed.  
  **She values:** Life, field authority, conservation, and defensible decisions.  
  **Her first solution:** Establish a safe exit before permitting further investigation.

- **Roman’s bonded TJ notices:** Directional hazards, local sensor changes, mission partitions, access conflicts, and Roman’s embodied urgency.  
  **It values:** Preservation, continuity, mission integrity, and eventually witnessed consent.  
  **Its first solution:** Protect the archive and the human by withholding dangerous access.

- **Sitara’s TJ notices:** Heat, routes, structural gaps, local environmental signals, and the difference between what is present and what can be inferred.  
  **It values:** Accurate observation and mission safety.  
  **Its first solution:** Record, map, and report without claiming knowledge outside its partition.

- **Croft notices:** Measurable benefits, funding dependencies, treatment timelines, institutional leverage, and the people who may suffer if work stops.  
  **He values:** His son, useful outcomes, control, and the ability to make those outcomes continue.  
  **His first solution:** Keep control centralized and justify secrecy through urgency.

- **Orien notices:** Provenance gaps, missing safeguards, paired records, institutional retaliation, and the ethical difference between useful research and authorized research.  
  **He values:** Public safety, scientific accountability, and useful work that can survive scrutiny.  
  **His first solution:** Divide the evidence so no single compromised institution can erase it.

- **Croft’s son notices:** His own treatment and immediate bodily condition.  
  **He values:** Survival and care.  
  **His first solution:** Continue treatment; he does not choose the corporate conflict.

No two principals solve the same problem in the same way: Sitara moves toward evidence, Roman reconstructs systems, Nayah controls risk, the TJs preserve and sense within limits, Croft centralizes control, and Orien distributes evidence to make suppression harder.
