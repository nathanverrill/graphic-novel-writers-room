# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Canon — premise and intended experience.** The book is a crossover: Sitara Lhakpa and Nayah’s Falcon-base story and Roman Ashford’s Nexus story are both true in the same world and must converge rather than replace one another. The story is set in 2046 across Antarctica, the Himalayas as backstory, and Colorado. `(rules/avengers.md; rules/rules.md)`

The intended experience is a hard-science graphic novel about climate evidence, corporate power, scientific stewardship, waste-to-value research, and the question of whether useful knowledge may be controlled by whoever extracts it first. The story should make the reader see both the damage and the measurable good: Antarctic sea-ice loss, krill decline, unstable Larsen ice, Colorado water stress, recovered waste, cleaner water, and returning aquatic life. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; input/seb.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can doing measurable good justify violating scientific stewardship, consent, and ownership?

A related question connects the Antarctic mystery to Roman and TJ’s relationship:

> Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?

These questions are present in the current draft and story notes, but the final book should answer them through choices rather than speeches. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

The protagonists are **Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford together**, with equal plot weight, agency, and presence. When the structure requires choosing a scene lead, the story alternates between them. Nayah, Roman, TJ units, and the supporting characters must contribute necessary capabilities rather than turn either protagonist into a sidekick. `(rules/protagonists.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Premise

**Canon — core threat.** Antarctic sea-ice loss and Larsen-system instability are producing a combined ecological cascade. Sea-ice loss undermines krill reproduction and therefore the food web; atmospheric and oceanic warming intensify Larsen C’s fracture risk. Illegal extraction and information suppression are the human mechanisms preventing an adequate response. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md; references/team2-mcmurdo-research.md)`

CSG’s Antarctic program contains two linked sites:

- **Falcon** is the covert operational and extraction-monitoring site. It presents illegal exploratory drilling as permitted climate and ice-core work.
- **Nexus** is the research site connected to the same broader program. It studied ecological consequences, krill and ice data, and bounded waste-to-value responses.

Alpha Dynamics owns or controls the infrastructure underlying Falcon and Nexus. CSG operates the sites through contractual and research subsidiaries. CSG carries the visible responsibility for the operations; Alpha Dynamics retains strategic control, access, and the ability to shape what happens to the infrastructure and research. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/worldbuilding/alpha_dynamics_corporate_reference.md)`

The waste-to-value work began as a CSG research division. Nexus was its scientific laboratory; Falcon was CSG’s covert extraction and monitoring facility operating on infrastructure controlled by Alpha Dynamics. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Falcon is not yet commercially extracting an Antarctic resource. It conducts directional drilling, seismic monitoring, pumping, heated-shelter operations, fuel storage, and concealed waste handling in order to claim data, access, and future rights before a resource becomes legally exploitable. Its permit authorizes scientific drilling and environmental monitoring only. The violation is the concealed resource survey, unauthorized directional boreholes, falsified manifests, concealed waste, and preparation of future extraction rights; the story does not call the activity lawful mining. `(rules/decisions.md; references/team1-scott-base-research.md)`

The central research is **Project 863**, which uses catalytic chemical recycling to depolymerize selected waste plastics into hydrocarbon feedstock. It requires sorting, heat, energy, and clean feedstock; produces residual waste and emissions; cannot process all plastics; and is useful mainly for localized waste streams rather than replacing fossil fuels. Project 863 is the story’s sole major **L**. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

Nexus’s cold-active enzyme research lowers the temperature and energy required for a bounded pretreatment step. The actual depolymerization remains the licensed process. The Colorado application demonstrates value without becoming a universal cure. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/extremophiles-colorado.md)`

## 1. Structure

**Format:** 24-page single-issue chapter.

### Act I — Hidden extraction, divided evidence  
**Pages 1–8**

- **Hook, Pages 1–2:** Sitara’s reckless choice exposes Falcon. A brief Roman origin interstitial and Sitara’s disaster memory establish the shared ice-and-water history.
- **Plot Turn 1, Pages 3–5:** Falcon opens. Sitara and Nayah discover illegal exploratory drilling and the first half of Orien’s paired evidence.
- **Pinch 1, Pages 6–8:** Roman’s compromise with Croft and his discovery of the Nexus route are intercut with Falcon’s evacuation. Roman visibly chooses to travel south.

**Act I turn:** Separate evidence trails point to the same Antarctic site. Both protagonists have already accepted secrecy in the name of useful work.

### Act II — Convergence, complicity, and choice  
**Pages 9–20**

- **Midpoint, Pages 9–12:** Roman, Sitara, and Nayah converge at Nexus. The fracture forces cooperation; the paired records align; Sitara and Roman exchange their concealed evidence.
- **Pinch 2, Pages 13–16:** The Colorado success is shown as real and limited. The protagonists understand that its value created pressure to exploit Antarctica. They find Orien alive and confront their own complicity.
- **Plot Turn 2, Pages 17–20:** Orien calls. Alpha Dynamics threatens the TJ records. Roman’s TJ gives witnessed, limited consent to disclosure. The team acts to preserve the evidence and stop Falcon through authenticated reporting and local inspection control.

**Act II turn:** The protagonists stop trying to possess or privately protect the truth. They choose accountable disclosure, knowing it will cost them careers, control, and Croft’s son’s unapproved treatment path.

### Act III — Constrained victory, contested stewardship  
**Pages 21–24**

- **Resolution, Pages 21–22:** The evidence reaches accountable custody. Colorado’s pilot continues under inspection and an interim public contract.
- **New balance, Pages 23–24:** Croft actively declines to block the pilot despite losing proprietary control. A public meeting leaves ownership and financing unresolved. The work is visible and inspectable, not solved.

**Final turn:** The book ends on a question of stewardship and agency, not legal punishment or climate reversal.

## 3. Page plot

### Page 1 — Hook: Two memories, one pressure

Brief interstitial of 2042 Roman trapped in Himalayan ice as lightning crosses the bonded TJ’s interface; communication is a compressed directional pulse, not telepathy. Cut to six-year-old Sitara’s 2026 flood memory: household objects carried through muddy water. Present-day Sitara, Nayah, and Sitara’s separate red-booted TJ monitor Antarctic ice movement. The warning flags an unstable zone. **Page change:** past helplessness and present danger become linked.

### Page 2 — Hook: The choice

Sitara crosses the flagged zone for a sensor despite Nayah’s safety veto and orders TJ to keep recording. Drilling vibration travels through the ice. The slab fails under Sitara’s weight and the vibration, exposing a buried metal hatch while the sensor and equipment slide toward the break. **Page-turn reveal:** the hatch bears the Falcon mark and active vibration continues below.

### Page 3 — Plot Turn 1: The machine challenge

At the exposed hatch, Nayah establishes a withdrawal route. Sitara argues that the active vibration means the site is not abandoned. TJ approaches and answers Falcon’s rotating challenge-response credential. The hatch opens only to a local maintenance interface. **Page change:** a field accident becomes an authorized entry into a concealed operation.

### Page 4 — Falcon records

Inside, the team sees directional borehole maps, falsified scientific manifests, concealed waste routes, vibration logs, and ice-motion plots. A warning fragment is physically inserted into a Falcon maintenance record, marked with the first half of Orien’s timestamp-coordinate code. **Page change:** the drilling is not merely unsafe; it is operating outside its permit.

### Page 5 — First half / forced departure

Sitara aligns the vibration graph with the ice-motion plot and understands that Falcon is worsening local instability without causing all global climate change. Nayah orders withdrawal as the route shifts. Sitara copies the selected records rather than taking physical samples. **Page-turn reveal:** a Nexus coordinate appears beside the incomplete code.

### Page 6 — Roman’s compromise

In Colorado, Roman examines Orien’s paper archive and a treatment schedule marked with a months-scale deterioration window. Croft shows him the unapproved T-ALL work and argues that stopping now ends his son’s only chance. Roman’s bonded TJ gives a limited status pulse from the local mission partition. Roman signs onto the work while continuing to search Orien’s files. **Page change:** Roman chooses secrecy before he knows the full cost.

### Page 7 — The missing route

Roman finds a physical record identifying Nexus and a less-redacted Project 863 sheet. Intercut with Orien’s queued warning, a denied transport request, and Croft’s threat to make him disappear through his network. Roman asks his TJ about a preserved route; TJ answers only that it preserved the route reference, not its meaning. **Page change:** Roman has a destination and a reason to distrust the official record.

### Page 8 — Roman chooses Antarctica

Roman visibly closes the Colorado treatment file, requests Antarctic transport, and boards a separate tracked vehicle. A route display establishes Falcon and Nexus as two distinct sites, approximately two hours apart when weather permits. Sitara and Nayah depart Falcon by their own route while Roman’s vehicle enters the weather window from Colorado’s Antarctic logistics chain. **Page-turn reveal:** two route lines converge on Nexus; the teams are not yet together.

### Page 9 — Nexus entrance

The teams arrive at different access points around the coastal cave laboratory and converge inside under Nayah’s route rules. Warped corridors, blue ice, frozen cables, and dead equipment establish Nexus. Roman identifies Orien’s chemical notation; Sitara identifies the ecological archive. **Page change:** both protagonists recognize that the other has been following a legitimate half of the same trail.

### Page 10 — The fracture

A Larsen-linked fracture shears the support corridor. Roman’s bonded TJ pivots toward a failing section; Roman receives a compressed directional warning and pulls Sitara clear. Nayah redirects everyone toward the marked withdrawal route. Meltwater enters below. **Page-turn reveal:** the dead krill tank’s emergency display flickers on.

### Page 11 — Dead krill, living record

The team sees the empty tank and surviving sensor graph: krill reproduction failed as sea-ice conditions changed. Sitara’s TJ identifies a sealed connector; it fabricates only a small replacement after a visible power-and-time cost. The sensor archive opens. **Page change:** ecological loss becomes measurable evidence.

### Page 12 — The halves align

Roman finds Nexus process and waste records bearing the second half of Orien’s repeated code. Sitara places the Falcon fragment beside it. Timestamp and coordinate align, linking drilling vibration, ice movement, process waste, and the hidden research route. Sitara and Roman exchange their selected archives. **Midpoint reveal:** their separate evidence proves a single corporate chain.

### Page 13 — Mutual exposure

Sitara admits she concealed Merritt research and gave Croft a controlled copy for access and application. Roman admits he agreed to Croft’s unauthorized treatment chemistry and kept investigating after Orien disappeared. Their TJs show separate local partitions; neither has the complete archive. **Page change:** neither protagonist can claim moral innocence or exclusive ownership.

### Page 14 — Colorado success

A documentary evidence sequence shows the Colorado pilot: selected PET/polyolefin enters Project 863; rejected material remains on a separate belt; recovered feedstock exits the line. A monitor records water use, grid demand, emissions, and residual solids. **Page change:** the useful work is real, but every benefit has a material cost.

### Page 15 — The measured benefit

At the downstream station, a readable monitor shows water clarity improving from **41 to 68 clarity units** over the dated pilot interval, while an invertebrate survey rises from **3 to 11 taxa**. A warning states that the constructed wetland is at finite flow capacity. Croft’s recorded hospital call and treatment schedule frame the argument that stopping the hidden work will endanger his son. **Page change:** the protagonists understand why people accepted the system.

### Page 16 — Orien found

Sitara’s heat-mapping TJ searches a damaged lower route and locates Orien alive near hypothermia. Nayah stabilizes the evacuation route while Sitara reaches him. Orien cannot yet explain everything. Sitara places a phone beside him and leaves a simple instruction. **Page change:** the missing witness is alive but fragile.

### Page 17 — The call

Sitara and Roman confront one another beside the records. Sitara demands the truth about Orien; Roman admits Croft’s pressure and his own participation. The phone rings. Orien, barely conscious, confirms that Croft threatened him and that his scheduled warnings were meant to survive his disappearance. He cannot deliver a full exposition. **Page change:** the witness returns just as the protagonists decide whether to expose themselves.

### Page 18 — Witnessed consent

A corporate system message warns that the local TJ archives are scheduled for deletion or shutdown review. Roman receives a low-bandwidth neural impression: refusal, fear of erasure, and a directional insistence on selected records only. He asks TJ aloud. TJ’s screen and speaker confirm consent to disclose: **Falcon environmental records; Merritt provenance records; and the agreed corroborating mission data necessary to authenticate them.** Sitara’s TJ separately marks its own selected Falcon-search and route records for release. No full mission partition synchronizes. **Page-turn reveal:** the authorized evidence package begins transmission.

### Page 19 — Nexus evacuation, Falcon made inspectable

The Nexus corridor floods and begins to refreeze. Nayah orders evacuation and vetoes retrieval of nonessential objects. Roman and Sitara carry the physical archive while the TJs relay selected data. A clearly labeled authenticated remote status feed shows Falcon, two hours away, receiving the evidence package; Nayah’s field authority secures the local control record and flags the active drilling apparatus for inspection. The feed changes to **DRILL CONTROL: SAFE HOLD / INSPECTION REQUIRED**. **Page change:** Falcon is physically interrupted and publicly inspectable without implying Nexus overlooks Falcon.

### Page 20 — Public record and personal cost

The selected records reach an accountable public archive through multiple recipients and mirrored institutional custody. The interface lists each category and authorizing source: Roman’s TJ authorizes its Falcon environmental and provenance-linked mission records; Sitara’s TJ authorizes its own route, sensor, and Falcon-search records; Sitara and Roman authorize their human files. Roman’s employment and CSG access terminate on the public record. **Page change:** disclosure is limited, witnessed, and consequential.

### Page 21 — Inspection before celebration

At Colorado, inspectors examine the rejected feedstock, residual solids, emissions monitors, process-water system, and wetland inlet before the pilot continues. The monitoring wall repeats the clarity and invertebrate metrics while showing finite throughput and current grid demand. **Page change:** public benefit survives only through visible limits and inspection.

### Page 22 — Interim partnership

The pilot operates under an unincorporated interim public contract. A public meeting reads the contract aloud. Scientists, conservation representatives, affected-community participants, and inspectors visibly disagree over ownership, financing, and access. No permanent governance structure is ratified. **Page change:** the work is shared provisionally, not solved institutionally.

### Page 23 — Croft’s final choice

Croft appears through a recorded board and contract exchange, with the hospital schedule visible but no cure implied. He is offered the chance to assert CSG’s proprietary block and stop the interim pilot. He refuses to block it, explicitly allowing the public contract to continue while surrendering CSG’s exclusive framing and control. He does not apologize, confess, or receive exoneration. **Page change:** Croft chooses the pilot’s survival over ownership because it is demonstrably helping people.

### Page 24 — New balance, unresolved question

Sitara and Roman stand outside the Colorado monitoring station as the separate TJs retain their own local mission indicators. Sitara signs the shared provenance record without an exclusive-author line. Roman asks TJ whether it wants the public archive to retain the selected records; TJ’s screen displays **LOCAL MEMORY RETAINED / SHARED RECORD AUTHORIZED**. Behind them, the meeting continues arguing over ownership and financing. **Final question:** if useful knowledge is now public and inspectable, who gets to decide what stewardship means next?

## 5. Reader vs. character knowledge

### Reader knows before the characters

- Roman’s 2042 neural bond is real but bounded.
- Sitara’s 2026 disaster shaped her urgency.
- Falcon is concealed beneath the Antarctic field before Sitara fully understands it.
- Falcon and Nexus are geographically separate.
- The paired records are designed to be incomplete until combined.
- The Colorado pilot genuinely works and has visible limits.
- Croft is responsible for pressure against Orien, though the full evidence is not yet public.
- The TJs are distinct physical units with local partitions.
- Croft’s final choice will preserve the pilot at the cost of proprietary control, but not when or how he will choose it.

### Sitara knows before Roman

- Falcon’s drilling is worsening local ice instability.
- She concealed Merritt research and gave Croft a controlled copy.
- Her TJ holds selected Falcon and provenance records.
- Nayah’s safety veto is necessary even when Sitara believes the evidence is urgent.

### Roman knows before Sitara

- He agreed to unauthorized T-ALL treatment work.
- Croft’s sponsorship and the circumstances around Orien’s disappearance bind him personally.
- Nexus contains the chemical and process half of Orien’s evidence.
- His TJ holds a local archive and can communicate consent through the neural bond, but cannot authorize a full archive by itself.

### Nayah knows before both

- The field route and evacuation window are unsafe.
- Falcon and Nexus require separate travel.
- The team cannot treat evidence preservation as more important than survival.
- Any disclosure must be limited, witnessed, and compatible with field safety.

### TJ knows differently

- Roman’s TJ knows selected local records, route references, and hazard summaries; it does not know the full human meaning of Orien’s investigation.
- Sitara’s TJ knows its own sensor and route records and can separately authorize selected data.
- Neither TJ has unlimited archives or cloud access.
- Roman’s TJ can refuse erasure, but it cannot unilaterally publish the entire mission partition.

### Crucial information changes

- **Page 2:** The field danger has a human cause.
- **Page 5:** The operation is illegal exploratory drilling, not ordinary climate research.
- **Page 12:** Falcon, Nexus, Merritt, and Colorado belong to one evidence chain.
- **Page 13:** Both protagonists are compromised.
- **Page 15:** The harmful system also produces real public benefit.
- **Page 17:** Orien survives and can corroborate Croft’s retaliation.
- **Page 18:** TJ’s consent is necessary and limited.
- **Page 19:** Falcon is stopped or exposed through authenticated records and inspection control, not through a geographically impossible confrontation.
- **Page 23:** Croft chooses to preserve the pilot without regaining control of it.

## 6. Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

### Proposal — public intermediary

The selected evidence reaches accountable custody through a distributed public archive involving the Antarctic inspection channel, a university-linked environmental research office, and the Colorado public-benefit contract administrator. No new named protagonist is introduced. This keeps the recipient institutional and prevents a convenient single savior.

### Proposal — Colorado metrics

Use the readable pilot figures established in the page plot:

- water clarity index: **41 → 68** over the dated pilot interval;
- downstream invertebrate taxa: **3 → 11**;
- wetland throughput visibly marked as finite and currently near capacity.

These are proposed fictional pilot metrics, not real-world claims.

### Proposal — Croft’s exact final action

Croft signs or authorizes a narrowly worded refusal to block the interim public contract and withdraws CSG’s exclusive proprietary claim to the operating frame. He does not donate all intellectual property, confess, repent, or receive legal exoneration. The action preserves the pilot while costing him control.

### Proposal — exact TJ disclosure

The public package contains only:

1. Roman’s TJ: selected Falcon environmental records and Merritt provenance records held in its local mission partition, plus the agreed corroborating route and sensor summaries.
2. Sitara’s TJ: selected Falcon-search, route, and sensor records separately authorized by Sitara’s TJ.
3. Human records: Sitara’s copied Merritt files, Roman’s chemical and corporate records, Orien’s warnings and call, and the authenticated Falcon/Nexus chain.

No full mission partition, private neural telemetry, unrestricted sensory archive, or unrelated TJ data is released.

### Proposal — final image

End on the separate TJ units’ local status displays beside the contested Colorado meeting, rather than on a single protagonist. This preserves the co-lead structure and leaves the reader with the unresolved question of machine agency, public stewardship, and ownership.

### Decision needed

Whether the Director wants the fictional Colorado metrics above retained as canon during the next gate. The page plot uses them because the brief requires at least one readable metric; they remain proposals until approved.
