# avengers — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Canon — premise and intended experience.** The book is a crossover: Sitara Lhakpa and Nayah’s Falcon-base story and Roman Ashford’s Nexus story are both true in the same world and must converge rather than replace one another. The story is set in 2046 across Antarctica, the Himalayas as backstory, and Colorado. `(rules/avengers.md; rules/rules.md)`

The intended experience is a hard-science graphic novel about climate evidence, corporate power, scientific stewardship, waste-to-value research, and the question of whether useful knowledge may be controlled by whoever extracts it first. The story should make the reader see both the damage and the measurable good: Antarctic sea-ice loss, krill decline, unstable Larsen ice, Colorado water stress, recovered waste, cleaner water, and returning aquatic life. `(rules/extremophiles-colorado.md; input/seb.md; input/prahul-notes.md)`

The central dramatic question is:

> Can doing measurable good justify violating scientific stewardship, consent, and ownership?

A related question connects the Antarctic mystery to Roman and TJ’s relationship:

> Who gets to own something simply because they were first to extract it?

These questions are present in the current draft and story notes, but the final book should answer them through choices rather than speeches. `(drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md)`

The protagonists are **Sitara Lhakpa and Roman Ashford together**, with equal plot weight, agency, and presence. When the structure requires choosing a scene lead, the story alternates between them. Nayah, Roman, TJ units, and the supporting characters must contribute necessary capabilities rather than turn either protagonist into a sidekick. `(rules/protagonists.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## 1. Structure

### Act I — Extraction hidden inside legitimate science  
**Pages 1–8: Hook, Falcon, and the paired warning**

Sitara’s decision to cross an unstable zone exposes Falcon. The evidence changes the immediate problem from “why is the ice moving?” to “who is using permitted science to establish illegal extraction rights?” In parallel, Roman’s dependence on Croft and complicity in unapproved treatment research lead him toward Orien’s missing records and Nexus.

**Act I turn: Page 8.** Sitara and Roman’s separate routes converge on Nexus. Neither can safely return to private investigation.

### Act II — Useful science, compromised people  
**Pages 9–18: Nexus, convergence, complicity, and consent**

The Nexus fracture forces the protagonists to cooperate physically before they trust one another morally. Their evidence completes Orien’s paired record: Colorado’s real success is materially connected to concealed Antarctic risk, restricted provenance, and Croft’s unauthorized medical program.

The protagonists must choose whether to preserve evidence publicly, knowing disclosure may end the Colorado pilot and Croft’s son’s treatment path. Roman’s TJ must also choose whether its local records may be disclosed.

**Act II turn: Page 18.** TJ gives witnessed consent to preserve and disclose the agreed records. The team commits to action rather than private possession.

### Act III — Evidence survives; ownership does not resolve  
**Pages 19–22: Evacuation, exposure, Colorado, and contested governance**

The team stops or exposes Falcon’s drilling and evacuates Nexus. The records enter accountable public circulation. Colorado’s pilot continues under inspection, showing real benefit and real limits. The issue ends with a public meeting and unresolved ownership, financing, and TJ personhood questions.

**Final question: Page 22.** If the work now belongs to everyone who must live with its consequences, who gets to decide what happens next?

## 3. Page plot

### Page 1 — Hook: Two unstable histories

Brief Himalayan interstitial: six-year-old Sitara watches floodwater carry household objects away. Present day: adult Sitara and Nayah monitor Antarctic ice while Sitara spots a sensor inside a flagged unstable zone. **Page-turn setup:** Sitara steps over the warning line.

### Page 2 — The exposed Falcon hatch

Sitara retrieves the sensor; drilling vibration travels through the ice. The slab fails beneath her, trapping equipment and exposing a metal hatch marked **FALCON**. Nayah orders withdrawal; TJ turns toward the opening. **Page-turn reveal:** Falcon is active beneath the ice.

### Page 3 — A door that recognizes TJ

The small search-and-rescue TJ scans the hatch and answers its rotating challenge-response credential. The hatch opens only to a local maintenance interface. Sitara chooses entry; Nayah establishes a withdrawal route and safety limit.

### Page 4 — The permitted operation is not the real operation

Inside Falcon, the team finds directional borehole maps, falsified manifests, concealed waste-routing, and drilling vibration records. Visual evidence shows a scientific permit being used to prepare future extraction rights.

### Page 5 — The first half of Orien’s warning

Sitara overlays vibration logs with ice-motion plots. A compressed warning contains one half of a timestamp-and-coordinate code and indicates drilling is intensifying local Larsen-system instability. Nayah orders them out. **Page-turn reveal:** the coordinate points toward Nexus.

### Page 6 — Roman’s bargain

Intercut Colorado material-recovery machinery with Roman searching Orien’s paper files. Croft shows Roman the treatment schedule for his critically ill son and asks him to redirect restricted research toward unapproved T-ALL work. Roman agrees, then finds a physical reference to Nexus.

### Page 7 — Orien’s disappearance

Orien’s scheduled warning remains unsent while his transport request is denied and his credentials vanish. Croft’s pressure is shown through access removal and institutional leverage rather than a speech. Roman finds a less-redacted Project 863 record and realizes Orien hid corroboration in Antarctic archives.

### Page 8 — Converging routes

Sitara’s team and Roman’s team travel across the weather-dependent Antarctic route toward Nexus. Their map traces meet while the weather window closes. Nayah allows Roman to join only under her field authority. **Page-turn reveal:** Nexus is a cave laboratory split by blue ice.

### Page 9 — The dead tank

The teams enter Nexus separately, then encounter one another at the ecological laboratory. A dead krill tank sits beneath emergency lighting; surviving sensors and sediment show failed reproduction as sea-ice conditions changed. Sitara recognizes Roman from their CU Boulder connection.

### Page 10 — The corridor shears

A Larsen-linked fracture propagates beneath Nexus, cutting emergency power and opening a support corridor. Roman’s bonded TJ reacts through a compressed neural alert before the ordinary alarm. Sitara witnesses the bond. The team must move the archive before meltwater floods the lower lab.

### Page 11 — Two TJs, two partitions

Different TJ units map different routes and cannot immediately retrieve one another’s full archives. A TJ fabricates a small replacement connector, overheating in the process. Nayah assigns tasks: Roman handles chemical records; Sitara handles ice and provenance; no one leaves without a route out.

### Page 12 — The records align

Roman finds Project 863 calibration sheets carrying the second half of Orien’s code. Sitara places them beside Falcon’s logs. The timestamps and coordinates align. Each admits the existence of concealed evidence. **Midpoint reveal:** Colorado’s useful process and Falcon’s ecological harm share a hidden chain of access.

### Page 13 — Sitara’s copy

Roman discovers Sitara’s concealed Merritt files: copied research, provenance restrictions, assays, and safeguards. Sitara admits she gave Croft a controlled copy for funding and laboratory access. She did not take organisms, but she did claim control over knowledge she had not earned the right to control.

### Page 14 — Roman’s copy

Sitara finds Roman’s treatment records and Croft’s authorization trail. Roman admits he agreed to the unapproved T-ALL work and continued after Orien disappeared. The two protagonists nearly separate, but the corridor shifts again and makes solitary escape impossible.

### Page 15 — The good result

A record and visual intercut show Colorado’s bounded success: recovered feedstock, clearer water, returning aquatic invertebrates. The same sequence shows rejected material, residual solids, emissions, water use, grid demand, and a throughput warning. The protagonists understand that the success is real—and that it created the incentive to scale Antarctic access. **Page-turn reveal:** Croft’s medical schedule places the feared treatment interruption within a month.

### Page 16 — Orien in the cold

Sitara’s TJ uses heat mapping to locate Orien in a damaged lower route before hypothermia. He is alive but barely responsive. Sitara leaves a phone beside him and returns to the archive route. Nayah refuses to let the rescue become an uncontrolled detour.

### Page 17 — The confrontation and the call

Sitara confronts Roman about his role in Orien’s disappearance and the treatment research. Roman accepts responsibility without claiming Croft’s motives excuse it. The phone beside Orien rings. Orien has regained consciousness and calls Sitara. **Page-turn reveal:** Orien’s voice confirms he survived Croft’s retaliation.

### Page 18 — TJ consents

A corporate system message threatens deletion or disabling of relevant TJ records. Roman first receives a compressed neural refusal and fear of erasure from his bonded TJ. He asks what TJ wants rather than deciding for it. TJ then confirms through screen and speaker, witnessed by Sitara and Nayah, that it consents to disclosure of the agreed records while refusing erasure. The team commits to preservation and public transfer.

### Page 19 — Evacuation

Nayah orders withdrawal as the corridor floods and refreezes. Sitara leaves behind material that cannot be safely recovered. Roman coordinates with his bonded TJ without treating the neural link as command. The team, Orien, and the essential archive reach the surface. **Page-turn reveal:** the Falcon drilling apparatus is stopped or exposed against a widening fracture.

### Page 20 — The record survives

Falcon, Nexus, ice, chemical, medical, and corporate records enter accountable public custody. Roman’s own complicity is included. His employment and access terminate; his reputation and relationship with Croft are lost. Croft’s response reframes the exposure as a threat to his son, but the record makes the concealment impossible to privatize.

### Page 21 — Colorado, measured

At the pilot, recovered feedstock and rejected material share a split composition. A monitoring wall shows improved water clarity alongside water use, power demand, emissions, residual solids, and finite throughput. Downstream, aquatic invertebrates return under observation. The pilot continues, but no one calls it a cure.

### Page 22 — Inspection before agreement

Inspectors examine the pilot and its limits. At the public meeting, the interim partnership contract is read aloud while scientists, conservation representatives, affected communities, and funders disagree over ownership and financing. Sitara and Roman stand among—not above—the participants; the separate TJ units retain their local memory. **Final question:** who decides what responsible use means now that the evidence is public?

## 5. Reader vs character knowledge

### Reader knows earlier than the characters

- Sitara caused or materially worsened the opening slab failure by crossing the flagged zone while drilling vibration was active.
- Falcon is concealed beneath the ice before Sitara fully understands its purpose.
- Orien’s warning is split across Falcon and Nexus.
- Croft’s pressure is responsible for Orien’s disappearance through threats, institutional leverage, and neglect, though the precise chain is initially incomplete.
- Roman and Sitara are approaching the same evidence from opposite directions.
- Roman’s TJ and Sitara’s TJ are separate physical units.
- The Colorado pilot genuinely works, so exposure has a real cost.

### Sitara knows before Roman

- She copied Merritt files and gave Croft a controlled copy.
- The ice-motion records indicate Falcon is worsening local instability.
- Her own urgency caused the Falcon exposure and put the team at risk.

### Roman knows before Sitara

- He agreed to Croft’s unauthorized T-ALL research.
- Orien’s disappearance is tied to Croft’s retaliation.
- The chemical and corporate records point toward Nexus.
- His TJ’s local archive contains evidence that CSG access patterns should not control.

### TJ knows before either protagonist

- The full archive is partitioned across separate units and cannot simply be treated as shared property.
- Roman’s bonded TJ’s records are at risk of erasure.
- The units can cooperate through selected summaries without possessing one another’s complete memory.

### Deliberate convergence

The midpoint makes these differences visible through behavior and records, not exposition. By Page 18, the important knowledge is shared, but consent remains individual: Roman cannot authorize TJ’s memory without TJ’s witnessed agreement.

## 6. Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

- **Proposal — public intermediary:** Use an existing public-contract or inspection channel rather than introducing a named institutional protagonist. The record can reach accountable custody through an authenticated public archive and inspection request.
- **Proposal — Croft’s final choice:** Croft preserves neither exclusive control nor a clean escape. His continued defense of the Colorado work while denying the legitimacy of suppression makes the pilot’s survival politically useful but does not redeem him.
- **Proposal — Orien’s call:** Keep the call short and evidentiary. Orien confirms survival and identifies Croft’s threat and the missing-warning chain; he does not narrate the entire plot.
- **Proposal — final image:** End on the public meeting with the separate TJ units visible near the edge of the room, each physically distinct, while the contract and ownership dispute remain unresolved.
- **Decision needed — exact date and weather window:** The Antarctic sequence should remain in a late-2046 field season; the precise date can be selected during scripting and lettering.
- **Decision needed — Croft and Orien visual designs:** Their physical appearances remain open and should be settled by the Character Designer.
- **Risk:** Too much hospital material could displace the Antarctic survival spine. Keep Croft’s son present through one call, one treatment schedule, and Croft’s choices.
- **Risk:** “Alpha Dynamics threatens TJ deletion” could imply remote control. Stage it as an access and record-erasure threat through corporate systems, not bodily control or automatic shutdown.
- **Risk:** The Colorado sequence could read as a triumphal cure. Keep finite throughput, rejected material, residual solids, emissions, water use, and power demand in the same visual field as the returning invertebrates.
- **Risk:** Orien’s survival could absolve Roman and Sitara. His call must increase accountability, not remove their responsibility.
- **Proposal — canon clarification:** Treat the book’s governing resolution as **public evidence plus constrained application**, not a new governance covenant. Ownership and permanent governance remain contested beyond Page 22.

## Key scenes

### The Ice Warning

Sitara crosses a flagged Antarctic zone to retrieve a sensor despite Nayah’s safety direction. Her weight and Falcon’s drilling vibration trigger the local slab failure.

### The Exposed Falcon Hatch

The broken ice reveals a live covert drilling site. The hatch is the first physical proof that the ecological danger has an institutional cause.

### TJ Opens Falcon

The small TJ uses inherited challenge-response hardware to open only the hatch and maintenance interface, demonstrating usefulness without granting magical access.

### The Paired Warning

Falcon vibration and ice-motion records contain half of Orien’s code. The warning establishes local drilling culpability without falsely making Falcon the cause of global climate change.

### Roman’s Compromise

Croft uses his son’s critical illness and Roman’s dependence to recruit Roman into unapproved treatment research. Roman accepts, making his later exposure consequential.

### Orien’s Empty Route

Orien’s warnings remain queued while his credentials, transport, and institutional support disappear. His disappearance is shown as coercive systems and neglect, not as a melodramatic abduction.

### The Converging Route

Sitara’s field team and Roman’s investigation arrive at Nexus through separate routes. Nayah’s authority governs the convergence.

### The Dead Krill Tank

The empty tank and surviving sensor graph make ecological loss concrete. The image should carry more information than an explanatory speech.

### Nexus Splits Open

A Larsen-linked fracture shears the support corridor. Meltwater, emergency power, blue ice, and archive movement turn the investigation into a survival problem.

### The Two Halves Align

Falcon’s ecological records and Nexus’s chemical records lock together through Orien’s repeated timestamp-and-coordinate code.

### The Two Compromises

Roman discovers Sitara’s concealed Merritt files. Sitara discovers Roman’s treatment work. Their collaboration begins only after both surrender the protection of secrecy.

### Colorado’s Real Success

The process produces recovered feedstock, clearer water, and returning aquatic life. Rejected waste, emissions, water use, residual solids, and power demand remain visible beside the benefit.

### Orien Is Found

Sitara’s TJ locates Orien before hypothermia. She leaves him a phone, creating a fragile route for his testimony without turning him into an exposition device.

### The Phone Call

Orien calls during the Roman–Sitara confrontation. His survival raises the stakes and confirms that the corporate story can be corroborated.

### TJ’s Consent

Roman receives a private, compressed neural refusal, then waits for TJ’s public screen-and-speaker confirmation. Consent is witnessed and limited to the agreed records.

### Evacuation

Nayah orders withdrawal. Sitara leaves material behind. Roman coordinates with TJ without commanding its private memory. People and essential records survive; the climate crisis does not disappear.

### The Public Record

The protagonists release evidence that includes their own wrongdoing. The victory is accountability, not innocence.

### The Colorado Inspection

Inspectors examine both the pilot’s benefit and its finite capacity. The inspection prevents the public meeting from becoming a purely rhetorical ending.

### The Contested Meeting

The interim contract is read aloud while ownership and financing remain disputed. No institution occupies the head of the table. Separate TJ units remain present as witnesses and unresolved legal subjects.
