# Antagonist — Benedict Croft

Croft is not a rich evil guy. Being rich does not mean being evil. He is a business man —
no chemical background, a Stanford MBA — with a public philanthropist's profile, and he is
caring in ways that are real. His open questions are in input/open-items.md.

## What is decided

**The guilt.** Roman's father died because of a favour Croft executed. Croft carries that
guilt; it is why he funded Roman (the Aspen mountain club / AspenX sponsorship came through
Roman's father's friend — Croft) and why CSG supplied Roman's TJ on the Nepal expedition.
To Roman, Croft reads as the man who saved his life.

**The son.** Croft's son has T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL): diagnosed at 17,
relapsed at 18, chronic at 19, now 20 and in critical condition after a failed stem-cell
transplant. Deterioration is a matter of months, not years — a race against time. The only
hope is in the Merritt extremophile research: Antarctic fungal L-asparaginase (a real
leukemia drug class) and meriolin derivatives (Antarctic-sponge-derived molecules with
laboratory activity against resistant T-cell leukemia). This is Croft's engine as an
antagonist: everything he diverts, hides and rationalizes serves his dying son.

**The defeat dialogue.** When Croft is finally beaten, he does not repent — he indicts:
"You think you're clean?" "You have blood on your hands." "By stopping this, my child
can't breathe."

## How it sequences across the arc

1. **Plot Turn 1 — trust.** Sitara, believing she rescued abandoned research rather than
   stole it, brings the Merritt material to Croft: CSG has the resources, Croft has the
   charm and the mission. Relations start good. Separately, Croft recognizes what the
   extremophile work could mean for his son and quietly diverts scarce resources and
   samples toward the leukemia angle.
2. **Pinch 1 — the favour.** Croft calls in the favour with Roman: work the enzyme
   chemistry for the cure, off the books, speedrun it. Roman dislikes how much runs under
   the radar, but he knows the cost of losing a family member and doesn't want anyone else
   to face it — so he agrees and works in secret, at the cost of his own Antarctic research
   time. Meanwhile Orien notices CSG "breakthroughs" mirroring unpublished Merritt work.
3. **Pinch 1 → Midpoint — the souring.** As the mirroring surfaces, Sitara grows
   suspicious of Croft's intentions and their relations sour. Her ambition finds another
   outlet: Alpha Dynamics, where the same extremophile science points at African sleeping
   sickness (T. brucei), a crisis growing with climate change — a cure would make her
   career and give her the spotlight. Who wouldn't want that?
4. **Orien.** Orien Keel, CSG's chief architect for public safety, threatens to blow the
   whistle. In the heat of the moment Croft has him contained in a safe hiding spot — not
   killed. But Orien saw trouble coming: before he was taken he split his evidence into
   paired clues that require Roman's and Sitara's secrets together to read.

## Science notes (hard-SF grounding)

- T-ALL: multiply relapsed/refractory T-ALL after allogeneic stem-cell transplant has a
  reported median overall survival around 12.8 months; months-scale deterioration is
  plausible.
- L-asparaginase is already a crucial ALL drug; researchers have isolated Antarctic fungal
  producers (Pseudogymnoascus strains) with low off-target glutaminase/urease activity.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428764/
- Meriolin derivatives (from variolins, originally extracted from the Antarctic sponge
  Kirkpatrickia variolosa) trigger mitochondrial apoptosis in Jurkat T-cell leukemia
  models, including Bcl-2-overexpressing and imatinib-resistant lines.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41420-024-01901-y
- An Antarctic fungus shows anti-Trypanosoma activity in lab tests — the Alpha Dynamics /
  sleeping-sickness thread.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S187439002501403X
