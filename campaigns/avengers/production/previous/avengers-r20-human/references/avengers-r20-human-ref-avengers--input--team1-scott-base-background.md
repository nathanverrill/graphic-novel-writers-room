**Biological Adaptations:** Evolutionary biologists have found that Sherpas possess unique genetic traits, including the *EPAS1* gene variant. Their bodies use oxygen with remarkable efficiency at cellular levels, producing higher energy (ATP) per oxygen molecule while keeping blood flow smooth without dangerously thickening the blood.

## **Naming Traditions & Daily Life**

Sherpa society traditionally relied on yak herding, high-pass trade with Tibet, and cold-climate agriculture.

* **Day-of-the-Week Naming:** Children are traditionally given a first name corresponding to the day of the week they were born:  
  * **Dawa** (Monday), **Mingma** (Tuesday), **Lhakpa** (Wednesday), **Phurba** (Thursday), **Pasang** (Friday), **Pemba**(Saturday), **Nima** (Sunday).  
  * Rarest days to be born  
* **Diet:** Staple foods include *tsampa* (roasted barley flour), *thukpa* (noodle soup), fermented *yak butter tea* (*suja*), and potatoes—which revolutionized the local food economy after their introduction in the 19th century.

## **Cultural Context**

"Lhakpa" is a Tibetan name commonly given to boys born on **Wednesday**, corresponding to the fourth day in the Tibetan calendar, and is associated with **Mercury**. The name carries meanings of **strength, courage, and resilience**, reflecting values important in Tibetan culture and Buddhism. It is widely used in regions such as the Tibetan Plateau, Nepal, Bhutan, and parts of northern India.
