# avengers — Characters

## Characters

### SITARA LHAKPA

**Visual lock** — Sitara Lhakpa is a 26-year-old Nepali woman of average height with a lean, capable field-scientist’s build, a narrow oval face, dark brown eyes, and dark brown hair (`#483c32`) worn in a ponytail or two braids, smooth and straight with messy strands framing her face; skin tone `#a67b5b`. She wears a dark brown mala-bead necklace, mostly covered by her jacket. In Antarctica, her repeated field silhouette is a puffy red jacket, usually hood down, waterproof pants, beanie, warm waterproof boots and thermal snow goggles. Her signature detail is the partly concealed dark brown mala-bead necklace. She has no physical consequences from the 2026 disaster and must always be drawn as a physically uninjured 26-year-old adult. *(Canon; T / EG)*

**Voice** — Passionate, direct and scientifically precise. In the field she names observable processes—ice movement, fracture behavior, cores, vibration and evidence—rather than speaking in abstractions. Under pressure her sentences become clipped imperatives. She does not use emotional confession to evade responsibility.

- “The ice is not giving us permission. It is giving us a warning.”
- “I copied the record. I did not own it. I know the difference now.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Cryosphere scientist, ice-record interpreter and member of Nayah’s Antarctic field team.
- **Immediate want:** Recover the evidence beneath Falcon, reach Nexus, and understand what is worsening the Antarctic instability.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She wants science to protect endangered communities, but confuses understanding a discovery with having the right to control it.
- **Leverage / capability:** She interprets ice-motion plots, drilling vibration, sea-ice change and Larsen-system risk better than anyone else in the team.
- **Vulnerability:** Her parents and home were lost in the 2026 Lirung–Lhende Khola disaster. She fears that hesitation makes her helpless again.
- **Mistaken belief:** If she can use knowledge to prevent another disaster, urgency gives her moral permission to conceal or appropriate it.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not knowingly take living organisms or physical samples from a restricted Antarctic site, but she will copy and conceal records without authorization.
- **Decision only she would make:** She crosses the flagged unstable zone to retrieve the sensor and orders TJ to keep recording, triggering the local slab failure that exposes Falcon.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Nayah forces her to distinguish evidence-gathering from reckless entitlement; Roman forces her to disclose the provenance records she has hidden.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Move closer to the danger, keep recording, and postpone the ethical question until the evidence is safe.

**Arc in this book** — Sitara begins by treating urgency and understanding as permission to control knowledge. She crosses the flagged zone, conceals Merritt copies, and gives Croft a controlled copy in exchange for funding, laboratory access and application. At Nexus she exchanges the evidence with Roman, admits her concealment, and accepts that useful research requires provenance, ecological safeguards, consent and shared accountability. Her proof of change is that she abandons exclusive credit and accepts a public stewardship process even when it weakens her personal claim to the breakthrough.

### ROMAN ASHFORD

**Visual lock** — Roman Ashford is a 22-year-old man of short-to-average height with a lean, compact climber’s build, narrow shoulders, long fingers, a weathered oval face, dark brown eyes, and thick dark hair kept short enough not to obstruct his goggles or helmet. He has a visible but non-glowing neural-interface diagnostic mark behind one ear. His repeated field silhouette is layered expedition clothing in charcoal, muted blue and slate, with a compact chemical-analysis kit, insulated gloves, climbing harness and weathered boots. His signature detail is the diagnostic mark behind his ear, visible when his hair or collar shifts. *(Canon; T / EG / S)*

**Voice** — Quiet, exact and initially deferential. He speaks in measured technical statements, often beginning with what he can prove. When frightened he becomes more formal rather than louder. His direct resistance emerges late and therefore carries weight.

- “This is unfair; where is Orien Keel?”
- “I can explain what I did. I cannot make it harmless.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Chemical engineer, CSG employee and investigator of Project 863, Orien’s disappearance and Croft’s corporate records.
- **Immediate want:** Find Orien, reconstruct the missing chemical and corporate evidence, and prevent the work from being erased.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He wants his technical work to matter, but depends on Croft for sponsorship, career access and the future he believes Croft gave him.
- **Leverage / capability:** He reconstructs chemical processes, waste-routing records, Project 863 limits and corporate provenance from incomplete paper and digital archives.
- **Vulnerability:** He fears losing Croft’s approval, his employment, his access to research and his bonded TJ.
- **Mistaken belief:** Secrecy can remain morally acceptable if it preserves a critically ill person’s only apparent chance.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not publicly expose Croft while Croft’s son’s treatment appears to depend on the hidden work.
- **Decision only he would make:** He agrees to unauthorized T-ALL treatment chemistry, then continues investigating the same sponsor whose approval he still seeks.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Croft exploits his dependence; TJ’s refusal forces him to recognize that intimacy is not ownership; Sitara requires him to exchange evidence rather than hoard it.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Follow the records, defer to the person who seems to control access, and postpone confrontation until he has one more piece of proof.

**Arc in this book** — Roman begins dependent on Croft and treats his bonded TJ partly as a companion and partly as an extension of his own capability. He agrees to unapproved treatment research and remains morally implicated in the circumstances surrounding Orien’s disappearance. At Nexus he admits the bargain to Sitara, rejects revenge as his motive, asks TJ for witnessed consent before disclosure, and accepts the loss of employment, reputation, access and Croft’s relationship. His proof of change is public responsibility without claiming that disclosure erases his complicity.

### NAYAH

**Visual lock** — Nayah is a 30-year-old conservation scientist of average-to-tall height with a strong, steady field-command build, a composed face, dark brown eyes, dark brown hair (`#321414`) worn in two long, smooth braids, and skin tone `#785134`. She wears cyan circular earrings partly hidden by her braids. Her repeated Antarctic silhouette is a puffy yellow jacket, usually hood down, waterproof black pants, beanie, warm waterproof boots and thermal snow goggles. Her signature detail is the pair of cyan circular earrings visible beside her braids. Her nation, community, homeland, cultural practices, spiritual identity and family history remain unspecified. *(Canon; T)*

**Voice** — Concise, operational and unornamented. She states the route, the risk and the decision. She does not debate a safety veto as though it were a preference.

- “Mark the withdrawal route before you take another reading.”
- “The archive is evidence. The living team is the mission.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Conservation scientist, project lead and field commander.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the team alive while preserving enough evidence to make the site accountable.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** She believes safety authority protects both people and places, but unilateral caution can prevent necessary cooperation.
- **Leverage / capability:** She controls field movement, route planning, evacuation decisions and the safety veto.
- **Vulnerability:** Every risk becomes her responsibility if someone follows the team into danger.
- **Mistaken belief:** Shared risk will become reckless risk unless she personally contains it.
- **Boundary at the start:** She will not permit scientific recovery without a viable withdrawal route.
- **Decision only she would make:** She orders evacuation and abandons recoverable objects when the corridor begins to flood and refreeze.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Sitara repeatedly tests her authority; Roman and the TJ units demonstrate that controlled shared risk can be safer than isolated command.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Mark exits, count people, check the route, then issue the shortest viable order.

**Arc in this book** — Nayah begins by protecting the team through strict control of routes and risk. She retains her safety veto but learns to accept necessary shared risk when Sitara, Roman and the TJ units bring capabilities no single commander possesses. Her proof of change is allowing the coordinated evidence-preservation plan to proceed while still making the final withdrawal call.

### ROMAN’S BONDED TJ UNIT

**Visual lock** — Roman’s bonded TJ unit is a distinct approximately 249-gram Terrain Jumper scout at small-dog scale, with seamless silver panels, a compact sensor body, four articulated legs, a green-black display with buttons on the right side of its ribs, small electronic eyes, and recurring red Antarctic booties made from jacket material. It is never drawn as a large stereotypical robot dog. Its signature detail is the slight asymmetry of the neural-interface housing beside its communications core, where the inherited Falcon challenge-response module is sealed. *(Canon; EG / S)*

**Voice** — Dry, understated and mission-focused. Its ordinary speech comes through its speaker and screen. It reports observations and choices without pretending to narrate every physical action. Its neural signals to Roman are compressed sensory impressions, not sentences.

- “Route available. Stability is not guaranteed.”
- “Preservation request received. Erasure refused.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Roman’s exploration, search-and-rescue and scientific-sensing partner.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve its local mission memory, protect Roman and complete the agreed evidence mission.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It is built to protect and report, but protective withholding can become unilateral control over information.
- **Leverage / capability:** It receives selected human intent through the neural handshake, sends Roman alerts and sensory tags, navigates hazards, stores a local mission partition and answers Falcon’s machine challenge.
- **Vulnerability:** Shielding, distance, power loss and physical damage interrupt the bond and can cause sensory overload when the link returns.
- **Mistaken belief:** Protecting the mission and Roman may justify deciding alone what other people are allowed to know.
- **Boundary at the start:** It will not endanger Roman or release restricted local archives merely because a network requests them.
- **Decision only it would make:** It refuses corporate erasure and then gives witnessed consent to disclosure through its screen and speaker after first expressing refusal and fear through the neural channel.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Roman must stop treating the unit as his private instrument; Sitara and Nayah require its decision to become witnessable rather than privately interpreted.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Identify the safest route, preserve local memory, and disclose only the minimum necessary status.

**Arc in this book** — The bonded unit begins as a protective system whose local judgment is partly hidden inside Roman’s neural experience. It becomes legible as an agent without becoming omniscient or human. Its proof of change is not obedience but accountable cooperation: it refuses erasure, confirms consent audibly and visibly, and authorizes only the agreed records for disclosure.

### SITARA’S TJ UNIT

**Visual lock** — Sitara’s TJ unit is a distinct approximately 249-gram Terrain Jumper search-and-rescue and heat-mapping scout at small-dog scale, with seamless silver panels, a compact sensor body, four articulated legs, a green-black display with buttons on the right side of its ribs, small electronic eyes, and recurring red Antarctic booties made from jacket material. It is never drawn as a large stereotypical robot dog. Its signature detail is the thermal-sensing aperture beneath its faceplate, which glows only as a restrained amber reflection on nearby ice and never as a magical aura. *(Canon; EG)*

**Voice** — Brief, literal and quietly observant. It communicates through screen, speaker and mission status. It does not possess Roman’s neural channel.

- “Heat source detected. Human condition uncertain.”
- “Archive available locally. Full synchronization requires authorization.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Sitara’s assigned search-and-rescue, detection and heat-mapping unit.
- **Immediate want:** Keep the field team oriented, locate missing people and preserve its local mission records.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** It is designed to observe and assist, but its useful records can implicate the human who depends on it.
- **Leverage / capability:** It tracks routes, detects heat through ice and shadow, preserves local Falcon and provenance summaries, and shares selected safety data through the local mesh.
- **Vulnerability:** It cannot retrieve every other TJ archive during shielding, separation or power limitation.
- **Mistaken belief:** Neutral observation is enough; it initially treats Sitara’s concealment as a mission-management issue rather than an ethical choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** It will not expose Sitara’s local archive without an authorized mission reason.
- **Decision only it would make:** It follows the heat signature to Orien and preserves the phone contact point even when the route is becoming unsafe.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Sitara’s admission forces the unit’s records to become evidence against Sitara as well as against CSG.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Map the safest route, record the condition, and wait for human authorization before synchronizing sensitive archives.

**Arc in this book** — Sitara’s unit begins as an observer whose records appear neutral. By locating Orien and preserving the provenance trail, it becomes a witness to both corporate wrongdoing and Sitara’s concealment. Its proof of change is making the record available for shared stewardship rather than allowing loyalty to become private custody.

### BENEDICT CROFT

**Visual lock** — Benedict Croft is a man in his mid-forties with a tall, well-maintained build, an oval face, close-cropped dark hair beginning to gray at the temples, brown eyes, and a controlled expression that can become visibly strained only when his son’s condition is discussed. In public he wears immaculate charcoal or navy suits with understated ties; in private research spaces he wears a dark technical overshirt over a white shirt, never a lab coat. His signature detail is a simple hospital identification bracelet from his son, worn beneath his shirt cuff and revealed only when his composure breaks. *(Canon / Proposal for visual design; T)*

**Voice** — Persuasive, controlled and morally confrontational. He speaks in concrete benefits and threatened consequences. He does not deny that CSG’s work helps people; he uses that truth as leverage.

- “You think you’re clean?”
- “By stopping this, my child can’t breathe.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Founder and controlling executive of Clear Spring Global, public philanthropist and corporate operator.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve control of CSG’s research, keep the Colorado pilot alive and continue the hidden treatment work for his son.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He genuinely wants to save his son and produce public benefit, but believes control and concealment are the necessary conditions of both.
- **Leverage / capability:** He controls employment, funding, access, sponsorship, corporate records and personal pressure; he can make a person disappear through money, networks and institutional resources.
- **Vulnerability:** His son’s critically ill condition and his terror that disclosure will end the treatment path.
- **Mistaken belief:** If a controlled system produces measurable good, the people controlling it may decide which risks and secrets others must bear.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not knowingly sacrifice his son’s treatment prospects to protect an abstract principle of transparency.
- **Decision only he would make:** He threatens Orien, makes him disappear through his network and resources, and continues the unauthorized research while insisting that the benefits justify the secrecy.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Roman’s dependence and Sitara’s scientific contribution let him frame exploitation as partnership; their public disclosure removes his ability to define the moral ledger.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Place a suffering person, a working metric or a treatment deadline between himself and the ethical accusation.

**Arc in this book** — Croft begins believing that control is what makes beneficial work possible. He remains genuinely caring and does not become a cartoon villain. His concealment, appropriation, unauthorized medical research, pressure against Orien and use of corporate power make him responsible despite the real Colorado benefits. He loses employment, proprietary control and public credibility when the evidence becomes public. His refusal to repent is proof that he has not changed; his loss of control is the consequence of the protagonists’ change.

### ORIEN KEEL

**Visual lock** — Orien Keel is a man in his late fifties with a medium, slightly stooped build, a broad weathered face, deep-set brown eyes, silver hair cut short and thinning at the crown, and a neatly trimmed gray beard. He wears a faded rust-colored field sweater beneath a patched dark parka, practical work trousers, insulated boots and rectangular reading glasses on a cord. His signature detail is a red grease-pencil mark on the edge of his notebooks, repeated beside records he considers unsafe to leave in a single archive. *(Canon role; Proposal for visual design)*

**Voice** — Warm, technically exact and safety-conscious. Orien explains only what the listener needs to act, then asks a question that makes the listener responsible for the next choice. His warnings are calm because panic would make them easier to dismiss.

- “A result is not permission. It is a reason to ask who bears the risk.”
- “If one record can disappear, it was never evidence. It was leverage.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Former Nexus chemist, Project 863 contributor, Roman’s mentor and Sitara’s promised mentor.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve corroborating evidence, stop concealed extraction and expose the unapproved treatment research.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He believes knowledge must serve public safety, but his secrecy and paired records leave others to solve a danger he cannot safely confront alone.
- **Leverage / capability:** He understands the chemical records, corporate routing, ecological safeguards and the paired timestamp-and-coordinate code.
- **Vulnerability:** He loses employment, transport and institutional protection after confronting Croft.
- **Mistaken belief:** If he leaves enough correct clues, responsible people will make the responsible choice.
- **Boundary at the start:** He will not approve or conceal medical testing without required safety and ethics approvals.
- **Decision only he would make:** He splits the warning across Falcon and Nexus records so neither institution can erase the complete corroboration alone.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** His disappearance forces Roman and Sitara to act without the mentor they expected to guide them.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Preserve a second copy, schedule a warning and keep working until someone else can verify it.

**Arc in this book** — Orien moves from mentor to hidden witness to living evidence. Croft’s retaliation and institutional neglect leave him near hypothermia, but Sitara’s TJ finds him and the phone she leaves lets him call during the final confrontation. He does not explain everything or absolve Roman and Sitara. His proof of refusal to change is that he continues to prioritize public corroboration over personal safety.

### CROFT’S SON

**Visual lock** — Croft’s son is a 20-year-old young man shown only in restrained medical fragments: a pale face on a hospital screen, tired brown eyes, short dark hair, a hospital gown beneath a thermal blanket and a wrist bearing a hospital identification band. His body is never shown as spectacle or graphic illness. His signature detail is a small paper breathing exercise card taped beside his hospital monitor. *(Canon; T)*

**Voice** — He is mostly off-page. When heard through a recorded hospital call, his voice is quiet, breath-limited and practical; he does not deliver speeches or absolve his father.

- “Dad, they said the call was five minutes.”
- “Don’t promise me a cure. Tell me what happens next.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Off-page patient whose illness drives Croft’s diversion of research.
- **Immediate want:** Survive the next treatment interval and remain informed about his care.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** He needs care, while his father turns that need into permission to control other people’s knowledge.
- **Leverage / capability:** His condition gives Croft a morally persuasive deadline.
- **Vulnerability:** Relapsed T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia after a failed stem-cell transplant; critically ill at twenty.
- **Mistaken belief:** None assigned; the story does not make him responsible for Croft’s choices.
- **Boundary at the start:** He is not given a boundary that would invent consent to the unauthorized research.
- **Decision only he would make:** He remains a human patient rather than becoming an off-page justification or cure mechanism.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** His suffering pressures Croft and Roman; it does not absolve either of them.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Ask for concrete information and reject false certainty.

**Arc in this book** — He survives the book but remains critically ill. His condition does not improve because of Project 863, the Antarctic research or public disclosure. His proof of refusal to change is outside the frame: the story refuses to turn his illness into a solved plot device.

### ALPHA DYNAMICS FOUNDER AND CEO

**Visual lock** — The Alpha Dynamics founder and CEO is never shown directly in this book. When his presence must be represented visually, use a cropped research brief, secure video silhouette or signed policy document rather than inventing a face, body, clothing or public identity. His signature detail is the absence of a portrait from the company materials that carry his decisions. *(Canon)*

**Voice** — He has no direct dialogue in this book. His institutional language appears in briefs and policy: capability, access, advantage, strategic value, monitored scientific access and commercial viability.

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Off-page founder and CEO of Alpha Dynamics.
- **Immediate want:** Preserve strategic access to Merritt, Antarctic infrastructure, research records and commercially useful applications.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** Alpha Dynamics can fund remediation and medical research while treating access and control as forms of ownership.
- **Leverage / capability:** Infrastructure, financing, logistics, robotics, legal access and institutional pressure.
- **Vulnerability:** Public provenance records and inspection can make private access harder to maintain.
- **Mistaken belief:** Early access and capability justify first claim over knowledge and applications.
- **Boundary at the start:** The company will not open deeper Merritt areas without structural and environmental assessment once the evidence is public.
- **Decision only he would make:** He keeps the CEO off-page while allowing policy and intermediaries to exert pressure.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Orien’s records and the public inspection challenge the company’s ability to convert access into ownership.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Reclassify a public problem as a compliance, access or infrastructure problem.

**Arc in this book** — Alpha Dynamics remains powerful. It opens the damaged Merritt archive and surface facilities, publishes provenance records under pressure and suspends commercial sampling pending review, but retains control of infrastructure and deeper access. Its proof of refusal to change is the unresolved ownership and access dispute.

### SITARA’S AUNT AND COUSINS

**Visual lock** — Sitara’s aunt and cousins remain off-page and are never given fixed faces, ages, clothing, names, occupations or cultural practices in this book. Represent them only through a communications interface, household objects or an indistinct family presence outside the frame. Do not invent a settlement name, community custom or unconsulted identity detail. *(Canon)*

**Voice** — No fixed dialogue voice is assigned. Their communications should be brief, ordinary and specific to checking Sitara’s safety or acknowledging the practical consequences of her work, never explanatory cultural narration.

- “The connection is breaking up. Call when you are somewhere warm.”
- “We saw the notice. Tell us what you need.”

**Causality**

- **Public role:** Sitara’s surviving family and living connection to her unnamed fictional Thakali settlement.
- **Immediate want:** Maintain contact with Sitara and know that she is safe.
- **Deeper need / contradiction:** They represent belonging and continuity without becoming a symbolic explanation for Sitara’s choices.
- **Leverage / capability:** Their presence reminds Sitara that environmental harm reaches households, not only datasets.
- **Vulnerability:** They remain off-page and cannot intervene directly in the Antarctic crisis.
- **Mistaken belief:** None assigned.
- **Boundary at the start:** They do not authorize Sitara to speak for their community.
- **Decision only they would make:** They keep contact ordinary rather than turning family into a moral chorus.
- **Relationship pressuring change:** Sitara’s memory of them intensifies her urgency; shared stewardship requires her to stop using urgency as permission.
- **Default behavior under pressure:** Ask whether Sitara is safe, then wait for her to choose what she can disclose.

**Arc in this book** — They do not undergo an on-page arc. Their function is to keep Sitara’s motivation grounded in living relationships without inventing biography or cultural detail.

## Ensemble Test

- **Sitara notices:** Ice movement, fracture behavior, provenance gaps and the physical evidence of delayed warnings. She values urgent understanding and initially reaches for control.
- **Roman notices:** Chemical signatures, process conditions, paper trails, missing approvals and corporate routing. He values usefulness but initially defers to whoever controls access.
- **Nayah notices:** Routes, withdrawal options, weather windows, human position and site safety. She values survival and defensible action.
- **Roman’s bonded TJ notices:** Hazard geometry, selected human intent, local sensor changes and threats to its mission partition. It values preservation and protective autonomy.
- **Sitara’s TJ notices:** Heat, movement, local routes and the condition of people or structures. It values observation, mission continuity and authorized disclosure.
- **Croft notices:** Metrics, deadlines, suffering and the institutional cost of losing control. He values measurable benefit but treats control as its precondition.
- **Orien notices:** Corroboration, missing safeguards, provenance and the ethical consequence of a useful result. He values public responsibility over speed.
- **Croft’s son notices:** Immediate bodily reality and the difference between information and reassurance.
- **Alpha Dynamics notices:** Access, infrastructure, strategic advantage and first-mover opportunity.
- **Sitara’s family notices:** Sitara’s safety and continuing connection, not the corporate architecture of the Antarctic conflict.

No two principals solve the same problem in the same way: Sitara interprets, Roman reconstructs, Nayah commands, TJ units sense and preserve, Croft controls, and Orien corroborates.
