# TJ — Bio-Inspired Form Factors Reference

## Overview

**Name:** TJ  
**Expanded name:** Terrain Jumper  
**Primary role:** Exploration, Search and Rescue (ESR)  
**Core design idea:** Small. Tough. Everywhere.  
**Platform concept:** Lightweight, low-power, bio-inspired robotic scout with multiple specialized forms  
**Base unit mass target:** Approximately 249 grams  
**Power model:** Ultra-low power, hours to days depending on mission  
**Sensing:** Multi-sensor  
**Architecture:** Swarm capable, one or many units  
**Operating environments:** Mountains, ice, caves, ruins, disaster zones, submerged areas

TJ is a lightweight exploration and search-and-rescue platform designed to go where people cannot safely reach, detect what people may miss, and help return people home.

Rather than relying on one fixed body plan, TJ uses multiple bio-inspired morphologies optimized for different environments and tasks.

The platform draws from:

- Canines
- Felines
- Insects
- Birds
- Fish
- Geckos
- Social insect swarms

The core idea is that form should follow mission.

---

# Design Philosophy

TJ is not one robot with one permanent body.

It is a family of related robotic forms sharing:

- Common intelligence
- Shared sensing architecture
- Mission context
- Communications
- Autonomous coordination
- Modular payloads
- Compatible software

Different physical forms are optimized for different types of mobility.

The system is designed around the idea that nature has already solved many movement problems through evolution.

# Form-Factor Selection Logic

Different mission conditions favor different TJ forms.

## Use canine form when:

- Terrain is mixed
- Human partnership matters
- General-purpose search is needed
- Stability and endurance are important

## Use feline form when:

- Terrain is steep
- Vertical mobility matters
- Quiet movement is useful
- Precision matters

## Use insect form when:

- Spaces are narrow
- Debris is dense
- Access is constrained
- Multiple tiny units are useful

## Use avian form when:

- Obstacles block ground routes
- Rapid reconnaissance is required
- Elevated mapping is valuable

## Use aquatic form when:

- Areas are flooded
- Under-ice exploration is needed
- Submerged access is required

## Use swarm mode when:

- Search area is large
- Multiple environments overlap
- Redundancy matters
- Specialized roles are needed simultaneously

# System Architecture

TJ should be understood as a shared intelligence expressed through different physical bodies.

The physical form can change while the broader system retains:

- Mission memory
- Mapping
- Identity
- Learned behavior
- Human relationship
- Shared objectives

This means TJ is better understood as a distributed robotic entity than as one permanent machine.

# Relevance to Roman Ashford

Roman can interact with different TJ forms as one continuous partner.

From Roman's perspective, the forms are not separate robots.

They are manifestations of TJ optimized for different conditions.

For example:

- Canine TJ accompanies Roman during general travel.
- Feline TJ helps during technical climbing.
- Aerial TJ scouts routes.
- Insect TJ enters spaces Roman cannot reach.
- Aquatic TJ explores flooded or under-ice environments.
- Swarm TJ performs distributed search.

This allows the Roman-TJ relationship to persist even when TJ's body changes.
