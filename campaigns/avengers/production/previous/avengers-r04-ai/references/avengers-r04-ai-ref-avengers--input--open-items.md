# Open items — the showrunner's questions

Extracted from drafts/avalanche-draft-with-questions.md: the questions the writers'
room left open in the seven-point draft, grouped by the part of the arc they concern
(rules/story-arc.md defines the arc). Reconcile these with your own open-items list;
they come from the showrunner.

## 1. HOOK — Two Glaciers, Two Discoveries

- Should these events be separate timelines?
- Why was Roman in Nepal specifically? Is there a connection to Sitara?
- Could Roman unknowingly meet Sitara's family, community, or mentor during the 2042 expedition?
- Was TJ gathering glacial data during Roman's expedition without Roman knowing?
- Does the neural link explanation make sense?
- Did those Himalayan surveys contribute to Merritt research?
- Why was Merritt's research hidden rather than published?
- Who decided certain Antarctic organisms should *not* be commercialized?
- What exactly does Sitara take: organisms, genomic data, enzyme sequences, research files, or all four?
- Why does she convince herself taking it is morally defensible?

## 2. PLOT TURN 1 — The Secrets Become Useful

- What brings Roman and Sitara to CU Boulder and into the same research program?
- What does Sitara actually bring back from Merritt Station?
  - Most importantly: does Sitara know she stole it, or does she initially believe she rescued abandoned research?
- Why does Sitara give the research to Benedict Croft?
- What can Roman/TJ do after four years of neural connection that nobody else can?
- Why does Roman work with CSG?
  - Does he believe CSG gave him TJ and therefore saved his life?
  - Is Croft a mentor or even hero to him?
  - Does Roman want to use his strange ability for something meaningful?
  - What has Roman consented to—and what hasn't he?
- What is the first Colorado success? This is especially important. We need something concrete enough for readers to think, *Maybe Croft is right.*
  - Restoring a contaminated stream?
  - Recovering phosphorus or another valuable material from waste?
  - Removing persistent pollutants from water?
  - Restoring water quality in a depleted reservoir?
  - Using Antarctic enzymes to transform agricultural/mining waste into something useful?
- What does Benedict Croft know at this stage?
  - Does he know Sitara's research came from Merritt?
  - Does he know about Roman's neural bond?
  - Does he know both and deliberately bring them together?
  - Or does he initially believe they're simply two exceptional young researchers?
  - When does legitimate scientific curiosity become appropriation for Croft?
- Why doesn't Sitara tell Roman her secret and vice versa?
- What do the two TJs know?
  - Does Sitara's TJ recognize Roman's TJ as abnormal?
  - Can Roman's TJ detect that Sitara's TJ contains encrypted Merritt data?
  - Do the TJs communicate outside their humans' awareness?
  - Are they required by CSG protocols to exchange data?
  - Does one TJ begin protecting information from the company?
  - Could the machines realize their humans are hiding things from each other before the humans do?
- What is Orien's role at Plot Turn 1?
  - Is he Sitara's scientific mentor and Roman's friend?
  - Does he celebrate their Colorado breakthrough?
  - Is he the person who believes science should move quickly enough to matter?
  - What tiny anomaly does he notice that nobody else does?
  - Does something in Sitara's research remind him of Merritt?
  - Does something in Roman/TJ's environmental data unexpectedly match Antarctic or Himalayan data?
- What is the first hint that Colorado, Antarctica and the Himalayas are connected?
- What is the moral bargain at the end of Plot Turn 1?

## 3. PINCH 1 — Orien Finds the Pattern

- What exactly did Orien discover?
- Did Croft order his disappearance?
- Does Croft know where Orien is?
- Did Orien deliberately disappear?
- Who erased his records?
- Why does Orien split the clue between Roman and Sitara?
- Did he know both of their secrets?
- Do we want TJ sharing date with out telling Roman?
- How much has TJ been uploading?
- Does TJ understand that Roman considers this a betrayal?
- Does Sitara's TJ know what she stole?
- Did her TJ preserve a copy without telling her?

## 4. MIDPOINT — "I Don't Trust You. I Need You."

- What does Roman discover about Sitara—and what does Sitara discover about Roman—that makes continued secrecy impossible?
- What clue from Orien can only be solved by Roman and Sitara working together?
- Why do they choose to trust each other despite having good reasons not to?
- What new discovery changes their understanding of Orien's disappearance?
- What do the two TJs know – and are they more willing to trust each other than Roman and Sitara are?
- What line are Roman and Sitara willing to cross to find Orien?
- What decision ends the midpoint and makes going back impossible?
- What do Roman and Sitara discover is going wrong because of the technology they helped CSG develop?
- How are the successes in Colorado directly connected to the emerging threat in Antarctica?
- What does each protagonist discover about their own responsibility?
- What does Croft reveal that makes him more – not less – sympathetic?
- What is the devastating revelation about Orien?
- What happens between Roman, Sitara and the two TJs when loyalty is tested?
- What apparently impossible choice ends Pinch 2? – This is the key question…

## 6. PLOT TURN 2 — Orien's Real Discovery

- What did Orien discover that changes Roman and Sitara's understanding of the entire mystery?
- Why was the Merritt research really hidden?
- Where is Orien, and why did he disappear rather than simply expose what he knew?
- What do Roman and Sitara realize they must sacrifice to use Orien's discovery?
- What is their new plan and why is it better than simply defeating Croft?

## 7. RESOLUTION — Give Up Control to Save the Work

- What exactly did Sitara steal? It needs to be visually understandable and scientifically plausible enough to drive the plot.
- Why was Merritt research concealed? Scientific caution, Antarctic Treaty obligations, Indigenous/community knowledge issues, corporate pressure, ecological risk—or preferably several overlapping reasons.
- What measurable good has CSG accomplished? We need to *see* restored Colorado environments so exposing CSG carries a genuine cost.
- What measurable harm is beginning? It should be ecological and systemic rather than apocalyptic.
- What did Orien discover that forced him to disappear?
- Who is actually pursuing Orien? Croft is more interesting if another actor – CSG board members, investors, a patent consortium, Merritt stakeholders, or government interests creates pressure he cannot completely control.
- What did Roman consent to when CSG gave him TJ? The legal answer and moral answer should differ.
- What does Sitara lose by telling the truth? If the answer is merely embarrassment, the choice isn't expensive enough.
- What does Roman lose? I would seriously consider making disclosure mean that CSG can legally deactivate or reclaim TJ.
- What does Croft lose by doing the right thing? Billions in IP value, control of CSG, his investors, perhaps the chance to transform his family's legacy.
- Why Himalayas \+ Antarctica \+ Colorado? This cannot just be geography. Each should embody a different relationship with environmental knowledge: Himalayas \= inherited/local knowledge; Antarctica \= scientific commons; Colorado \= commercialization/application.
- What is the final irreversible decision? The climax should require Sitara, Roman, TJ and Croft each to surrender something they value.
- What happens to Merritt?
- Why was this research hidden in the first place?  That question gets progressively more interesting. At first Sitara assumes timid scientists buried something revolutionary. Croft sees wasted innovation. Roman sees a mystery. Orien discovers there was a reason.
- Do we have a strong thematic spine worthy of the Waste-to-Value challenge while keeping the actual stakes firmly grounded in Antarctic ecology, Colorado conservation, Himalayan ecosystems, scientific ownership, and the relationship between humans and intelligent machines?
- Does the central tension of the story make sense? : two young researchers are secretly helping Colorado with knowledge that may never have been theirs to use, and the disappearance of the one person they both trust forces them to decide whether doing measurable good can justify violating scientific stewardship, consent, and ownership.

## Antagonist — Benedict Croft

- Is CSG inherited oil wealth (Croft as successor to the oil throne) or his self-made father's empire that Benedict is expanding into water/clean tech? Either way, is the extremophile discovery his chance at a final push out of oil's shadow?
- Is his public philanthropy genuine conviction, reputation management, or both?
- How driven is Croft day to day? He is already successful — does the son's illness reignite an ambition that had gone quiet?
- How old is Croft? Father of a 20-year-old suggests late 40s/50s; confirm and drop the "young father" alternative.
- Does the son survive? Does stopping Croft actually cost the cure, or do Roman and Sitara find a legitimate path to it (which would soften "by stopping this, my child can't breathe")?
- Does Sitara ever learn Roman is secretly working the cure chemistry for Croft, and when? (This crosses the equal-presence rule: her discovery of it should be her scene, not his.)
- When does Croft learn about Roman's neural bond with TJ — and does he ever weaponize that knowledge?
