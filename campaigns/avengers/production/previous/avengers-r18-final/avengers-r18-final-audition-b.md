## Page 1 (right) — Two origins, one warning line

**Panel 1.** The Himalayas, 2042. Eighteen-year-old Roman is wedged in blue ice at the bottom of a crevasse, one arm pinned, the other stretched toward his TJ unit braced against the wall beside him. A climbing cable runs from Roman's harness up out of frame. Lightning forks down the cable and through the ice, arcing in a literal circuit between Roman's outstretched hand and TJ's sensor housing — no glow, no telepathy, just current finding the shortest path.

CAPTION: The Himalayas — 2042.
SFX: KRAKKK

**Panel 2.** Nepal, 2026. Floodwater tears through a narrow valley — a cooking pot, one rubber sandal, a framed photograph spinning in the current. Small in the middle distance, a six-year-old girl is hauled back from the bank by an adult's bare arm; we see the arm, not the face.

CAPTION: Nepal — 2026.
SFX: ROARRR

**Panel 3.** Antarctic Peninsula, 2046. Wide ice shelf, blinding blue-white. Sitara (red jacket, hood down) and Nayah (yellow jacket, hood down) stand at a line of orange warning flags. Sitara's TJ crouches at her heel, sensor dish angled into the flagged zone.

CAPTION: The Antarctic Peninsula — 2046.
NAYAH: Sensor forty's dark. Log it dead, we move to the next site.
SITARA: It's not dead. It's transmitting on the same channel it's been "dead" on for a week.
NAYAH: That's called malfunctioning, Sitara.

**Panel 4.** Low angle: Sitara's boot lifts over the flag line, snow beneath it webbed with cracks nobody's looked at yet. TJ pivots to follow her, torn between the two humans. Nayah's hand comes up — not stopping her, just marking the moment.

SITARA: Or somebody wants us to think that.
NAYAH: Ten seconds, then I'm logging this as insubordination.
SITARA (already moving): Make it fifteen.
SFX: creeeak

PAGE CHECK: Plants both origin bonds (Roman/TJ lightning, Sitara's flood loss) before the reader needs them; page turn = has she already crossed the line. Three location sheets on one page (1942 Himalaya crevasse, 2026 Nepal flood, 2046 Antarctica) — necessary for the hook per brief, flagged as a production cost. No invented Nayah/Thakali/community detail per non-negotiable #10.

## Page 2 (left) — Reveal: Falcon

**Panel 1.** Sitara crouched over a half-buried sensor post inside the flagged zone, gloved hand closing on it. Faint vertical lines in the snow suggest vibration traveling up through the shelf — not from her.

SITARA: There you are—
SFX: hmmmMMM

**Panel 2.** The slab under her splits along a jagged line, her weight suddenly wrong.

SFX: KRAKK
NAYAH: SITARA — MOVE!

**Panel 3 (large panel).** The slab gives way beneath her; the sensor and a boot bag vanish into the dark below. Her headlamp catches a scarred metal hatch sunk in the ice, stenciled letters half-melted clean: **FALCON**. TJ leans over the new edge above, sensor-eyes fixed on the hatch, not on her.

SFX: GROOOAN

**Panel 4.** Nayah hauls Sitara back onto solid ice by the collar. Sitara is still turned toward the hole.

NAYAH: Nobody says "there's a door" and just stands there.
SITARA: It's got a lock. Locks mean somebody's still using it.

PAGE CHECK: Even-page reveal delivers Falcon per brief. Changed information: the danger has a name and a lock. Page-turn pull: what opens for it.

## Page 3 (right) — TJ answers the door

**Panel 1.** Inside the new crevasse mouth, TJ picks its way down to the hatch alone, red booties bright against blue ice, faceplate close to a recessed panel beside the lock. Nayah and Sitara watch from above.

NAYAH: That thing isn't cleared for whatever's down there.
SITARA: It's not asking permission. Watch its display.

**Panel 2.** Close on the hatch panel: a rotating amber pattern pulses across it. TJ's green-black screen answers in the same rhythm, a beat behind.

TJ (display): SIGNAL MATCHED — CREDENTIAL ACCEPTED
SITARA: It already knows the handshake.
NAYAH: Whose.

**Panel 3.** The lock disengages; the hatch swings inward, spilling stale, colder air and dim emergency light up into the crevasse.

SFX: HSSSK-clunk

**Panel 4.** Sitara steps toward the opening. Nayah's hand lands flat on her shoulder — not stopping her, setting terms.

NAYAH: Ten minutes. Line stays clipped to you the whole way down.
SITARA: Ten minutes.
