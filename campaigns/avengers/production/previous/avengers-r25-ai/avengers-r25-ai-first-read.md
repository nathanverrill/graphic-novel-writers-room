# First-Read Reactions: Avengers Auditions

## Version A

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, Panel 4:** The micro-moment of Sitara’s boot hovering right at the flagged line, paired with the seismic sound effect and her line "It’s still recording," immediately frames the tension as active curiosity rather than just running away from danger.
- **Page 2, Panel 4:** The drop into the crevasse exposing the stamped **FALCON** hatch is a clean, sharp visual stop. The page break works because the threat instantly shifts from "unstable ice" to "buried industrial secret."

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, Panel 1:** The self-conscious metadata inclusion ("*No red booties in this origin scene*") reads like a note to the artist or showrunner rather than story text. It pulled me out of the fiction momentarily to think about production constraints.

### I was confused
- **Page 3, Panel 3:** The description says "*The TJ’s green-black screen reproduces the bars and dots.*" Since the previous panel established the hatch display is showing them, I had to double-check whether the robot was scanning it, reflecting it, or broadcasting something it already held in memory.

### I felt something
- **Page 2, Panel 3–4:** The sudden drop of the ice slab gave a sharp, physical jolt—the quick transition from methodical retrieval to a structural collapse underfoot.

### I predicted
- When the ice broke away on Page 2, I guessed they’d find an old research station or military cache, but the specific stamping of "FALCON" made me predict the robot’s origin is tied directly to whatever is buried down there.

## Version B

### Pulled me forward
- **Page 1, Panel 4:** The dialogue overlap (*"Make it fifteen"* / *creeeak*) creates an immediate rhythm of push-and-pull between Nayah and Sitara that makes the page turn feel inevitable.
- **Page 3, Panel 2:** The exact moment the amber pattern pulses and TJ’s screen answers *a beat behind* makes the machine feel like an active participant rather than a mere tool.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- **Page 1, Panel 1:** The editorial aside (*"no glow, no telepathy, just current finding the shortest path"*) feels like an internal debate in the writers' room being fought on the page, breaking the atmospheric chill of the Himalayan opening.

### I was confused
- **Page 1, Panel 3–4:** The transition from Nayah telling Sitara to log the sensor dead to Sitara stepping across the flags happens so quickly that I lost track of whether Nayah gave permission or if Sitara is actively insubordinate. The dialogue balances on a knife-edge of ambiguity.

### I felt something
- **Page 2, Panel 3:** The visual of the sensor and boot bag vanishing into the dark while Sitara’s headlamp catches the half-melted stenciled letters **FALCON** gave a nice rush of claustrophobic dread.

### I predicted
- When Nayah tells Sitara to log the sensor dead on Page 1, I immediately predicted that Sitara’s stubbornness would trigger the collapse on the page turn.

---

## Comparison

### Which one I would keep reading, and the moment that decided it
**Version A.** The decision was sealed on **Page 3, Panel 4**, when Nayah sets a hard time limit (*"Five minutes. Then out"*) and Sitara answers with absolute focus (*"We record first"*). Version A keeps the human interaction tight, economical, and free of the meta-commentary that occasionally clutters Version B's panel descriptions. Version A trusts the imagery to do the heavy lifting.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each
- **Version A:** A cold, precise descent into a buried industrial mystery where the machines know more than their handlers.
- **Version B:** A sharp, argumentative scramble on unstable ice that occasionally pauses to argue with its own production guidelines.
