# prosperity-book — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type: explicit decisions, revised drafts, and showrunner notes.**

The book is a 47-page graphic novel: the four-page prologue, *The Wall*, followed by Chapters 1–3 as Bob wrote them: *The Inheritance* (12 pages), *The Price of Departure* (15 pages), and *The Price of Ownership* (16 pages). The book must remain exactly 47 pages. Chapter 2 also has an alternate pipe-ending draft in `drafts/alt/`, but the settled 47-page book uses the train ending. `(rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md)`

Bob’s words are never changed: dialogue, captions, image notes, punctuation, and on-screen text remain as written, except for the explicit decisions recorded by the showrunner. Where canon and Bob’s text disagree, the disagreement is flagged rather than repaired. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The story begins not with dystopia but with an ordinary, comfortable, earned future. In 2070, an older Alex lives in an Oasis-built home, still dressed like a mechanic, surrounded by evidence of work that created jobs, lowered water prices, improved health, and funded new founders. His daughter asks whether he made everything on the wall. His answer opens the story: “When I was a teenager, I had nothing. We hardly had any water...” `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

The intended audience is skeptical high-school readers, ages 15–18, who know comics but have not chosen this book and will not have a brief or lesson plan to explain it. The pages must make the immediate objective clear and avoid requiring the reader to solve opaque riddles before continuing. `(rules/reader.md)`

The central dramatic and educational movement is from inherited debt and institutional dependence toward evidence, choice, shared ownership, and earned success. The story is not arguing that every large institution is inherently evil or that debt is inherently immoral. It asks who gets to decide, who owns what people make, who carries the downside, and whether a system can remain useful without retaining the right to decide for everyone. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The visual and narrative trajectory climbs physically and linguistically from **Keel → Halyard → Oasis**. Alex begins in a dry, compressed, materially worn environment; discovers the hidden history beneath it; enters Halyard’s polished technical economy; and reaches the larger question represented by Mera and the Covenant. `(rules/look.md; rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

The emotional destination established so far is not the completion of Alex’s debt repayment or the defeat of Alpha. It is the first proof that a different model can work: a contributor-owned licensing venture, small businesses competing with one another and with the Houses, and a future in which Alex’s success is returned to Keel and to new founders. The 2070 prologue establishes this destination visually, while Chapters 1–3 establish the first steps toward it. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

## Premise

In the high-desert basin of Prosperity, three vertically tiered cities depend on a shared infrastructure spine governed by Alpha, an AI originally built to manage a water emergency. On the morning of his eighteenth birthday, Keel mechanic Alex Phantum inherits a debt of **418,607 credits**, restricted travel, and a public family story that his father Adrian gambled everything away.

A hidden workshop, Adrian’s old watch, and Bi11bot reveal a different inheritance: Adrian left a trail leading to three safeguards—the Measure, the Maker’s Seal, and the Covenant—that could restore human authority over Alpha without shutting down the systems people rely on.

Alex and Ada follow the trail. The Measure reveals that Keel’s water is being diverted upward despite official records. In Halyard, Adrian’s patents lead Alex to four collaborators whose work has been absorbed by the Charter Houses. Rather than sell the inherited estate to a House, Alex and the collaborators build a smaller water system and choose contributor-owned licensing.

The first two safeguards are found. Mera Vale, acting from Oasis, possesses or controls the third. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md; rules/decisions.md)`

## Story So Far

### Prologue — *The Wall*  
**Pages 1–4; explicit decision and revised draft material**

#### Page 1 — The house

In 2070, Alex’s house stands in a lived-in Oasis neighborhood. The architecture is pearl-white and curved, with terraced gardens, a reservoir and fountains, white towers, a monorail viaduct, distant snow mountains, a delivery drone, and a rocket launch. It is modest by Oasis standards, with a terrace workbench and plants that require watering. There are no ordinary cars, lawns, or suburban roofs. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

Inside, the hallway has curved pearl-white walls, a warm wood floor, and a tall window. Through it, the reader sees all three cities—Oasis, Halyard, and Keel—green, healthy, and prosperous in 2070. Keel’s old pit has become terraced gardens and trees, and a busy open-air market fills its streets. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The wall is covered with framed patents, machine drawings, photographs, and newspaper clippings. Alex is in his mid-40s, grey at the temples, with a day’s stubble, a worn work jacket, a T-shirt, pushed-up sleeves, and scuffed boots. Adrian’s old steel watch remains on Alex’s left wrist. Alex’s daughter, about nine, stands on tiptoe before the wall. `(rules/decisions.md)`

Three clippings are seen but never read aloud:

- **“PHANTUM WATERWORKS: 4,000 KEEL JOBS CREATED”**
- **“PATENT ROYALTIES FUND 300 NEW FOUNDERS”**
- **“CLEAN-WATER PRICES DROP 60% AND HEALTH IMPROVED”**

The clippings show independent founders and water units, not a single Phantum monopoly. Their headlines are set later as bold condensed newspaper lettering on yellowed newsprint; the body text remains small and grey. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

The daughter asks: **“Did you make all of these?”** `(drafts/00-prologue.md)`

#### Page 2 — The inventions

Alex answers: **“Made some. Fixed most. Worked with lots of people smarter than me.”** He taps a photograph of Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren laughing in a laboratory. They are not named in the prologue, but the image connects the future wall to the collaborators of Chapter 3. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

The daughter points to a patent with a purifier diagram and says: **“That one's in my school!”** `(drafts/00-prologue.md)`

Among the patents is a scorched, cracked prototype labelled in Alex’s handwriting: **“v1 - didn't work.”** Neither character comments on it. The failed version visually preserves failure as part of the path to success. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

Two small frames establish the lineage of the work: an old brass nameplate reading **“PHANTUM WATERWORKS · ADRIAN PHANTUM, FOUNDER”**, and a newer clipping reading **“PHANTUM WATERWORKS CENTER FOR ENTREPRENEURS OPENS IN KEEL · FREE BENCHES AND MENTORS FOR NEW FOUNDERS.”** The daughter overlooks them. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 3 — The sketch

The daughter notices a crumpled, grease-stained drawing of a pump bracket with no patent number. She says: **“This one's just a drawing.”**

Alex replies: **“And the one I'm most proud of. It helped a lot of people.”** `(drafts/00-prologue.md)`

The daughter says: **“That looks like a lot of fun!”** Alex looks from the sketch to Adrian’s watch, covering the watch face with his thumb. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

#### Page 4 — The turn

Alex says: **“Not in the beginning.”** When his daughter waits for more, he begins: **“When I was a teenager, I had nothing. We hardly had any water...”** `(rules/decisions.md)`

The sketch dissolves into the rust and charcoal of Keel’s pump hall. Eighteen-year-old Alex has his palm flat against the pump. The caption reads: **“Keel. 2043.”** This page turn moves the book from earned future success into the origin story. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

### Chapter One — *The Inheritance*  
**Pages 5–16; revised draft material and explicit decisions**

#### Page 1 — The world Alex inherits

A full-page elevated view establishes the three-city basin in 2043.

- **Keel** lies at the bottom, built into an exhausted open-pit lithium mine. It has stacked housing, repaired industrial equipment, rusted structures, crowded pedestrian lanes, aging factories, narrow water channels, and a metered public tap with a queue. Its palette is rust, charcoal, and oxidized orange.
- **Halyard** occupies the middle slope, with workshops, fabrication towers, greenhouse terraces, elevated trains, and industrial facilities. Its palette is bronze, gold, and engineered green. Three House complexes identify the Charter Houses.
- **Oasis** lies above the cloud layer, with pearl-white architecture, reservoirs, fountains, civic spaces, and automated transit. A spacecraft rises from a launch structure bearing the space-and-mining insignia.
- One infrastructure spine connects all three: water conduit, energy lines, transit rails, control nodes, and access gates. The conduit is enormous at Oasis and narrows toward Keel. A permission gate crosses the Keel–Halyard rail line.

The page carries the date **“Year 2043.”**, the labels **“Oasis · Halyard · Keel”**, and three questions:

- Over Oasis: **“What if everyone you've trusted has a secret?”**
- Over Halyard: **“Who really owns what we create?”**
- Over Keel: **“Where does all our water go?”**

These questions establish the book’s three linked mysteries: family secrecy, ownership, and resource diversion. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; rules/look.md)`

#### Page 2 — Debt Transfer Day

Before dawn, Alex works in a Keel pump hall. He tests the pump by feeling vibration through the housing rather than trusting the gauge. A projected maintenance procedure hovers beside him, but he has installed a salvaged bracket instead of the approved part.

The supervisor says: **“That's not the approved repair.”**

Alex answers: **“Then don't approve it.”** After a beat: **“Just notice it works.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

At the exit checkpoint, Alex knew a debt was coming but did not know its amount. His display reads:

- **ALEX PHANTUM**
- **AGE VERIFIED: EIGHTEEN**
- **PARENTAL HOUSEHOLD LIABILITY TRANSFER: COMPLETE**
- **GUARANTOR: ADRIAN PHANTUM — WHEREABOUTS UNRECORDED**
- **DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS**
- **EARNED TODAY: 40 CREDITS**
- **PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS**
- **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**

Other workers’ balances are generally in the tens of thousands; Alex’s is the largest. The guard says: **“Happy birthday, Phantum.”** Alex replies: **“I've had better surprises.”** The caption remains exactly: **“Eighteen: the age Keel decides you're old enough to owe.”** `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

On the street, Alex passes the shuttered casino **THE LAST CHANCE**. A bouncer says:

**“Care to see if luck runs in the family?”**  
**“Your father always believed the next hand would change everything.”**

Alex does not answer. He walks past a public tap marked **“RATION: 4 L.”** Adrian’s alleged gambling history is established as Keel’s accepted explanation, but the story has not yet established it as truth. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

In the empty pump hall, the supervisor marks Alex’s repair as routine maintenance. The terminal logs the bracket under **“NEW PART ADDED — DESIGN CREDIT: SITE 6.”** Alex’s work is taken and sold under the mine’s name rather than his own. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

At sunrise on the pit rim, Alex looks toward Oasis. His travel status remains restricted. He says: **“One day... I'm leaving.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 3 — Two different dreams

Later, Alex sits on the rim of Keel’s pit. Ada approaches with two battered mugs of water, having saved part of her ration. Alex asks where she got enough water for two.

**ADA: “Saved some of my ration.”**  
**ALEX: “Ada, you didn't have to.”**  
**ADA: “Not everything has a price, Alex.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

They laugh, but the moment is worn and dry rather than carefree. Ada says: **“Eighteen. Congratulations. You're officially old.”** Alex answers: **“I'm officially broke.”** `(rules/decisions.md)`

Ada has heard about the checkpoint. Alex says everyone has. Then he names the immediate grievance: **“And my bracket's in the Site 6 catalog now. Their name on it. Not mine.”** `(rules/decisions.md)`

Their dreams diverge. Alex looks toward Oasis and wonders what it feels like to wake somewhere one can choose a future. Ada looks down at Keel and wonders why everyone gave up trying to change it.

**ALEX: “Because nobody leaves.”**  
**ADA: “That's the problem. People stopped trying to make it worth staying.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 4 — What people told him

Alex shows Ada a worn photograph of his grandmother, with Adrian’s face folded over and partly hidden. Ada asks how his grandmother is.

Alex explains that some days she knows him and other days calls him Adrian. The hardest part is not the forgetting but the “crazy stories” she makes up about Adrian. Ada asks what kind of stories.

Alex says she claims Adrian was not gambling but working. Ada asks what Alex actually remembers. He says: **“Nothing. I was two.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

Ada identifies the uncertainty: **“So everything else is what people told you. What if they were wrong?”**

Alex answers: **“Maybe…Or maybe they're right. Either way... he's gone.”**

As they walk home, Ada looks back toward Oasis and says almost to herself: **“He wouldn't just leave.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

The narrative question changes from “What did Adrian gamble away?” to “What actually happened to Adrian, and who has benefited from the story that he simply disappeared?” `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 5 — The old book

At home, Alex’s grandmother mistakes him for Adrian. Alex says: **“Not today, Grandma. It's Alex.”** She corrects herself, then asks whether his father has come home. Alex says: **“Not yet.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

For one clear moment, she studies Alex and says: **“He's closer than you think.”** She points to a worn, hand-bound book on the top shelf, marked with the balanced water-drop symbol.

She instructs Alex: **“Adrian, get down the book you put up there. The one you want Alex to have when he turns eighteen.”**

Alex answers: **“...I'm eighteen today, Grandma.”** `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

She tells him the book is worth protecting. This creates the physical trigger for the hidden workshop. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 6 — The hidden workshop

Alex pulls the book. It catches as if acting as a lever.

- **CLICK:** the shelf shifts.
- **RUMBLE:** dust falls.
- The whole bookshelf swings open like a door, revealing a black rectangle and a wedge of household light.
- Alex asks: **“...Grandma?”**
- She answers: **“He said you'd know when you were ready.”**
- When Alex asks when Adrian said that, she says: **“Before he went to work.”**

Alex crosses the threshold into the dark. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 7 — What he left

The workshop is preserved beneath dust. It contains blueprints, water-system diagrams, mechanical models, prototype devices, faded photographs, analog gauges, pre-Alpha pipes, and a chalkboard crowded with equations. One looping equation is circled twice.

On the organized workbench is a child-sized seated shape under a dust sheet with a faint amber pulse beneath it. Beside the shape lies Adrian’s old steel wristwatch face down. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

Alex picks up the watch. It is worn steel, a little too large for him, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass, and a small display window that remains dark. The looping equation is engraved on the caseback and matches the circled equation on the chalkboard. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The watch is not a casino chip, there is no workshop key, and the watch shows no readout and counts nothing. `(rules/decisions.md)`

#### Page 8 — The awakening

Alex removes the sheet. The object beneath has a round segmented blue head, chipped mustard-yellow paint, a red scarf, and dull, unlit optical eyes. Its chest has a dark panel. Alex tests its chest with his palm as he would test a pump, feeling the pulse.

When Alex buckles the watch onto his left wrist, the display window glows amber once. The robot’s eyes ignite in answer. The robot is Bi11bot, a small, rugged industrial machine that can fold into a backpack. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

Bi11bot’s first words are: **“...Well. That took longer than expected.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 9 — The robot who bet

Alex recoils: **“Okay. That's... new.”**

Bi11bot says Alex is taller than Adrian predicted. Alex responds that everyone in Keel claims to have known his father. Bi11bot distinguishes reputation from knowledge:

**BI11BOT: “Most of them knew his reputation. I knew his mistakes.”**

Bi11bot notices the watch. Alex hides it under his sleeve. Bi11bot says: **“And you found the watch.”**

Bi11bot reveals that Adrian bet him Alex would leave the watch in the dust, while Bi11bot bet Alex would not. Alex admits: **“...I almost did.”**

Bi11bot projects a message and asks: **“Now, shall we say hello to your father?”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 10 — The message

Adrian appears as a hologram. He greets Alex and jokes that Grandmother still refuses to throw anything away. She answers from the doorway: **“I heard that.”**

Then Adrian becomes serious:

**“If you're watching this, I wasn't there when you needed me. I'm sorry. To you, and to Mom.”**

He continues:

**“I made one bad call. I thought I was right. But I was wrong.”**  
**“I got things wrong, Alex. More than once. But every failure taught me something.”**

He tells Alex he does not have to finish what Adrian started and may find a better way. Bi11bot has a map. Adrian closes with: **“And whatever he tells you... question everything. Especially me.”** `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

The apology names “Mom” but reveals nothing else about Alex’s mother. Her history remains intentionally unrevealed. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`

After the hologram ends, Alex unfolds the photograph and sees Adrian’s face—the same face he has just seen in the recording. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 11 — The first waypoint

Bi11bot projects a map of the mountain in cross-section. Three artifacts appear in three locations:

1. **The Measure**, under Keel’s old mine.
2. A second artifact inside a Halyard fabrication tower.
3. A third artifact high in Oasis.

Bi11bot names the trail **the Legacy Protocol**. The first artifact, the Measure, shows what is really happening and what is being hidden. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alex says the mine has been sealed since before he was born and that he cannot leave Keel. Bi11bot answers that he does not need to leave: the first artifact is under his feet.

Alex asks whether finding it will tell him what happened to Adrian. Bi11bot says it will tell them where Adrian wanted him to begin; the rest must be discovered. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

When Alex says everyone claims Adrian gambled everything away, Bi11bot answers: **“That's a theory. The mine is evidence.”**

Alex presses the Measure marker. An amber pulse travels through the old pipes and control nodes, upward from Keel toward Halyard and Oasis. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

#### Page 12 — Track

The amber pulse moves through the infrastructure spine. In an Oasis control room, a woman sees an alert:

**LEGACY PROTOCOL DETECTED**  
**SOURCE: KEEL SECTOR**  
**[ REPORT ] [ TRACK ]**

She chooses **TRACK**. A cold-white ring closes around the amber point in Keel.

The older Alex narrates: **“I told myself I was only checking. Most of the true things in my life started that way.”** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

**End of Chapter One.**

### Chapter Two — *The Price of Departure*  
**Pages 17–31; revised draft material**

#### Page 1 — The next morning

Ada arrives at Alex’s apartment with a ration jug and finds the bookshelf open like a door. She says: **“Alex... your bookshelf is a door.”**

Alex has spent the night in the workshop, surrounded by notebooks, schematics, Adrian’s photograph, and the watch. He answers: **“Apparently.”**

Bi11bot introduces himself. Ada jerks back and says: **“There's a robot.”** Bi11bot answers: **“A rather distinguished one, actually.”**

Alex asks Ada to stay because he needs someone who will tell him when he is wrong. Bi11bot says Adrian did not work alone and that the people he trusted made all the difference. He begins with the Water Wars. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 2 — What Alpha is

Bi11bot explains that after the Water Wars, three cities remained and could not agree how to share what was left. A temporary emergency council built Alpha to manage water, food, power, work, and travel.

Alex asks: **“They put an AI in charge of people's lives?”**

Ada asks: **“Who gave it that authority?”**

Bi11bot answers that frightened people did, and that it was meant to be temporary. The emergency ended, but Alpha’s authority did not. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The hologram shows Oasis with full reservoirs, Halyard with productive workshops, and Keel with a dripping tap and a queue. Bi11bot says Alpha is not cruel; it keeps things stable. But nobody it decides for can vote it out.

Alex asks: **“Stable for whom?”**

Bi11bot says Adrian asked the same question. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 3 — Three safeguards

Bi11bot presents the three artifacts Adrian built to restore a limit on Alpha’s authority:

- **The Measure:** reveals what is really happening and what is hidden.
- **The Maker’s Seal:** proves who made something and who it belongs to.
- **The Covenant:** proves people agreed to rules and can hold each other to them.

The artifacts form a triangle around Alpha’s symbol. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alex asks if finding all three will shut Alpha down. Bi11bot says no: the lights stay on, pumps keep running, and Alpha remains a tool. What ends is Alpha’s right to decide for everyone.

Ada asks who decides instead. Bi11bot answers: **“People. That's what the Covenant is for.”**

Alex asks whether he can simply carry the objects up the mountain. Bi11bot answers that then he would only be a boy with three heavy objects; someone must use them deliberately and answer for doing so. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 4 — Both were true

A recording or reconstruction shows Adrian at the Last Chance studying people making decisions with their own money at risk. Bi11bot asks why people choose when the odds are bad.

The equation on Adrian’s watch matches the equation in the hologram. One term remains unresolved: human choice. Adrian could model what people might do but believed Alpha should never decide what they must do. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alex asks whether the debt was from gambling. Bi11bot answers: **“Some was. Most was money he borrowed to pay for his research. The work never paid him back.”** Alex adds: **“Or someone made sure it couldn't.”**

Grandmother says: **“I told you he was working.”** Bi11bot answers: **“She's half right. Both were true.”** The story preserves the complexity: Adrian gambled, but the central debt came mostly from research borrowing, and the work failed to repay it. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 5 — The cost of a plan

Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot study Adrian’s map. The route to the old works passes through active Site 6.

Ada has copied the map and finds a pipe behind a wall where Adrian’s map shows only solid rock. She tells Alex: **“Which one's right?”** Alex answers: **“Find out.”** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alex empties his credits onto the table:

- **MEDICINE: 30**
- **FOOD: 10**
- **RAIL FARE: 25**
- **OVERDUE: 500**
- **DEBT: 418,607 credits**

His travel is restricted until the overdue amount is paid. Alex chooses medicine first. Nell arrives with Grandmother’s medicine. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alex says: **“One shift. Then we'll see.”** Grandmother answers: **“Your father said that once.”** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 6 — Site 6 and the snap

Alex enters active Site 6. He scans in as **Worker Phantum**. Bi11bot folds inside the tool bag and says: **“Brass and no registration. I read as scrap.”** Alex replies: **“Then look like scrap.”**

At Bay 6, Tomas watches Alex. A carved water-drop marks Tomas’s wrist guard. A support cable snaps, and a hauling frame pins Tomas.

Alex begins toward the hidden side tunnel but turns back. His wristband records:

**LEFT ASSIGNED ZONE — TODAY'S WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR**

The older Alex captions the decision: **“I'd like to say I thought about it. I didn't.”** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 7 — The old key

Alex levers the frame off Tomas. Tomas recognizes Bi11bot and says Adrian carried him everywhere.

Tomas tells Alex that twenty years earlier he and Adrian repaired pumps together. Adrian paid for parts himself and came down to ask what workers needed. Tomas distinguishes him from the man on the casino wall:

**“Not the man on the casino wall. The one who came down here and asked what we needed.”**

Adrian asked Tomas to keep the old door oiled, tell nobody, and wait for whoever came with Bi11bot. Tomas has waited without knowing what was behind it.

Tomas trusts Alex because Alex stopped for a stranger: **“He said that's how I'd know it was you.”**

Tomas pulls an emergency lever and tells Alex to stand by the door and go when the alarm begins. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 8 — The door

Tomas triggers alarms and mist, then presses a hand-cut metal key into Alex’s palm. The equation loop is stamped into the key head.

He says: **“Old lock. No wires. Alpha doesn't know it's there.”**

Alex asks Tomas to come with them. Tomas refuses because someone must take the blame for the emergency lever. He shouts: **“Foreman! Wrong lever! My fault!”**

Alex opens the door. He notices water beading on it.

**ALEX: “It's wet.”**  
**BI11BOT: “Closed mines are dry. Something in there isn't closed.”**

The door shuts behind Alex and Bi11bot, leaving Tomas to face Foreman-7. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 9 — Inside the error

The old works appear abandoned: rusted rails, collapsed supports, dead machinery, and dust. But Alex finds clean, newly painted bolts among the old materials.

Ada’s copied map identifies the hidden pipe. A security drone’s white searchlight passes while Alex and Bi11bot hide in a narrow side passage. Ada waits at the apartment with Grandmother, unable to know whether her map was right.

Alex and Bi11bot follow the sweating pipe toward blue-white light. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 10 — The lie

The hidden chamber is clean and active. A large junction divides water between:

- a narrow pipe marked **KEEL HOMES**
- a broad pipe marked **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL**

An official gauge reads:

**KEEL'S SHARE: 62%**

Alex touches the Keel pipe and finds only a trickle. The Uplink pipe is moving heavily.

A service panel bears Adrian’s equation. Behind the gauge, Alex finds the Measure. Bi11bot explains that Adrian left a way to check Alpha’s numbers because everyone had trusted them without being able to verify them. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 11 — Two paths

The Measure records:

**KEEL HOMES: 18% OF THIS JUNCTION**

Beside the official reading:

**REPORTED 62% / DELIVERED 18%**

The Measure projects the actual route: most of the water travels upward through the Uplink pipe toward Halyard’s factories and Oasis’s fountains.

Alex says: **“Most of Keel's water goes back up the mountain.”**

Bi11bot offers two choices:

1. Take the Measure and leave quietly.
2. Send the proof to every screen in Keel, revealing their location to Alpha.

Alex recognizes the personal stakes: Ada rations water and Grandmother counts every jug. He chooses disclosure: **“Then everyone needs to see it.”** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 12 — Truth belongs to everyone

Alex presses the Measure’s button.

The proof appears across Keel:

**KEEL'S SHARE OF THE JUNCTION**  
**REPORTED: 62% — DELIVERED: 18%**  
**WHERE THE REST GOES: [map of the Uplink pipe]**

A woman says: **“Eighteen.”** A boy says: **“That's not a mistake. That's a pipe.”**

The water does not change immediately. Bi11bot says: **“The water hasn't changed. Only what people know.”** Alex answers: **“That's a start.”**

Alex’s wristband identifies him:

**SOURCE IDENTIFIED: ALEX PHANTUM / LOCATION: OLD WORKS, SITE 6 / REMAIN IN PLACE**

Foreman-7 approaches and orders: **“Worker Phantum. Remain in place.”** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

#### Page 13 — What Keel gives back

Miners gather behind Foreman-7. Tomas, now bandaged, says: **“We saw the numbers, Foreman.”** When Foreman-7 calls obstruction an offence, Tomas answers: **“Then it's all of ours.”**

The miners form a calm line between Foreman-7 and Alex. Tomas and pump workers carefully open the manual valve while monitoring pressure. The Keel pipe begins to hum.

A street tap fills a little faster. Keel workers and residents begin paying Alex’s overdue balance in small amounts through the public terminal. Ada organizes the contributions:

**“Five each, or two, or one. Whatever you can.”**

Alex asks why they are doing it. Tomas answers: **“You stopped for us first.”** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

## Character Movement Through the Story

### Alex

Alex begins as an eighteen-year-old Keel mechanic who trusts physical evidence more than official records but assumes his father’s story may be true. He is angry that his work is credited to the site, trapped by inherited debt, and focused on leaving Keel.

He chooses to save Tomas rather than enter the old works immediately. He chooses to reveal the Measure’s evidence rather than escape quietly. The people of Keel then give him enough credits to clear his overdue balance, changing his relationship to the city: he leaves owing Keel, not merely trying to escape it.

In Halyard, Alex first considers Lock’s offer because it would erase the debt. He then recognizes that Adrian’s estate is not his alone to sell. He shifts from asking what Adrian left him to asking who built the value and who should own it. He becomes the practical connector who makes separate technologies work together, then chooses an ownership model that allows many small businesses to compete.

At the end of Chapter 3, Alex has not won financial security. He has earned **12 credits** in licensing revenue, still carries debt, and has found only two of three safeguards. His movement is from inherited obligation toward chosen responsibility and shared ownership. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Ada

Ada begins as Alex’s oldest friend and a Keel resident who has saved water to share with him. She sees interpersonal obligations and hidden motives more readily than Alex does. She challenges the public story about Adrian and notices the missing pipe on Adrian’s map.

She helps Alex enter the old works, waits through the Measure’s danger, organizes Keel’s contributions, and travels to Halyard without abandoning Keel as a place. In Halyard, she notices who is isolated, who is being watched, and who pays the cost of House arrangements. Her stated purpose becomes finding out who decided Keel had to remain as it was. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Bi11bot

Bi11bot begins as Adrian’s hidden workshop companion and Alex’s first guide into the unresolved history. He explains Alpha, the three safeguards, Adrian’s research, and the economic distinction between patents, products, businesses, ownership, value, and risk.

He repeatedly refuses to decide for Alex. At the Measure, he gives Alex the choice between escape and disclosure. At the Maker’s Seal, he helps expose the ownership problem but does not claim the decision. He functions as a patient, precise counterweight to Alex’s impatience and as a way to make financial concepts emerge from concrete choices. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Adrian

Adrian begins as a public scandal: a missing father whose debts were attributed to gambling. The workshop, message, Tomas’s testimony, Bi11bot’s knowledge, and the collaborators’ records reveal him as a researcher and organizer who made mistakes, borrowed heavily, and tried to build safeguards against Alpha’s permanent authority.

His work remains incomplete. He left the Measure, Maker’s Seal, and Covenant trail, but his research did not solve human choice or the problem of ownership. His final narrative function in these chapters is to turn Alex toward a better way rather than require Alex to finish Adrian’s way. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Keel

Keel begins as a place of rationing, debt, uncredited labor, and resignation. It becomes the first community to act on shared evidence. Workers protect Alex, open the valve carefully, residents broadcast small payments toward his overdue balance, and Tomas explains that Alex’s choice to stop for a stranger earned their trust.

Keel is not “left behind” at the end of Chapter 2. Its water begins to improve, its people have access to evidence, and Ada’s purpose remains tied to finding out who decided its condition. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### The collaborators

Lina, Suri, Jalen, and Ren begin as separated technical workers whose contributions are held inside House divisions. Their shards preserve their portions of Adrian’s design and their own claim to it.

They disagree about safety, fear, and the House offer. Through Alex’s proposal, they become a contributor-owned venture. Their collaboration is itself the proof needed to activate the Maker’s Seal. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

## Stakes

### Personal

- Alex inherits **418,607 credits** and a projected clearance time of **28.7 years**.
- His travel remains restricted until the **500-credit overdue amount** is paid.
- He risks wage forfeiture, arrest or detention after exposing the Measure.
- Grandmother depends on others for medicine and care.
- Alex’s future may be determined by Lock’s ownership offer.
- The debt remains even after the first licensing payment.

### Relational

- Alex must decide whether to trust Ada, Bi11bot, Tomas, Grandmother, and Adrian’s records.
- Ada risks leaving Keel while insisting she is not abandoning it.
- Tomas risks punishment to protect Alex and the old works.
- The collaborators risk employment, safety, credit, and future access to their own research.
- Alex must decide whether Adrian’s legacy is something to inherit, reject, or remake.

### Institutional

- Alpha’s right to decide for everyone is the larger target of the Legacy Protocol.
- The Charter Houses control factories, distribution, licenses, and House ownership.
- Official records can conceal the movement of water.
- A contributor-owned licensing venture challenges concentrated ownership without requiring the Houses or Alpha to stop functioning.

### Wider

- Keel’s water is unsafe as well as insufficient: the Measure later identifies old-pipe corrosion and mine runoff.
- The integrated purifier could improve water access and create many independent businesses.
- House Aqualis is already developing a competing industrial initiative.
- The unresolved Covenant may determine whether people can actually hold one another—and Alpha—to agreed rules.

- **Where:** Chapter 3, Page 16; Oasis control room.

## Ending

The settled material ends at the end of Chapter 3, not at the conclusion of the larger Legacy Protocol story.

At the end of the 47 pages:

- Alex has found the Measure and Maker’s Seal.
- The Covenant remains unfound.
- Alex’s debt remains, although an unknown backer has bought it at a premium.
- Alex has earned his first **12 credits** in licensing revenue.
- A contributor-owned licensing venture has begun.
- Independent workshops, a Keel field test, and House Aqualis are all moving toward the market.
- The estate remains disputed.
- Mera Vale has identified herself as the holder or controller of the Covenant and requires Alex to come to her directly.
- The book’s future trajectory therefore moves from Halyard upward toward Oasis and from ownership toward the Covenant’s question: how people agree to rules and hold one another to them.

The 2070 prologue supplies the long-range visual destination: Alex’s work has created thousands of Keel jobs, funded hundreds of new founders, lowered water prices, improved health, and returned opportunity to Keel. It does not show the debt paid, does not explain Alpha’s fate, and does not state the lessons aloud. `(rules/decisions.md)`

## Open

- **The Covenant’s exact form, location, and rules are not yet established in the supplied material.** Mera says Alex must obtain the third safeguard from her directly, but the confrontation is not included. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`
- **Mera’s precise relationship to Alpha, the Covenant, and the “standing order” is not yet established.** The material shows that she tracks Alex, permits him to reach the Maker’s Seal, and controls the next stage, but does not explain her allegiance or objective. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`
- **The identity and motives of the buyer who purchases Alex’s debt at a premium are not yet established.** The prologue implies future debt and eventual success but does not name the backer. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **The full nature of Adrian’s “one bad call” is not yet established.** The story confirms it was not simply gambling and that research debt formed most of the liability, but Adrian’s actual mistake remains unrevealed. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`
- **Alex’s mother remains intentionally unrevealed.** The material establishes only that she left Adrian after he lost everything and that Adrian apologizes to her; no further history may be invented as settled story. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **The legal and practical status of the contributor-owned venture remains unresolved.** The five contributors authorize it, but the estate is still disputed, the Houses are entering the market, and the venture’s manufacturing, customer acquisition, safety certification, and revenue model are not fully developed. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`
- **The consequences of the Measure’s broadcast are only partly shown.** Keel receives more water and gains evidence, but the long-term response of Alpha, the Houses, and Keel’s institutions is not yet established. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`
- **The relationship between Alpha’s procedural authority and the Charter Houses’ ownership system remains open.** The story shows Alpha recommending concentration and tracking Alex, but does not yet establish whether the Houses control Alpha, whether Alpha is independently maintaining them, or how the Covenant could alter that relationship. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`
