# prosperity-book — Story

## What the Showrunner Is Reaching For

**Material type: explicit decisions, revised drafts, and showrunner notes.**

The book is a **62-page graphic novel**: the four-page prologue, *The Wall*, followed by Chapters 1–3 as Bob wrote them: *The Inheritance* (12 pages), *The Price of Departure* (19 pages), and *The Price of Ownership* (27 pages). Chapter 2 also has an alternate pipe-ending draft in `drafts/alt/`, drawn alongside the train ending. The book runs from pages 1–62, no more and no fewer. `(rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md)`

Bob’s words are never changed: dialogue, captions, image notes, punctuation, and on-screen text remain as written, except for the explicit decisions recorded by the showrunner. Where canon and Bob’s text disagree, the disagreement is flagged rather than repaired. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The story begins not with dystopia but with an ordinary, comfortable, earned future. In 2070, an older Alex lives in an Oasis-built home, still dressed like a mechanic, surrounded by evidence of work that created jobs, lowered water prices, improved health, and funded new founders. His daughter asks whether he made everything on the wall. His answer opens the story: “When I was a teenager, I had nothing. We hardly had any water...” `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

The intended audience is skeptical high-school readers, ages 15–18, who know comics but have not chosen this book and will not have a brief or lesson plan to explain it. The pages must make the immediate objective clear and avoid requiring the reader to solve opaque riddles before continuing. `(rules/reader.md)`

The central dramatic and educational movement is from inherited debt and institutional dependence toward evidence, choice, shared ownership, and earned success. The story is not arguing that every large institution is inherently evil or that debt is inherently immoral. It asks who gets to decide, who owns what people make, who carries the downside, and whether a system can remain useful without retaining the right to decide for everyone. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The visual and narrative trajectory climbs physically and linguistically from **Keel → Halyard → Oasis**. Alex begins in a dry, compressed, materially worn environment; discovers the hidden history beneath it; enters Halyard’s polished technical economy; and reaches the larger question represented by Mera and the Covenant. `(rules/look.md; rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

The emotional destination established so far is not the completion of Alex’s debt repayment or the defeat of Alpha. It is the first proof that a different model can work: a contributor-owned licensing venture, small businesses competing with one another and with the Houses, and a future in which Alex’s success is returned to Keel and to new founders. The 2070 prologue establishes this destination visually, while Chapters 1–3 establish the first steps toward it. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

## Structure

### Prologue — *The Wall* — Pages 1–4

A framed, hopeful future establishes the destination: older Alex has helped make Prosperity healthier and more distributed. His daughter’s questions lead to the grease-stained pump-bracket sketch and the turn into Keel in 2043.

**Turn:** Page 4 moves from the future’s evidence of success to Alex’s teenage hand on a failing pump.

### Act I — Inheritance and Evidence — Pages 5–16

Alex turns eighteen, inherits Adrian’s debt, and learns that his father’s public story may be false. The hidden workshop gives him Bi11bot and the Legacy Protocol. He commits to finding the first safeguard.

**Act turn:** Page 16 ends with Alpha tracking the activated Legacy Protocol.

### Act II — Departure and Collective Action — Pages 17–35

Alex and Ada learn Alpha’s history and the purpose of the three safeguards. Alex enters Site 6, chooses Tomas over escape, finds the Measure, and broadcasts proof of Keel’s stolen water. Keel protects him and clears his overdue balance. He leaves by the authorized train, owing the community rather than escaping it.

**Midpoint/reversal:** Pages 29–30 reveal that the official 62% allocation is only 18% physical delivery, then force Alex to choose between quiet escape and public evidence.

**Act turn:** Page 35 reveals Adrian’s collaborator trail in Halyard and confirms that Alex is being followed.

### Act III — Ownership and Shared Value — Pages 36–62

Halyard reveals the House economy and Adrian’s 312-patent estate. Lock offers debt relief for ownership. Alex finds the four collaborators, integrates their separated technologies, and chooses contributor-owned licensing over a guaranteed House sale. The Maker’s Seal activates through shared contribution. Mera reveals herself as the gatekeeper of the Covenant.

**Climax:** Pages 58–59: Alex refuses exclusive transfer and the five contributors activate the Maker’s Seal.

**Ending turn:** Pages 60–62 establish first revenue, continued debt, an unknown debt buyer, and Mera’s direct challenge. The Covenant remains ahead.

## Page plot

### Prologue — Pages 1–4

- **Page 1:** In 2070, older Alex’s lived-in Oasis home and the prosperous basin establish the destination; his daughter asks whether he made everything on the wall.
- **Page 2:** Alex identifies collaborators, a purifier in his daughter’s school, a failed prototype, Adrian’s company, and the Keel founders’ center.
- **Page 3:** The daughter notices the grease-stained pump-bracket sketch; Alex calls it the invention he is most proud of because it helped people. The watch links him to the past.
- **Page 4 — Page-turn reveal:** Alex begins, “Not in the beginning”; the future dissolves into eighteen-year-old Alex’s hand on a Keel pump.

### Chapter One — Pages 5–16

- **Page 5:** The three-city basin establishes the vertical class system, shared infrastructure, and the central questions of water, ownership, and trust.
- **Page 6:** Alex repairs a pump, receives the 418,607-credit debt transfer, hears the gambling story, and discovers Site 6 has taken credit for his bracket.
- **Page 7:** Ada gives Alex ration water; their different dreams—escape versus changing Keel—come into conflict.
- **Page 8:** Alex admits he remembers nothing of Adrian; Ada challenges the accepted story and says Adrian would not simply leave.
- **Page 9:** Grandmother directs Alex to Adrian’s hand-bound book and the hidden workshop mechanism begins.
- **Page 10 — Page-turn reveal:** The bookshelf opens into a preserved workshop; Alex crosses the threshold.
- **Page 11:** Alex finds the watch, the matching equation, and the dust-covered child-sized robot.
- **Page 12:** Alex tests and wears the watch; Bi11bot wakes, establishing the watch as a technical key and keepsake.
- **Page 13:** Bi11bot recognizes Alex and the watch, distinguishing Adrian’s reputation from his mistakes.
- **Page 14:** Adrian’s holographic apology gives Alex permission to question the legacy rather than obey it.
- **Page 15:** Bi11bot maps the three safeguards and directs Alex to the Measure beneath Keel.
- **Page 16 — Page-turn reveal:** The Legacy Protocol activates through the infrastructure spine; Mera’s control room chooses TRACK and Alpha begins watching Alex.

### Chapter Two — Pages 17–35

- **Page 17:** Ada discovers the workshop and Bi11bot; Alex asks her to join because he needs someone who will tell him when he is wrong.
- **Page 18:** Bi11bot explains the Water Wars and Alpha’s emergency origin.
- **Page 19:** The three safeguards are introduced; the goal is to restore human authority without shutting Alpha down.
- **Page 20:** Adrian’s unresolved equation frames human choice as the problem Alpha must not decide.
- **Page 21:** Alex learns that Adrian both gambled and borrowed for research; Grandmother confirms the partial truth.
- **Page 22:** The team plans the route through Site 6; Alex discovers he has only 52 credits and cannot travel with a 500-credit overdue balance.
- **Page 23:** Alex buys Grandmother’s medicine and goes to his shift; Grandmother echoes Adrian’s old promise.
- **Page 24:** Alex enters Site 6 with Bi11bot disguised as scrap; Tomas watches him.
- **Page 25:** A hauling frame pins Tomas; Alex turns away from the old-works route to save him and forfeits his wage.
- **Page 26:** Tomas recognizes Bi11bot, identifies Adrian as a pump worker, and explains that he kept the old door.
- **Page 27:** Tomas tests Alex’s character, then pulls the emergency lever so Alex can reach the door.
- **Page 28:** Tomas gives Alex the hand-cut key; Alex enters the wet, supposedly sealed mine while Tomas takes the blame.
- **Page 29:** New bolts, Ada’s copied pipe, condensation, and a searchlight lead Alex to the hidden chamber.
- **Page 30 — Page-turn reveal:** The official gauge claims 62% for Keel while the physical pipe barely moves; the Uplink carries the actual flow upward.
- **Page 31:** Alex finds the Measure behind the gauge and records 18% delivery against the reported 62%.
- **Page 32:** The Measure maps the diversion; Bi11bot offers quiet escape or public disclosure.
- **Page 33:** Alex broadcasts the proof across Keel; the water remains unchanged, but the public now sees the pipe.
- **Page 34:** Alpha identifies Alex; Foreman-7 approaches; Tomas and the miners form a collective barrier.
- **Page 35 — Page-turn reveal:** Keel opens the valve carefully, pays Alex’s overdue balance, and Alex leaves by train with Ada and the Measure; a hidden agent receives orders to follow but not take it.

### Chapter Three — Pages 36–62

- **Page 36:** Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot arrive in Halyard, where House insignias, elevated transit, and controlled prosperity replace Keel’s compression.
- **Page 37:** A contribution kiosk shows an inventor receiving 14 credits while Helion owns her work; Alex sees the cost of “scale.”
- **Page 38:** The Public Registry reveals Adrian’s 312-patent estate, including restricted and disputed claims.
- **Page 39:** Lock approaches and offers debt clearance, employment, and House ownership in exchange for Adrian’s estate.
- **Page 40:** In the rented room, the archive identifies the water-system cluster and four collaborators.
- **Page 41:** Tomas’s cloudy-water message establishes that the Measure exposed delivery but did not clean contamination; Lina’s Aqualis facility becomes the first destination.
- **Page 42:** Lina shares the membrane fragment and explains Adrian left the work to all five contributors.
- **Page 43:** Suri shares the biofilter fragment; Ada observes the human cost of institutional abandonment.
- **Page 44:** Jalen shares distributed-node logic while warning that Helion watches patterns; he refuses to join.
- **Page 45:** Ren shares mineral-recovery and atmospheric-capture fragments; the complete design reveals Adrian’s cross-House argument.
- **Page 46:** The first integrated prototype fails at 11%; each House technology works alone but not together.
- **Page 47:** The team identifies the minimum viable system and its cost; the problem is interoperability and scale, not invention alone.
- **Page 48:** Alex tests the physical connectors, identifies incompatible threading, and begins making a salvaged sleeve.
- **Page 49:** The sleeve joins the systems; simulated efficiency reaches 94%, proving Alex’s practical contribution.
- **Page 50:** Lock enters with the second offer, citing pattern surveillance and offering financial security.
- **Page 51:** Lock explains that the Houses would own the technology and gives Alex fourteen days before review.
- **Page 52:** Alex and Ada weigh debt relief against ownership; Alex says the work was never his alone to find.
- **Page 53:** Alex proposes licensing to many small businesses; Bi11bot identifies manufacturing and survival as the remaining risks.
- **Page 54:** The four collaborators return before dawn; Jalen argues for Lock’s safety while Ren argues for credit.
- **Page 55:** Alex proposes a locally buildable system licensed to many entrepreneurs; the group agrees to test the model.
- **Page 56:** Prototype 1 is too expensive; the team removes nonessential features and folds the membrane to preserve surface area.
- **Page 57:** Suri and Jalen cycle the biofilter and nodes; Ren recovers waste heat; Prototype 23 reaches 91% simulated efficiency at a fraction of industrial cost.
- **Page 58:** At Charter review, Alex refuses the administrative transfer and names the four collaborators as co-builders.
- **Page 59 — Page-turn reveal:** All five authorize the contributor-owned venture; Adrian’s watch completes the fifth socket and activates the Maker’s Seal.
- **Page 60:** Lock warns that a prototype is not a market; an independent workshop, a Keel field test, and House Aqualis begin competing approaches.
- **Page 61:** Alex receives 38 credits in licensing revenue, with 12 applied to debt; the debt remains and an unknown buyer now holds it.
- **Page 62 — Final reveal:** Mera’s Oasis control room receives the Maker’s Seal event. She sets the water glass askew, identifies herself, and says Alex must obtain the Covenant directly from her.

## Reader vs character knowledge

### Reader knows more than Alex

- The prologue shows that Alex eventually creates jobs, lowers water prices, and funds new founders.
- The reader sees Mera tracking the Legacy Protocol before Alex knows anyone is watching.
- The reader sees the Alpha field agent follow Alex on the train before Alex notices.
- The reader knows the future includes a functioning contributor-owned model, though not whether this specific venture survives unchanged.
- The reader knows the Covenant remains the next objective before Alex reaches Mera’s final challenge.
- The reader sees Mera’s control room and the glass alignment as deliberate visual evidence of controlled authority.

### Alex knows more than the reader

- Alex knows the physical feel and limits of the pump bracket and connector problem before the reader sees him solve them.
- Alex knows what he personally experiences in Keel’s work and ration systems; the reader sees only selected evidence.
- Alex carries the emotional history of Grandmother and Adrian that the book deliberately leaves incomplete.
- Alex knows the exact practical risks of leaving the workshop and entering Site 6, though the reader shares enough information to follow his objective.
- Alex does not know the identity of the debt buyer or Mera’s allegiance.

### Other characters know more than Alex

- Grandmother knows Adrian’s workshop was meant for Alex.
- Bi11bot knows Adrian’s mistakes and the Legacy Protocol’s intended route, but not every answer about Adrian’s fate.
- Tomas knows Adrian’s worker history and the old door.
- The collaborators know their own contributions and the extent to which House records omit them.
- Mera knows or controls the next safeguard but has not explained the Covenant’s object, rules, or constitutional role.

### Objective truth held open

- Adrian’s ultimate fate is unknown.
- Alex’s mother’s identity and later history are unknown.
- Mera’s institutional allegiance and relationship to Alpha are unknown.
- The debt buyer’s identity and motives are unknown.
- The Covenant’s physical form and activation sequence are unknown.
- Alpha’s final constitutional status is unresolved.
- The contributor-owned venture has begun but is not guaranteed to succeed.

## Unresolved decisions and proposed canon changes

### Canon

- The primary Chapter Two route is the authorized Keel–Halyard train.
- The alternate pipe ending remains separately drawn comparison material, not a second route in the primary book and not an additional page count.
- The book ends on Mera’s direct challenge.
- Alex’s debt remains after the first licensing payment.
- The Measure exposes conditions but does not purify water.
- The Maker’s Seal activates through shared contribution, with Adrian’s watch completing the fifth socket.
- The Covenant remains unrevealed in physical design and is held or controlled by Mera.
- Alpha remains a procedural system and tool, not a humanoid villain or simple monster.

### Proposal

- The unknown buyer who purchases Alex’s debt at a premium may be connected to the larger constitutional conflict, but this book should not identify that buyer.
- The Houses may be reformable in some domains while remaining structurally invested in concentrated ownership; the next volume can test whether reform or displacement is possible.
- Alex’s contributor-owned venture should face real certification, manufacturing, and retaliation problems in the next volume rather than succeeding by authorial guarantee.

### Decision needed

- Whether the separately drawn pipe ending is presented as an appendix, production comparison, or withheld alternate.
- Whether the next volume identifies the debt buyer before or after Alex reaches Oasis.
- Whether Mera is an Alpha custodian, surviving council agent, independent administrator, or another authority.
- Whether the Covenant’s physical form is designed before the next development pass or preserved as a reveal.
- The eventual constitutional relationship among Alpha, the Houses, the three-city council, and the Covenant.

### Risk

- The large amount of technical and institutional information could make Chapter Two’s workshop scenes feel explanatory rather than dramatic. Keep Alex’s immediate objective—reach the Measure—visible in every explanatory sequence.
- The four collaborators could blur into a single “team” if their different pressures are not maintained: Lina values safety and water quality, Suri protects an unfinished living system, Jalen fears surveillance, and Ren prioritizes continued making.
- Lock’s offer must remain materially attractive. If the House system is presented as only villainous, Alex’s refusal loses its cost.
- The prologue’s hopeful future must remain an earned destination, not proof that every present problem is solved.
- Mera’s final line must open the next question without answering the Covenant prematurely.
- The text in the draft contains minor continuity and wording concerns, including the Chapter Three “urgent evoke,” but execution should flag rather than silently repair them under the showrunner’s instruction.
