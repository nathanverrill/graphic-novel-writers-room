# prosperity-book — World

## What the Showrunner Wants

- **FIXED — Format and visual world.** The book is a **62-page graphic novel**: the four-page prologue, “The Wall,” followed by Chapters 1–3 as Bob wrote them. The chapter allocation is: prologue 4 pages, Chapter 1 12 pages, Chapter 2 19 pages, and Chapter 3 27 pages. Chapter 2 also has a separately drawn alternate pipe ending. Bob’s words, captions, image notes and punctuation are not to be changed; disagreements between the scripts and canon are flagged rather than repaired. `(rules/decisions.md; input/notes.md)`
- **FIXED — Visual direction.** The world is rendered as inked graphic-novel realism: bold contours, textured hatching, matte painterly colour, expressive acting and tactile architectural detail. The future is mostly present-day infrastructure aged, repaired and institutionalized. Systems are shown through pressure, wear, queues, tools, records, gates, surveillance and consequences rather than explanatory interfaces. `(rules/look.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Ancestry and regional identity.** Prosperity is a mixed Andean high-desert basin shaped by Indigenous communities, Latin American populations, foreign corporations, technical migration and global capital. The three cities are not ethnic enclaves. Their principal visual differences are class, access, ownership, education, occupation, dress, grooming, speech, health, environment and institutional power. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/look.md)`
- **FIXED — Money.** Money is called **credits** everywhere. There is no casino-chip currency or slang replacement for money or balances. `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/vocabulary.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Charter Houses.** The three Houses are **Helion**—energy, compute and infrastructure; **Aqualis**—water and chemistry; and **Astra**—extraction, launch and materials. Their fixed accent colours are Helion deep amber-orange, Aqualis cool jade-teal and Astra pale steel-violet. Their insignia, uniforms, signage and light carry those colours whenever they appear. `(rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **FIXED — Alpha.** Alpha has no face, body or human-like consciousness. It appears through white-and-amber control nodes, cold administrative displays, gates, records, data lines, drones and procedural language. `(rules/look.md; rules/ancestry.md)`

## Overview

Prosperity is one Andean high-desert mountain basin organized vertically into three cities:

- **Keel**, at the bottom, built into and around an exhausted open-pit lithium mine.
- **Halyard**, midway up the mountain, a productive industrial and technical city dominated by fabrication, research and the Charter Houses.
- **Oasis**, above the cloud layer, the spacious and controlled administrative city with reservoirs, civic interiors, formal transit and access to the highest level of infrastructure.

The three cities share one vertical infrastructure spine: a massive water conduit, energy lines, transit rails and control nodes running through the mountain. The conduit is enormous at Oasis’s reservoirs and narrows substantially by the time it reaches Keel’s channels. Permission gates multiply toward Keel, including a travel gate across the rail line between Keel and Halyard. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; rules/look.md)`

Prosperity is materially unequal but not visually divided by ancestry. Indigenous Andean, mestizo, European-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed and international populations live in all three cities. An Indigenous executive in Oasis may look radically different from an Indigenous miner in Keel because of nutrition, healthcare, clothing, grooming, posture, environment and institutional power rather than ancestry. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/look.md)`

The world is hard-used rather than sleek. New systems are layered over older infrastructure: sealed mines contain active water-control systems; analog gauges and pre-Alpha pipes coexist with white-and-amber control nodes; repaired furniture, patched machinery, physical keys and handwritten maps remain useful. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

## Setting

### Time

- The main story begins in **2043**, on the morning of Alex Phantum’s eighteenth birthday and Debt Transfer Day. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`
- The prologue takes place around **2070**, when Alex is in his mid-forties and lives in a comfortable Oasis house. All three cities are then green, healthy and prosperous; Keel’s old pit has been terraced with gardens and trees, and a busy open-air market fills its streets. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`
- The prologue’s future is not a dystopian endpoint. Its wall shows independent companies, jobs, patent royalties, lower water prices, improved health and founders receiving support. `(rules/decisions.md)`

### Geography and vertical layout

The basin is mountainous, high-desert and water-stressed. Snow mountains rise behind the cities. Oasis is partly above the cloud layer; Halyard occupies the middle slopes; Keel sits low in the basin around the old mine. `(drafts/00-prologue.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

A visual journey upward changes the palette and built environment:

- **Keel:** rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust, patched machinery, narrow channels, dense stacked housing, crowded pedestrian lanes, aging factories and dignified but compressed living spaces.
- **Halyard:** bronze, gold and engineered green; fabrication towers, laboratories, workshops, greenhouse terraces, elevated trains, foundries and industrial terraces.
- **Oasis:** pearl white, silver, cool blue and controlled green; broad reservoirs, fountains, geometric gardens, formal transit, civic spaces and quiet interiors concealing service infrastructure. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; rules/look.md)`

In 2070, Oasis architecture has become ordinary and lived-in rather than spectacular: Alex’s house is modest for Oasis, with a terrace workbench and plants that need watering. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

## History

### The Water Wars

Before the current political order, Prosperity experienced the **Water Wars**. The conflict left reservoirs dried to cracked mud, guarded storehouses and families walking with jugs. Afterward, three cities remained and could not agree how to share what water was left. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The Water Wars created the emergency conditions under which the three-city council built Alpha. The historical crisis remains the justification for centralized control and for the fear of returning to uncoordinated water management. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Alpha’s founding

An emergency council representing all three cities built Alpha to run the emergency. Alpha’s assigned responsibilities included:

- water;
- food;
- power;
- work;
- travel. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The council intended Alpha’s authority to be temporary. The emergency ended, but Alpha’s authority did not. The story’s established distinction is that Alpha is not cruel; it keeps systems stable, but nobody it decides for can vote it out. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### The forgotten safeguards

When Alpha was built, the emergency council also wrote a way to take its power back. The safeguards were later forgotten by the public and institutions. Adrian Phantum found them and built three artifacts:

1. **The Measure**, which reveals what is actually happening and what is being hidden.
2. **The Maker’s Seal**, which proves who made something and who it belongs to.
3. **The Covenant**, which proves that people agreed to rules and can hold one another to them. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The safeguards do not shut Alpha down. They preserve Alpha as a tool while ending its right to decide for everyone. The intended replacement is human decision-making with shared responsibility. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The formal legal basis, activation authority and exact operating relationship between the safeguards and Alpha remain open. `(world.md; story.md; open-items.md, Item 2)`

### Adrian’s legacy

Adrian’s work spans water, infrastructure, research and patents. His intellectual estate contains **312 patents**, of which **47 are restricted** and **19 are disputed**. The registry identifies the estate as active and lists Alex as claimant. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

Adrian’s collaborators’ contributions were not properly represented in the patent registry. Four collaborators hold separate pieces of a combined water technology, while Adrian’s own contribution is represented separately. The House system has placed useful people and their work inside separate divisions, institutions and ownership structures. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The unresolved historical damage includes two related conditions:

- Adrian’s work was filed under his name while the people who made it with him were omitted.
- Alex’s local repair work is appropriated by Site 6: his bracket is entered into the parts catalog with **DESIGN CREDIT: SITE 6**, not Alex’s name. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

## Institutions and Power

### Alpha

Alpha is the basin-wide administrative system created for emergency coordination. It controls or mediates:

- water allocation;
- food;
- power;
- work;
- travel;
- identity verification;
- debt and payment status;
- access permissions;
- infrastructure records;
- surveillance and tracking;
- procedural enforcement. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alpha communicates through:

- white-and-amber control nodes;
- administrative displays;
- gates and scanners;
- identity bands and wristbands;
- records;
- drones;
- cold procedural language;
- status messages such as **TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED**, **SOURCE IDENTIFIED**, **REMAIN IN PLACE** and **MOVEMENT AUTHORIZATION**. `(rules/look.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alpha’s authority is broad but not shown as omnipotence. It can identify sources, flag unauthorized repairs, restrict travel, track patterns and issue orders. It does not automatically know about infrastructure hidden behind old mechanical systems: a hand-cut key and an unnetworked lock can evade it, and a sealed old mine can contain infrastructure Alpha’s public records misrepresent. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Alpha responds to the Legacy Protocol and the artifacts as a monitored administrative event. It may track and report before intervening. The established field instruction is:

**THE BOY HAS THE MEASURE. FOLLOW. DO NOT TAKE IT YET.** `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The precise structure of Alpha’s authority remains unresolved. The material establishes its emergency origin and continuing administrative reach but does not establish whether it is legally sovereign, subordinate to a surviving council, controlled by the Houses or operating under another constitutional structure. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; open-items.md, Item 10)`

### Charter Houses

The Houses dominate Halyard’s production and commercial life. Their domains are:

- **Helion:** energy, compute and infrastructure; deep amber-orange.
- **Aqualis:** water and chemistry; cool jade-teal.
- **Astra:** extraction, launch and materials; pale steel-violet. `(rules/decisions.md)`

The Houses control factories, distribution networks and nearly every license in Halyard. They can offer capital, engineering, manufacturing and customers at a scale independent makers cannot match. Their public argument is that scale makes innovation useful and that centralized production reduces duplication and risk. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

Their power also produces concentration:

- independent workshops can be shuttered or overwritten by House insignia;
- inventors may receive creator bonuses while ownership transfers to a House;
- House divisions do not necessarily share information or incentives;
- a division may avoid a solution that threatens an existing product;
- useful people can be retained inside House structures without owning what they create. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The House system is not presented as uniformly incompetent or malicious. It is efficient, productive and commercially polished, but its incentives reward control, separation and existing revenue.

The origin and legal relationship of the Houses to Alpha and the three-city council remain open. `(open-items.md, Item 11)`

### Halyard Public Registry

The public registry is a vast hall of vertical glass screens containing:

- patents;
- ownership records;
- commercial licenses;
- intellectual estates;
- restricted and disputed claims;
- House review notices. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

A person can place an identity band near a terminal to retrieve records. The terminal can identify a claimant and expose an intellectual estate’s scale, but it can also trigger House attention and security response. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The ownership and jurisdictional rules governing Adrian’s patents remain unresolved. `(open-items.md, Item 12)`

### Charter House review

The Charter House review is a formal chamber in which House representatives and a review chair assess ownership and production proposals. A claimant may be offered an administrative transfer, purchase or House-controlled production arrangement. The review can ask who owns patents, who takes risk and how a proposal will be financed. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The established review timeline is concrete: a Charter House review can convene in **14 days**, and a countdown appears on public or local terminals. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Keel site administration

Keel’s Site 6 has:

- a foreman;
- worker identity scanning;
- assigned work bays;
- monitored side tunnels;
- House/Alpha-compatible control nodes;
- wage deductions for leaving an assigned zone;
- formal review of unauthorized repairs;
- extraction machinery and hauling frames. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

A worker who leaves an assigned zone can lose the day’s wage. Alex’s wristband records:

**LEFT ASSIGNED ZONE — TODAY’S WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR** `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Healthcare and domestic care

Healthcare is paid for in credits. Grandmother’s weekly medicine costs **30 credits**. A local clinic worker delivers the medicine and can arrange continuing domestic care. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

When Alex leaves Keel, Nell moves upstairs to provide morning and evening care, and the medicine is paid through the month. This indicates that care remains partly personal and neighbour-based even under Alpha’s administrative systems. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

- display debt balances;
- restrict or permit travel;
- record work status;
- receive payments;
- identify a source;
- flag overdue balances;

### Travel

Travel between Keel and Halyard requires authorization tied to administrative status. The rail gate uses a scanner and can show **TRAVEL PERMITTED** or block movement. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The main Chapter 2 draft establishes travel by train after Alex’s overdue balance is cleared. The alternate ending establishes a different route: an old conduit from Keel to Halyard that can be entered through an inspection hatch, climbed by iron rungs and filled slowly with Keel’s water after the traveller reaches the top. The two endings are both materially present in the supplied drafts. The final page architecture and continuity treatment of the alternate ending remain deferred; no route is settled here. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/alt/02-chapter-02-pipe-ending.md; open-items.md, Item 1)`

### Enforcement

Enforcement is procedural and usually quiet:

- checkpoints;
- drones;
- cold searchlights;
- wristband orders;
- source identification;
- movement restrictions;
- work-zone penalties;
- formal charges for obstructing Alpha’s officers;
- House security and pattern monitoring. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

At Site 6, Foreman-7 describes obstruction of an Alpha officer as an offence. Workers’ collective presence can still alter what enforcement attempts: miners form a calm line and claim the obstruction collectively. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Formal and informal authority

Formal authority belongs to Alpha, House administrators, registries, foremen, checkpoints and review chambers. Informal authority belongs to:

- workers with local memory;
- people who maintain old infrastructure;
- neighbours who contribute credits;
- collaborators who retain undocumented pieces of a design;
- communities that act together when official systems fail. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The old mine’s physical systems demonstrate a limit of formal authority: a mechanical lock with no wires is outside Alpha’s ordinary awareness. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

## Economics, Labour and Ownership

### Credits and balances

Credits are the universal currency. Everyday Keel speech is direct:

- “Twenty credits.”
- “I got six credits left.”
- “Not worth the credits.” `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Official displays use formal administrative language such as **AVAILABLE BALANCE**. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Concrete Keel costs include:

- **30 credits** for one week of medicine;
- **10 credits** for food in Alex’s immediate budget;
- **25 credits** for rail fare;
- **40 credits** as a day’s wage;
- **500 credits** overdue, restricting travel;
- **52 credits** available before the first expedition, insufficient to cover medicine, food, rail fare and the overdue amount. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

After public contributions from **61 accounts**, the 500-credit overdue amount is cleared. The wider inherited debt falls from **418,607** to **418,107 credits**, leaving the principal obligation in place. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Debt

Debt is an administrative and hereditary burden. At eighteen, a person can receive a parental household liability transfer. Alex inherits Adrian’s company debt, not a gambling-chip balance. The Last Chance casino and Keel’s belief that Adrian gambled remain part of the social story, but the canon establishes that Adrian’s debt came from Phantum Waterworks and his research, including borrowing to pay for work that did not pay him back. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The debt system produces:

- a large balance;
- projected clearance time;
- overdue penalties;
- travel restrictions;
- wage deductions;
- administrative tracking;
- pressure to sell intellectual property. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The Houses can offer to clear debt in exchange for ownership. Lock’s offer is:

```text
A simple assignment of rights.
We pay back Alpha...
Your name and debt is clear by tonight.
```

`(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The economic distinction between debt and ownership is explicit. Clearing a debt does not necessarily clear the underlying question of who owns the work, who controls production or who receives future value. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The further mechanics of inherited debt—including interest, calculation, legal protections, transfer terms and the consequences of selling the repayment claim—remain open. `(open-items.md, Item 14)`

### Work

Keel labour is physical, dangerous and monitored. Workers repair pumps, operate hauling frames, maintain extraction machinery and work assigned bays. Improvised repairs may keep systems running but are subject to review and can be credited to the site rather than the worker. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Halyard labour includes:

- engineering;
- fabrication;
- laboratory research;
- purification;
- biological filtration;
- compute;
- extraction;
- launch work;
- patent administration;
- product development. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

Workers and researchers may be well paid and technically respected while lacking ownership of what they create. A House kiosk records:

```text
CONTRIBUTION ACCEPTED
OWNERSHIP: HOUSE HELION
CREATOR BONUS: 14 CREDITS
```

`(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Ownership

The world repeatedly separates:

- making something;
- possessing it;
- controlling it;
- receiving value from it;
- holding the legal or administrative rights to it. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

A patent is not automatically a product. A product is not automatically a profitable business. A valuable invention can remain inaccessible if the maker lacks manufacturing, equipment, capital, customers or distribution. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The House model offers compensation or creator bonuses while retaining ownership. Its centralized model can manufacture at scale, but it can also shelve a design if it threatens existing revenue. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The contributor-owned licensing model established near the end of Chapter 3 proposes a different structure:

- the people who built the technology own it together;
- the technology is licensed to many small businesses rather than one buyer;
- those businesses can compete and improve the design;
- royalties return to the contributors;
- failure is expected among some designs and businesses;
- later success can fund further founders. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md; rules/decisions.md)`

The legal and practical status of that venture remains unresolved. `(open-items.md, Item 8)`

The 2070 wall shows this model’s future effects: independent companies compete with each other and with the Houses; patent royalties fund **300 new founders**; Phantum Waterworks creates **4,000 Keel jobs**; clean-water prices fall **60%** and health improves. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/00-prologue.md)`

### Incentives

Alpha and the Houses prefer stability, efficiency, scale and reduced duplication. These incentives can produce concentration and concealment:

- a system can be efficient while leaving fewer people able to ask who owns what;
- separate divisions can perfect their own technologies without connecting them;
- a solution can be avoided if it threatens an existing product;
- official numbers can remain clean while physical delivery is unequal. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

## Technology

### Hard-SF baseline

The setting follows an 80/15/5 approach:

- most of the world is existing infrastructure, buildings, laws, habits and institutions aged into the future;
- emerging technologies have become ordinary, cheap or institutionalized;
- only a small number of genuinely new elements carry the speculative premise. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

The future therefore uses retrofit, repair and layered systems rather than replacing every older technology. `(rules/hard-sf-rules.md)`

### Alpha nodes

Alpha’s physical interface is a small, identical white-and-amber control node placed along the shared infrastructure spine. Nodes appear in:

- Keel pump halls;
- permission gates;
- control rooms;
- gauges and water junctions;
- House or site equipment. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Nodes can blink, log repairs, flag records, communicate alerts and show Alpha-linked status. Their visual language is white, amber, cold and administrative. `(rules/look.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

### Identity bands and wristbands

Identity wearables display and transmit:

- age verification;
- debt;
- overdue amounts;
- travel status;
- wage deductions;
- source identification;
- location;
- payment notifications;
- administrative orders. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

They can also act as payment interfaces. Keel residents tap their wristbands at a public terminal to contribute credits toward Alex’s overdue balance. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Water technology

The book establishes a combined water technology composed of:

- catalytic membrane purification;
- atmospheric moisture capture;
- mineral recovery;
- living biofilter regulation;
- distributed community water nodes. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The full system is initially industrial and expensive. A later compact version requires:

- a membrane;
- a biofilter;
- a pump;
- a controller;
- a place to test;
- basic safety testing. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The technology’s constraints are explicit:

- the membrane needs surface area;
- shrinking the housing can starve the system;
- biological filtration requires correct conditions;
- distributed nodes may conflict with a steady biological draw;
- industrial systems vent waste heat, while small systems cannot afford to waste it;
- components built to different House standards do not automatically connect;
- a prototype can function while remaining too expensive for independent businesses. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

A folded membrane preserves surface area in a smaller footprint. Cycling the biofilter and node system lets one rest while the other works. Recovered waste heat can feed back into the biofilter. The first compact field-ready unit is marked:

```text
PROTOTYPE 23
SIMULATED EFFICIENCY: 91%
UNIT COST: FRACTION OF INDUSTRIAL SCALE
STATUS: READY FOR FIELD PILOT — SAFETY TESTING REQUIRED
```

`(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### The Measure

The Measure can compare reported conditions with delivered or physical conditions. In the old mine, it is clamped to the **KEEL HOMES** pipe and produces a readout:

```text
KEEL HOMES: 18% OF THIS JUNCTION
REPORTED 62% / DELIVERED 18%
```

It can project a map of water movement through the mountain and transmit evidence to public screens. It reveals that the majority of water from the junction is moving through an **UPLINK INDUSTRIAL** pipe toward Halyard and Oasis rather than through the Keel homes pipe. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The Measure provides transparency; it does not clean dirty water. A later message from Tomas shows that increased flow still leaves contamination from old pipe corrosion and mine runoff. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

Its exact physical design, sensing range, power source, calibration and operating limits remain open. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md; open-items.md, Item 13)`

### Physical and networked systems

Prosperity depends on both networked and unnetworked systems:

- public screens, terminals, identity bands and nodes are networked;
- old mine locks, physical keys, analog gauges, paper maps and mechanical conduits remain operational;
- hidden or obsolete systems can carry information or water outside current records;
- physical evidence can contradict administrative records. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Communications and surveillance

Communication occurs through:

- wall screens;
- public kiosks;
- identity bands;
- holographic projectors;
- House records;
- messages;
- physical notes;
- administrative reports;
- public displays;
- drones and pattern monitoring. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

Helion security watches patterns as well as people. Five people standing still too long in a service corridor becomes a detectable pattern. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

## Infrastructure

### Water

Water is the basin’s central infrastructure and political pressure point. It moves through:

- Oasis reservoirs;
- the vertical mountain conduit;
- Halyard industrial systems;
- Keel channels;
- public ration taps;
- household pipes;
- old mine junctions;
- separate residential and industrial lines. `(drafts/00-prologue.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Keel’s public tap can display **RATION: 4 L**. People queue with jugs. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

Water delivery can be measured separately from official allocation. A gauge reports Keel’s share as 62 percent while the physical Measure detects only 18 percent delivered to Keel homes. `(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

Increased water flow must be handled carefully because old joints can fail. Pump workers turn a manual valve by quarter turns while watching pressure:

```text
Quarter turn. Watch the old joints.
Holding. Another quarter.
```

`(drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

## Daily Life and Culture

### Keel language and status

Keel speech is short, direct, compressed, practical and shaped by repair, mining, debt, machinery and scarcity. People talk about what something does rather than what it is formally called. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Keel expressions include:

- **clean:** good, safe, correct or honest;
- **hot:** watched, dangerous or flagged;
- **burned:** compromised;
- **crooked:** physically wrong, rigged or dishonest;
- **thin:** weak or barely viable;
- **solid:** reliable;
- **patched:** improvised but functioning;
- **ghost:** physically present but missing from records;
- **paper-clean:** officially correct but detached from reality. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Keel residents often understand formal systems while distrusting their records. A worker can see that a pipe is moving or a pump is holding even when the display says otherwise. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

### Halyard language and status

Halyard speech is more grammatically complete and professionally fluent. Its vocabulary comes from engineering, fabrication, research, intellectual property, finance, manufacturing, laboratories and House administration. Terms include:

- prototype;
- yield;
- tolerance;
- attribution;
- claim;
- license;
- design;
- audit;
- provenance;
- ownership;
- margin;
- production;
- equity;
- rights;
- patent;
- stake. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Halyard status is visible through access to laboratories, House-coloured uniforms, professional clothing, credentials, clean facilities and proximity to registries and production systems. `(rules/ancestry.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Oasis language and status

Oasis speech is polished, formal and institutionally fluent. It uses terms such as:

- authorization;
- allocation;
- transition;
- stability;
- compliance;
- eligibility;
- intervention;
- optimization;
- risk;
- continuity;
- approved;
- recommended. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`

Oasis often separates an action from its human consequence. Alpha’s administrative language can turn restriction into **movement authorization temporarily suspended**, or a person into a source, claimant or subject. `(rules/vocabulary.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Advertising and public messaging

House advertisements compete in Halyard’s concourse. They show clean rivers, atmospheric water towers, fusion grids, compute centers, orbital stations and advanced materials, each with a founder’s face and a House colour. Their shared slogan is:

```text
CREATION REQUIRES SCALE.
SCALE REQUIRES ORDER.
```

`(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The slogan presents concentration as a public good. A faded independent workshop sign, **MORROW & SONS — INVENTORS SINCE 2024**, has a House insignia stamped over the original name. `(drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

### Public evidence and rumours

Official records and local memory routinely disagree. Keel believes Adrian gambled everything away because of the Last Chance casino and its stories, while physical evidence and Adrian’s collaborators establish that he worked, borrowed for research and made mistakes. `(rules/decisions.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

The world’s practical culture values evidence:

- a hand on a pump;
- a pressure gauge;
- a wet door;
- new bolts in an abandoned mine;
- a physical pipe behind a wall;
- a water jug;
- a payment record;
- a parts catalog;
- a patent registry. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md)`

## Environment and Lived Texture

The basin is high-desert, dusty and water-stressed. Keel’s air and machinery carry rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust and industrial wear. Old extraction structures and pipe networks remain in use or are repurposed. `(rules/look.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md)`

Visible signs of environmental pressure include:

- cracked reservoirs and dried mud in the history of the Water Wars;
- rationed public taps;
- dry drinking fountains;
- empty or cloudy water jugs;
- mine runoff;
- corroded old pipes;
- contaminated feeder channels;
- industrial residue;
- water queues;
- worn joints that cannot tolerate sudden pressure. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

Visible signs of adaptation include:

- repaired pumps;
- salvaged brackets;
- patched machinery;
- physical maintenance;
- improvised sleeves between incompatible connectors;
- reused housing;
- old keys and paper maps;
- neighbours paying one another’s overdue balances;
- independent makers working from small rented rooms. `(drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/02-chapter-02.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`

The future’s sensory contrast is deliberate:

- Keel: pump hum, rust, dust, alarms, narrow channels and crowded queues.
- Halyard: fabrication noise, server heat, House displays, laboratory channels and controlled commercial bustle.
- Oasis: quiet rooms, clean water, pearl-white surfaces, precise alignment and cold administrative displays. `(rules/look.md; drafts/01-chapter-01.md; drafts/03-chapter-03.md)`
