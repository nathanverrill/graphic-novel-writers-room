# prosperity-book — Development Brief

## 1. Logline

On his eighteenth birthday, a Keel mechanic inherits his absent father’s crushing debt and discovers a hidden trail that exposes stolen water, stolen credit, and a system built to decide people’s lives for them; to escape, he must choose whether to sell his father’s legacy or share its power.

## 2. Theme

Useful systems become unjust when the people who depend on them cannot question them, own their work, or share responsibility for the consequences.

## 3. Tone & comps

- **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** — ecological pressure, humane technology, and systems larger than any one person.
- **The Expanse** — class-shaped infrastructure, political consequence, and hard-used future technology.
- **Erin Brockovich** — evidence made public by an ordinary person whose persistence turns private harm into collective action.

The book is hopeful without being soft, politically legible without becoming a lecture, and skeptical of both concentrated capital and unaccountable administration.

## 4. Audience & rating

**Audience:** Skeptical high-school readers, ages 15–18, with crossover appeal for adults.

**Rating:** Teen. Class inequality, debt, environmental contamination, workplace danger, surveillance, dementia, institutional coercion, and implied past conflict. No graphic gore, sexual content, or profanity is required.

The immediate objective of every sequence must remain clear. Mystery may invite the reader forward, but the reader must not need to solve an opaque riddle to understand what Alex is trying to do.

## 5. Format

- **62-page graphic novel**, pages 1–62, no more and no fewer.
- Prologue, *The Wall*: pages 1–4.
- Chapter One, *The Inheritance*: pages 5–16, 12 pages.
- Chapter Two, *The Price of Departure*: pages 17–35, 19 pages.
- Chapter Three, *The Price of Ownership*: pages 36–62, 27 pages.
- Full colour, standard portrait graphic-novel format.
- The primary bound narrative uses the train ending for Chapter Two.
- The pipe ending remains separately drawn comparison material, not a second route in the primary story and not an additional page count.

Bob’s draft remains the page plot. Dialogue, captions, image notes, punctuation, and on-screen text are not to be substantively rewritten during execution. Any continuity concern is flagged rather than silently repaired.

## 6. Visual direction

Inked graphic-novel realism: bold contours, textured hatching, matte painterly colour, expressive acting, tactile architecture, and gritty handmade texture. Every character has one clear visual focus in a panel.

Prosperity is a hard-used future, not a sleek one. Present-day infrastructure has been repaired, automated, institutionalized, and layered over older systems. Show pressure, wear, queues, tools, gauges, water jugs, records, gates, surveillance, hands, and consequences before explanatory interfaces.

The visual journey climbs **Keel → Halyard → Oasis**:

- **Keel:** rust, charcoal, oxidized orange, dust, narrow channels, dense housing, patched machinery, crowded lanes, and dignified compression.
- **Halyard:** bronze, gold, engineered green, laboratories, fabrication towers, House signage, greenhouses, server heat, and controlled commercial bustle.
- **Oasis:** pearl white, silver, cool blue, controlled green, reservoirs, geometric gardens, quiet interiors, precise alignment, and administrative distance.

Prosperity’s populations are mixed throughout the basin. Ancestry is never shorthand for intelligence, morality, competence, poverty, wealth, political position, or support for Alpha. Class, access, ownership, occupation, education, health, dress, grooming, speech, environment, and institutional power create the principal visual differences.

No reference images were supplied. The visual locks in the approved character and world files are the reference standard.

## 7. Canon

The following is binding for the whole room.

### Story and time

- The main story begins in **2043**, on Alex Phantum’s eighteenth birthday and Debt Transfer Day.
- The prologue takes place around **2070**, with Alex in his mid-forties.
- The prologue is an earned, hopeful future: all three cities are green, healthy, and prosperous; Keel’s pit has become terraced gardens and a market.
- Alex begins wanting to leave Keel. He chooses Tomas over an easier escape, broadcasts the Measure’s evidence, accepts Keel’s collective help, and later chooses shared ownership over Lock’s debt-clearing offer.
- The book ends with the Measure and Maker’s Seal found, the Covenant still ahead, Alex’s debt still present, first licensing revenue received, and Mera requiring Alex to come to her directly.
- The prologue’s future is the destination, not a claim that the larger Legacy Protocol story is complete.

### World

- Prosperity is one Andean high-desert basin organized vertically into Keel, Halyard, and Oasis.
- The cities share a vertical infrastructure spine of water, energy, transit, and control systems.
- The Water Wars preceded Alpha’s creation.
- A three-city emergency council built Alpha to manage water, food, power, work, and travel. The emergency ended; Alpha’s authority remained.
- Alpha is not a human-like villain. It has no face, body, or human consciousness. It appears through white-and-amber nodes, records, gates, displays, drones, data lines, and procedural language.
- Alpha remains a tool in the intended safeguard outcome; the safeguards do not switch off essential infrastructure.
- The Charter Houses are Helion—energy, compute, infrastructure; Aqualis—water and chemistry; and Astra—extraction, launch, materials. Their fixed colours are deep amber-orange, cool jade-teal, and pale steel-violet.
- Money is called **credits** everywhere.
- Debt is inherited through the parental household liability system. Alex’s transferred balance is **418,607 credits**, with projected clearance in **28.7 years**. The **500-credit overdue balance** restricts travel; Keel clears that overdue amount through 61 small contributions, leaving **418,107 credits**.
- The primary Chapter Two route is the authorized Keel–Halyard train.
- Adrian’s estate contains **312 patents**, including **47 restricted** and **19 disputed** claims.
- The contributor-owned licensing venture is a real choice and operating intention, but its eventual legal success is not guaranteed within this book.

### Safeguards

- **The Measure** reveals the difference between reported and delivered conditions. It exposes Keel’s reported **62%** allocation versus **18%** physical delivery and broadcasts evidence; it does not purify water.
- **The Maker’s Seal** proves shared authorship and ownership. It activates through the five contributors’ shared action, with Adrian’s watch completing the fifth socket.
- **The Covenant** proves that people have agreed to rules and can hold one another to them. It is the safeguard that converts human agreement into enforceable shared authority.
- The safeguards are a revocation mechanism deliberately concealed in old infrastructure and distributed among people rather than held by one institution.
- The Covenant is physically held or controlled by Mera Vale in Oasis. Its exact object design is deliberately withheld from this book so the reveal remains available to the next volume.
- Activation requires deliberate human use and public responsibility, not mere possession of three objects.

### Character and continuity locks

All fixed descriptions, ancestry rules, speech rules, established lines, relationships, and visual details in `characters.md` are canon. In particular:

- Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended; his mother remains entirely unrevealed beyond leaving Adrian after he lost everything.
- Alex wears Adrian’s oversized steel watch on his left wrist from the workshop onward.
- Ada is mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla, with copper hair, freckles, light teal-blue eyes, and strong Andean facial structure.
- Bi11bot’s chest marking is **Bi11**. He is warm, worn, expressive, and never menacing.
- Adrian’s ultimate fate remains unknown. His absence is not canonically explained as death, capture, or voluntary disappearance.
- Mera’s authority and motives remain unknown beyond her connection to the Covenant, Alpha monitoring, and the standing order to follow Alex without taking the Measure.
- The daughter’s settled prologue design is warm-brown skin, freckles, brown eyes, long curly dark-brown hair, orange **H2O** jacket, teal ringed-planet shirt, dark cargo trousers, orange-and-black sneakers, and smartwatch.
- No new ancestry, family history, or mother-story may be invented to fill visual or dramatic space.

## 10. Non-negotiables

- Exactly 62 pages in the primary book.
- The draft’s scenes remain in draft order; no new scenes, cuts, or rearrangements.
- The showrunner’s words and explicit decisions outrank all room preferences.
- The book is hopeful but does not claim the larger conflict is solved.
- Alex’s choices, not Adrian’s instructions or Bi11bot’s explanations, drive the story.
- Evidence must be concrete and visual: pumps, pipes, gauges, water, records, tools, payments, and bodies in space.
- Alpha is procedural and systemic, never a humanoid villain.
- The three cities are mixed populations, never ethnic enclaves.
- The watch is a keepsake and technical key, never currency.
- The Measure exposes; it does not purify.
- The Maker’s Seal responds to shared contribution, not a lone heir.
- The Covenant remains the unresolved next step.
- No one may reveal Alex’s mother, Adrian’s final fate, Mera’s full allegiance, or the debt buyer as settled fact.
- The final image and emotional endpoint remain Mera’s direct challenge from Oasis.
