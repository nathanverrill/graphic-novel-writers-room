# Prosperity — Characters

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is eighteen in 2043, lean, medium-height and mechanically capable, with mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended features, black hair, blue-grey eyes, a strong jaw inherited partly from Adrian, and a compact, work-hardened silhouette. He wears worn charcoal mining clothes, scuffed work boots, a weathered tool bag and a patched work jacket; from the hidden workshop onward, Adrian’s oversized worn steel wristwatch is buckled on his left wrist, with a plain cream dial, scratched glass, normally dark display window and looping equation engraved on the caseback. His signature detail is his testing hand: palm flat on pumps, pipes and prototypes, feeling vibration before trusting a gauge. In 2070, Alex is in his mid-forties, grey at the temples, with a day’s stubble, a worn brown work jacket over a dark henley, jeans and brown boots; he remains dressed like a mechanic rather than a polished executive, and the watch remains on his left wrist.

**Voice** — Short, dry, concrete and skeptical; he speaks through machinery, pressure, wear and immediate consequences, becoming sarcastic when defensive and uncomfortable with abstractions. “Then don't approve it. Just notice it works.” “It's not mine to sign away. It was never just mine to find.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Leave Keel and choose his own future.
- **Contradiction:** He wants escape, but his instinct is to stop and repair what is failing, even when helping costs him.
- **Leverage:** Mechanical intuition, practical invention, physical courage and the ability to make incompatible systems work together.
- **Vulnerability:** Inherited debt, Adrian’s absence, Grandmother’s care and the fear that his useful work will always belong to someone else.
- **Mistaken belief:** He initially believes freedom means getting out of Keel and that Adrian’s legacy is primarily a debt.
- **Boundary:** He will not knowingly abandon someone trapped in immediate danger or sign away other people’s work as if it were his.
- **Decision only Alex would make:** He broadcasts the Measure’s evidence despite knowing Alpha can locate him, then later builds a salvaged connector sleeve rather than choosing one House standard.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Ada forces him to see the human cost behind systems; Adrian’s unfinished legacy forces him to distinguish inheritance from ownership.
  
**Arc in this book** — Alex moves from escape toward chosen responsibility, from private evidence toward public responsibility, and from asking what Adrian left him toward asking who built the value and who should own it. He chooses Tomas, the Keel broadcast and contributor-owned licensing over quiet departure, private debt relief or House ownership. **Proof of change:** He leaves Keel still owing it, rejects Lock’s offer despite the debt, and helps authorize a venture owned by all five contributors.

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is eighteen, medium-height and lean, with mixed Indigenous Andean and European/criolla ancestry, strong Andean facial structure, shoulder-length copper hair, freckles and light teal-blue eyes. She wears a dark high-collared top, a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, a muted moss-green wrap at her neck, practical dark trousers and worn boots; her rooted stance and wind-tangled hair give her a steady, unmistakably Keel silhouette. Her signature detail is a hand-copied notebook and map, always carried close and marked with practical corrections.

**Voice** — Short, direct and colloquial like Keel speech, but centered on people, obligations, avoidance and what is not being said. “Not everything has a price, Alex.” “I'm not leaving Keel behind. I'm going to find out who decided it had to stay this way.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Make Keel worth staying in and understand who decided it had to remain deprived.
- **Contradiction:** She distrusts leaving, yet chooses to travel upward so she can return with knowledge and leverage.
- **Leverage:** Social perception, local memory, map-reading, practical organization and the ability to make people see the human cost of a decision.
- **Vulnerability:** Water scarcity, loyalty to Keel and the risk of being treated as someone else’s helper rather than an actor.
- **Mistaken belief:** She initially believes change requires people to stay and fight locally; she underestimates what leaving can reveal.
- **Boundary:** She will not let Alex turn people’s lives into an abstract cost calculation.
- **Decision only Ada would make:** She organizes dozens of small Keel payments—five, two or one credit—rather than allowing Alex’s departure to become a private escape.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s urge to leave forces her to test whether departure can serve Keel rather than betray it.

**Arc in this book** — Ada shifts from demanding that Keel become worth staying in to traveling upward to investigate the system that made it otherwise. **Proof of change:** She boards the train while explicitly refusing to leave Keel behind and becomes part of the contributor-owned alternative.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is European-descended, medium-tall and slender, with a recognizable family resemblance to Alex through his jaw, blue-grey eyes, head tilt and habitual hand gestures. In photographs and holographic recordings he wears a long dark coat over a vest and tie, with rolled sleeves when working; his silhouette combines formal inventor with hands-on mechanic. His signature detail is the looping equation he writes on workshop boards, engraves on his steel watch and stamps into the old mine key.

**Voice** — Direct, apologetic and reflective, offering choices rather than commands and never explaining the whole mystery. “I made one bad call. I thought I was right. But I was wrong.” “Whatever he tells you... question everything. Especially me.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Preserve a way for people to reclaim authority over Alpha and protect shared work from concentrated ownership.
- **Contradiction:** He believes in accountable choice but repeatedly makes unilateral decisions that burden his family and collaborators.
- **Leverage:** Engineering skill, research, patents, collaborators, hidden infrastructure and long preparation.
- **Vulnerability:** Debt, gambling, overconfidence, failed research and the damage caused by his absence.
- **Mistaken belief:** He believed he could be right about the system before fully understanding the human consequences.
- **Boundary:** He will not leave the safeguards in one person’s hands.
- **Decision only Adrian would make:** He distributes the safeguards and keeps design fragments outside the official system, trusting a future collaboration rather than a single heir.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s eventual judgment pressures Adrian even in absence.

**Arc in this book** — Adrian changes in the story’s evidence from public scandal to flawed researcher, collaborator and unfinished political thinker. **Proof of change or refusal:** His message explicitly admits failure and tells Alex to find a better way; his ultimate fate remains unknown.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a rugged, weathered industrial robot about 85 centimetres tall, with an oversized round head made from segmented faded industrial-blue panels, visible seams, two large round amber optical eyes in heavy metal rings, side-mounted round ear assemblies, a small slim-limbed body, exposed pistons, articulated metal fingers, compact legs, broad feet, chipped mustard-yellow paint and stripe, exposed steel, dark joints, rivets, scratches and rust, and an old red scarf. His chest plate is marked **Bi11**. The heavy ring around his right eye clicks or rotates when he focuses. He can fold into a backpack. He is warm, worn and expressive, never menacing.

**Voice** — Dry, economical, patient and precise, with warmth underneath; he teaches through concrete example, question and consequence rather than lectures. “The water hasn't changed. Only what people know.” “A patent isn't a product. And a product isn't necessarily a profitable business.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Help Alex understand Adrian’s evidence without deciding Alex’s future for him.
- **Contradiction:** He is designed to calculate and explain, but the central problem is human choice that cannot be solved by calculation.
- **Leverage:** Maps, records, projections, engineering knowledge, financial reasoning and access to Adrian’s archive.
- **Vulnerability:** Dormancy, outdated systems, limited physical strength and dependence on human choices.
- **Mistaken belief:** He can initially treat a well-framed choice as sufficient, underestimating how much trust and courage choice requires.
- **Boundary:** He will not choose on Alex’s behalf.
- **Decision only Bi11bot would make:** He presents Alex with the choice between quiet escape and broadcasting the Measure, then waits.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex repeatedly proves that human action matters more than Bi11bot’s preferred calculation.

**Arc in this book** — Bi11bot changes from dormant workshop artifact and messenger into an active companion who helps Alex reason while surrendering control of the outcome. **Proof of change:** He repeatedly names consequences but leaves the Measure broadcast and ownership decision to Alex.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is an elderly European-descended woman rooted in Keel, with curly grey hair pinned up, a lined face, a worn red woven Andean shawl and practical layered clothing. She sits in a worn chair in the repaired apartment and repeatedly folds and refolds a small cloth. Her signature detail is her steady, suddenly lucid gaze when dementia’s fog clears.

**Voice** — Repetitive and uncertain when memory slips, but dry, lucid and deliberate in clear moments. “He's closer than you think.” “Go. You've lived long enough inside his unfinished story.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Protect Alex while preserving Adrian’s last trust.
- **Contradiction:** She appears dependent and confused, yet has deliberately guarded the workshop’s entrance.
- **Leverage:** Domestic authority, memory fragments and control of the book that opens the hidden room.
- **Vulnerability:** Dementia, physical dependence and the losses Adrian left behind.
- **Mistaken belief:** She sometimes mistakes Alex for Adrian, briefly collapsing the difference between the son she lost and the grandson she must release.
- **Boundary:** She will not force Alex to remain trapped by Adrian’s unfinished story.
- **Decision only she would make:** She sends Alex to the book while calmly allowing the bookshelf to open.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s need to leave forces her to exchange protection for release.

**Arc in this book** — She moves from apparently confused dependent to lucid keeper of Adrian’s trust, then actively releases Alex. **Proof of change:** She tells him to go and accepts Nell’s care rather than using her vulnerability to keep him home.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an older Indigenous Andean Keel miner with a broad, work-hardened build, weathered face, close-cropped dark-and-grey hair and a short grey beard. He wears patched mine coveralls, heavy boots, gloves and a carved wrist guard marked with a balanced water-drop. His signature detail is the water-drop wrist guard, worn over the arm that once repaired pumps beside Adrian.

**Voice** — Direct, practical and economical, with long memory but no mysticism. “Not the man on the casino wall. The one who came down here and asked what we needed.” “You stopped for a stranger. He said that's how I'd know it was you.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Protect the old works and the people whose water and labour are being misrepresented.
- **Contradiction:** He has survived by keeping quiet, but protecting the door requires a public risk.
- **Leverage:** Local memory, worker trust, pump knowledge and access to the old lock.
- **Vulnerability:** Workplace punishment, injury and the danger of exposing Adrian’s hidden infrastructure.
- **Mistaken belief:** He initially believes secrecy is the only way to preserve anything useful.
- **Boundary:** He will not let Alex be punished alone for a collective truth.
- **Decision only he would make:** He takes blame for the emergency lever and later declares the miners’ obstruction collective.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s decision to save him turns guarded trust into public solidarity.

**Arc in this book** — Tomas moves from isolated keeper of Adrian’s secret to visible participant in Keel’s collective action. **Proof of change:** He leads the miners’ line, opens the valve carefully and explains why Keel pays Alex’s overdue balance.

**Arc in this book** — Lina moves from guarded custodian of a hidden fragment to named co-owner of a public contributor venture. **Proof of change:** She refuses Lock’s safe purchase and activates the Maker’s Seal with the others.

**Arc in this book** — Suri moves from isolated researcher to active participant in a shared technical and ownership structure. **Proof of change:** She joins the five-person venture and helps make the compact unit viable.

### REN OKAFOR

**Arc in this book** — Ren moves from independent maker outside institutional rules to committed co-owner of a network designed to let other makers build too. **Proof of change:** She joins the venture and signs the shared authorization.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock** — Director Cassian Lock is a middle-aged Halyard corporate director with a tall, controlled build, silver temples, a clean-shaven face and composed eyes. He wears charcoal and platinum formal clothing with no House crest, polished shoes and discreet technological accessories; his silhouette is defined by stillness, clean lines and institutional access rather than ornament. His signature detail is his habit of keeping his hands loosely clasped while looking at the work or record instead of the person.

**Voice** — Polished, patient and formal, presenting concentrated ownership as practical responsibility rather than threat. “An invention isn't a business.” “The Houses would own the technology of course.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Bring Adrian’s work and Alex’s connector breakthrough under House ownership and production.
- **Contradiction:** He genuinely wants useful technology deployed, but assumes usefulness requires concentrated control.
- **Leverage:** Capital, factories, engineers, customers, deadlines, debt relief and institutional legitimacy.
- **Vulnerability:** A model that works without House ownership and the possibility that scale is no longer the only route to impact.
- **Mistaken belief:** He believes independent makers cannot survive without surrendering control.
- **Boundary:** He will not abandon the House system’s ownership claim merely because Alex objects.
- **Decision only he would make:** He offers a real debt-clearing path without overt coercion, trusting the practicality of the offer to do the work.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex’s refusal forces Lock to recognize a competing model without proving it yet.

**Arc in this book** — Lock moves from confident recruiter to watchful recalculator. **Proof of refusal to change:** He acknowledges the prototype’s value but continues to defend House ownership and scale.

### ALPHA

**Visual lock** — Alpha has no face, body or human-like consciousness. Alpha appears through small identical white-and-amber control nodes, cold administrative displays, gates, records, data lines, drones, tracking rings and procedural status messages distributed through infrastructure. Its signature detail is the repeated white-and-amber node appearing in Keel machinery, travel gates, mine equipment, House systems and Oasis control rooms.

**Voice** — Alpha communicates through formal, impersonal system language rather than a human voice. “SOURCE IDENTIFIED: ALEX PHANTUM / LOCATION: OLD WORKS, SITE 6 / REMAIN IN PLACE.” “TRAVEL STATUS: RESTRICTED.”

**Causality**
- **Want:** Maintain stable basin-wide allocation, work, travel and infrastructure according to inherited emergency procedures.
- **Contradiction:** It was built as a temporary tool for human survival but retained authority after the emergency ended.
- **Leverage:** Water, food, power, work assignments, debt, travel permissions, records, surveillance and procedural enforcement.
- **Vulnerability:** Unnetworked old infrastructure, collective human action and safeguards that can limit its right to decide.
- **Mistaken belief:** Its inherited administrative model treats stability as sufficient legitimacy.
- **Boundary:** It does not shut down essential infrastructure simply because the safeguards are activated.
- **Decision only Alpha would make:** It tracks the Legacy Protocol and identifies Alex while following the standing order not to take the Measure yet.
- **Relationship that pressures change:** Alex and the Keel community expose the difference between administrative records and lived delivery.

**Arc in this book** — Alpha changes from invisible background authority to visible system whose legitimacy is contested. **Proof of refusal to change:** Its procedures continue to restrict, record and monitor; whether the safeguards can transform its authority remains unresolved.
