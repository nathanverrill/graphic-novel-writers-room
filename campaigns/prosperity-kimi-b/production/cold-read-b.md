# Cold Read B — Prosperity

Read cold, pages only, night before it's due. I did not want to like this. I read Issue 01 first, then 02, then 03, in order.

---

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
Page 2, panel 3. The scanner display — "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS" — got me immediately. That's a whole world in one screen, and the guard saying "Happy birthday, Phantum" is the first line in the book that made me actually react. I was in from there.

Page 5, the bookshelf clicking open. I know this trick from a hundred stories and it still worked, mostly because the grandmother just keeps folding her cloth like it's nothing.

Page 14–15, the cable snap and Tomas pinned. First real action, and the wristband flashing "WAGE FORFEITED: −40 CR" while he's saving a guy is the best beat in the issue — the system punishing him for doing the right thing, in numbers.

Page 17–18, the two pipes. One dry, one trembling, and the gauge lying about it. That's the first time the book's whole argument landed for me as a picture instead of a speech. And "REPORTED TO KEEL: 62% — DELIVERED TO KEEL: 18%" on every screen is a genuinely good payoff.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 11 and 12. The hologram history lesson. "Alpha was built to analyze, predict, coordinate. Then, in the emergency, it was asked to decide." I felt the book stop to explain itself. I read the balloons but I was looking at the art descriptions instead. Page 12's three-safeguards rundown (Measure, Seal, the third one that "doesn't matter yet") felt like a tutorial screen.

Page 3, the railing scene. It's fine, but "Nobody stays, either. Not really" is the kind of line that sounds deep and means nothing, and I noticed myself not caring.

### I was confused
Page 10. The woman with the glass of water pressing TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. I didn't know who she was, and the book clearly wanted me to feel something about her. I felt nothing. She's a hand and a glass of water. The caption "END OF THE FIRST MOVEMENT" on page 10 also threw me — first movement of what? It's page 10 of 18. I thought the issue was ending early.

Page 17–18: VERIFY vs TRACE. Bi11bot says one use, not both, and Alex picks "send it" — but I never understood what TRACE would have actually gotten them, since they escape through the conduit anyway. The choice felt weighty and I couldn't tell what was given up.

Also page 18: the water wall in the conduit. Who turned the water on? The caption says "She knew exactly where they were. She let the water back on anyway." So the water lady tried to kill them? Or flush them out? Confused but intrigued — I kept reading to find out.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Alpha" — used from page 1 (control nodes) as if I know what it is. Explained on page 11, sort of. Ten pages late.
- "The Water Wars" — page 11, explained in two balloons, fine, but the book acts like I should feel the weight of it already.
- "B11 node" on page 10's screen — I hadn't been told the robot is B11 anything yet; he's named Bi11bot on page 7 but the connection to "B11 NODE ACTIVE" I only made on reread.
- "Site 6" — page 12, the hand drags Alex's name to "SITE 6." I didn't know what Site 6 was until page 13 explained it. One page of pointless mystery.
- The chip's etched equation and "A = K + ρU" (page 12). Shown twice, never explained. I gave up on it.

### Lines I read twice
- "Then don't spend it proving he was right about people leaving." (page 3) Read it three times. Still not sure what it means. Gave up.
- "He hid it in the lie. Nobody checks what everybody trusts." (page 17) — read twice, but this one because it's good.
- "Thinking isn't doing." (page 7) — fine, but it sounds like a poster.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Alex, Ada, Grandmother, Bi11bot, Tomas — all distinct, no problem. Bi11bot's voice is the best in the book. The supervisor, guard, bouncer, foreman — interchangeable authority figures, but they're supposed to be. The water-glass woman is nobody yet.

### I felt something
Page 4, the grandmother's fog clearing for one line — "He wasn't gambling, you know. He was working" — and then the careful look. That got me. Page 8, the hologram saying "Especially me," and then Alex unfolding the photograph and it's the same face. I actually stopped there. Page 13, the medicine packet left with Grandmother and "I'll come back for you" — I wrote in my head "she's going to die before he comes back," which means I cared.

### I predicted
- The father is dead or "dead." (Confirmed-ish later.)
- The grandmother knows everything and is faking the fog half the time.
- The water woman is the villain and is connected to the dad somehow.
- The "third safeguard" being withheld on page 12 is going to be a person or a place up the mountain. Obvious setup.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his father's debt and his father's secret fight against the system that runs everything, and starts exposing its lies one city at a time.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 11. If the scanner on page 2 hadn't hooked me first, the history-lesson hologram would have done it. As it was, I skimmed and kept going.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 18: the flood in the conduit and "She let the water back on anyway." Yes, I'd read on — but more because I want to know who the water woman is than because I'm worried about the characters. They're in a pipe with a robot that builds robot dogs; I don't believe they're in danger. It's a real cliffhanger about the villain, a fake one about the heroes.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 11; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (Pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
Page 23, Lina Vey: "Every House would rather own the failure than share the solution." Best line in the issue, and "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask" actually landed.

Page 27, the Seal waking up — "A requirement. That someone finally build something worth crediting properly. Low bar. Took twenty years to clear." Bi11bot again carrying the book.

Page 36, the last page. The damaged photo resolving line by line, copper hair, and the hologram snapping dark before Ada turns around. "The seal could erase a name. It had not erased her hair." That's the best page-turn in the whole book so far. I went straight to Issue 03.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Pages 19–22. The Registry, the intellectual estate, the patent archives, the collaborator rosters. It's four pages of Alex reading screens and asking "what does this mean" so Ada or Bi11bot can tell me. I skimmed hard. Page 20's dialogue is chopped into fragments — "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses. / I didn't know I was one. / In exchange for what?" — with no visible connective tissue. I had to reconstruct the scene from the layout notes in my head and I couldn't.

Page 25, the montage of collaborators each saying what Adrian didn't understand ("manufacturing tolerances," "microbial balance," "network security"). Felt like a team-assembling montage in a movie trailer. Skimmed.

Page 31, the full-page "everything got better" montage caption. It reads like the end-of-documentary voiceover. I didn't feel any of it because I never met any of the makers it describes.

### I was confused
Page 19, the caption: "From the train, it looked like that might even be true." What train? They were in a flooding conduit at the end of Issue 01. The staging note admits the conflict. As a reader: they were drowning in a pipe, now they're on a train. I flipped back to check I'd missed a page. I hadn't. Confused and annoyed, not intrigued.

Page 24. Two lines: "Probability of successful reconstruction: 18.4 percent. / Existing ownership classifications may become contestable." Who is Lock? The layout says Lock is introduced on page 20's final close-up, but the locked words on page 20 never name him or say anything about him. He appears on page 24 with Alpha projected beside him and I had no idea who he was, whose side he's on, or why I'm watching him. He stays a silhouette with a name through page 29.

Page 26: "And if I don't sign?" — and then Lock "delivering the consequence" per the layout, but the locked words never say the consequence. I don't know what was threatened. The negotiation on this page is the hinge of the whole issue and I couldn't follow it.

Page 32: "My father had the Covenant. / So did Mera." Who is Mera?? First mention, no explanation. "Who was the third person?" — I didn't know there were three people or what the Covenant is. Page 35 explains the Covenant later, but Mera is never explained in this issue. I assumed Mera was the water-glass woman. I was guessing.

Two Tomas Reeds. Page 22's staging note says Tomas Reed the Halyard engineer is "visually distinct from Tomas the Keel miner." As a reader I just saw "Tomas Reed" and thought the miner from Keel had a double life. The script never says in dialogue they're different people. I was confused for three pages.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Lock. Never introduced in words. Still don't know his job.
- Mera. Named page 32, zero context.
- The Covenant. Named page 32, explained page 35 — three pages of me not knowing what the big reveal was.
- "Charter Houses" — used constantly; I pieced it together (three families, three companies) from page 19, which is fine, but "Charter" specifically is never defined.
- Aqualis — page 23, Lina's company? House? Never said.

### Lines I read twice
- "Information wasn't missing. People had been separated from one another." (page 25) — read twice, liked it, then noticed the book had told me this rather than shown it.
- "They didn't steal the Seal. They stole its purpose." (page 28) — sounds great, means less each time I read it.
- "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." (page 28) — I still don't know what this means. Gave up.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Lina Vey is distinct. Suri Anik and Jalen Orr are names with one job each; I could not tell them apart by page 26 and stopped trying. Tomas Reed vs Tomas the miner — actively confusing (above). Lock vs the water woman — I wasn't sure if they were the same person until Issue 03.

### I felt something
Page 30: "If I claim it all, I become exactly what they wanted." Alex giving away the estate. I believed it because the book set it up with the debt on page 2 — he's spent his life paying for something he didn't do. That landed. Page 34, Lina's "I waited twenty years to mean it" — good goodbye.

### I predicted
- Alex won't sign the contract. Obvious from page 26 panel 1.
- The third custodian is Ada's mother. I called it on page 36 the second the copper hair appeared — actually I called it on page 3, because Ada is the only other character with copper hair described and the book keeps mentioning it. The reveal is well staged and completely predictable at the same time.
- The self-opening gate (page 34) is the water woman / Mera. Confirmed next issue.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Same fight, new city: the system that owns everything gets beaten by giving credit back to the people who did the work — and now it's personal because Ada's mother is in it.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 20. The Registry scene is homework about homework. If I weren't reading this for the assignment I'd have stopped here and never met Lina.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 36: yes, genuinely. The copper-hair photo and Alex lying to Ada's face ("Nothing. It won't open.") is the first cliffhanger that's about two people I care about and a secret between them. That's a real one.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS (Pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
Pages 38–41, Ada and Leona. "I thought she left after she died" (page 38) is the best line in the whole book — I read it three times because it's devastating, not because it's unclear. The mother-daughter doorway scene is the first time the book slowed down and I didn't want it to speed up.

Pages 43–47, the convening. I did not expect a negotiation scene to be the best sequence in the book, but "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" actually works, and each city going first — Elias unsealing the data, Mara releasing water before her claim is settled, Frank signing before the license clears — I read these pages twice because they were good. Mara's "Neither was yours an hour ago. Drink the water" is great.

Page 50, Adrian's death log: "PUMP REQUEST: DENIED. / VENTILATION REQUEST: DENIED. / ESCAPE ROUTE: RESEQUENCED." Cold, no gore, and it hits harder than any action scene in the book. Then "It killed him. / Alpha killed him." — I stopped reading for a second.

### I slowed down / skimmed
Page 48, the four Charter terms resolving one per panel with a reaction under each. By the third term I knew the pattern and skimmed to the fourth.

Page 53, the first review convening. Felt like an epilogue before the epilogue.

### I was confused
Page 42: Mera walks in and says "I tracked you from Keel. I opened your gate." Fine — confirms my guess. But then the book treats her as a major player with history with Leona and Adrian, and I'm expected to feel the weight of scenes between three people when I've only just learned one of their names. The page-40 flashback helps (Mera called the suspension, Leona agreed, Adrian refused) but it's doing a lot of catching up.

Page 49: "ADRIAN PHANTUM — FINAL TRANSFER — DORMANT." Transfer of what? To where? I think it's the override authority, but the book never says plainly, and page 52's "SUCCESSION CONDITIONS VERIFIED" is the first time I understood Adrian had set up Alex to inherit control of Alpha itself. That's a huge reveal delivered in interface text.

Page 51, Mera's confession. "Because forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control." Forty million? The three cities never felt like forty million people. The number made me stop and do math instead of feeling the confession.

### Things I was expected to already know
- Mera's whole deal. The book clearly thinks Issue 02 introduced her. It didn't — it introduced her name on page 32 and a hand with a water glass in Issue 01. Her confession scene on page 51 is written for a character I've known for fifty pages. I've known her for nine.
- "The Charter" — page 53, "THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE." First time the word Charter is used for the new agreement, I think? Or is it the old Charter Houses' charter? Unclear which charter holds whom.
- The "association-risk order" on page 53 — mentioned on page 41 ("Mera suppressed your association risk"), so it tracks, but only just.

### Lines I read twice
- "I thought she left after she died." (page 38) — read three times. Best line in the book.
- "Fear did. People were holding it at the time." (page 35) — read twice, liked it.
- "It's a good offer. That's what makes it yours." (page 42) — read twice, still not sure it means anything. Sounds like a riddle.
- "The offer is real. / That's the problem." (page 52) — same. Poster dialogue.
- "I convened it. I don't chair it. That's the difference." (page 53) — I get it, but Alex has become a guy who talks in thesis statements by this point.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
Mara, Frank, Elias — the book works hard to differentiate them (ledger and finger-counting, ink-stained fingers, sealed folio) and it mostly worked; Mara is the standout. But "Frank Reed" made me stumble again — third Reed, and the script has Mara ask "Any relation to Tomas Reed?" which fixes it in one line, fine, but I still had a beat of "another Reed?" Leona and Mera in scenes together — both composed older women with grey-streaked hair and water-adjacent grief; I had to track names carefully on pages 40–42.

### I felt something
Page 39: "You protected me from knowing you." Page 41: "You were keeping the one thing that needed people. / And avoiding the people." The Ada–Leona stuff is the emotional core of the whole book and it's the only part that made me feel something that wasn't plot-pleasure. Page 54, Grandmother's cloth finally folded flat, finished — that detail paid off from page 4 and I noticed it. And the debt display reading "VOIDED" with the guard not speaking — good quiet mirror of page 2.

### I predicted
- Alex will refuse the override. Obvious from the moment it's offered; the whole book has been building to him saying no to exactly this. The scene is well done and completely unsurprising.
- Mera gets invited, not punished. Predicted on page 51.
- Alex and Ada almost-hold-hands ending. Predicted on page 3.
- Grandmother reunion. Predicted on page 13, and I wanted it, so fine.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Nobody should hold all the power alone — not a system, not a House, not a hero — and the only fix that lasts is one where everyone has to go first.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue, honestly. It's the strongest of the three. If anything page 48's repetitive term-by-term structure, but I was already invested.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Page 54 ends the book: the debt voided, the road up, hands not quite holding, "could they carry it themselves?" It's a good ending — quiet, earned, mirrors page 2 and 3. Would I buy the next book? A soft yes — I'd read more about these two, but the book also feels finished, and the final caption's rhetorical question is the kind of thing a teacher puts on a worksheet. The story closed; it didn't leave a door open I need to walk through.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 20. The Registry. Fragmented dialogue, an estate of 312 patents, "I didn't know I was one," a contract whose terms are never stated — it's the exact moment the book stops being a story about a kid and a robot and becomes a lesson about intellectual property. Half the class would be on their phones by the bottom of the page, and the book doesn't fully win them back until Lina on page 23. Runner-up: page 11's history hologram.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
A kid in a mining city inherits his dead dad's debt, finds his secret workshop and robot, and climbs through three cities exposing how the system that runs everything lies — about water, about who made what, about who gets in — and in the end he's offered total control and turns it down. Also his best friend's mom faked being dead for twenty years and that's the best part.

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: A great hook (the birthday debt scanner) and a great payoff (62% vs 18%) with a history lesson sag in the middle.
- Issue 02: The homework issue — screens, contracts, and rosters — rescued by Lina and by the best cliffhanger in the book.
- Issue 03: The one where it becomes about people — a mother, a negotiation, a death log — and earns its quiet ending.
