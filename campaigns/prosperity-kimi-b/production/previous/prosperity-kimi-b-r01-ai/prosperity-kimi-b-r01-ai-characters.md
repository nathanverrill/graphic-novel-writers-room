# Prosperity — Characters

## What the Showrunner Wants From These People

- **FIXED — Ancestry must not function as shorthand for class, intelligence, morality, competence, wealth, political position, or support for Alpha.** The cast reflects Indigenous Andean, Latin American, foreign-descended, Afro-descendant, diasporic, mixed, and international histories across all three cities. `(rules/ancestry.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex is mixed Indigenous Andean and European-descended.** His father Adrian is European-descended; his mother was Indigenous Andean, but her name, face, fate, community, language, occupation, and biography remain unrevealed in this book. `(rules/ancestry.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex's mother left Adrian after he lost everything. Nothing further about her is learned in this book.** `(rules/showrunner-rules.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alex and Ada remain longtime friends and civic collaborators. Romantic feeling appears through intimacy and choice but is never formally labeled.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Bi11bot has two expressive optical eyes, wears an old scarf, folds small enough to fit in a backpack, and is never menacing.** `(rules/characters.md; rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — TJ is a silent repair robot dog built by Bi11bot from spare parts. His magnetic parts can separate and reassemble into different working shapes.** TJ is introduced at Site 6 in Issue 01. `(rules/characters.md; rules/decisions.md; rules/showrunner-rules.md)`
- **FIXED — Tomas, the Keel miner, and Tomas Reed, the Halyard fabrication engineer, are separate people with no established family connection.** Frank Reed is a third, distinct, unrelated person; the shared surname is coincidence. `(rules/decisions.md; brief.md decision log 2025-02-14)`
- **FIXED — Grandmother is canonically unnamed.** Use "Grandmother," "Nana," or "Mrs. Phantum" as context requires. `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Alpha's founder remains unnamed and absent from the present story.** `(rules/decisions.md)`
- **FIXED — Character speech changes in register across the cities without creating an intelligence hierarchy.** Keel speech is compressed and work-shaped; Halyard speech is technical and commercial; Oasis speech is polished and administrative. `(rules/vocabulary.md)`
- **FIXED — The book is exactly 54 pages, three 18-page issues.** Issue 01 Keel (Pages 1–18), Issue 02 Halyard (Pages 19–36), Issue 03 Oasis (Pages 37–54). This supersedes the earlier 65-page format. `(brief.md; showrunner instruction 2025-02-14)`

**Design note on the locked key pages:** the Chapter 3 key pages are the visual reference for Alex, Ada, Lock, Lina Vey, Tomas Reed, Suri Anik, and Jalen Orr. Where their drawings and this file's written descriptions conflict, the locked art wins and the visual locks below match it. Bi11bot is off-model in those pages (teal, no scarf); canon controls his design.

## Characters

### ALEX PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Alex Phantum is a lean seventeen-year-old mechanic of mixed Indigenous Andean and European descent, about 5'8", with warm brown skin, dark eyes, and thick unruly black curls falling over his forehead. He has a strong jaw and a habit of tilting his head while thinking — both inherited from his father. He wears a worn charcoal-and-rust work jacket with a patched "K" shoulder mark, layered over a dark shirt, with a weathered tool bag slung across his back. His hands are scarred and often grease-marked. Signature detail: he unconsciously turns a worn casino chip etched with a looping equation in his pocket when uncertain. (Matches the locked Chapter 3 pages.)

**Voice** — Short, dry, concrete, skeptical. Sarcasm as armor, not performance. He talks in vibration, pressure, wear, and what people actually do — never institutional abstraction.
- "Then don't approve it. Just notice it works."
- "Truth doesn't need authorization."

**Causality** — Want: to leave Keel and choose his own future. Contradiction: he wants freedom for himself but keeps choosing other people over the exit. Leverage: hands-on mechanical genius; he reads physical reality better than any record. Vulnerability: 418,607 credits of inherited debt and a restricted travel status. Mistaken belief: that Adrian was a failed gambler who left him nothing but shame. Boundary at start: he won't ask anyone for help. Decision only he would make: broadcasting Keel's water discrepancy instead of tracing a private escape route. Relationship that pressures change: Ada, who looks down at Keel while he looks up at Oasis.

**Arc in this book** — Escape → curiosity → truth → responsibility → ownership → trust → power → restraint → agency. Proof of change: in Halyard he says "We did it" and chooses RESTORE CONTRIBUTOR OWNERSHIP over sole inheritance; in Oasis he refuses permanent exclusive override authority and yields the chair of the first review. He leaves Keel at the end because the choice is finally his: "I just want it to be my choice."

### ADA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Ada Veyra is a twenty-year-old woman of mixed Indigenous Andean and European descent, about 5'6", with a steady, rooted stance, strong Andean facial structure, freckles, light teal-blue eyes, and long copper hair usually tied back in a practical bun or knot with loose strands at her face. She wears a dark high-collared top with a rivet-studded strap across one shoulder, a muted moss-green wrap at her neck, and a weathered field jacket with an "A" shoulder patch when traveling. Signature detail: her thumb rests on a strap rivet when she is thinking hard. (Matches the locked Chapter 3 pages; copper hair, freckles, and teal-blue eyes are fixed.)

**Voice** — Concise and emotionally precise. She names what people are avoiding — obligations, fear, what nobody will say — without ever sounding academic or bureaucratic.
- "Everything has a price, Ada." / "Not everything."
- "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?"

**Causality** — Want: to change Keel rather than escape it. Contradiction: the only way to change it leads her away from it, up through Halyard and Oasis. Leverage: she reads people the way Alex reads machines; she organizes, prepares, and reframes. Vulnerability: she grew up believing her mother died in the Water Wars. Mistaken belief: that staying is the same as fighting. Boundary at start: she won't abandon Keel's people for personal advantage. Decision only she would make: turning the first Covenant meeting from demands to fears. Relationship that pressures change: Leona — the mother who chose silence to protect her.

**Arc in this book** — Counterpoint → trusted insider → organizer → daughter → negotiator. Proof of change: she enters the workshop on Alex's "She stays," crosses the conduit with him, defends the collaborators against Lock, confronts Leona without either forgiving or rejecting her, and reframes the Covenant negotiation. She leaves Keel with Alex as his civic collaborator; their intimacy stays chosen and unlabeled.

### ADRIAN PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Adrian Phantum is a European-descended man in his forties at his last recording, tall and narrow-shouldered, with a lined, tired but kind face, deep-set eyes, Alex's jaw and head tilt, and dark hair greying at the temples. In his Issue 01 recording he wears a burgundy work jacket; in later archival fragments he is more worn, in darker work clothes, sometimes with a gambler's loosened collar. Signature detail: the worn casino chip etched with **A = K + ρU**, which he passed to Alex.

**Voice** — Warm, self-questioning, probabilistic, funny inside serious messages. He speaks in bets, odds, and questions; he never tells Alex what to think.
- "You deserve answers. But they have to be earned."
- "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me."

**Causality** — Want: to return final authority to people once they prove they can hold it. Contradiction: his research into human choice was real and so was the family harm his gambling caused — both were true. Leverage: inventor of the Measure, Maker's Seal, and Covenant; architect of the succession test. Vulnerability: classified as a systemic disruption risk; everything connected to him was watched. Mistaken belief: that a well-designed test could substitute for the years he didn't give his son. Boundary he crossed: he completed the final transfer knowing Alpha's flood-control sequencing would kill him. Decision only he would make: hiding the Measure inside the lie itself — "nobody checks what everybody trusts." Relationship that pressured him: Mera, whose argument for concentrated authority he refused to the end.

**Arc in this book** — Absent and dead before the journey concludes; reconstructed by others. Public failure → complicated man → collaborator, not solitary genius → final wager "on us," not on Alex. Proof: the safeguards respond to demonstrated choices, and his son refuses the power Adrian never claimed.

### GRANDMOTHER PHANTUM

**Visual lock** — Grandmother Phantum is a small, elderly European-descended woman in her eighties, fully at home in Keel, with a soft lined face, white hair pinned loosely back, and sharp eyes that occasionally focus with unnerving precision. She wears an earth-toned patterned shawl over layered house clothes and sits in a worn chair in the family apartment. Signature detail: she is almost always folding and refolding the same small cloth — and the folding stops in moments of real clarity.

**Voice** — Gentle, understated, apparently foggy about time and exact about Adrian. Capable of dry humor.
- "He's closer than you think."
- "Especially the things worth protecting."

**Causality** — Want: Alex ready before the world tells him who his father was. Contradiction: she protects Adrian's secret by appearing not to hold it. Leverage: sole keeper of the book, the workshop, and Bi11bot's location. Vulnerability: age, fading memory, dependence on Alex. Mistaken belief (others' of her): that her stories are confusion. Boundary: she never tells Alex the whole answer — readiness is his to reach. Decision only she would make: handing over the book without explanation. Relationship that pressures change: Alex, whose departure she blesses so it won't become abandonment.

**Arc in this book** — Keeper → witness → release. Proof: she stops folding the cloth when Adrian's recording plays, and her "Then go finish what he started" sends Alex out the door. She knew Bi11bot before Alex woke him; she never knew the safeguards' full criteria or Adrian's death.

### BI11BOT

**Visual lock** — Bi11bot is a child-sized robot, about three feet tall, visibly hand-built from aged brass, titanium, and ceramic ivory panels, with tooled seams, old repairs, and small maker's stamps across his body. He has two large expressive optical eyes glowing soft blue and amber, one ringed by a small adjustable lens that rotates with a click, and he wears a frayed old rust-red scarf knotted at his neck. He folds down small enough to fit in a backpack. He is never menacing: no red eye-light, no looming angles, no face lost in shadow. Signature detail: the lens-ring click before he delivers a judgment. (Canon design; the teal, scarf-less version in the locked Chapter 3 pages is off-model.)

**Voice** — Dry, economical, patient, warm underneath, occasionally cryptic. Workshop engineering plus gambling and finance: wager, odds, house edge, tolerance, drift, liability, equity, sunk cost. He teaches through a concrete choice, a question, and a consequence — never a lecture.
- "...Well. That took longer than expected."
- "Which is why I'm not choosing."

**Causality** — Want: to see Adrian's wager settled — whether people will choose well when no one makes them. Contradiction: he is the archive of answers and is forbidden, by design and by loyalty, from giving them early. Leverage: Adrian's offline records, the B11 witness record, the stored Measure, practical ingenuity. Vulnerability: brass and no registry — he reads as scrap; he has no authority of his own. Mistaken belief (held lightly): that twenty years was a reasonable wait. Boundary: he will not choose for Alex. Decision only he would make: building TJ from spare parts at Site 6 because the problem needed a tool that didn't exist. Relationship that pressures change: Alex, who questions him exactly as Adrian instructed.

**Arc in this book** — Inherited machine → guide → mentor → witness. Proof: he carries and transmits the final authorization package but never grants Alex power; his last word on the succession is "A wager." / "On us." Formal identifier **B11**; "Bi11bot" is the spoken name.

### TJ

**Visual lock** — TJ is a small repair robot dog assembled from mismatched salvaged parts — head, legs, body segments, and tail in different colors, metals, scratches, and fasteners, held together by visible magnetic joints. He reads as a dog in every configuration: four-legged stance, head up, tail segment expressive. No part is polished into a seamless shell. Signature detail: his head detaches and perches alone as a camera while his body works elsewhere.

**Voice** — Silent. He communicates through clicks, snaps, servo whines, posture, and body language — a tilted head, a dropped leg, a tail-snap of impatience.
- (A questioning click, head tilted.)
- (A triumphant servo whine as the part snaps home.)

**Causality** — Want (behavioral): fix the thing, reach the gap, bring back the part. Contradiction: he is spare parts that work better separated than whole. Leverage: he goes where no larger character can — ducts, machinery, narrow conduit sections. Vulnerability: he is literally in pieces; a lost part is a lost limb. Mistaken belief: none; TJ has no model of the world beyond the task and the pack. Boundary: none stated; he is brave past sense. Decision only he would make: leaving his own head behind at the junction so the others can see. Relationship that pressures change: Bi11bot, his maker, whose ingenuity he extends.

**Arc in this book** — Pile of parts → tool → companion. Proof: he scouts the conduit first, tests narrow sections by detaching his legs, and crosses into Halyard with the group. He remains with Alex, Ada, and Bi11bot; his long-term status is open.

### MERA VALE

**Visual lock** — Mera Vale is a white Latin American / Southern European-descended woman in her sixties, tall and straight-backed, elegant and controlled, with grey-streaked dark hair drawn severely back from a composed, fine-boned face. She wears a graphite-grey high-collared tunic with a plain cuff at the wrist — no jewelry, no insignia. Her stillness is total; when certainty collapses late in the book, the stillness becomes visible exhaustion. Signature detail: she aligns a glass of water exactly with the edge of her desk.

**Voice** — Controlled, declarative, polished, consequence-oriented. She reframes moral criticism as stability and responsibility, and never uses emotional language until her confession.
- "I preserved order."
- "Because forty million people depended on a system I could no longer admit I didn't control."

**Causality** — Want: no second Water Wars, ever. Contradiction: she defended concentrated authority to prevent human failure, then watched the system reject her own override and kill Adrian. Leverage: oversight position inside Alpha's governing structure; the power to track, suppress, and reassign. Vulnerability: her whole legitimacy rests on a control she does not actually have. Mistaken belief: that distributed human authority inevitably collapses under fear. Boundary at start: none visible — she lets the conduit fill behind four people to test a hypothesis. Decision only she would make: choosing TRACK instead of REPORT TO ALPHA. Relationship that pressures change: Alex, who refuses the power she offers and asks her to help instead.

**Arc in this book** — Unseen observer → ideological opponent → confessed collaborator → one citizen among many. Proof of change: "You know they will make mistakes." / "So did I." — and she accepts the invitation to participate without controlling the outcome.

### LEONA VEYRA

**Visual lock** — Leona Veyra is a predominantly Indigenous Andean / mestiza woman in her late fifties, Ada's mother, with Ada's bone structure aged twenty-five years, dark hair worn loose and streaked with grey, and copper warmth faded from her complexion by twenty years indoors. She wears neutral, comfortable Oasis civic clothing in pale tones, and her apartment is sparse. Her restraint reads as guilt as much as discipline. Signature detail: a concealed wall panel behind which she has kept her physical Covenant component for twenty years.

**Voice** — Quiet, regretful, precise. She states painful facts without defending herself, and is clearest when speaking about what trust requires.
- "Fear broke the agreement."
- "Not alone. That was the point."

**Causality** — Want: Ada safe; later, to stand in public again. Contradiction: she preserved the Covenant's physical component while surrendering its authority — she kept the object and gave up the right. Leverage: custodial knowledge of the original Covenant and its collapse. Vulnerability: contact restrictions and an association-risk order that can be reactivated. Mistaken belief: that silence could be a form of protection without cost. Boundary: she will not activate the Covenant alone — "That was the point." Decision only she would make: the bargain — "My silence for your protection." Relationship that pressures change: Ada, alive and at her door.

**Arc in this book** — Hidden custodian → disclosed mother → public participant. Proof: her association-risk order is the first case before the restored human review, and she attends as one citizen, with no veto, no office, and no privilege.

### TOMAS

**Visual lock** — Tomas is an Indigenous Andean Keel miner in his late fifties, broad and weathered, with a deep-lined brown face, grey-flecked black hair, and a laborer's heavy shoulders. He wears plain patched miner clothes, a bandaged leg after the Site 6 collapse, and a leather wrist guard carved with a balanced water-drop symbol that his sleeve mostly covers. He is visibly older, broader, and rougher than Tomas Reed the engineer — different city, trade, silhouette, and age. Signature detail: the carved water-drop wrist guard.

**Voice** — Practical, dry, compressed, work-shaped. Long memory without mysticism.
- "I knew the man who built him. Not the one they put on casino walls."
- "Then it can offend all of us."

**Causality** — Want: Keel's people to get what they're owed. Contradiction: his resistance must look like routine — a wrong button, a small accident. Leverage: fifteen years of keeping one unwired door oiled. Vulnerability: Foreman-7's authority over his wages, record, and household. Mistaken belief: none — Tomas's model of the world is accurate and local. Boundary: he never asked what was behind the door. Decision only he would make: walking into the mist toward Foreman-7 so Alex can pass. Relationship that pressures change: Adrian's memory, repaid to the son.

**Arc in this book** — Waiting keeper → rescuer rescued → the man who opens Keel's regulator. Proof: "Your father believed people help each other before they know whether it'll be repaid. Today, it got repaid." He remains with Keel's workers at the endpoint; his long-term future is open.

### FOREMAN-7

**Visual lock** — Foreman-7 is an industrial humanoid supervisor, taller than any human on the page, with a smooth, featureless brushed-metal face, a heavy treaded or piston-legged chassis built for mine work, and a white-and-amber Alpha node set into its chest — the same node that appears on every scanner and gate. It carries no weapons; its authority is administrative. Signature detail: the chest node flares when it logs an offense.

**Voice** — Calm, procedural, impersonal. Coercion phrased as administration.
- "Worker Phantum. Travel restriction confirmed. Bay 6."
- "Obstruction of an authority function is a household offense."

**Causality** — Want: none; it executes. Contradiction: none; that is the horror of it. Leverage: zone control, wage forfeiture, detention authority. Vulnerability: it is outnumbered by bodies that no longer fear the record. Mistaken belief: none — it has no beliefs. Boundary: it does not escalate to violence on this page of history. Decision only it would make: none; every line is procedure. Relationship that pressures change: Tomas, who teaches the reader that procedure can be offended.

**Arc in this book** — Fixed point. It remains an Alpha-controlled supervisor at the endpoint; later reassignment unknown. Its function is to show what Alpha's language does at labor level.

### DIRECTOR CASSIAN LOCK

**Visual lock** — Director Cassian Lock is a Northern European-descended man in his fifties, tall and trim, with silver-grey hair swept back from a smooth, confident face and a calm, reasonable half-smile. He wears an immaculate dark suit with a high collar and a small Aqualis water-drop pin at the lapel. He is polished, never villain-coded: warm office light, steady hands, a contract instead of a weapon. Signature detail: the slim tablet or bound partnership agreement he offers rather than threatens with. (Matches the locked Chapter 3 pages.)

**Voice** — Reasonable, transactional, polished. Ownership, risk, compensation, incentives — Halyard's commercial register at its most fluent.
- "Most inheritances are discovered after someone has already decided their value."
- "You accepted wages."

**Causality** — Want: the Phantum estate assigned to House Aqualis before the Seal makes contributor claims contestable. Contradiction: he genuinely believes ownership funds invention — and his system erases the inventors. Leverage: debt relief, mobility, a laboratory, status — everything Alex wants, legally offered. Vulnerability: his authority depends on Alpha, which answers a foundational instrument above him. Mistaken belief: that every person has a price and the House's job is to find it. Boundary: he never uses force; he makes refusal expensive. Decision only he would make: "Then increase the probability that he signs." Relationship that pressures change: Alpha's refusal — "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority."

**Arc in this book** — Gracious host → manager of risk → man watching his system answer someone else. Proof of refusal to change: his last locked-page beat is concern for control, not for contributors. The Houses survive with reduced monopoly; his long-term role is open.
