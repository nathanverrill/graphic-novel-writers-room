# Cold Read — Prosperity (Writer B)

Read cold, pages only, night before it's due. I did not want to like this. I read Issue 01 first, then 02, then 03, in order.

---

## ISSUE 01 — KEEL (Pages 1–18)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 2, panel 3. The scanner display. "DEBT BALANCE: 418,607 CREDITS / EARNED TODAY: 40 / PROJECTED CLEARANCE: 28.7 YEARS." That's the first thing in the book that made me feel something, and it's a screen, not a person. "Happy birthday, Phantum" is a good nasty line. I was in from here.
- Page 5, the bookshelf clicking open. Fine, yes, secret room, I know this move, but the grandmother just sitting there folding her cloth while it happens got me.
- Page 7, panel 3. The robot waking up. "Well. That took longer than expected." I like Bi11bot immediately. He's the best character in the issue and it's not close.
- Page 17, the two pipes. One dry, one trembling. I understood the whole injustice of the book in one panel. This is the moment the book is actually about, and it works.
- Page 18, the screens flipping to "REPORTED: 62% — DELIVERED: 18%." Good. And then the wall of water in the pipe. I turned the page fast.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 1. A big map with four captions. I get it, three cities, rich on top, poor on bottom. I've seen this exact opening in like five other books. I skimmed it and went to page 2.
- Pages 11–12. The history lesson. Water Wars, Alpha, "asked to decide... temporarily," then the Measure, then the Maker's Seal, then "the third waits further up the mountain." It's three pages of a robot explaining the setting. I caught myself reading balloons and skipping the hologram descriptions. The "A = K + ρU" diagram meant nothing to me and I moved on.
- Page 13. Planning scene. Map, credentials, "every third junction." I skimmed. It felt like the book doing homework of its own.

### I was confused
- Page 10. Who is this woman? Grey hair, glass of water, presses TRACK instead of REPORT. I get that I'm not supposed to know yet, but she gets a whole page and a caption saying "END OF THE FIRST MOVEMENT" and I had no idea what movement. Confused but intrigued — barely. Mostly I thought "okay, villain."
- Page 12, panel 4. Her hand drags "ALEX PHANTUM" to "SITE 6." Then page 13 says his shift got reassigned to the site bordering the old works. So she sent him there on purpose? Why would the villain route him toward the thing she's trying to keep hidden? I read it twice. Still don't get it. (Later pages suggest she wanted him to arrive, but in the issue it just reads as the plot pushing him through a door.)
- Page 16, panel 4: Ada is just... already down there. She beat them into a sealed mine alone. "Same page from memory." I believed she'd try; I didn't believe she'd succeed faster than the guy with the robot and the key.
- Page 18: why does taking the Measure off the gauge set off the alarm AND start the water? Who turned the water on? The caption says "she let the water back on anyway" — so Mera tried to drown them? After TRACK instead of REPORT? I couldn't tell if she was trying to kill them or herd them, and I don't think the issue knows either.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "Alpha" — used on page 1 (control nodes) as if I know what it is. Explained on page 11, sort of. Ten pages late.
- "The Water Wars" — page 11, explained in one balloon. Fine, but the book leans on it hard for one sentence.
- "The Measure," "the Maker's Seal," "the third one" — introduced page 12 as a numbered set of magic objects. I know this structure from video games. I wrote "collect three things" in the margin.
- "B11 NODE / LEGACY PROTOCOL" (page 10) — never explained what a legacy protocol is. I guessed.
- The chip's etched equation and the chalkboard equation (pages 2, 6) — matching, clearly important, never said what it is. I assume it's the A = K + ρU thing. Still don't know what that is.

### Lines I read twice
- "He wasn't gambling, you know. He was working." (page 4) — read twice because it's the grandmother suddenly being lucid and I wasn't sure the book meant me to notice. I noticed.
- "He hid it in the lie. Nobody checks what everybody trusts." (page 17) — read twice because it's good.
- "Safer with me than in a pocket." / "The conduit climbs to Halyard. It can't climb if it's full." (page 18) — had to reread to get that the empty pipe is the escape route. Got it on the second read.
- "A = K + ρU" — gave up. I don't know what ρ is and the book isn't telling me.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Alex, Ada, Bi11bot, Grandmother, Tomas — all distinct. Tomas especially, one scene and I know him.
- The supervisor, the guard, the bouncer, Foreman-7 — interchangeable authority figures, but they're supposed to be.
- The grey-haired woman (page 10) has no name in this issue. She's "glass of water lady" to me.

### I felt something
- Page 4, the grandmother calling him Adrian. That landed.
- Page 8, the hologram: "Trust Bi11bot. But question everything... Especially me." I actually said "okay" out loud. That's a good dad message.
- Page 13, "Medicine first. I'll earn the rest." and the promise to come back. Landed because I knew it was a setup for the ending.
- Page 15, Tomas: "He asked me to keep one door. Fifteen years I've oiled it." Best line in the issue.

### I predicted
- The dad is not a deadbeat gambler, he's a secret genius. Predicted from page 2, confirmed page 8.
- The grandmother knows everything. Predicted page 4, confirmed page 5.
- The "third safeguard" is a person or needs people. Predicted page 12 ("never for one person alone" is a giveaway).
- The grey-haired woman is connected to the dad somehow. Not confirmed yet.
- The flood at the end isn't trying to kill them, it's pushing them up to Halyard. We'll see.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
A kid inherits his father's debt and his father's secret mission to prove the system that runs three cities is lying about water.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 11. If I'd hit the history-lesson hologram before the scanner display hooked me, I'd be on my phone. As it is, page 11 is where I started skimming and page 14 is where I came back.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes. The water in the pipe is a real cliffhanger — people I like are in the pipe. The "she let the water back on anyway" caption is a trick on top of it, but the flood itself is enough. I'd read issue 2 tonight.

VERDICT Issue 01: put down: page 11; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 02 — HALYARD (Pages 19–36)

### Pulled me forward
- Page 23. Lina Vey. "I've been waiting twenty years for someone to ask." She's the best new character and she gets one page.
- Page 25, the montage where the collaborators each name what Adrian didn't understand. "He was, however, excellent at finding people who did." That's the first time the dad felt like a real person instead of a plot ghost.
- Page 27. "I'm not signing away people I just found." Good. And the Seal waking up because someone finally built something worth crediting — I liked that the magic object has a moral tripwire.
- Page 36, panel 3. The damaged photo resolving line by line — copper hair. I knew instantly. My stomach dropped for Ada. Best panel in the issue.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Pages 19–22. The Registry, the estate, the patents, the collaborator rosters. Four pages of admin. I understand "his dad left him an intellectual estate" in one panel; the book takes four pages. I skimmed hard.
- Page 28. More registry talk. "They didn't steal the Seal. They stole its purpose." is a good line buried in a page I was skimming.
- Page 31. A full-page montage caption telling me Halyard got better. It reads like the epilogue of a different book. I felt nothing; I hadn't seen any of these people before page 23.

### I was confused
- Page 19, the caption: "From the train, it looked like that might even be true." What train? They were in a flooding pipe last page. I flipped back to page 18 to check if I missed a train. I didn't. Confused and annoyed, not intrigued.
- Page 20: "I didn't know I was one." One what? The balloon before it is "I guess he never worked for the Charter Houses" — so... a Charter House? A person? I read the page three times. I think he means he didn't know he was an estate holder, but the line doesn't connect to anything. Gave up.
- Page 22: "Then we start with the one who still wants to be found." Who? Next page it's Lina, but nothing on page 22 singles her out. The roster has four names.
- Page 24: Lock. Who is Lock? He appears on page 20's layout description (a close-up) with no dialogue, then page 24 he's sitting with Alpha, then page 26 he "delivers the consequence" — but the locked words on page 26 don't include the consequence. Alex asks "And if I don't sign?" and the page ends. I never learned what happens if he doesn't sign.
- Page 26: "We did it." Did what? The prototype isn't shown working until page 27. The negotiation on page 26 has no actual terms in the words — I never learned what the contract said.
- Page 29: "Request denied. Foundational instrument has priority." Denied to whom? Lock asked Alpha something and I don't know what.
- Page 32: "My father had the Covenant. / So did Mera." Who is Mera?? First time the name appears. I flipped back through both issues. She's the glass-of-water lady, I think, but the book never says so. Confused and detached — this is supposed to be a big reveal and I had to do detective work to know who was being revealed.
- Page 33: a full-page splash with no words at all. It just sits there. I turned the page thinking my copy was missing text.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "The Charter Houses" — page 20, used as if established. Issue 01 said "three House insignias" in an art description on page 1. That's it.
- "Aqualis" — page 23, Lina's company, never introduced.
- "The Covenant" — page 32, brand new, treated as huge. It is never explained in this issue. Page 35 explains it, three pages later, in the next section.
- "Mera" — page 32. See above. This is the worst one in the book.
- "Lock" — pages 20, 24, 26, 29. Never introduced, never given a first name, never said what his job is. He's "man at desk who is worried."
- "TRACE" and "VERIFY" from page 17 — I remembered these were the Measure's two options, but the issue never mentions what VERIFY cost them. The traced route just... doesn't come up.

### Lines I read twice
- "Your father left many things badly labeled." (page 20) — read twice, liked it.
- "I didn't know I was one." (page 20) — read three times, gave up.
- "Not everyone. Only those the system was taught to forget." (page 28) — read twice. Good line, but I had to think about whether it made sense. It half does.
- "Someone both of them agreed the world should forget." (page 32) — read twice because it's the hook and it's good, but "both of them" means Adrian and Mera and I didn't know who Mera was, so it didn't land the way it was supposed to.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Lina Vey: distinct, immediately.
- Tomas Reed, Suri Anik, Jalen Orr: three names, one scene each, one personality trait each (tolerances, microbes, security). I could not tell you which is which an hour later. Also Tomas Reed vs. Tomas the miner from Issue 01 — same first name, and I genuinely thought it was the same guy with a double life until the staging note said otherwise. A reader doesn't get the staging note.
- Lock and Mera: two pale authority figures in control rooms with glasses of water. I had them merged into one person until page 42 of Issue 03.

### I felt something
- Page 30: "If I claim it all, I become exactly what they wanted." Felt it. The choice was real because the offer was real.
- Page 36: the copper hair. The only moment in the issue that hit me in the chest. And then Alex lies to Ada — "Nothing. It won't open." — and I actually cared that he lied.

### I predicted
- The third custodian is Ada's mother. Predicted the second the hair resolved, and honestly predicted it at page 32 ("someone the world should forget" + Ada's unexplained dead mom energy from Issue 01).
- Alex won't sign. Obvious from page 26.
- Lock will lose control of his own system. Page 29, yes.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Now it's about who gets credit for ideas — the kid climbs to the middle city and breaks the Houses' ownership racket with his dad's second magic object.

### Where I would have put it down
Page 20. The Registry scene is homework about homework, and "I didn't know I was one" made me feel dumb in the bad way. If Issue 01 hadn't ended on a flood I'd have stopped here.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
Yes, but only because of page 36. The copper-hair reveal is a real cliffhanger — it's about Ada, who I care about, and Alex lying to her gives it teeth. Pages 32–33 (Covenant, Mera, wordless splash) did nothing for me; page 36 saved the issue.

VERDICT Issue 02: put down: page 20; next issue: yes

## ISSUE 03 — OASIS (Pages 37–54)

### Pulled me forward
- Pages 38–39. "That's my mother." / "I thought she left after she died." That line is brutal and I read it three times because it's perfect. The whole reunion scene is the best writing in the book. "You protected me from knowing you."
- Page 42. Mera's offer: water for Keel, review for Halyard, homes for the displaced — just bury the Covenant. It's a genuinely good offer and the book knows it. "It's a good offer. That's what makes it yours." Great.
- Pages 43–47. The convening. I expected to hate a five-page meeting and instead it's the best stretch of the issue. "Not what do you want. What are you afraid of losing?" — and then each delegate answers honestly. Elias opening the folio first, Mara releasing water before her claim is settled ("Drink the water"), Frank going third and hating it. I believed all three of them.
- Page 50. The dad's death. Pump denied, ventilation denied, escape resequenced. No body, just a gauge. It killed him and documented it. That's horrifying in exactly the right way for this book.
- Page 52. Alex declines the override. "The offer is real. / That's the problem." I predicted he'd refuse and I still felt it.

### I slowed down / skimmed
- Page 37. Oasis is creepy-perfect, nobody holds doors, "Belonging takes longer." I got the point in panel 1 and the page kept making it.
- Page 48. Four terms resolving on a wall, one per panel, each with a reaction line underneath. It reads like a PowerPoint. I skimmed it because pages 43–47 had already done all the work emotionally.
- Page 53. The first review convening, the empty chair, "I convened it. I don't chair it. That's the difference." The book explains its own theme out loud and I didn't need it.

### I was confused
- Page 42: Mera says "I tracked you from Keel. I opened your gate." Okay, so she IS the glass-of-water lady — confirmed at last, twenty pages after her name first appeared. But then: she opened the gate, she let the water flood the conduit, she routed Alex to Site 6... so she's been steering the whole plot, and the book never makes her answer for the flood nearly killing four people. Alex just... doesn't bring it up. Confused and a little detached.
- Page 49: "FINAL TRANSFER — DORMANT." Transfer of what? The override authority, I figured out on page 52, but page 49 presents it as if I should gasp and I just squinted.
- Page 50: Mera is suddenly "present in the room." When did she get there? Last I saw she was in the apartment on page 42. I flipped back. No transition. Minor, but it yanked me out of the book's most important scene.
- Page 51: "OVERRIDE REQUEST: M. VALE — LOGGED — REJECTED." So she tried to save Adrian and the system refused her. That reframes her completely — she's not the villain, she's a coward who failed once and spent twenty years covering it. I liked this, but it arrives so late and so fast that I had to reread pages 50–51 to rebuild my whole picture of her.

### Things I was expected to already know
- "The Charter" (page 53: "THE CHARTER HOLDS BOTH ACCOUNTABLE") — new word for the agreement, I think. Never introduced.
- "Association risk" (pages 41, 53) — Mera suppressed Ada's "association risk." I sort of get it (being near Adrian's people got you flagged) but the term is used like I know the system.
- The third safeguard being the Covenant — the book deferred it for two issues and then explained it in one lamplight scene on page 35, which is fine, but by then I'd already been told on page 32 that it was a huge deal, so the explanation felt like catching up, not revealing.

### Lines I read twice
- "I thought she left after she died." (page 38) — three times. Best line in the book.
- "Fear did. People were holding it at the time." (page 35) — twice. Good.
- "You preserved the part where nobody had to know." (page 51) — twice. Alex's best line.
- "Keel's arguing about water again — it needs people who can lose an argument gracefully." (page 54) — twice, because it's charming and because it's Ada finally saying what she wants.
- "No system could guarantee them the future now — could they carry it themselves?" (page 54) — twice, once because it's the thesis and once because I was deciding whether I minded being asked directly. I didn't, much.

### Who I could tell apart, and who I couldn't
- Mara, Frank, Elias: three delegates, and for once the book separates them — Mara counts on her fingers, Frank has ink-stained hands, Elias has a sealed folio. I could tell them apart by their fears, which is better than by their clothes. Mara is my favorite of the three.
- Leona: distinct and real, immediately.
- Mera: finally a person instead of a silhouette, but it took 51 pages.
- "Frank Reed" vs "Tomas Reed" — page 43 has Mara ask "Any relation?" and Frank says "None. Common name." The book knows it's a problem and patches it with a joke. I still stumbled.

### I felt something
- The Ada/Leona scenes (38–41). All of it. "All these years. You were keeping the one thing that needed people. / And avoiding the people." That's the whole book in two lines.
- Page 45: "I'm showing you my fear." Landed.
- Page 50: the dad dying at the cradle, finishing the transfer knowing. I put the book down for a minute. In a good way.
- Page 54: the grandmother's cloth folded flat, finished. "You came back. / I said I would." The promise from page 13 kept. I noticed and it got me.

### I predicted
- Ada's mom is the third custodian. Confirmed page 38.
- Mera tried to save Adrian and failed. Half-predicted — I knew she was connected, not that she'd tried.
- Alex refuses the override. Confirmed page 52. Predictable but earned.
- Alex and Ada leave together at the end, not quite holding hands. Confirmed page 54. Fine.
- The empty chair at the head of the table stays empty. Confirmed page 53. Saw it coming from a mile away.

### What I think this book is about, in one sentence
Three cities learn to decide things together instead of letting one system — or one person — decide for them, and it turns out the whole book was about that sentence.

### Where I would have put it down
Nowhere in this issue. It's the strongest of the three. If anything, page 48's PowerPoint wall is the weakest page, but by then I was finishing no matter what.

### The last page: do I need the next issue, and why
It's the last issue, so: would I buy the next book? The ending is quiet — two kids walking uphill, hands not quite holding, "could they carry it themselves?" It's not a cliffhanger and it's not trying to be. I'd buy a next book about these two, yes, but I'd also be fine if there wasn't one. That's either the right ending or a slightly too-satisfied one, and I can't fully decide.

VERDICT Issue 03: put down: none; next issue: yes

## Whole book

### The page that would have lost my whole class
Page 20. The Registry. Four pages of estate admin starting here, a line ("I didn't know I was one") that doesn't parse, and a villain (Lock) who never gets introduced. Half the class would be on their phones by panel 3 and the copper-hair payoff on page 36 would happen to an empty room. Runner-up: page 11, the Water Wars lecture.

### What I would tell a friend it was about
"A kid in a mining city inherits his dad's giant debt on his seventeenth birthday, finds a secret workshop and a robot, and it turns out his dad built three things that can prove the system running the cities is rigged — so he climbs the mountain city by city, and by the end he gets offered control of everything and turns it down. Also his best friend's dead mom isn't dead. It's better than it sounds, except the middle issue is boring for like ten pages."

### One-sentence aftertaste of each issue
- Issue 01: A debt scanner, a secret room, and a wall of water — the one that made me keep reading.
- Issue 02: Ten pages of paperwork rescued by a photograph of copper hair.
- Issue 03: A dead mother, a good offer refused, and a meeting that somehow made me feel more than the flood did.
