# Project Context: EVOKE Prosperity

## Status

This file contains **project-level context**, not a finished plot. It should be updated as the story is approved. Agents must not treat proposed story material as canon unless it is moved into the Canon section.

## Lineage

EVOKE is an educational storytelling and project-based-learning model originally developed with the World Bank Institute to engage young people in solving real-world social challenges. The original experience combined a graphic novel, missions/quests, game mechanics, social learning, and real-world action.

The transferable EVOKE idea is not "read a story about heroes." It is: **see people confront a difficult future, recognize that the relevant skills can be practiced now, then act in your own community.**

For EVOKE Prosperity, preserve that bridge between:
1. fictional near-future problem solving,
2. learnable social-innovation behaviors,
3. real-world agency.

## Working purpose

Create a graphic novel experience that makes **prosperity** feel like something people can understand, question, design, and help create — not an abstract economic word delivered as a lesson.

The story should create curiosity first. Educational meaning should emerge through decisions, tradeoffs, visible systems, and consequences.

## Working audience

Primary audience: young people / learners who may not identify as economists, technologists, policy experts, entrepreneurs, or "future leaders."

Design for a diverse audience across places and life experiences. Avoid assuming wealth, stable infrastructure, elite education, or a single cultural definition of success.

## Story principle

The heroes should not simply receive the correct answer from an expert. They should:
- notice something others miss,
- investigate how a system actually works,
- encounter competing definitions of a good outcome,
- collaborate across different skills and perspectives,
- try something,
- discover an unintended effect,
- adapt,
- leave the reader with a credible sense that systems are made by people and can be changed by people.

## Prosperity: working dramatic frame

Treat prosperity as multidimensional and contested rather than synonymous with "more money."

Possible dimensions the story may make concrete:
- secure access to essentials,
- meaningful work and opportunity,
- time and autonomy,
- health and safety,
- resilient local systems,
- education and capability,
- trust and cooperation,
- environmental durability,
- ability to create, own, participate, and recover from shocks.

These are **story ingredients, not a checklist**. Use only what the approved story needs.

## Avoid

- prosperity as a lecture,
- a magical technology that solves structural problems,
- a billionaire/genius savior,
- a villain whose only function is to represent "bad economics,"
- treating communities as helpless recipients,
- flattening different places into a generic "developing world,"
- equating entrepreneurship with founding a venture-backed startup,
- implying that every tradeoff has a painless win-win answer,
- jargon that characters would not naturally use,
- pages that resemble a policy report with character heads attached.

## Desired feel

- near-future, grounded, visually specific,
- hopeful without being naïve,
- energetic and problem-solving,
- systems-aware but human,
- young people with meaningful agency,
- moments of surprise, friction, humor, and consequence,
- enough ambiguity for readers to argue about the choices.

## EVOKE learning bridge

Where appropriate, structure story moments so they can inspire a real-world learner action:
- **Notice** — observe a local system, pattern, bottleneck, asset, or unmet need.
- **Investigate** — ask people affected; gather evidence; map incentives and constraints.
- **Imagine** — produce multiple possibilities before selecting one.
- **Prototype** — make the smallest real test.
- **Collaborate** — recruit people with different knowledge or stakes.
- **Adapt** — learn from consequences.
- **Share** — make the insight usable by others.

The graphic novel should work as a story even if the reader never sees the learning framework.

## Proposals under consideration

Nothing in this section is canon.

- Define one visible local system through which "prosperity" can become dramatic rather than abstract.
- Give principal characters genuinely different definitions of what a successful outcome would be.
- Let an apparently successful intervention create a second-order consequence that forces the heroes to redefine the problem.
- End with changed capability and a new question, not a claim that prosperity has been permanently solved.

## Open decisions for the Editor-in-Chief

1. Where does this EVOKE Prosperity story take place?
2. How far in the future?
3. What concrete system carries the story: food, water, energy, housing, work, mobility, finance, education, climate adaptation, or another domain?
4. Who is the reader following?
5. What does each major character initially believe prosperity means?
6. What visible pressure makes action urgent?
7. What real-world behavior should a learner feel capable of trying after reading?
8. Target page count and delivery format?
