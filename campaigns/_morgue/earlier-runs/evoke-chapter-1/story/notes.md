# Continuity and Critique: Chapter 1 - The Inheritance

## Verdict
**Needs a pass.** The narrative beats and visual contrast are strong, but there are significant "Animation-Board" pacing issues on Page 1 and a critical contradiction regarding the "Debt" mechanism that needs to be resolved to ensure the systemic pressure feels real rather than arbitrary.

## Findings by Severity

### Blocker
- **Page 1, Panel 5-6 / Page 2, Panel 1**: **Causal Gap in Debt Mechanism.** The script introduces the debt via a wrist-comm alert, then immediately jumps to a public "Debt Board." It is unclear if the debt is a private liability that just became public, or if the "Legacy Transfer" is a public event. If the transfer is a systemic "event," the wrist-comm is redundant; if it's private, the immediate public shaming on the board feels like a teleportation of information.
    - **Repair**: Clarify if the "Legacy Transfer" triggers a public broadcast. The "Debt Board" should be the primary source of humiliation, with the wrist-comm acting as the personal "lock" that prevents him from leaving.
    - **Role**: Scripter

### Major
- **Page 1, Panels 2-4**: **Animation-Board Syndrome.** We have four panels to show Alex fixing a valve. This is too many micro-steps for a scene that is meant to establish "competence" and "stagnation." It sags the pacing before the catalyst.
    - **Repair**: Combine Panels 2, 3, and 4 into two panels. One for the action/effort, one for the result/exit. Trust the reader to infer the "listening to the metal" through a single well-composed shot.
    - **Role**: Penciller / Scripter

- **Page 4, Panel 1**: **Character Introduction / Pacing.** The Grandmother is introduced and exits the scene in a single panel. This feels like "plot puppetry"—she exists only to hand over the book.
    - **Repair**: Give the Grandmother a moment of characterization or a specific interaction that reinforces the "Choice" theme. Perhaps she is resisting the "numbers" in her own way.
    - **Role**: Scripter

### Minor
- **Page 2, Panel 5**: **Visual Continuity.** Alex is clutching a "small metal chip." The Bible mentions he turns this when anxious. This is a good plant, but the script should explicitly link this gesture to his internal vow to ensure the visual shorthand is established.
    - **Repair**: Specify the "turning" motion in the panel description.
    - **Role**: Penciller

- **Page 4, Panel 6**: **Perspective Shift.** The jump from the intimate amber glow of the workshop to a bird's-eye view of Oasis is a massive spatial leap. While narratively effective, it risks disorienting the reader if not framed as a "system view."
    - **Repair**: Ensure the panel clearly depicts a screen or a map interface to signal we are now seeing through Alpha/Mera's eyes.
    - **Role**: Penciller

## Plausibility Ledger
- **The Debt (T)**: The concept of inheritable debt as a "systemic cage" is canon. The implementation as a digital "Legacy Transfer" holds up.
- **The Workshop (L)**: The existence of a pre-Alpha analog blind spot in Keel is a license used to allow the bot's survival. This is consistent with the Bible's description of Adrian's final transmission.

## Continuity Ledger
- **Alex's State**: Competent technician $\rightarrow$ Systemic debtor $\rightarrow$ Activated legacy user.
- **Possessions**: Inherited metal chip (P2), Leather-bound book (P4), Brass plate (P4).
- **Knowledge**: Alex knows he is in debt; Ada suspects Adrian's story is incomplete; Mera/Alpha now know Alex has activated the bot.

## Questions for the Editor-in-Chief
- **The Debt's Nature**: Should the 418k credit balance be a "hard" limit (e.g., he cannot buy a ticket out of Keel) or a "social" limit (e.g., he is marked as a failure)? The script treats it as both; confirming the specific systemic restriction will help the Scripter sharpen the stakes.
