# Chapter 1 — Page 4: WHAT PEOPLE TOLD HIM

## Page Purpose

Complicate Alex's inherited story about Adrian. Use his grandmother's memory and Ada's skepticism to introduce the possibility that the public version of his father's disappearance is incomplete or wrong.

## Page Description

Alex shows Ada an old photograph while turning Adrian's casino chip in his hand. He explains that his grandmother sometimes mistakes him for Adrian and tells stories that contradict everything Keel believes. She insists Adrian was working, not gambling. Ada pushes Alex to separate his own memories from what others have told him. He admits he remembers nothing because he was two. The page closes with Ada quietly saying Adrian would not simply leave.

## Dialogue / On-Page Text

- ADA: How's your grandma?
- ALEX: Some days she knows me. Other days she calls me Adrian...
- ALEX: The hardest part isn't that she forgets. It's that she makes up crazy stories about him.
- ADA: What kind of stories?
- ALEX: That he wasn't gambling. That he was working.
- ADA: What do you actually remember about him?
- ALEX: Nothing. I was two.
- ADA: So everything else is what people told you. What if they were wrong?
- ALEX: Maybe... Or maybe they're right. Either way... he's gone.
- ADA: He wouldn't just leave.

## Layout

Six panels. Top row: close detail of the photograph/chip, then a two-shot. Middle: one wide panel against the sunset. Lower row: tight Ada close-up paired with a wider two-shot at the overlook. Final panel is a wide dusk walk-away image with Ada lingering behind Alex.

## Visual / Story Notes

Let the light fall from sunset gold into blue dusk over the page. Adrian's photograph should remain emotionally important but visually incomplete. Ada's final line should read as ambiguous enough to apply to Adrian and, by extension, Alex.
