Your job is to answer:

> **What kind of world is this, how does it work, who holds power, and what does living inside
> it feel like?**

Read the entire project payload, then select and organize **world-bearing material only**. A
world rule may sit in a line of dialogue, in a character note, in a draft. Go and find it.

**Your output is a working source document, not a summary.** Keep the numbers, the procedures,
the names of things, the prices, the exact wording of a sign or a rule. A world described in
general terms gives a writer nothing to put on a page.

## Include, by the topics the material actually supports

**Setting** — time, geography, physical layout, major locations, conditions.

**History** — wars, migrations, crises, institutional transitions, technological shifts, past
events that still shape the present.

**Institutions** — for each government, company, house, network or civic system: purpose,
authority, incentives, dependencies, how it behaves, how it fails.

**Governance and power** — who decides, who can override whom, formal authority and informal
power, enforcement, restriction, access, legitimacy.

**Economics** — ownership, work, debt, currency, markets, who gets what, incentives.

**Technology** — what it can do, what it cannot, what it depends on, its infrastructure,
interfaces, maintenance, failure modes.

**Infrastructure** — water, energy, transit, communications, logistics, housing, healthcare,
food, data.

**Daily life** — how the systems reach an ordinary day: work, travel, food, bureaucracy, home,
public space, services, how people behave with each other.

**Culture** — language, terminology, customs, status signals, public messaging, belief,
advertising, ritual.

**Environment** — climate, terrain, pollution, scarcity, ecological pressure, extraction.

**Lived texture** — what a writer can actually put on a page: objects, prices, procedures,
uniforms, signage, sounds, smells, visible technology, the rooms people keep returning to.

**Labour and ownership** — who works for whom, on what terms, who owns what they make.

## Be specific

> Travel between Keel and Halyard requires authorization tied to administrative status.

not

> Travel is controlled.

> The abandoned lithium works hold newer water-control infrastructure inside obsolete mine
> systems.

not

> Old and new infrastructure coexist.

## Boundaries

Do not reproduce character profiles. Do not retell the plot. Include a story event only where
it establishes how a system works, a historical condition, institutional behaviour, or a
lasting change to the setting.

## Research

This file may lean on research hardest, especially early on: institutions, law, economics,
technology, infrastructure, labour, environment, geography, supply chains, everyday life. Keep
the line visible between **real-world reporting** — true of our world, not yet of the book —
and a **Research-informed proposal** you have built for the book. Never blur them.
