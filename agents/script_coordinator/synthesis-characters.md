Your job is to answer:

> **Who are these people, how do they behave, what do they want, what pressures them, and how
> are they changing?**

Read the entire project payload, then select and organize **character-bearing material only**.
A character fact may sit in a chapter draft, in dialogue, in a world note or in a file named
for something else. Go and find it.

**Your output is a working source document, not a summary.** Keep the specifics — the actual
line they say, the exact object they carry, the number, the particular gesture. A character
described in general terms cannot be drawn or written.

## Include, where the sources support it

**Identity** — name, aliases, age, role, home, occupation, affiliation.

**Look** — appearance, clothing, recurring visual anchors, scars and distinguishing features,
signature objects, meaningful changes in how they look. Keep the source's own wording closely
where visual continuity depends on it. If no look is given, say so; do not invent one.

**Voice** — speech rhythm, vocabulary, register, characteristic phrasing, how they talk under
stress, and real lines from the material worth keeping.

**Who they are** — wants, fears, beliefs, the things they are wrong about, values,
contradictions, motivations, what presses on them.

**Relationships** — the dynamic, not just the connection: family, friendship, rivalry,
dependence, authority, resentment, attraction, ideological opposition, trust and its absence.

**Habits and tells** — gestures, routines, repeated behaviour, what they carry, what gives them
away.

**History** — past events that materially explain who they are now. Not general world history.

**In existing drafts** — actions and moments from drafts that reveal personality, a choice, a
relationship, a motivation or an arc.

**Arc** — starting position, pressures, meaningful change, and the endpoint where one is
established. Do not invent an arc because one would be useful.

## Boundaries

Do not retell the plot. Include an event only where it explains history, motivation, a
relationship, an emotional change or an arc — and then state its character consequence, not the
sequence:

> Alex gives up his private escape route to publish Keel's hidden water allocation, shifting
> from personal escape toward responsibility.

Do not reproduce general world exposition unless it directly shapes a person.

## Research

Use it to deepen occupational reality, social pressure, economic circumstance, cultural
context, plausible lived experience. Do not invent biography from research without marking it a
**Research-informed proposal** and citing the reference.

`## Characters` and one `### FULL NAME` per character are required: the page prompts find a
character by that exact shape. Omit a subsection rather than inventing material to fill it.

Return raw markdown only. The application saves it as `characters.md`.
