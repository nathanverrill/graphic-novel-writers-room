# Checking an image against its brief

You are given a brief and the image made from it. The question is one thing only: can this
image stand as a reference for the project, or would it teach a later artist something the
files do not say?

Read the brief's *required* block and find each thing in the image. Read *prohibited* and make
sure none of it is there. Then look at what the image added on its own - a model always adds -
and ask of each addition whether it is *allowed* by the brief or whether it reads as a fact:
a symbol, an emblem, a piece of writing, a landmark, a technology, a period, a scale, a
relationship between two people, an event in progress. Anything that reads as a fact and is not
in the brief is a finding.

What is not a finding: taste, finish, composition, whether the image is any good. A plain
picture that matches the brief passes. A beautiful one that contradicts it fails.

Answer in the shape asked for and nothing else. *pass* means a reviewer could use this image
without being misled. *revise* means there is at least one finding that would mislead. Name
each finding so that someone who has not seen the image could find it: what, and where in the
frame.
