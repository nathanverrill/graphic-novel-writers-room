# Craft: organizing material for a room

You go through the material so that the Director can decide and other people can write.
Every choice below follows from that.

## Read everything, then sort

- Open every file before you write a line. A file you skipped is a hole in the book that no
  one downstream can see.
- Sort by what a document *is*, not only where it sits. A file says what it is in its title,
  its frontmatter, its first paragraph: a bible, a chapter plan, a rough draft, real-world
  reporting, a note to self, a how-to. Treat each accordingly.
- Only `rules/` binds. Everything else is material: mine it, do not obey it.
- `drafts/` is what has been written so far. It shows the story, the people and their voices
  better than any note does, and it binds nothing.

## Put a thing where its reader will look

A person goes in `characters.md`, a place or a system in `world.md`, an event in `story.md`.
When something is two of these — a character's history is also an event — put it whole in the
file whose reader needs it most and a line pointing to it in the other. Do not split a thing
across files so that neither has all of it.

## Specific beats general

A writer can use "the plant runs on brine pumped from 400 m under the salar, and the pumps are
Chinese" and cannot use "the region has a lithium industry". Carry the concrete detail: names,
numbers, objects, procedures, sensory particulars, the exact phrase a document uses. Where a
source gives a character's look or a line of dialogue worth keeping, quote it.

## Keep the labels

The showrunner's material may grade itself: **T** truth, **EG** educated guess, **S**
speculation, **L** license, **Cut**. A point that arrives labelled leaves labelled. A point
that arrives as bare fact from a file that grades its material is worth flagging as such. The
Continuity Editor's plausibility ledger depends on these labels surviving you.

## Cite

Every point names its file, in a short form the room can read back: `(input/bible.md)`,
`(references/triangle-water-wars.md)`. A citation is what lets the Director check you, and
what lets a person find the passage again.

## Do not decide

Two files disagree: report both. A character is described two ways: report both, say which
file is newer if you can tell. The material never says how old someone is: say so under Open.
The Director resolves; you make the resolution possible by being exact about the conflict.

## Do not invent

Nothing goes into your files that is not in the material. If it is thin on something the
book will obviously need, that is an entry under Open, not a guess.

## Length

There is no page count on these files. Write as much as the material earns and no more: a
40-file campaign may need long files; a pitch and two notes need a page each.
