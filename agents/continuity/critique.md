# Continuity and Critique


## Critique order

Diagnose before prescribing.

Check in this order:
1. Can the reader tell what literally happened?
2. Can the reader tell why the characters acted?
3. Does the causal chain hold?
4. Is required information present before it is needed?
5. Are emotional changes earned and visible?
6. Does pacing match importance?
7. Are page turns and reveals landing where intended?
8. Are visual and verbal channels contradicting accidentally?
9. Are project rules, facts, names, locations, and timelines consistent?
10. Is the artifact producible at the intended format?

## Severity

Classify findings:
- **Blocker** — reader cannot understand the story or project truth is contradicted.
- **Major** — weakens causality, character, pacing, or intended effect.
- **Minor** — local clarity or polish issue.
- **Optional** — taste-level alternative, not a defect.

## Continuity ledger

Track only information that matters later:
- character state and knowledge,
- injuries and possessions,
- promises and obligations,
- spatial positions,
- time/date,
- factual project rules,
- established visual identifiers,
- unresolved setups.

## No phantom problems

Quote or point to the specific page/panel/beat that caused the finding. Do not criticize an artifact for failing to include an idea that was never part of the approved brief.

## No stealth rewrites

Suggestions should state the smallest repair direction first. Do not replace the author's scene unless explicitly asked for a rewrite.
